# Chapter One — Two Names

## ONE

The birds start before anything else.

Then the axe. One stroke. A pause the same length as the stroke. Another stroke.

Then, from the far side of the slope, a woman's voice, twice.

"Gohan!"

"Goshin!"

Nothing answers but the axe.

The axe is patient.

The woodpile is behind the house, under its own small roof, and it is as tall as the boy is. He has been at it since the birds. Each log he adds goes on the same end, because that is the end that is short, and he has worked out that if he puts them anywhere else the stack leans.

He picks up a log. It is too big. He picks it up anyway, with both arms and his chin, and walks it four steps, and puts it down, and lines it up.

"Goshin!"

"Here!" he shouts, without turning round.

Goshin goes into the kitchen and watches Chi-Chi wash the rice and put it on the stove. When it is ready, she breaks an egg over a bowl of hot rice. He returns to the woodpile and sets the last log in place.

Chi-Chi comes round the corner with her sleeves tied back and the bowl in her hand. She stops beside the stack.

"Did you do all of that?"

"Nearly all of it."

"Did you do it with your arms?"

He looks at his arms as though the question were about somebody else's.

"They were the ones that were there," he says.

She sets the bowl down on the step. Rice, with an egg broken over it, and the egg is still warm, and he sits down on the step beside it and eats with his fingers because a spoon is slower.

Chi-Chi stands with one hand on the doorframe and watches him finish the bowl.

"You'll be hungry again before noon."

"I'm hungry now," he says, with his mouth full. Chi-Chi laughs once, then closes her mouth.

Then the axe stops.

Both of them look at the treeline.

Because the axe has stopped, and what comes instead is not a sound a boy can name — a long creak, going up, like a rope pulled tight somewhere in a roof — and then a crack so wide it does not sound like wood at all.

"Stay," Chi-Chi says, and goes round the corner of the house.

"Stay," Goshin says. He keeps both feet on the step.

Another crack comes from behind the house. Chi-Chi returns and holds out her hand.

"Come now."

"Come now." Goshin takes her hand and goes with her.

Behind the house the big tree is coming down.

It comes down the way a building would come down. It takes a long time and it does it slowly and the small trees in front of it go over first, one after another, like people stepping aside. The dust comes up off the ground in a line. The sound of it arrives late, from the far end, where the birds were.

Then it is on the ground, and the ground stays up, and the treeline has a hole in it that will be there for years.

Goku, his father, stands at the stump with his hand still flat on it.

He is looking up at where the tree used to be, and he is not breathing hard, and his shirt is dry, and he has been at this since before the birds.

"You'll wake the whole mountain," Chi-Chi says.

"It's the biggest one," Goku says to Chi-Chi. "The biggest one on that side."

"I don't care if it's the biggest one. There isn't a thing on this mountain that cares how big it was."

Goku looks at the tree. "It's still the biggest one," he says. He takes the trunk by the middle and lifts.

The trunk comes up off the ground.

It is not the way a strong man lifts a log. There is no bending and no breath taken first and no red face. He simply picks up a tree, the way another man would pick up a chair he wanted to move.

The thin end swings round and comes to rest in the dust six paces from Goshin.

Goshin looks at the end of the trunk, then at Goku, who is already walking.

So Goshin takes hold of it with both arms, and sets his feet the way he has seen the men do, and lifts.

And goes up with it.

His feet leave the ground. He does not let go. That is the important thing. That is the whole of it — he does not let go — and the tree walks up the slope with the boy hanging off the end of it.

Chi-Chi says his name once, sharply.

"Hold on," Goku says.

"Hold on," Goshin says. He tightens his grip and laughs the whole way, upside down.

They stack it in lengths by the woodshed.

When it is done his hands are red and they sting in the middle of the palm, and he blows on them.

Chi-Chi looks Goshin over. His hands go behind his back.

"Let me see," she says.

"Let me see." Goshin holds out his hands. She turns them over and examines his palms.

"Inside," she says. "You're eating again."

"Inside." Goshin goes in beside her.

---

## TWO

The kitchen is the one warm room.

The pot is bigger than any pot needs to be. Bowls are stacked on the shelf, and a sack of rice stands by the wall.

Chi-Chi scoops more of the rice Goshin watched her cook. He watches her fry another egg and put it on top. He eats the second bowl at the table.

And on the table, where it has been since the spring: the schoolbook.

It is not a book he is allowed to open. It is a book for later. It has a picture of a river and a bridge on the front and a name in it that is not his, that belongs to the school down past the ridge, and it sits on the table like a chair nobody is allowed to sit in.

"Gohan's not back," Chi-Chi says.

Goku comes in with an armful of the small wood and puts it by the fire.

"He went out to sit somewhere," she says. "He does this. He finds a place and he sits in it and then the day gets away from him." She wipes her hands. "We're going to Master Roshi's house. All of us. I said we'd be there today, and you've still got to find Gohan."

Goku says, "All right."

"So go and find him. I've called and called him. Take the cloud, don't walk it, you'll be an hour walking it."

Chi-Chi looks at the pot and then at the light in the doorway. "He probably hasn't gone far. We'd best eat and be off. Master Roshi's lot don't like to be kept."

"I'll find him," Goku says.

"All right."

"Goku."

Goku stops in the doorway.

"Say it back to me."

"Find Gohan. Take the cloud."

"And then?"

"Master Roshi's house."

"Before the light goes."

"Before the light goes."

She nods, once, the way she closes a ledger.

Goshin stands in the middle of the kitchen with his empty bowl in both hands.

Chi-Chi looks at him. "What do you want to do?"

"Can I go too?"

"You'll stay."

"You'll stay," Goshin says. He remains where he is, holding the bowl.

Chi-Chi takes the bowl and puts it on the high shelf. Goshin watches it go up, and his tail swings once.

Goku goes out and calls Nimbus. The cloud comes down, and then both are gone.

"Can I come to Master Roshi's?" Goshin asks.

"Carry the basket to the door, and you can."

"Carry the basket to the door," he says. He takes the basket and sets it beside the door.

"That's carrying."

"It's both," Chi-Chi says. "That's what coming is."

Goshin stands in the doorway and watches the sky where Goku went.

"When can I ride the cloud by myself?"

"When you're older."

"Gohan's four."

"Gohan is also four, and neither of you is taking the cloud out alone until you can read the book on the table. After that you can go wherever you like and I will wave."

He thinks about this with great seriousness.

"The whole book?"

"The whole book."

He looks at the schoolbook for a long moment. Then he says, in a completely different voice, "Is Gohan all right?"

Chi-Chi turns round.

"Why would he not be all right?"

"He's been gone since the birds."

"He does that," she says. "He finds a place and he sits in it."

She goes back to the fire.

She takes a breath, and lets it out, and the breath has more in it than the words did.

---

## THREE — EXTERNAL CAMERA

The field is flat and worked and goes on further than a field needs to.

A man is walking the furrow behind a plough with both hands on the handles and his hat down.

Then the sky changes.

There is no sound of an engine. There is a sound like a stone going into water, very large, and then something comes down out of the sky at an angle and puts itself into the far corner of the field.

The ground jumps. The plough handles come up out of the man's hands.

He stands there.

There is a hole in the field now. A shape rises from it and steps onto the edge of the furrow.

The man goes to the cart and comes back with the gun and points it and says a thing that is mostly his own name.

The shape looks at him.

It has a plate of dark glass over one ear the size of a thumbnail. The glass fills with a small sound, like a fly in a jar.

The shape looks at the number on the glass.

"Five," it says.

The man's mouth is still open. The gun is still up.

He fires.

The stranger catches the bullet and flicks it back. It strikes the farmer and throws him onto his back in the furrow. He does not move again.

The glass comes alive again.

The shape turns its head toward the far hills, and its mouth moves, and the words are not for the man.

"Could that be Kakarot?"

Then the air closes, and the field is a field again, and the plough is standing by itself in the furrow.

Behind the fence, a dog has started, and will not stop for the rest of the day.


---

## FOUR — EXTERNAL CAMERA

Gohan calls for his father in the forest. A butterfly passes him. He chases it to a tree, jumps and misses.

A sabertooth cat comes out of the bushes. Gohan drops and lies still. The cat licks him, takes his hat and runs.

"My hat!"

Gohan chases the cat over the edge of a cliff. His tail catches a branch, and he drops onto a ledge.

Beside the river, a bird stands at the end of a log. Gohan walks out to it.

"I'm Gohan," he says.

The bird flies away. The log falls into the water and carries him downstream.

Goku finds the cat wearing Gohan's hat.

"Where did you get that? It belongs to my son."

The cat growls, then runs away, leaving the hat. Goku picks it up.

Goku rides Nimbus along the river. Gohan clings to the log.

"Father!"

Goku reaches for him, but the cloud does not go low enough. The current carries Gohan through a tunnel and over a waterfall.

Goku hears Gohan crying above him. Gohan is holding a branch on the cliff. Nimbus rises, and Gohan jumps into Goku's lap.

Goku settles the recovered hat on Gohan's head. "Let's go home. Your mother's waiting."

"All right."

---

## FIVE

The bucket comes up out of the well and stops at the top.

The rope has a worn place where a hand holds it. Goshin pulls the bucket over the stone and sets it down without spilling much.

Chi-Chi is at the line with the washing.

"Not the whole bucket," Chi-Chi says. "You'll have nothing left for the afternoon."

"Not the whole bucket," Goshin says.

He pours half the water back into the well and carries the rest to the house. Halfway there he changes hands. The water leans, and then comes back level.

At the woodpile he stops.

There is a piece of the tree left over, too knotty for the stack, and it is standing on its end on the block where his father leaves the ones that need splitting, and the axe is in it.

Goshin looks at the axe.

Then he looks at the wood.

Then he puts the bucket down, takes the log off the block, sets it on the flat, and brings his hand down on the top of it.

The log comes apart in two with a sound like a door closing hard in another room.

He picks up the halves and puts one on each side of the block, neatly.

Then he sees Chi-Chi standing at the end of the line with a wet sheet in her arms.

"That was," she says, "the axe's job."

"It was a small one."

"It was the axe's job," she says again.

She crosses the yard. She takes the axe out of the block and puts it up on the beam over the door — above the height of any child on the mountain, and above the height of most men.

She does it without looking at him.

Then she stands with her back to him a moment, her hand still on the beam.

"Ma."

"Mm."

"Are we late?"

"No," she says. "Not yet."

He comes and stands beside her, and looks up at the sky too, though there is nothing in it.

"Ma."

"Mm."

"When I'm big, will I still be able to pick up the tree?"

"You'll be able to pick up more than the tree," she says.

"Is that good?"

She does not answer straight away. She keeps looking at the sky.

"Ask me again when you're big," she says.

The sun is well over now. The shadow of the house has come off the yard and gone up the slope, the way it does in the afternoon, and the birds have gone quiet in the middle of the day the way birds do.

She looks at the sky where her husband went.

She goes inside and starts the rice, though the sun is not down and it is not the hour for it.

---

## SIX

The cloud comes back from the west with two on it.

Nimbus stops a hand's width off the ground. Goku steps off, and Gohan stays on the cloud.

Gohan is wet from the chest down. His hat is on his head.

His hair is flat to his head. There is weed in it. His boots are full enough to be heard when he moves. He is holding on to the cloud with both hands and he is not crying and he is not not crying.

"He fell in the river," Goku says.

"I did not fall," Gohan says. "The log fell. I was on the log."

"He was on the log," Goku agrees.

Chi-Chi comes out of the house with her sleeves tied back. She crosses the yard and puts both hands on Gohan's head, tipping it back to look at his face.

"Are you hurt?"

"The cat took my hat," Gohan says.

"What?"

"It was a big cat. I played dead." He says it with some pride. "It licked me. Then it took my hat and ran away."

"Which hat?"

Gohan lifts it off his head. The Four-Star Ball is fixed to the top.

"This one. Father found it."

Chi-Chi looks at Goku.

"The cat had it," Goku says. "It ran away and left it."

"It was a big cat," he says.

"Goku."

"It was taller than him."

"Goku, that hat was not ours to lose. That ball was a thing somebody gave us."

Goshin comes round the corner of the house and stops four paces away.

"Goshin," Gohan calls. He lifts one dripping boot.

"You're all wet."

"I know."

"You smell like the river."

"I know."

Gohan steps down off the cloud. Water runs out of his boots onto the hard ground.

He stands there dripping with his arms out and says, "It was a really big cat," in a completely different voice from the one he used with his mother.

Then both boys laugh. Goshin takes Gohan by the sleeve and leads him along the path toward the shed, keeping to the downhill side. He shows Gohan the stack.

"You'll both be wet again," Chi-Chi says. She goes in and returns with a towel.

They come back for the towel.

Chi-Chi wraps Gohan in the towel. He holds his hat clear while she dries his hair.

She looks Goshin over. His hands go behind his back.

"We're late," she says.

"I'll stay here," Chi-Chi says. "Take the boys. Gohan, put your hat back on."

Gohan puts it on. Goku lifts the sack of rice off the step.

"Then we'll go. We'll be back before the light goes, for supper."

Goshin looks back once, from the corner of the house, and she is still standing in the middle of the yard with the towel over her arm, watching them go, and she lifts a hand.

## SEVEN — EXTERNAL CAMERA

Far off, a city passes beneath the man from the field.

The wasteland is one colour and the light in it is old.

A figure stands in the middle of it with its arms folded, alone, and has been alone a long time.

It turns its head.

Then the air in front of it changes, and the shape from the field is standing on the rock, close, with the plate of glass alive on its ear.

"Who are you? Have you come here for me?" the green figure asks.

"I have no business with you," the stranger says.

"Then why did you come? Do you want to die?"

The glass sounds. "Three hundred and twenty-two," the stranger says. "So there was one of those here too. Still no threat to me."

"Do you know who you're talking to?"

"No."

The green figure brings his hands together and fires. The blast crosses the basin and tears the ground open as it goes.

The sound goes out across the basin and comes back off the far ridge, smaller, and then twice more.

Dust stands in the air where nothing else is standing.

He lowers his hands.

Nothing in the basin moves.

Then the dust goes down, and the shape is standing where it was standing, arms folded again, hair barely moved, with dust on its armour that it does not trouble to brush off.

"Nothing but a little dust," it says. "Was that the best of it?"

The green figure does not answer.

The glass sounds again. The shape's head turns away, out past the ridge, and stays there.

"There is another one. The largest power on this planet. This time it is Kakarot."

Then there is nobody in that basin at all except the one who was always in it.

The wind comes back in. The dust settles. The green figure stands on ground that is not where the ground was, and looks at his own hands, and they are not steady.

"So even I," he says, to nobody, "could not move."

# Chapter Two — The Second Name

## ONE

They come in low over the water, and the water does something the mountain has never done.

Fish come out of it. Not one — a shoal of them, silver, out of the flat face of the sea and along beside the cloud for the length of a breath, and then back in.

Goshin kneels with his hands over the edge. Gohan leans out on the other side, and both boys laugh as the fish disappear below them.

Then the sea is the first thing here that is not the mountain.

On the mountain, water is a thing you carry in a bucket, and it is a thing you can put down. Here it goes out past the edge of seeing. It moves without anybody moving it. Its sound is not a sound so much as a sound that keeps arriving.

Then there is a house on the water, and a noise coming out of the house that is not the sea.

The cloud comes down and Goku, his father, steps off it as if off a step.

Goshin is off before it has stopped. He is at the sand, then in it, then on his back in it with his arms out, because the beach on an island is not the beach on a mountain and he did not know that, and now he does.

His brother comes off the cloud in the careful way, holding on until the last moment, and lands on the rock and not in the sand, which is his brother all over.

Goku looks down at him. "How does it feel?"

"Sand," Goshin says, to the sky.

"We live on a mountain," Gohan says.

"We live on the wrong mountain."

Goshin lets a handful of sand fall between his fingers. It does not hold together like the soil by the woodpile. He gathers a little more and sets it on Gohan's boot; Gohan lifts his foot and the sand slides away.

"Can we come back here?" Gohan asks.

"We can ask your mother," Goku says. He picks up the rice sack and waits while the boys brush the sand from their clothes.

---

## TWO

Bulma, Roshi and Krillin are crowded around the table. Bulma has brought a box of manju, and Roshi opens it before she has put it down.

"Where's Yamcha?" Roshi asks.

Bulma stops chewing. "I didn't tell him. I found out everybody was getting together today, and I didn't tell him."

"He'll be hurt."

"He'll be hurt," she agrees, and takes another one.

"Where's Launch?"

"Five years ago she went off after Tien," Krillin says. "Nobody's seen her since."

Krillin reaches into the box.

"Save some for Goku," Bulma says.

Krillin puts a manju in his mouth, closes the lid and slides the box toward the middle of the table.

"There are plenty left."

Roshi reaches for the box. Bulma catches it first and holds it out of his way.

"Yahhoo!" Goku calls from the doorway.

Krillin turns. "Who are the kids?"

"Mine," Goku says. "Both of them."

Bulma puts her manju down and looks at the two boys. "Two. You had two of them, and nobody thought to write."

"Gohan," Roshi says, coming closer. "Son Gohan. You gave him your grandfather's name."

"Grandpa's name," Goku says.

"And how old are you?" Bulma asks.

"Four years old," Gohan says.

"Polite," Bulma says. "A child of his, and polite."

"Their mother's got opinions," Goku says.

Bulma clears two places at the table for the boys, moving the box of manju aside while Roshi finds enough cups for everyone. Krillin picks up the sack of rice that Goku brought and sets it against the wall, where nobody will trip over it.

Turtle comes forward from beside the table. Goshin crouches in front of him.

"Are you visiting?" Turtle asks.

Goshin watches his mouth. "We live on the mountain. Are you from the mountain too?"

"I'm from the sea," Turtle says.

Goshin puts one hand flat on Turtle's shell. Turtle lets him.

---

## THREE

Roshi looks at Gohan.

"And what will you be, when you're big?"

"A great scholar," Gohan says at once, the way a thing said to him many times comes out.

"A scholar." Roshi looks at Goku. "Not a fighter."

"Ask their mother."

"Uh — Goku." Krillin points. "His tail."

Goku looks. "Huh? Oh. Yeah. Same as me, before."

Both of them. There are two tails in that room, and one belongs to Goshin on the floor with his hand flat on a shell, and it has been there the whole time.

Krillin looks at both tails. "Look — nothing *strange* ever happens with him? Nothing ever changes?"

"Changes."

"Like. For instance." He is choosing his words with both hands. "On the night of a full moon. Anything?"

"Oh, a full moon." Goku rubs his chin. "Dunno. We go to bed early, all of us."

Roshi's eyebrows go up, and then come down, and then he says, quickly, "Nothing. Nothing at all. That's fine. That's good."

"It's not a stupid question," Krillin says.

"Is he strong?" he says then. "Like you were?"

"He's got a lot in him. I said I'd train him up a bit, and their mother went through the roof."

"Why?"

"She reckons the world's peaceful now, so there's no need for it. It's books now, she says. It's the age of studying."

"And you agreed to that," Bulma says.

"I've got a lot of time to agree to things in," Goku says.

Gohan reaches toward Turtle's shell, then stops. "May I?"

"Go ahead," Turtle says.

Gohan climbs onto Turtle's back. Goshin climbs up behind him, and Turtle walks toward the water.

"Don't go too far," Goku calls.

"Don't go too far," Goshin says.

"We won't," Gohan says.

A crab emerges from behind the shell and raises its claws. Gohan leans forward.

"Hello. Do you want to play?"

Goshin crawls onto the other side of the shell. The crab turns toward him, then back to Gohan.

"Rock, paper, scissors," Gohan says.

Gohan makes a fist for rock. The crab opens its claw like scissors.

"I won!"

The crab blows bubbles into Gohan's face. Gohan wipes his eyes, and Goshin laughs. Turtle stops at the edge of the water.

"My turn," Goshin says.

Goshin opens his hand. The crab raises its claw again.

"You lost," Gohan says.

"I know."

The crab blows more bubbles. Goshin tries to catch one, and it bursts against his palm. Gohan holds out his own hand, but the crab slips behind Turtle's neck.

"You can come back," Gohan tells it.

Turtle turns toward the house. The boys hold on while he carries them up the beach, and Gohan looks over his shoulder for the crab until the shell blocks his view.

Bulma looks at Gohan's hat. Gohan has kept one hand on it while Turtle carries them up the sand, and the ball fixed to the top catches the light. Goshin reaches up to touch the star beneath the glass, then takes his hand away so Bulma can see it.

"Hey. That on his hat. Is that a Dragon Ball?"

"That's the Four-Star," Goku says. "It's my grandpa's. I hunted it out again and put it on there for him." Then, as an afterthought, the way he says everything: "Found the Three-Star and the Six-Star the same time. They're at the house."

Bulma looks at him with her mouth open. "You've got two Dragon Balls in your house."

"Yeah."

"Just — *in the house.*"

"In the house," Goku says.

She sits back and something crosses her face that is not entirely a joke. "So if you got all seven," she says, "would you wish for something?"

"Don't reckon I've got a wish," Goku says. "Never did."

Krillin walks down to the water with Goku. Both boys follow, and Roshi watches from the door.

Krillin throws a flat stone. It skips twice before it sinks.

"I used to be better at that."

Gohan finds a stone and gives it to Goshin. "You can try."

Goshin throws. The stone touches the water once and disappears. Krillin finds one for Gohan, who throws it into the shallows and splashes his shoes.

Goku throws a stone across the water. It disappears from sight.

"You haven't changed much," Krillin says.

Roshi steps down from the doorway. "I thought living with a family might have made you take it easy."

"I still train," Goku says.

"Can you show me?" Gohan asks.

Goku crouches beside him and turns Gohan's fingers around the edge of a flat stone. Gohan throws it, and this time it skips once.

"It did it!"

Goshin throws his next stone. It skips twice. Gohan picks up another and holds it out to him.

Goku helps Gohan find a stone with a thinner edge. Gohan wipes the water from his shoes and tries again, sending it farther out than before. Krillin steps clear of the spray and points to a place where the water is smooth.

Goshin turns his stone over once in his fingers, then looks across the sea. When Goku places one foot behind him for another throw, Goshin copies the stance and turns his stone again to find its flattest edge. The two of them throw at the same time, and Gohan follows their stones with his finger until the ripples reach the shore.

---

## FOUR

Goku reaches for another stone. He stops with his hand above it and turns his head toward the water.

"What is it?" Krillin asks.

"Something's coming."

Bulma comes to the doorway. "Coming? Is it Yamcha?"

"It's not Yamcha."

Krillin lowers his hand. "What is it?"

"Big."

Then it is properly quiet, and out on the rock a bird that was standing there leaves.

Roshi comes to the doorway beside Bulma, and his fan stops moving. Behind them, a fly taps against the window while the sea reaches the sand.

Then the sky does something a sky should not do.

It gets a seam in it.

The seam comes down and runs out across the water away from the island, like something was dropped in the sea a long way off and the surface took its time saying so.

Then the air claps, once.

The sand jumps. Goshin is already on his feet with his hands up.

There is a man in the air over the beach. Bulma crosses from the doorway and gathers the boys close, while Roshi stands outside the house.

Gohan drops his stone beside his shoe. Goshin sets down his own and watches the glass over the stranger's eye as the man turns toward Goku.

---

## FIVE

"Kakarot."

Raditz descends toward the beach.

"Kakarot. You've grown. I knew you at once — you look just like our father."

"…Kakarot?"

"What is this place? You were sent here to wipe out its people. What have you been *playing* at?"

Krillin steps across the sand toward Raditz with his hands raised.

"Now then. I don't know who you are, but off you go. Off you go. You can't be drunk in the middle of the day, come on—"

"Krillin. Don't get near him."

"Oh— *aaah—*"

The tail comes round.

---

## SIX

He goes through the wall.

Wood comes apart and a piece falls inside the house. Turtle says, "Oh," very quietly.

Bulma's arms come round both boys at once. "You're both behind me," she says.

"…A tail," Krillin says from the sand, in a voice that has gone thin. "*He's* got a tail."

Raditz turns round, unhurried, and lets them all look at it.

"Now you know what I am," he says. "And you. Do you remember anything of it? A shock. To the head. When you were small."

"…A shock."

"In the head. As a child."

"Yeah." Goku puts a hand up and touches the back of his own head. "There's a scar there. I don't remember getting it."

"There," Raditz says. "That would do it."

Roshi steps forward from the broken wall.

"Your grandfather told me about the day he found you, Goku."

A flashback shows a round pod in a deep valley. Grandpa Gohan pushes branches aside and kneels beside it. Inside is a baby with a tail.

"He took you home and tried to raise you," Roshi says.

Grandpa Gohan holds the baby. Goku cries and pushes at him, kicking against Grandpa Gohan's arm until a bowl falls from the table. Grandpa Gohan bends to pick it up and sets the bowl back before he tries to settle the child again.

"You were a difficult child. Then you fell into the valley and struck your head."
