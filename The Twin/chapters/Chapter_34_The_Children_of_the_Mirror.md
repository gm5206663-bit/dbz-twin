# Chapter 34 — The Children of the Mirror

*Anime episode reference: Dragon Ball Z, Episode 40 (the children, their history, the meteor swarm and the journey onward, adapted for the living-character branch).*

## Seven Guns

The boy on the ledge looks at the child behind him, then at the weapons hanging near the ceiling.

"Freeza," he says.

The name means nothing to Goshin. It does something to the other children, though. The little girl grips the boy's sleeve, and a taller child pushes out of the passage beside the table.

"Don't tell them anything, Zeshin."

"I'm not telling them where we're going," Zeshin says.

"You told them his name."

"I asked who you were afraid of," Goshin says. "I didn't ask where you're going."

The child from the passage faces him. "You can take our guns without touching them. Why should we believe anything you say?"

"Bun," Zeshin says, "they could have hurt us already."

"Maybe they're waiting."

Bulma folds her arms. "We weren't the ones who filled the corridors with traps. We were trying to get out."

"You broke two of our machines!"

"Your machines shot at our ship!"

The little girl flinches again. Bulma sees it and bites back the rest of what she was going to say. The child's hands are still shaking.

"We didn't know they were machines," Gohan says to Bun. "We stopped firing when we thought somebody might be inside."

Bun looks at him, but his expression does not soften. "You say that now."

Gohan is still holding the seventh gun. He turns its muzzle toward the floor and lays it on the table, away from everyone. Goshin keeps the other six aloft.

"What do you want us to do with them?" Gohan asks Zeshin.

Bun answers first. "Give them back."

"Not while somebody might shoot Bulma," Goshin says.

"We won't shoot if you leave!"

"We'd like to," Bulma says. "Our ship is still clamped down, and the outside door is shut."

Zeshin climbs down from the ledge. He keeps a cautious distance from Piccolo, whose hand is resting on the bent mechanical claw. Up close, Zeshin looks older than Gohan, but not old enough to hide how frightened he is.

"Put them under that," he says, pointing to the transparent dome on its side. "Where we can see them."

Bun turns on him. "Zeshin—"

"They can take them again whenever they want. You saw that." Zeshin looks up at Goshin. "Just don't point them at anybody."

"I won't."

Goshin brings the weapons down slowly. Gohan slides the seventh one beside them, and Piccolo turns the dome over to cover the little pile. Bun watches every movement. The guns are still there for the children to take once the visitors leave.

"I'm Bulma," she says. "And this is Piccolo."

"We know their names," Bun says. "They told us."

"You didn't know ours. I thought that might help."

"It doesn't."

Piccolo releases the claw. "What are you going to do with us?"

Zeshin glances toward the passage where feet have been moving behind the wall. "I have to ask the others."

A shrill tone interrupts him.

Another answers it from farther inside the ship. The children stop talking. Zeshin looks up at a speaker over the door.

"That's the forward watch."

A smaller boy bursts into the dining room. "Zeshin! The rocks are coming right at us!"

The floor jumps beneath them. Plates slide off the table and break. The little girl falls against Zeshin's leg; Gohan catches the edge of the table before it strikes her.

A second impact sounds through the hull. Somewhere beyond the door, metal tears with a long, high squeal.

"They're not on the course!" the boy cries.

Zeshin runs for the passage. Bun follows him as far as the doorway, then stops. A much younger child is standing there, white-faced.

"Naro's still in the sleeping room," she says.

Bun wheels around. "I told him to come with you."

"He went back for his blanket."

The ship lurches again. A panel drops from the ceiling toward the group at the wall.

Piccolo's arm stretches across the room. His hand closes around the falling panel before it reaches the children. He lifts it clear and places it against a pillar.

"Move away from that ceiling," he tells them.

Gohan turns to Bun. "Where's the sleeping room?"

"Down the right passage."

Bun runs. Gohan goes with him.

"Wait!" Zeshin looks from them to Bulma. The forward alarm is still sounding. "You know how to fly your ship?"

"I know how to make it go where I want it to go. Mostly."

"Ours has different controls."

"Then show me."

Bun has disappeared down the passage with Gohan. Zeshin hesitates only a moment longer, then takes Bulma by the hand and pulls her toward the bridge.

"Come on!"

Goshin looks after his brother. Piccolo is already moving the younger children toward an inner doorway. The little girl who fired at Bulma is trying to help another child stand.

"I'll stay here," Goshin tells Piccolo. "Gohan's with Bun."

"Good. Keep them away from the outer wall."

A third blow rolls through the ship, harder than the others. This time the lights go out.

## Inside the Hull

Emergency lamps come on along the floor. Their red glow leads Gohan and Bun through a corridor lined with small doors. An alarm sounds somewhere ahead, followed by the heavy thud of a bulkhead closing.

"Naro!" Bun shouts.

A faint voice answers from behind a door at the end of the corridor. Bun pulls the handle. It moves, but the door does not open.

"Stand back," Gohan says.

Bun does not move immediately. "He's right behind it."

"I'll lift it from this side. I won't push it in."

Gohan gets his fingers beneath the bent edge and pulls. The door folds outward, away from the sleeping room. He sets it aside, and Bun squeezes past him.

A boy sits under a low bed with a blanket wound around his shoulders. A shelf has fallen in front of him, spilling clothes and a little wooden figure across the floor.

"I couldn't get the door," Naro says.

"You were supposed to stay with Miri!"

"I wanted my blanket."

Bun's anger lasts only until the boy crawls out. He puts both arms around Naro, holding him so tightly that the blanket slips.

"Don't do that again," he says into the child's hair.

Naro nods. Bun notices Gohan standing in the doorway and loosens his grip.

"Can you carry him?" Gohan asks.

"Yes."

"Then I'll go first."

They reach the corridor as something strikes the outer hull again. The wall buckles inward, and a length of metal tears loose overhead. Gohan catches it with one hand before it can fall across Bun and Naro. A thin jet of steam hisses from a broken pipe above him.

"Go!" he says.

Bun runs toward the inner doorway, Naro's face buried against his shoulder. Gohan follows, carrying the twisted metal away from them. At the end of the corridor he lowers it against the wall.

The steam is spreading toward an open electrical panel. Gohan remembers the blanket lifting when he gathered his ki in their cabin, and how Piccolo had told him to pay attention to what the movement did around him. He draws the energy close now, keeping it narrow, and presses the broken pipe shut. The hiss dwindles and stops.

The bulkhead behind him has sealed the damaged outer section. No one has to go through it into the airless space beyond. Gohan checks that Bun and Naro are clear, then runs after them.

At the bridge, Bulma braces herself against a chair while Zeshin pulls a lever with both hands. A broad display shows curved lines, a mark for their ship and a scattering of bright points moving toward it.

"Those are the rocks?" she asks.

"The ones the forward scanner can see. The little ones don't always show."

"Which way is the ship turning?"

Zeshin points. Bulma studies the marks for a second, then points to another control. "Can you bring us down underneath that group?"

"I tried. The engines answered, but the next group came up from below."

The ship shudders. One of the bright points vanishes against its mark.

"What about the big one?"

"It wasn't there when we set the course."

"Well, it is now." Bulma leans closer. The projected path of the largest rock crosses the ship's path, with a cluster of smaller ones behind it. "If we turn too soon, we'll run into the rest. Can you hold this heading for a few seconds?"

"I can try."

"Don't try. Tell me which control holds it, and I'll help."

Zeshin shows her. Together they pull two levers until the mark for the ship steadies. An indicator trembles at the edge of the display.

"Your engines need the turn soon," Bulma says. "Do you have anything that can move that rock?"

"The little machines are in the bay. They aren't strong enough for one that size."

The bridge door opens. The little girl who fired at Bulma comes in, followed by Goshin and Piccolo. The other children are in the inner room, with two older ones looking after them. Miri clutches the back of a chair.

"The forward display in the dining room went dark," she tells Zeshin. "Piccolo said this one might still work."

Goshin studies the moving points. "I might be able to push it."

Bulma looks at him. "From in here?"

"I can try. I don't have to break the wall."

Another impact rattles the bridge. Bulma looks back at the projected path, then at the covered outer ports. "Can you show him the rock without showing him the sky?"

Zeshin does not understand. Piccolo explains as he moves to a second screen.

"Their tails. An unknown moon could make them transform."

Miri stares at Goshin's tail. "Transform into what?"

"Something much too big for this room," he says.

Zeshin works a control. A picture appears on the second screen, and Piccolo puts a hand out to keep Goshin back while he checks it. The magnified view is filled by a jagged rock, its surface broken by deep cracks. No moon or open sky is visible in the frame.

"That one is safe to look at," Piccolo says. "Keep it on that view."

Goshin steps beside him. The rock is coming closer. He remembers the guns above the table: six separate shapes held where he wanted them while the children moved below. This time the object is beyond the wall, and far larger.

He raises his hand. His ki presses against the hull; a frame beside the screen rattles. He eases the pressure there and reaches farther, carrying it past the wall toward the rock.

On the screen, the stone shifts.

"It's moving!" Miri cries.

Goshin guides it sideways. The projected path on Bulma's display bends away from the ship. The rock turns, exposing a long pale seam, and slides clear of the heading.

"There's more behind it," Zeshin says.

Bulma points to the cluster. "If we can get through the gap before they reach it—"

Goshin extends his other hand. His ki spreads beyond the first stone and catches several more, turning them away from the path rather than breaking them into a spray against the ship. He feels the answering weight, then the rush of the rocks moving where he sends them.

As the stones turn, Goshin feels himself becoming stronger. The new strength stays when he releases the first of them; there is no red light around him. Piccolo feels the change even as he catches a loosened bridge panel before it falls on Miri.

"Now, Zeshin!" Bulma says.

Zeshin pulls his lever. Bulma moves the second with him. The ship rolls beneath their feet and turns through the opening Goshin has made. The remaining points sweep past the projected path.

One smaller rock still strikes. The impact throws Miri toward the console. Piccolo catches her with his free hand and sets her beside the chair.

Then the forward alarm changes to a slower, intermittent tone.

Zeshin keeps his grip on the lever. Bulma watches the display until the last mark has passed behind them.

"All right," she says. "Ease it back. Slowly."

He does. The ship steadies.

Goshin lowers his hands. On the screen the diverted stones recede, and he watches the gap they left until the last one is clear.

Gohan appears in the doorway with Bun and Naro. "Is everybody all right?"

Miri runs to Naro. "I told you not to go back!"

"I got my blanket."

"You could have got another blanket!"

Naro holds it out to her. Miri takes one end, then pulls him close beneath it. Bun stands behind them, breathing hard. He looks at Gohan, then at Goshin and the screen.

"You moved the rocks," he says.

"Some of them," Goshin replies. "Bulma and Zeshin turned the ship."

Bun's hands close into fists. "Freeza's soldiers can do things like that too."

The quiet that follows is not the quiet Bulma expected after the alarm.

## The People Behind the Name

They gather again in the dining room after the older children have checked the inner rooms. The damaged outer section remains sealed. Zeshin says the air is holding, but he wants to inspect the readings again before they continue on their way.

The seven guns are still beneath the dome. Bun pulls a chair close enough to reach it. Zeshin notices but does not ask him to move.

"You saved Naro," Bun says to Gohan. "And you helped us with the rocks. I saw it."

"Then why do you still think we're soldiers?" Bulma asks.

"Because I've seen what people who can fly and shoot light can do."

His voice has sharpened. Gohan waits, remembering how the children flinched when he stepped toward them before.

Zeshin sits on the edge of the table. "If they wanted to kill us, they had chances. They didn't take them."

"Maybe they want the ship."

"They have a ship. They asked us to let it go."

"Maybe they want to know where we're going."

"We haven't told them."

Miri is beside Naro, still holding the blanket. "The others didn't ask," she says.

Bun looks at her.

"The people who came before," she goes on. "They didn't ask us anything."

For a moment no one speaks. Then Bulma lowers herself onto a chair across from Zeshin, leaving the table between them.

"Who is Freeza?"

Zeshin rubs a mark on the table with his thumb. "The people who attacked our home said they were taking the planet for him. They called him Lord Freeza."

Piccolo's expression changes. "Taking it from whom?"

"From everyone who lived there."

Gohan thinks of Raditz speaking about planets, as if the people on them were things that could be cleared away. He does not say the thought aloud yet.

"What happened?" he asks.

Bun looks down at the little wooden figure Naro is holding. One of its arms is missing. He takes it carefully and turns it over in his hand.

"My father was fixing the wheel on our cart," he says. "It kept making a noise. He told me to fetch the oil."

He remembers the morning clearly enough to hear that noise again.

The cart stands outside their house, one wheel tipped up on a stone. Bun comes back with the oil in a jar and finds his father looking toward the square. A shadow has crossed the roofs. At first Bun thinks a cloud has covered the sun.

A round ship settles beyond the market. Then another comes down near the road. People leave their stalls and gather at a distance. Someone calls a greeting. The first figures to emerge wear hard armour, and one of them has a small device over an eye.

Bun's father puts the cart wheel down.

"Inside," he tells Bun.

A man in the square asks the strangers what they want. One of the armoured figures raises a hand. Light bursts from it, and the man disappears in the glare. Stalls splinter. The jar slips from Bun's hand and breaks on the road.

His father seizes him and runs. People are shouting now, not in greeting. Another burst strikes a roof behind them, scattering tiles into the lane. A woman pulls two children beneath a doorway. Bun's father pushes Bun toward them.

"Get to the ships," he tells the woman. "Take him with you."

"Come too," Bun says.

His father turns back toward the square. Others are running that way, calling for their families. Bun tries to follow, but the woman catches his arm. A blast hits the end of the lane. Through the dust he sees his father fall, and then the woman is dragging him around the corner.

At the landing field, adults are lifting children into ships. One man stays outside to close a hatch for the children already aboard. An armoured stranger drops from the sky. Bun hears him shout that the planet is to be given to Lord Freeza, that its people should be grateful. Another burst strikes the field.

The woman pushes Bun through the door of the mirrored ship. Zeshin is there, pulling smaller children away from the entrance. Miri is crying for her parents. Bun tries to get back out, but the hatch closes between him and the field.

The ship rises. Through a narrow window he sees two other vessels lift from the ground. A flash catches one; it breaks apart. The second vanishes behind smoke, and another flash follows. Bun keeps watching for it until the cloud covers everything.

He never sees his father get up.

Back in the dining room, Bun gives the wooden figure to Naro. The younger boy holds it against the blanket.

"We got away," Bun says. "I don't know what happened to everyone else."

Zeshin's fingers are still against the mark on the table. "My parents were at the field. They put us aboard. We couldn't go back for them."

Miri presses her face into the blanket. Gohan wants to tell her that her parents might have escaped, but he does not know that. He sits on the floor near her instead, far enough away that she can move if she wants to.

"I'm sorry," he says.

She does not answer. After a while, she sits down too, with Naro beside her.

Piccolo looks at Zeshin. "The people you saw—did they have tails?"

"No," Zeshin says. "Not the ones I saw."

Bun shakes his head. "Neither did the man at the field."

Goshin's tail curls around his waist. "Raditz said Saiyans killed people on planets and sold the planets afterward."

Bulma looks at him. "You think this Freeza could be buying them?"

"I don't know."

"Or the Saiyans could be working for him," Gohan says.

Piccolo rests his hand on the back of a chair. "The children heard a name. They saw what happened on their world. We don't know whether Raditz or the two on Earth had anything to do with it."

Bun's gaze moves between the brothers' tails and Piccolo's face. "There are people like that on your planet?"

"Two Saiyans came to Earth," Gohan says. "They attacked our friends. Father and the others were watching them when we left."

He remembers Vegeta awake at the quarry. "I don't know what's happened since."

"And Raditz?" Miri asks.

Goshin touches the edge of the table. "He was our uncle. He took us away from home and tried to make Dad help him."

"He died," Gohan says. He remembers the field and his father's face after the fight. "We went home."

Bun looks at the dome over the guns. "You still have your home."

"Yes," Goshin says. He wishes there were another answer he could give.

Zeshin rises. "We have somewhere we're trying to reach. Our parents traded there. We thought the people might help us."

"Might?" Bulma asks.

"We haven't spoken to them. We don't know if they remember us, or if they'll let all of us stay."

"How long will it take?"

"A long time." He looks toward the passage where the other children are waiting. "We're going anyway."

Bun gets up from his chair. "We should check the damaged section again. If the seal goes, we need to know before we turn the engines up."

Zeshin nods. Before they leave, Bun lifts the dome just enough to take one of the guns. He carries it with the muzzle toward the floor. The other six remain beneath the dome.

## A Watch on Earth

Dr. Mori kneels beside Nappa in the shade of the quarry wall. Her case is open, but she has not taken anything from it yet. Tien stands a few paces away, with Chiaotzu beside him.

"I brought a clean dressing," she says. "May I look at the scrape on your arm again?"

Nappa watches her hands. "No needles."

"No needles. Last time you didn't want the scrape covered. That's still your choice."

He considers her, then shifts his arm away from the wall. "Quickly."

"As quickly as I can without making it worse. Tell me if you want me to stop."

Chiaotzu holds the small container of water while she works. Nappa keeps his eyes on the doctor, but he lets her clean the scrape and put the dressing on. When she is finished, she asks if he will drink. He takes the bottle from her and does it himself.

Across the tracks, Vegeta watches from beneath the edge of the loose cover. Goku and Krillin stay on either side of him. His tail lies against the stone, and his hands are free.

"Is that supposed to make you fit to fight?" Vegeta asks.

Nappa twists the cap onto the bottle. "You wanted me dead. I don't remember asking what you'd prefer."

Vegeta's expression hardens. Goku takes a step that keeps the two Saiyans in his view. Tien turns toward Vegeta as well, but does not leave Nappa or the doctor.

"You were beaten," Vegeta says.

"So were you."

For a moment Vegeta's fingers press into the dirt. Krillin moves closer. The prince sees him and lets his hand relax, though his eyes remain on Nappa.

Dr. Mori closes her case. "I'll come back if you want me to. The offer stands whether he approves or not."

Nappa gives her no promise. He does not return the bottle, either.

"You," Vegeta says before the doctor can leave. "Can you get me fit to fight?"

Dr. Mori turns toward him. "I can't promise that. I'd have to examine you first."

He looks down at his damaged armour. Freeza is stronger than he is; the Earth Dragon Balls might give him a way to challenge him, but not while he is trapped on this rock. He has refused the doctor before. He does not intend to lie here forever to prove he can do it.

"You can look at my side. No needles, and I'm not leaving with you."

"All right. You can tell me to stop. If I think it isn't safe, I'll stop too."

Goku moves between Vegeta and the path to the car while Krillin stays on the other side, watching the prince's hands. Dr. Mori checks that she has a clear way back before she comes closer.

"May I listen to your breathing?" she asks.

Vegeta gives a curt nod. She tells him where she will place the stethoscope, then listens through a gap in the broken armour. He answers two questions about the pain, though his jaw tightens when she asks him to draw a deeper breath.

"That's enough," he says.

She takes the instrument away at once. "There's bruising, but I can't tell how badly you're hurt from this. If you want a closer examination, I can ask the hospital team about bringing the equipment."

"I haven't agreed to that."

"I know."

Across the tracks, Nappa gives a rough laugh. "So you let her fuss over you too."

"I didn't ask for your opinion."

"You didn't ask before you tried to kill me, either."

Vegeta's hand presses against the rock. Goku remains where he is until the doctor has returned to her case. Whatever the examination shows, Vegeta is still looking for a way off the quarry floor.

Goku looks toward the closed case. "There really aren't any needles in there?"

"There aren't today."

"Good."

Krillin grins. "She wasn't offering you one."

"I know that."

"You moved when she opened the case."

"I was watching Vegeta."

"With both arms behind your back?"

Goku laughs, but his eyes go back to the prince. Vegeta has not moved from the shade.

After the doctor has walked clear, Goku glances at the sky. He cannot feel his sons from here anymore. He had called after the ship for them to bring back a good story; now he finds himself wondering which part they will tell first.

"You're thinking about them," Krillin says.

"Yeah. Gohan wanted to ask those people about Piccolo's family. I bet he's got a hundred questions already."

"And Goshin?"

"He'll have a hundred and one. He'll ask the extra one while Bulma's trying to answer the first."

Krillin laughs. Vegeta watches them without joining in.

"You sent them into space," the prince says. "You can't protect them there."

Goku turns to him. "They're strong. They made their own choice, and they aren't alone."

"You have no idea what is out there."

"Then tell me."

Vegeta smiles, the same small, unpleasant smile he gave before the ship left. "Find out for yourself."

Goku wants to press him. He also sees the angle of Vegeta's raised chin and the strip of sky beyond the cover. He stays where he can watch the prince's hands.

Krillin feels the little pouch at his belt. The last whole Senzu is still inside. A bean could heal Vegeta, but neither the bean nor the cover would make him safe to leave unguarded.

"We'll find a better place for the watch," Krillin says quietly.

"We will," Goku replies.

They have not found it yet. Tien and Chiaotzu remain with Nappa, and Goku sits down facing Vegeta while the wind shifts the edge of the cloth.

## The Way They Choose

When Zeshin and Bun return to the dining room, Bulma asks to see the damaged readings. Zeshin brings her to a panel on the safe side of the sealed bulkhead. Bun comes too, carrying one of the guns with its muzzle pointed at the floor.

"I'm not putting it away yet," he tells her.

"I noticed. Keep it pointed down, please."

He does. Bulma opens her tool case and studies the panel. The markings are unfamiliar, but Zeshin shows her which line represents the seal and which shows the air on either side.

"The seal is holding," he says. "That reading hasn't changed."

"Good. This one keeps jumping. Is there a second sensor?"

Zeshin points to a smaller line. Bulma compares them while he taps the edge of the casing. A loose connection makes the smaller reading flicker.

"There," he says.

"You found it. Can you hold this open?"

He does while she tightens the connection. The line settles. They watch it for a while, then Zeshin closes the casing himself.

"We'll keep checking," he says.

"Please do. I haven't examined your whole ship, and neither of us knows what that rock did to every part of it."

They return to the dining room. Gohan is sitting with Miri and Naro; Naro has let him look at the wooden figure. Goshin is showing another child how to make it stand against the edge of a cup without falling over. The child knocks the cup by accident, and Goshin catches it before it spills.

"Can you teach me that?" the child asks.

"Maybe. It took us a lot of practice."

"Can you teach me now?"

"We have to go soon," Bulma says. "And first somebody has to open the bay."

Zeshin looks at Bun. Bun's hand tightens on the gun, but he does not raise it.

"They're leaving," Zeshin says.

"I know."

"We can open the clamps from the bay controls."

Bun studies the four visitors. "I'll come with you."

"All right."

Miri stands. "They said they were going to Namek."

Zeshin turns back. "You did say that, didn't you?"

"Do you know it?" Piccolo asks.

"We passed that part of space. There was a mark for Namek on one of our parents' charts. I think it was about two weeks ago."

Bulma is already opening her notebook. "Did you land there?"

"No."

"Then what did you see?"

"A mark and a heading. I can show you the chart."

He brings a thin panel from the bridge. Its lines and symbols are different from the ones on Kami's ship, but it has recognizable patterns of stars. Bulma spreads her own notes beside it. Piccolo stands over her shoulder while Zeshin points out the route they followed.

"There's a shorter way from here," Miri says. She puts a finger near a label. "KS162."

"It goes toward the mark for Namek," Zeshin agrees. "But there's a current along that route. Our parents used to talk about it. The old chart says ships can get pulled off their heading."

Bulma looks up. "How strong is it?"

"I don't know. We didn't go through it."

"And the chart is old?"

"I don't know how old."

"All right." She copies the label, the surrounding star pattern and the warning rather than writing down a single direction by itself. "We'll have to compare this with what our ship can actually read."

"You don't have to take the short way," Bun says.

"I know. Thank you for telling me about it."

He looks suspicious of the thanks. "Zeshin told you."

"You made sure I knew I had a choice."

Bun has no answer for that. He looks away first.

Zeshin leads the four visitors, Bun, Miri and Naro back to the bay, where their ship still rests under the clamps. Before anyone opens the outer door, Bulma goes aboard with Gohan to check the food. He retrieves the bags from beneath the bed, makes sure the folded letter is still inside his, and puts them back. There is enough for the planned voyage, though he knows Bulma has been counting the supplies.

She takes out several sealed packets and a container of water. "We can spare these. Ask them if they want them."

Gohan carries the supplies back. Miri accepts the water; Zeshin takes the packets after asking what is in them. Bun examines the seals before they put anything away.

"We have food too," he says.

"Good," Bulma replies. "Keep yours. That way you'll have more."

Gohan hands the wooden figure back to Naro. "I hope you can find someone to fix its arm."

"I can," Miri says. "I just need the piece."

Naro searches the blanket and produces the missing arm. He had tucked it into a fold.

"You had it the whole time?" Miri asks.

He nods solemnly. She takes it, and Gohan smiles as she begins telling Naro exactly what he will have to hold while she repairs it.

After that, Zeshin opens the bay panel Bulma examined earlier. He shows her which control releases the clamps. Goshin straightens the one he bent on their way in, keeping his hands clear of the ship's support.

"That's better," Bulma says.

"I could have straightened it before."

"You could have. You didn't."

Goshin grins. "I'm doing it now."

The other clamps withdraw at Zeshin's command. Their ship rests on its own supports again. Bun stands near the inner door with his gun lowered; Piccolo gives him room rather than passing close.

"Will the outer door open from here?" Bulma asks.

"From the bridge. The bay seals first."

"We'll all be aboard before you open it."

Zeshin nods. He looks at Piccolo. "You said you're going to Namek. Are you from there?"

"I don't know," Piccolo says. "Someone told me my people came from there. I'm going to find out."

"I hope you do."

Piccolo inclines his head. "I hope you find the people you're looking for too."

Gohan pauses beside Miri. "You don't have to believe everything we said."

"I believe you helped Naro."

"He helped me too," Naro says. He lifts the blanket. "He got my blanket out."

"Bun got you out," Gohan says.

Bun shifts the gun to his other hand. "You opened the door."

Bun meets Gohan's eyes. He still does not smile.

"If you do meet anyone who says they work for Freeza," he says, "don't wait for them to tell you what they want."

"We'll be careful," Gohan says.

Goshin stands beside him, looking from the children to the large closed door. "You be careful too."

"We will," Miri says. "Bun checks everything twice."

"Because you don't check anything," Bun replies.

"I checked Naro's blanket."

"After he went back for it."

Naro clutches the blanket under his chin. Goshin laughs, and after a moment Miri does too. Bun's mouth twitches, though he keeps hold of the gun.

The four travellers board their ship. Zeshin takes the other children through the inner door and heads back to the bridge. Piccolo checks the view through the partially opened cover while the brothers face away. The bay is still enclosed. Bulma seals their door and takes her seat.

A low sound travels through the floor as the larger ship's inner bay door closes. Then the outer door opens. Piccolo watches the instruments and the narrow view, keeping the unknown sky away from the boys.

Bulma plays the recorded command. Their ship lifts, turns and passes out of the mirrored vessel. She does not uncover the boys' window for the departure.

Once they are clear, she asks Piccolo whether the other ship is following. He checks. The great curved hull has turned onto a different heading.

"They're going their own way," he says.

Gohan takes his seat beside Goshin. Through the wall they can feel no more than the hum of their own engines. The children have a destination, a damaged ship to watch and one another; they have not promised to trust the strangers they met.

Bulma lays Zeshin's copied warning beside the recorder. "Let's see whether your old home can understand any of this," she says to the ship.

## Beyond KS162

They spend the next stretch of the journey comparing the children's chart with the instruments aboard. Bulma can recognize enough of the star pattern to turn toward KS162, but she cannot simply read the label into Popo's recorder and expect Kami's ship to understand it. Piccolo helps her check the recorded course words. When they are ready, she changes heading in stages and watches the display after each one.

"If this current is where Zeshin's chart says," she tells the boys, "we'll see it on the instruments before we reach the worst of it. I hope."

"You hope?" Gohan says.

"The chart was old. I didn't make it."

Goshin sits with his back against the covered window. "Can we go around if we find it?"

"If we can tell where it ends. Or we can turn back and take the longer route. We're not going straight into something just because somebody called it a shortcut."

Later, a line on the panel begins to waver. A second line moves with it. Bulma checks the engines; they are answering, but the course mark is drifting sideways.

"There it is," she says.

The cabin tilts. Gohan catches his bag as it slides out from beneath the bed. Goshin is beside Bulma's chair, steadying it with one hand as she reaches for the recorder.

"Can I help move the ship?" he asks.

"Let me try the controls first. I know which way they're pushing, and I don't want the hull bent around us."

"All right. Tell me if you need me."

She plays a command. The engines change their hum, and the course mark begins to turn. For a few seconds it moves farther than Bulma wants; she corrects, waits and corrects again. Piccolo watches the covered port and the instruments while the boys secure the loose equipment.

The pull eases. Bulma lets out her breath and checks the new heading against the chart.

"We're through the part I can see on the display," she says. "Whether we're where we meant to be is another question."

A fresh tone sounds. A warning flashes on a part of the panel she has not learned to read.

"Piccolo?"

He stands beside her as she opens a narrow section of the outer cover. Both brothers turn toward the cabin wall. Piccolo checks the view before speaking.

"There's a planet ahead."

Bulma leans closer. Green and blue curve beyond the glass, bright against the dark. It is beautiful enough to make her forget the warning for an instant.

"Could that be Namek?" Gohan asks.

"It could be," she says. "It could also be somewhere the current carried us."

Piccolo keeps watching the unfamiliar world. The warning continues to flash beneath Bulma's hand.

"We'll find out what that means before we land," he says.

Goshin turns his head toward their voices, but not toward the opening. "Do you think there are people there?"

"Maybe," Piccolo says.

He remembers the children behind the mirrored hull, and the name they gave the people who destroyed their home. Ahead lies a world none of them has seen. Behind them, on another course, the children are still travelling toward a place they hope will take them in.

Bulma closes the cover and reaches for her notebook. The warning flashes again.
