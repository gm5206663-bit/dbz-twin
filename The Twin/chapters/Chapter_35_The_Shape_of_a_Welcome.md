# Chapter 35 — The Shape of a Welcome

*Anime episode reference: Dragon Ball Z, Episode 41 (the apparent arrival, the helpful strangers and the search, adapted for the living-character branch).*

## The Warning

The warning flashes again beneath Bulma's hand.

She opens her notebook to the page where she copied the children's chart. The little line Miri pointed out, KS162, sits beside a cluster of stars and three hurried question marks. Bulma draws a fourth question mark beside it.

"That's not a translation," Piccolo says.

"I know. I'm putting it there so I remember where I started being annoyed."

Gohan and Goshin sit on the edge of the bed, facing the closed window. Gohan has his book open, but he has not turned a page. Each time the tone sounds, Goshin looks at the panel.

"Can Popo read it?" he asks.

"Popo isn't here," Bulma says. "His recordings are, though."

She sets the little recorder beside the controls and plays the command for the ship's course display. A line appears on the screen, curving toward a marked point. Another line, thinner and broken, ends a short distance away.

"That one changed," Gohan says. "It was on the other side before we went through the current."

Bulma checks the sketch she made earlier. "You're right."

She plays the command for the stored destination, then the command for the current position. The same warning symbol appears beside the destination line. When she switches the destination display off, the symbol disappears; it returns when she calls the route back.

She repeats the test once more, changing only the display. The result is the same.

"It looks like a course warning," she says. "Those marks came apart in the current, and the symbol's linked to the route display. My best reading is that we aren't where the ship expected to be."

"How far off?" Piccolo asks.

"I don't know yet, and I can't translate every mark. It doesn't tell me what's wrong with that planet." She taps the symbol. "This isn't a name, and I haven't found a sign that the hull has a hole."

"Is it Namek?" Goshin asks.

"The warning doesn't answer that either."

Bulma looks over her shoulder at him. "I was hoping for a nice clear sign too."

She opens a narrow part of the window again. Both boys turn toward the cabin wall while Piccolo checks the view. The planet has grown larger. White cloud streaks curl over green land and blue water.

"Nothing bright and round in this part," he tells them. "Keep watching for changes."

They lean close enough to see the world through the opening. Goshin presses a hand to the glass. The planet seems to fill the whole strip of it.

"There are so many clouds," Gohan says.

"There are on Earth too," Bulma says. "You just don't usually look down on all of them at once."

She closes the cover rather than widening the view. The boys turn back to her.

"We could try the old course again," she says, "but I don't know how the current will push us if we go back through it. If there are people down there, they might have a better chart. I'd like to get a closer look before we decide."

Piccolo nods. "So would I."

Gohan sees Piccolo's hand settle on the back of Bulma's chair. The green land outside might hold the people King Kai felt. It might not. Either way, Piccolo is farther from home than he has ever been, looking toward a world that might hold people he has never met.

Bulma checks the course once more, then plays a command. The engines deepen their hum.

The ship descends through the bright edge of the clouds. Bulma uses the covered display for the approach, watching the instruments while the boys secure the recorder and the bags. Piccolo stands beside her and checks each new portion of the sky before the brothers look at it.

Below the cloud, the ground resolves into long plateaus and scattered lakes. A ridge drops sharply into a broad green valley. There are rounded shapes among the trees, too regular to be rocks.

Piccolo closes his eyes.

"I can feel someone," he says. "Two people, I think. Down near that ridge."

Goshin comes to stand beside him. He can feel them too, distant but distinct from the four familiar presences in the ship. He wants to go down at once and see their faces.

"Can you tell what they are?" Bulma asks.

"No."

"Well, they might know where we are."

The ship jolts as it passes through a rough layer of air. Bulma steadies the controls, waiting for the craft to answer before she corrects again. A few moments later she guides it toward a wide, flat shelf above the valley.

"Everyone hold on."

The feet touch stone with a thump. The cabin rocks once, then settles.

Goshin lets go of the bedpost. "That was good!"

"Of course it was good," Bulma says, though she stays at the controls long enough to check the engine readings. "Give me a minute before anybody opens that door."

She takes a small air sampler from her case and fits it to the hatch seam. A narrow strip on its face changes colour. Bulma compares it with the scale beside the strip, then checks the other readings.

"There's oxygen, and nothing my sampler tests for is showing up as dangerous. That doesn't tell us everything about the air, so let's not run off eating the plants."

"I wasn't going to," Goshin says.

"You were looking at the plants."

"I was looking at the whole planet."

Piccolo opens the hatch a little and steps outside first. He looks along the ridge, over the valley and up through the visible sky. There is a bright sun behind thin cloud, but no full moon or other full, moonlike disc in sight from the shelf.

"I don't see one," he calls. "We'll check again if the sky or the ground changes."

The boys come to the hatch. The air smells damp and sweet. Tall, thin trees stand in the valley below, their rounded crowns swaying in a breeze that has not yet reached the shelf.

Gohan spots a pale building among them. "Is that where the people are?"

"They're closer than the building now," Piccolo says.

Two figures have emerged from the trees. They stop at the foot of the ridge and wave.

Goshin waves back. A faint crack sounds beneath the ship.

He looks down. A line has appeared in the stone beside one of the landing feet.

"Bulma—"

The line races across the shelf. The landing foot sinks, and the ship tilts toward the edge.

## The First Welcome

Bulma catches the hatch frame. Gohan grabs her other arm as the floor slopes under them. The stone beneath the ship breaks away in a broad sheet, carrying the front landing foot with it.

Piccolo shoots upward, one hand reaching for Bulma. Gohan gets her to the doorway, and Piccolo takes her clear of the tilting hull. Loose rock tumbles into the valley.

Goshin stays inside. He spreads his ki past the outer wall, feeling for the underside of the ship as it starts to fall.

He catches it across the lower rim. The sudden drop stops, but the ship swings toward the collapsing cliff. Goshin shifts the pressure along the hull and brings the craft level again.

Outside, Gohan dives after a slab turning toward the two figures below. He pushes it away from them with his ki, sending it down onto an empty stretch of ground. One of the strangers pulls the other back from the smaller stones that follow.

"Are you all right?" Gohan calls.

"We are now!" the shorter figure answers.

The ground is still giving way. Piccolo carries Bulma over the falling rock and sets her down beyond it, then turns toward the ship. Goshin eases the craft away from the cliff, keeping its weight spread between many small points of pressure. The work he did with the meteors is still in him. He has more than enough strength to lift the craft; the challenge is holding its fragile hull without crushing it.

As he adjusts his hold, he feels the broad load and the thinner edge of the hull together, more clearly than before. His power deepens through the effort, answering more readily as he corrects each small turn. He lowers the ship onto the open ground beside Piccolo. Its feet press into the grass. This time the earth holds.

For a moment Goshin remains where he is, hands raised, feeling along the hull. Nothing is pulling it sideways. He lets his ki withdraw and hurries out of the hatch, the strength and the finer sense still with him.

"Did I bend anything?" he asks Bulma.

She is already circling the nearest landing foot. "Let me see."

Gohan lands beside his brother, grinning with relief. "You moved the whole ship."

"The cliff moved it first."

"You stopped it."

Goshin looks at the broken shelf. There are still stones falling from its edge. "You got those people out of the way."

"One of them helped."

The strangers are approaching now. Both have green skin and long, pointed ears. The shorter one has the lined face of an old man and a pair of antennae that curve above his brow. His companion is broader, with a heavy jaw and the same remarkable green colour. They stop several steps away.

Gohan looks from their faces to Piccolo's. He has seen Kami smile, and Piccolo on a few occasions when Piccolo did not know he was being watched. Something in the old man's face reminds him of both.

Piccolo says nothing.

"A fine catch," the older stranger says to Goshin. "I thought that ship was going to break its back."

"Is anyone hurt?" asks the other.

"I don't think so," Bulma says. She straightens from the landing foot. "The ship seems all right too, but I'm going to check it properly."

"I'm Raiti," the older man says. "This is Zākuro. We've been friends for more than three hundred years, though he likes to pretend it hasn't been that long."

"Three hundred?" Bulma asks.

"He counts every argument twice," Zākuro says.

Goshin laughs. Raiti turns to Piccolo, and the smile on his face becomes gentler.

"You've come a long way."

Piccolo steps forward. "Is this Namek?"

"It is," Raiti says.

Piccolo's eyes narrow. "How many people live here?"

"Not as many as there used to be. The storms scattered us." Raiti glances toward the valley. "There are villages beyond these hills."

King Kai said there were fewer than a hundred he could sense. Piccolo studies the two men in front of him. Their faces and their answer give him hope, but neither is the same as a map or a conversation with someone who remembers Kami.

"I was told there were storms," he says. "A very long time ago."

Raiti nods. "A terrible time. Are you looking for someone?"

Piccolo hesitates. "I want to speak to your people. An older man from Earth came from a place like this when he was a child. He doesn't know who sent him away."

"The elder may know something of those days. I can take you to him."

"And can he tell us how to reach the direction our ship was following?" Bulma asks. "We came through a current."

"He has charts."

That is enough to bring Bulma closer. "Good. I'd like to compare them with ours."

Zākuro comes up beside her. Before she can move, he reaches out and takes hold of the end of her nose.

"Welcome," he says solemnly.

Bulma stares at him. "Let go."

He does. She rubs her nose and takes a step back.

"It's a greeting here," he explains.

"Where I come from, people usually say hello before they grab you."

"Then hello."

Goshin covers his mouth. Gohan is trying not to laugh as well. Piccolo watches Zākuro's hand until it is safely at his side.

"I'm Bulma," she says. "Those two are Gohan and Goshin. And that's Piccolo."

"You look a great deal like us, Piccolo," Raiti says.

"I've noticed."

Raiti offers the boys a bow instead of his hand. Gohan bows back. Goshin considers his nose, then bows too.

Bulma walks around the rest of the hull. The landing feet have scraped the shelf, and dirt has collected along the lower rim, but she finds no obvious tear. She tests the hatch, and Goshin helps her check the catch on a narrow access plate beneath it. It closes firmly. She makes a note to examine the underside more carefully before they leave.

Zākuro watches her. "I can help with a repair."

"I'll tell you if I need one. First I want a look at those charts."

She kneels by her case and takes out a small round instrument with a screen. Gohan recognizes it from stories his father has told.

"The Dragon Radar?"

"I brought it along." Bulma presses the button on top. "We don't know whether there are Dragon Balls here, but I wasn't going to travel all this way without checking."

Lights appear on the screen. Several dots cluster near the middle.

"Oh!" Bulma turns the radar so Piccolo can see. "It's getting something."

"Does that mean they're Dragon Balls?" Gohan asks.

"It means the radar is reacting. I made it for the ones on Earth; I've never tested it on another planet."

Raiti peers over her shoulder. "You're looking for the star balls."

Bulma turns to him. "You have them?"

"We do. Some were hidden when the storms came. The elder keeps what he can find."

Piccolo looks at the dots. "We didn't come here to take anything from your people."

"Of course not. The elder will be glad to speak with you either way." Raiti points beyond the trees. "There is a lake on the way to his village. If one of the balls is there, we could bring it to him."

Gohan looks at Piccolo. "We could help find it."

Piccolo considers Raiti, then Bulma. "If the elder knows about them, I want to hear it from him."

"So do I," Bulma says. "And if there's a signal right on the way, I'm curious."

Raiti beams. "Then we'll go together."

Zākuro looks toward the hatch. "I can keep an eye on your ship."

Bulma's hand closes over the recorder in her pocket. Piccolo glances at the stranger, then at the broken cliff. They have only just met these two.

"I'll stay," Goshin says.

Gohan turns to him. "Are you sure?"

"I want to see what else is here, but someone should watch the ship. You go and tell me what the elder says."

"We'll come back for you," Gohan promises.

"You'd better. And if I need you sooner, I'll raise my ki twice, like this." Goshin lifts two fingers in quick succession. "You'll feel it."

"We will," Piccolo says.

Zākuro smiles. "I'll stay too. We can look at the landing feet."

"From outside," Bulma says. "Don't open anything until I get back."

She closes the hatch herself. Goshin remains beside it, while Zākuro moves far enough away for Bulma to see that his hands are empty.

Raiti points to a small flying vehicle with a rounded cabin and a propeller-like engine. It stands beside a low shed in the grass, within sight of the ship's hatch, its metal skin patched in several colours.

"My Goku will take us," he says proudly.

Gohan stops. "Your what?"

"My Goku. She's fast, and she's never let me down. Well, not often."

Bulma and Piccolo exchange a glance. Goshin stays beside the hatch with Zākuro. He stares at the painted name on the vehicle's side.

"That's Dad's name," he says.

"Is it?" Raiti looks surprised. "Then he must be a fine fellow."

Gohan laughs, but Goshin keeps looking at the vehicle. He cannot remember anyone telling Raiti their father's name.

"He is," Goshin says.

Raiti climbs into the little craft and starts its rattling engine. Piccolo offers Bulma his arm.

"I'll carry you. You can read the radar from there."

"Gladly. I don't think my teeth would survive a ride in that thing."

"It runs beautifully!" Raiti calls over the noise.

Gohan rises beside them. Piccolo looks over the visible sky in the direction they are going; the sun is still behind its veil of cloud, and he sees no full disc ahead. Gohan keeps his eyes on the low hills and Piccolo's back as they follow Raiti's vehicle toward the lake.

Goshin watches from beside the ship until they have passed below the ridge. Zākuro remains with him.

## The Lake

Raiti's vehicle lands at the edge of a broad lake. The water is clear near the shore and much darker beyond a ring of reeds. Bulma checks the radar, walking along the bank until one of the lights sits near the centre of the screen.

"Somewhere out there."

"I'll look," Gohan says.

He leaves his shoes on the bank and dives. For a few seconds Bulma can follow the pale movement of his clothes below the surface. Then he vanishes into the deeper water.

Piccolo watches the lake. Raiti rolls up his sleeves.

"I'll help him."

"Wait," Bulma says. "Do you know how deep it is?"

"Deep enough to be cold. Not deep enough to worry about me."

He slips in. Ripples spread around him and meet the rings left by Gohan.

Gohan finds a shelf of stone under the water and follows it down. Fine silt rises where his hand brushes the bottom. He moves slowly, looking beneath the rocks and into a narrow gap between them. A small fish darts past his face. He follows it out of the cloud of silt, but he sees no orange ball.

When he surfaces, he pushes his wet hair back and takes a breath. "I couldn't find it. There's a lot of mud on the bottom."

"The signal's still here," Bulma says. "Try a little farther to your left."

Gohan dives again. This time he moves the loose stones gently with his ki, making room to search without stirring the whole bottom. He finds an old branch, three more fish and a hollow where the current has gathered pale shells.

When he comes up, Raiti is nowhere in sight.

"Piccolo?"

"I can still feel him."

A moment later Raiti breaks the surface farther out, holding something above his head.

"Here it is!"

Sunlight glows through an orange sphere in his hand. Five red stars shine inside it.

Bulma lets out a delighted cry. "It really does look like one!"

Raiti swims to the bank and gives it to her. It is smooth and cool, small enough for her to hold in both hands. Gohan climbs out beside him and reaches for his shoes.

"I thought I looked there," he says.

"It was tucked under a ledge," Raiti tells him. "Easy to miss."

Gohan looks back at the water. He had not seen a ledge where Raiti came up, but the bottom was hard to make out through the stirred silt. He takes the cloth Bulma offers and dries his feet.

"Can the balls here grant a wish?" Bulma asks.

"The elder knows their secrets," Raiti says. "We'll ask him."

Piccolo holds out his hand. Bulma gives him the sphere, and he turns it slowly. He has seen Earth's Dragon Balls, but he does not know whether one from another world would feel the same. If this is what Raiti says it is, someone here may share an ability with Kami.

He gives it back to Bulma. "We'll take it to him."

Raiti starts toward his vehicle. "There is another signal ahead, isn't there?"

Bulma checks the radar. "Yes. Near a group of buildings."

"Old ruins. We pass close by on the way to the elder."

Piccolo looks toward the hills beyond the lake. "Then let's not spend all day there."

## At the Quarry

Vegeta remembers the giant's hand closing around him.

He had expected a beast when Kakarot's son looked down at him. The boy's eyes had been clear, and his fingers strong enough to close around the prince. Gohan had destroyed the artificial moon; back in their ordinary bodies, Goshin had still been stronger.

Such strength could be useful against Freeza, though Vegeta has no way to know how much the tyrant holds back. First he needs to escape Earth. Then he can decide how to use the boy—and claim the wish himself.

Kakarot's sons seem to have gone looking for the Namekian's people. Perhaps they are headed for Namek. If that world has Dragon Balls, Vegeta intends to find them.

A short scuff on the stone brings him out of it.

Kakarot is practising a series of quick movements a few paces away. He turns, checks his footing and throws a punch into the empty air. The cloth tied near Vegeta's shade flutters.

"You're doing that on purpose," Krillin says.

Goku stops. "Doing what?"

"Making me watch you instead of him."

"I'm watching him too."

"I can see that. I'm watching both of you."

Goku grins. Vegeta keeps his face still. Across the tracks, Tien and Chiaotzu remain near Nappa. Nappa has changed position against the wall, but the dressing is still on his arm.

Goku returns to a loose stance, facing Vegeta. "Goshin told me he could hold you in one hand when he was big. Did you try changing direction in the air to get around him?"

Vegeta's jaw tightens. "You weren't there."

"No. I wish I'd been there." Goku looks across the broken quarry. Then he grins. "Sounds like Gohan and Goshin gave you a real fight, though."

"I saw you almost step on my foot just now," Krillin says. "Try changing direction around that. And we're supposed to be keeping Vegeta from trying anything."

"I know. I didn't say we should let him up and fight."

Vegeta shifts one hand on the stone. Goku's stance changes immediately, and Krillin moves nearer. For an instant the prince considers the distance between them, the cover above him and the open quarry beyond. Then he leaves his hand where it is.

A familiar voice calls from the track. "Goku!"

Chi-Chi is coming down the slope with a cloth bag over one arm. Goku waves, but neither he nor Krillin leaves Vegeta.

"I brought the shirt you tore," she says. "I thought you might want it before you make another one look the same."

"Thanks!" He takes it and unfolds it. The seam at the shoulder has been neatly mended. "You're getting fast."

"I'm getting practice."

She looks across at Nappa, then back at Vegeta. The prince's tail is visible against the ground, and one end of the loose shade cloth flutters above him.

"Has the doctor been back?" she asks.

"She cleaned Nappa's arm," Krillin says. "He let her put a dressing on it. Tien's still talking to him about what else he wants."

"And him?" She looks at Vegeta.

"She examined him a little last time," Goku says. "She couldn't tell how badly he was hurt. We keep offering more help."

"I didn't ask for a nurse," Vegeta says.

"You might need water and food," Chi-Chi replies. She takes a bottle and a wrapped portion from the bag and sets them within his reach, then walks back to Goku. Vegeta watches her go. He leaves the bottle where it is, but draws the food toward him and unwraps it himself.

Goku pulls off his dusty shirt and puts on the repaired one. Chi-Chi smooths the shoulder once, checking her work.

"I was sewing something for the boys too," she says. "It'll be ready when they come back."

"Two things?"

"Two. Goshin won't be wearing his brother's because you forgot which was which."

"I didn't forget. I put them in the wrong bags."

"Last time."

Krillin laughs. Chi-Chi's smile fades as she looks up at the clear sky. At home she still listens for the telephone.

"I hope they're eating properly," she says.

"Bulma packed enough," Goku tells her.

"That's what she said."

"And Piccolo's with them."

Chi-Chi nods. She trusts Piccolo to care for them. That does not make the distance any smaller.

Goku takes her hand for a moment. "When they get back, I want to hear everything they saw."

"After they've eaten."

"After they've eaten," he agrees, and this time he does not try to bargain about how quickly that might happen.

Vegeta looks away from them. The bottle remains on the stone beside him, and the guards remain where they can see his hands.

## The Ruin

The second signal leads them to a low building of weathered stone. Its roof has partly fallen in, and thin roots hang through a crack above the entrance. Raiti brings his vehicle down outside while Piccolo lands with Bulma and Gohan beside the doorway.

"Five thousand years old," Raiti says, looking up at the broken roof. "Perhaps older."

Inside, steps lead down past carvings worn smooth by time. Bulma shines a small light across the wall. Figures stand in rows, their faces long and their heads rounded. None has the antennae Piccolo can see on Raiti's brow.

Gohan touches the stone beside one carving, careful not to rub the face. "Were these people from here?"

"Ancient people," Raiti says. "We looked different then."

Piccolo studies the figures. "Do you know who made them?"

"That was a long time before me."

The answer is possible. Piccolo knows too little about this world's past to call it false. Still, he looks back at the carvings as they descend.

The radar's signal grows stronger near a stone chamber. A long lid rests on a raised block in the centre. Bulma moves her light across it, then looks at Raiti.

"Is this a grave?"

"An old resting place. The ball was hidden here to keep it safe."

Piccolo puts his hand against the lid. "You said the elder keeps them. Does he know you're taking this one?"

"I've helped him look for them before. He'll want it brought back."

Gohan watches Piccolo. If the people who live here have a custom for their dead, he does not want them to break it just because the radar is beeping.

"We can leave it and ask him first," Gohan says.

Raiti lifts both hands. "You have my word. We won't damage anything."

Piccolo waits another moment, then moves the lid just enough for Bulma to look inside.

Something white grins back at her.

"Aah!" She jumps into Gohan, who catches her before she falls against the wall. Her light swings across a skull and a set of old bones. An orange sphere rests between the skull's open jaws.

Raiti peers in. "There it is."

"You might have mentioned the bones!" Bulma says.

"I thought you knew what a resting place was."

"I do. I wasn't expecting it to be smiling at me."

Gohan lets her steady herself, then looks into the chamber. "Can I move the ball without touching anything else?"

"Try," Piccolo says.

Gohan reaches with his ki. The sphere shifts a fraction, catches against the teeth and rolls back. He changes the angle and lifts again, carefully drawing it free. This one has two stars. He brings it to Bulma without disturbing the skull.

Piccolo replaces the lid. The stone settles with a muted scrape.

Bulma puts the second sphere beside the first in her bag. "If your elder wanted someone to keep these safe, he chose an unusual hiding place."

"No one came looking here," Raiti says cheerfully.

"Except us."

On the way up, the radar gives another short chirp. Bulma looks at it, then presses the button to switch it off. The screen goes dark. A thin sound seems to continue for an instant after her finger lifts.

She turns the radar over. "That was odd."

"What was?" Gohan asks.

"Probably nothing. I'll check the contacts when we're back at the ship."

Outside, she stops Piccolo before he lifts her. Raiti is walking toward his vehicle, humming to himself.

"He's very helpful," she says quietly.

"That's good, isn't it?" Gohan asks.

"It can be. We met him a little while ago, and he knew right where to take us. I want to meet this elder before we decide how much he knows."

Piccolo nods. "I do too."

"You think he's lying?"

"I think he's a stranger," Piccolo says. "That isn't the same thing."

Raiti calls from the vehicle. "Ready? There's still time to look for the next one."

Bulma switches the radar on again. Piccolo looks at the screen. The remaining nearby signal lies beyond a stand of narrow trees.

"After that," he says, "we go to the village."

"After that," Raiti agrees.

## Beside the Ship

Goshin draws the cliff and a small ship in the dirt, giving the ship six legs instead of four.

"In case the ground breaks again," he explains. "Bulma might not like it. She likes changing things herself."

Zākuro laughs. He listens as Goshin describes the mountain near home, but when Goshin asks about his family, Zākuro draws a line in the dirt and does not answer immediately.

"We came a long way too," he says at last.

"From another village?"

"A long way," Zākuro repeats.

Goshin does not press him. He remembers Bun's face when they asked about the people who attacked his home. Some questions need more time.

He stands and walks around the ship again. The lower rim is higher on one side where the ground slopes. He can still feel the shape of the hold he used when the cliff broke: the weight spread along the outside, the small changes needed to keep it level. He reaches out with his ki now, lightly enough that the ship does not move.

A loose bit of gravel trembles beneath the rim. He lifts it and two other pieces, keeping them separate while he feels along the metal. The contact is clearer than it was before the meteor swarm. He can attend to the stones and the broad curve of the hull together, and the sense of where each pressure belongs stays with him when he sets the gravel down.

Zākuro watches him from the other side of the hatch. "You can do a great deal without touching things."

"I can move things I can reach with my ki."

"Can you open the door that way?"

"Probably. But Bulma said not to open it."

"I only wondered. I've never seen a ship quite like yours."

Goshin walks back to him. "Do you have one?"

Zākuro's eyes go to the valley. "Not one that can take us where we want to go."

"What about Raiti's little ship?"

"It can't take us there either." Zākuro looks toward the shed. "It has your father's name on it, apparently."

"Yes." Goshin pictures the painted letters on the little vehicle. "I don't think we told you his name."

"It's a common name here."

"Is it?"

"Very common."

Goshin knows only the two people they have met here. He cannot tell whether Zākuro is joking. He picks up his twig and draws a little figure beside the six-legged ship.

"That's him?" Zākuro asks.

"It's supposed to be."

The face is only a circle with spikes around it. Goshin adds a wide grin, much like the one on his own child-sized face. His unruly black hair rises in the same spikes as Goku's, although his eyes are Chi-Chi's. Zākuro crouches beside him, looking at the drawing for longer than Goshin expects.

A click sounds behind them.

Goshin turns. Zākuro is still crouching at his side, but the narrow access plate beneath the hatch has shifted. The lower catch is moving outward.

Goshin is there before the plate can open. He places his hand against it and holds it shut.

"Did you do that?"

Zākuro rises. "It may have come loose when you moved the ship."

"It wasn't loose when we checked."

"I could take a look inside."

"Bulma said not to."

Zākuro comes closer. "If something broke, she would want to know."

"She can look when she gets back."

The catch presses against Goshin's palm again. He looks down at it. There is no hand on it but his own. He closes the catch with a small push of ki and steps between Zākuro and the plate.

Zākuro stops. His smile is still there, but it has become very careful.

Goshin can feel Gohan and Piccolo beyond the hills. He lets his ki rise twice, two short, deliberate pulses as they agreed. He cannot tell them what happened with it, but they will feel the signal.

"What did you do?" Zākuro asks.

"I called my brother."

## The Moving Signal

Bulma's radar beeps as they reach the trees. The light is no longer staying in one place.

"It's moving," she says.

Raiti cuts his engine and peers down. A heavy shape pushes through the undergrowth below. Branches shake. An orange sphere flashes between a pair of clawed hands, and a large reptilian head rises above the leaves.

"That's mine!" Bulma shouts before she catches herself. "I mean—don't eat that!"

The creature opens its mouth and swallows the sphere.

Gohan flies after it. He has grown up around animals that are much larger than he is; this one looks frightened rather than angry. It crashes through the trees, heading toward a hollow where green vapour hangs over the ground.

"Don't follow it there!" Raiti calls. "The marsh is dangerous!"

Bulma starts after Gohan anyway. Piccolo catches up with her and brings her down on a dry rise above the hollow. Raiti lands his vehicle nearby, scrambling out as the creature's cry echoes through the trees.

Below them, the reptile reaches the edge of the green water. It slips. Gohan darts forward and reaches for its shoulder, intending to turn it back toward the bank.

His hands pass through the shape.

For a startled instant he sees the curve of its neck against his arms, but feels nothing there. He stops himself above the water and flies backward. The creature plunges into the marsh. Vapour closes over it.

Gohan returns to the dry rise. "Piccolo! I couldn't touch it."

"Couldn't reach it?"

"I did reach it. My hands went right through."

Raiti hurries to the edge. Beneath the green surface, a pale shape appears where the creature fell. Bulma puts a hand over her mouth.

"That water dissolves living things," Raiti says. "The gas is dangerous too. I tried to warn you."

"I'm sorry," Bulma says. "I thought you were stopping us because you wanted the ball."

"No harm done."

Gohan looks from the pale shape in the water to his own hands. He had seen the creature. He had been close enough to touch it. He reaches for a small stone at his feet with his ki and lifts it; the stone rises as he expects. He sets it down again.

The radar indicates the remaining signal is somewhere in the hollow. Raiti opens a compartment in his vehicle and produces a folded suit.

"This will protect someone for a few seconds," he says. "We can tie a rope around you and pull you out before it melts."

"No," Piccolo says.

Raiti blinks. "I thought you wanted the ball."

"I want to know why Gohan couldn't touch what he saw. And I won't send him into that water to find out."

Gohan is relieved Piccolo says it, though he has not been about to put the suit on. "Maybe I can move the ball from here."

He extends his ki toward the place Bulma points out. The surface ripples, but he cannot get hold of anything he recognizes as a sphere. He tries a little farther to one side, then the other. Something gritty shifts beneath the water; he draws it toward the bank, where a clump of mud breaks apart against the reeds. No ball comes with it.

"It might be farther down," Bulma says, studying the screen. "Or the signal might not be exactly where I think it is."

Raiti lifts the suit again. "If he goes in quickly—"

"I said no." Piccolo turns toward him. "Take us to the elder."

Before Raiti can answer, Gohan feels two familiar rises of ki from the direction of the ship, close together. The signal they agreed on.

"Goshin!"

Piccolo has felt it too. Bulma looks from one to the other.

"What's happened?"

"I don't know," Gohan says. "He wants us to come back."

He is already in the air. Piccolo takes Bulma, checking the visible sky ahead as he turns, and follows him over the trees. Raiti calls after them, then starts his vehicle with a spluttering roar.

They reach the ship in moments. Goshin is standing at the hatch, and Zākuro is a few paces away. The ship is upright and appears untouched.

Gohan lands beside his brother. "Are you all right?"

"Yes. The access plate started opening. He wanted to look inside after Bulma said not to."

"I only offered to help," Zākuro says. "The catch must have shaken loose."

"It moved while you were beside me," Goshin says. "I shut it again."

Bulma goes to the plate. The catch is closed now. She runs a finger along the edge and finds a fresh line in the dirt where it shifted.

"Thank you for stopping it," she tells Goshin. "Nobody touches the ship without asking."

Raiti's vehicle arrives, engine rattling. He climbs out and looks at Zākuro, then at the four travellers gathered by the hatch.

"A misunderstanding?" he asks.

"Maybe," Bulma says. "I'd still like that chart."

She takes out the radar. The screen is lit. She switches it off and opens the little compartment at the back, intending to check the contacts. The power cell comes free into her hand.

The screen is dark, but she hears a soft, regular beep.

Bulma holds the cell up. "Do you hear that?"

Gohan listens. "Hear what?"

Piccolo shakes his head. Goshin looks at the radar, then at Bulma's face.

The sound stops.

Bulma closes her fingers around the cell. "I heard it after I took the power out."

Raiti begins to speak, but Piccolo steps between him and Bulma.

"You said you'd take us to the elder," he says. "You told us this was Namek."

"It is."

"Then tell me where the village is. Now."

Raiti's smile fades. Zākuro moves away from the hatch, not toward it. Goshin feels his brother come to stand at his side; on the other side, Bulma slips the recorder farther into her pocket.

For a moment nobody moves.

The grass around the ship begins to bend in a slow circle. Goshin reaches toward it with his ki. He can feel the ship's familiar weight, the closed plate and the firm ground beneath the landing feet. The grass still seems to be moving, but he cannot feel the wind that should be moving it.

"Gohan," he says, keeping his eyes on Raiti and Zākuro, "stay where you can see Bulma."

"I can see her," Gohan says. He takes one step to give Piccolo room, then looks directly at Raiti. "What happened at the marsh?"

The older man's hand closes around the door of his little vehicle. Behind him, the line of trees seems to shift against the ridge.

Piccolo does not look away from either stranger.

"Answer him."
