# Chapter Twenty-Two — A Year Between Mornings

*Anime episode reference: Dragon Ball Z, Episode 21 (first part).*

Scorched and split target boards are stacked beside the mountain path. Gohan chalks two circles on a fresh one while Chi-Chi braces it against a solid part of the slope. Six rocks move above the clearing at Goshin's direction; the largest is wider than his shoulders.

**A YEAR BETWEEN MORNINGS**

**NARRATOR:** Months have passed since Goku began his direct lessons with King Kai. On Earth, nearly a year has passed since Raditz's warning. His sons have kept training, while the two Saiyans continue their journey.

---

## The New Board

Gohan faces the left-hand circle. Chi-Chi steps back onto the path.

"The left one?" she asks.

"Yes. I want to stop before it reaches the right."

Gohan draws his hands to his side. Blue light gathers between his palms, and his tail lifts behind him.

"Kamehameha!"

The wave strikes inside the left circle. Gohan closes his hands, and the light vanishes. A black mark reaches the circle's edge but leaves the right side untouched.

Gohan checks the scorched board. "I stopped it sooner. There's room for yours, Goshin!"

He returns to the path while Goshin sends the six rocks along separate paths, well clear of the target.

Goshin cups his hands. The rocks keep moving as blue light grows between his palms. He aims at the right circle and fires.

The wave passes through the board and cuts a channel into the rock behind it. Goshin crosses the clearing and catches a falling piece of wood before it lands. He sets it on the path, then brings the still-moving rocks down one at a time.

Gohan goes to the board. "There's a hole all the way through."

"We'll need another board," Chi-Chi says.

Goshin turns the fallen piece in his hands. Its cut edge is smooth.

"Can we use it for a smaller target?" he asks.

"If we put it against something solid," Chi-Chi says. "Not against the cracked bank."

Gohan lifts the damaged board. Chi-Chi takes one end, and they carry it down the path. Goshin carries the small piece beside them. He sets it beside the door when they reach home.

## King Kai's Lesson

On the little planet, Goku plants his feet. Red-edged light rises around him as he calls up the Kaiō-ken. Bubbles holds a branch out of his path.

"Go on," King Kai says. "Show me the turn."

Goku crosses the grass in a rush, circles the tree and stops beside the house. The wind of his passage shakes the leaves. He lets the light fade and looks back.

"I can keep it steady while I move now."

"Better than when we began," King Kai says. "But the Kaiō-ken puts a strain on your body. Don't force more through it than you can control."

"I know." Goku flexes his hands. "Can we practise the Genki-dama again?"

King Kai points to the sky. "That draws on more than your own power. The grass, the tree, the ground and the air can each give a little. Don't take it by force."

Goku raises both hands. A light gathers above his palms and grows into a small sphere. He turns toward King Kai, holding it together as he moves.

"It used to come apart when I did that," he says.

"You've worked hard on it. Now let the energy go. We'll find a clear target for the test."

Goku lowers his arms. The light spreads and fades above the grass. Gregory lands on the fence beside Bubbles.

"Will the target be larger than I am?" Gregory asks.

"Much larger," King Kai says.

"Then I approve."

## A Roof for the Boat

Gohan takes Hatch's first boat drawing from beneath his hat. He lays it beside a clean page and holds a pencil over the paper.

"Pigero wanted a roof over the deck," he says. "Hatch said they hadn't decided."

Goshin leans on the table. "Could the roof come off?"

"It might get lost."

"Could it fold?"

Gohan draws a folding roof above the deck. Chi-Chi comes in with the board from the clearing.

"What happens when it rains?" she asks.

Gohan adds two short lines for drains. "The water goes down these."

"Away from the door," Chi-Chi says.

The telephone rings before Gohan can finish the second line. Chi-Chi answers it and passes the receiver to him.

"It's Hatch. He wants to know if you have another idea."

Gohan tells Hatch about the folding roof. He holds the pencil between his fingers while he listens.

"No, we didn't put it on your drawing. We made another page."

Goshin waits at his shoulder. Gohan asks Hatch whether he can speak too, then holds out the receiver.

"Will this drain be in the way of the stairs?" Goshin asks.

He listens, then looks at Gohan's page. "All right. We can move it."

Gohan takes the receiver back. "I'll show you the page when we visit."

When he puts the telephone down, Chi-Chi asks whether the director has heard from the school. Gohan shakes his head.

"Hatch said Chiko is still waiting for an answer."

"Then we won't pretend one has come."

Gohan lays the new page beside the first drawing. "They can still use their room this week, can't they?"

"Yes. The director asked them again when we visited last week. They still want to share it, and she says they can while she looks for a lasting arrangement."

Goshin picks up the broken piece of board from the door. He turns it over, then sets it down beside the drawings.

"Can we ask Kami for somewhere else to train?" he says. "Somewhere we don't have to use the mountain."

Gohan looks up. "Could we ask him today?"

Chi-Chi takes the receiver again. "I'll call Bulma. If she's free, she can take us to the Lookout."

## The Room Below

Bulma's aircar comes to rest on the Lookout tiles. Mr. Popo waits beside the doorway, and Kami joins him as Chi-Chi and the boys climb out.

"I picked up some blank school forms," Bulma tells Chi-Chi. "They're on the back seat if the director needs them."

"I'll ask her. Thank you for bringing us."

Gohan carries Hatch's drawing under his arm. Goshin has the small piece of board in one hand and a pencil in the other.

Goshin tells Kami how his wave went through the board and into the rock behind it, and how he caught the falling piece while the other rocks kept moving. Gohan describes the shots they have been practising. Kami listens without interrupting either of them.

"There is another room," Kami says. "It is not the room Popo used for the mind exercise. This one is a physical place, and it is much harder on the body."

"What's in it?" Gohan asks.

"A place to live, food and a wide space for training. The gravity is about ten times Earth's, the air is thin, and it can become very hot or very cold. Time passes differently inside."

Bulma folds her arms. "How differently?"

"A year in that room is about one day outside it."

Gohan looks from Kami to the doorway. "A whole year?"

"For the people inside," Mr. Popo says. "They eat, sleep and grow during that year. People outside do not live it with them."

Chi-Chi turns to Kami. "And you thought this was a place for children?"

"I thought you should know it exists. No one needs to go in."

"If they do, can they come back out whenever they need to?"

"The door opens from inside," Popo says. "Each person can spend only two outside days there in a lifetime. That's about two years inside. Go beyond it, and the exit disappears."

"They'd be trapped?" Chi-Chi asks.

"Yes. If the room is too difficult, they should leave."

"Has Father been in there?" Gohan asks.

"When he was young," Kami says. "He came out before a month had passed."

Goshin looks toward the passage. "How many people can go in?"

"Two at a time. The living space is arranged for two."

Gohan steps beside his brother. "I want to go with Goshin."

"I want to go with you," Goshin says. He takes Gohan's hand. "For the whole year."

Chi-Chi looks at their joined hands. "You'd be there without me or your father. For you, it won't feel like one day."

Gohan squeezes Goshin's hand. "I know. I want to get stronger. I don't want anyone to take us away again."

Goshin looks down at the broken piece of board. "I want to get stronger too. My wave went through the board and hit the rock behind it. There won't always be an empty place behind what I'm aiming at."

"Then we'll have to learn where it's safe to aim," Gohan says.

"And how to stop," Goshin adds. "I want to be able to protect people."

Chi-Chi draws the boys close. "You may both change your minds. If either of you is ill, or something happens that you can't manage, you come out. A year is not more important than you are."

"What if one of us wants to stay?" Gohan asks.

"You can still come out," Chi-Chi says. "I won't make either of you stay."

"If you need to leave, I'll come with you," Goshin tells him. "We can decide together."

Chi-Chi holds them a moment longer, then looks up at Popo. "Show us the door. And before anyone goes in, we're going to prepare."

## What They Need

Popo leads them to a set of doors and opens one enough to reveal a white floor, a table and a cupboard.

"There is water and food," he says. "You can bring more. The door stays here."

Gohan peers around Chi-Chi. "Is the training space past the table?"

"Yes. Stay near the building until you know your way."

Goshin peers past him. The white floor seems to have no end. He moves closer to Gohan.

Popo closes the door without taking them through. Bulma crouches beside the school forms.

"I can bring extra supplies in capsules. Clothes too. If they're really spending that long in there, they'll need more than what fits now."

"Rice, dried vegetables and cooking oil," Chi-Chi says. "And the pot with the lid that locks."

"I'll ask Dad for a cold-storage capsule for the rest."

Gohan points at the forms. "Can I take my books?"

"Of course. We'll choose enough for the year."

"And paper for Hatch," Gohan says.

Goshin lifts the broken board. "And targets. Big ones."

Bulma makes a list. "If I bring blocks, can you promise not to put them on my car seats?"

"We can put them in the back," Gohan says.

"That's what I was afraid of."

At the mountain house, Chi-Chi sets a pot on the stove. Both boys stand where they can see as she washes the rice and measures the water. She lets Gohan stir while Goshin watches, then gives Goshin the spoon for the next pot.

"Turn the heat down when it starts to boil," she says.

Goshin watches until the lid begins to tremble. "Like this?" He turns the handle a little.

"That's it. Don't put your hand over the steam."

Gohan checks the pan of vegetables, and Chi-Chi shows them how to move it away from the flame. Goshin takes a piece when she says they are ready. It is hotter than he expects; he blows on his fingers while Gohan laughs.

"You don't have to make everything on your own," Chi-Chi tells them. "Take turns, and look after each other."

"We'll take turns washing the pot too," Gohan says.

Goshin looks at the pot. "Can we take two?"

## The First Week

The next morning, Bulma flies the family back to the Lookout with the capsules. Popo carries the schoolbooks and a stack of paper, and Chi-Chi takes the food containers. Gohan holds the folded drawing while Goshin keeps the small piece of the old board in his pocket.

At the door, Chi-Chi kneels and puts an arm around each boy.

"I'll be at the Lookout," she says. "If you open the door, Popo or I will be here. You don't have to wait until the year is over."

Gohan hugs her tightly. Goshin puts his arms around both of them.

"Will you be here when we come back?" he asks.

"Yes."

He holds on another moment, then lets go so Gohan can lift his bag. Popo opens the door, and the brothers step through together. It closes behind them.

The next step pulls Gohan's foot down hard. His knees bend with the weight of the bag; he catches it against his shoulder and changes his footing. Goshin's own feet land heavily. He braces, takes the other handle and helps lift the bag onto the table.

Gohan takes a breath, then another. The air feels dry and thin against his throat, and the silence beyond the living space has no birds or distant voices in it.

"The air feels different," he says.

"And everything pulls harder," Goshin says. He lifts off the floor, feels the stronger gravity and settles again. Gohan rises beside him, making a short turn before they set down together. They pause to get used to the thin air.

Goshin looks back. "Can you feel Mother?"

Gohan shakes his head. "Only you."

Beyond the living space, white floor stretches into a white distance. There are no trees and no mountain to tell them which way the wind is blowing.

"We should stay near the door today," Gohan says.

"I want to know where it is," Goshin agrees. Gohan hands him Hatch's drawing, and Goshin puts it beside the clock, where both of them can see it.

They unpack the capsules together. Gohan puts the schoolbooks on the shelf; Goshin carries the extra food to the cupboard. They find the beds, the washroom and the kitchen, with the door in sight from the table. An hourglass stands beside it.

Goshin runs one finger over the glass. "Does that show a whole year?"

"Popo said it does. We could count the days too."

Gohan draws small boxes across a page. Goshin adds another line beneath the first, and they leave the pencil beside it.

For their first meal, Gohan washes the rice and Goshin measures the water. Goshin keeps an eye on the pot until the lid trembles and turns down the heat. They eat at the table with the hourglass between them.

"I didn't burn it," Goshin says.

"You nearly forgot the water."

"I remembered before it was on the stove."

Gohan tries a mouthful, then pushes his bowl closer. "It's good."

After their first full day and night, they put a mark in the first box. That morning they set two marks a short distance apart. Goshin crosses to the second and stops cleanly on the line, adjusting his footing to the heavier pull.

"Now make it harder," he tells Gohan.

Gohan moves the second mark farther away and comes after him. Both lift off; Goshin turns aside and catches his brother's wrist, but Gohan twists free and reaches the line first. They land laughing and set the marks farther apart again.

On the fourth day, they unpack Bulma's training blocks. Gohan places one at the far end of the marked space and returns to the doorway. Goshin lifts four smaller blocks with his ki and sends them around the target.

He fires his Kamehameha through the opening between them. The wave strikes the target and leaves a deep cut across its face. He narrows and stops it at the mark he chose; all four moving blocks stay on their paths in the heavier gravity.

A larger stone block waits beside the target. Goshin lifts it with both arms and flies it to the door, then sets it down for the next exercise. Gohan brings him water and turns the marked target around so they can use its other side.

On another day, a cold wind reaches them across the white floor. The boys hurry back to the living space, where they wrap themselves in blankets and rub warmth into their hands. Goshin presses his cold fingers to Gohan's cheek. Gohan yelps and catches his wrist; a moment later both are laughing beneath the blanket.

They do not go out again until the wind has eased. In the days that follow, the air still makes them work for each breath, but they can stay on the marked floor longer before needing to rest.

On a hot afternoon, the white distance shimmers. Gohan hangs their washed shirts near the living space, and the water dries almost as fast as he can fasten them.

"I wish they'd do that while we were wearing them," Goshin says, tugging at his damp collar.

Gohan laughs and hands him the last shirt. Goshin lifts it with his ki and flaps it toward his brother until Gohan grabs the other end. For a moment they pull it back and forth, laughing, before they hang it up to dry.

## A Week Without Sky

After seven full days, Goshin marks the next box on their page. Gohan puts the pencil down beside him and looks toward the white distance.

"There isn't even a cloud," he says.

Goshin looks at the drawing by the clock. "We could draw one."

They make a picture of the mountain house, with a cloud above it big enough for both of them. Gohan draws the crooked gate; Goshin gives the cloud a face. It looks nothing like Nimbus, which makes Gohan laugh.

When the laughter stops, Goshin touches the little house on the page. "I miss Mother."

"Me too."

"And Father. I hope he's finding the way to King Kai."

Gohan puts the drawing beside Hatch's. "We'll show them both when we get home."

Goshin sits with him until they are ready to go back to the marked floor.

## Days on the Page

Gohan puts two blocks in a line, with a space between them. He stands at the first mark and looks toward the second.

"I want to move before I fire," he says. "If I stay in one place, it's easier to reach me."

Goshin takes a position beside the blocks. "Do you want me to come after you?"

"Start when I pass the first mark."

Goshin grins and settles into position.

Gohan's first step is heavy, but the next takes him past the mark. Goshin lifts off and follows. Gohan turns around the first block and brings his hands together as he moves.

His wave strikes the second block above the line he drew. Goshin stops beside him before either of them reaches it.

"I aimed too high," Gohan says.

"You didn't stop to fire."

"I didn't want to. Let's move the mark."

Goshin shifts the nearer mark. Gohan tries a shorter turn, then a longer one, recording each attempt on the paper Chi-Chi left.

After lunch, Goshin leaves the pencil beside the page for the next morning's mark.

The lines fill. Gohan reads aloud after breakfast, and Goshin follows the words with a finger. They practise writing on the spare paper, wash their clothes and take turns checking the supplies. They cook together often; on other evenings one sets the table while the other tends the stove. Sometimes they sit beside Hatch's drawing and add ideas to a separate sheet.

One afternoon Goshin wants another round after Gohan has put the blocks away. "Come on! I nearly caught you that time."

Gohan stands with his hands on his knees, catching his breath. "Not yet!" he says, more sharply than he meant to.

Goshin's grin falls. "After we eat?"

"After we eat and rest."

Goshin looks toward the blocks, then picks up the water bottle and brings it to him. They sit on the doorstep of the living space while Gohan drinks. Nothing moves in the white distance. Goshin starts telling him about a new path for the blocks; by the time the bottle is empty, Gohan is explaining how he would try to get through it.

One evening Gohan folds a boat from a spare page and sets it in a basin of water. Goshin folds another and makes a hole near the back.

"What is that for?" Gohan asks.

"A drain."

Water comes through the hole, and Goshin's boat tips over. Gohan laughs and catches it before it reaches the bottom. Goshin looks at the wet paper, then laughs too.

"It needs a smaller drain," Goshin says.

They fold a new boat together and float it beside Gohan's. Gohan blows on his, and Goshin blows on the other. Both boats reach the edge of the basin before the paper gets wet.

"Yours was first," Gohan says.

"Let's go back the other way."

That night Gohan is already asleep when Goshin sets the boats near the clock. Goshin goes to his own bed and pulls the blanket up.

When the first page of boxes is full, Gohan starts another. Goshin places the completed page beneath the clock.

## The Letter

One evening, Gohan puts his pencil down before the page is finished. The light in the living space is on, and the training blocks wait where the brothers left them.

"I miss Mother," he says.

Goshin stops folding his shirt. He sits beside Gohan and looks at the unfinished page.

"Do you want to go out?"

"I don't know. I wanted to stay, but I didn't know it would feel this long."

"Mother said we could come out."

"I know. What if you still want to stay?"

Goshin looks toward the door. "I miss her too. And Father. Sometimes I think he might come home while we're in here."

He glances toward the stove. "When the pot rattles, I keep thinking I'll hear her in the kitchen."

"She'd tell us to turn it down," Gohan says, managing a small smile.

Gohan touches Hatch's drawing. "Would you be angry with me for leaving?"

"No. I do want to train, but I meant what I said. If you need to go out, I'll come with you."

Gohan's eyes fill. He presses his sleeve against them. Goshin puts an arm around him, and they sit together with the folded shirt between them.

"Can we decide tomorrow?" Gohan asks.

"Yes."

"I want to write to her tonight. She won't get it until we leave, but I can tell her what we've been doing."

Goshin moves the pencil closer. Gohan writes about the rice, the books and the first time he landed beyond the third mark. When he finishes, he leaves a space at the bottom.

"Do you want to put something here?"

Goshin draws the little target board from home. Gohan adds the four sticks, then draws their mother beside the path.

The next morning Gohan folds the page and puts it beneath Hatch's drawing.

"I want to stay," he says. "If I change my mind, I'll tell you."

"All right. I want to stay too."

Gohan takes the first training block from beside the door. "Then we should use the other side today."

Goshin picks up the chalk. "I want to make a smaller mark this time." He follows Gohan onto the white floor.

## A Faster Turn

The next morning Gohan rises from the white floor under his own ki, as he did at home. The room's heavier pull changes the way he has to balance, but he flies past the nearer block and turns back toward Goshin.

"I'm going to take the farther mark without slowing down," Gohan says.

Goshin lifts off beside him. "Then I'll try to catch you before it."

Gohan darts between the blocks. Goshin follows, matching his turn, but Gohan changes direction and reaches the mark from the other side. They set the blocks farther apart and go again.

Over the following days they choose different courses under the heavier gravity. Gohan gathers a wave without dropping out of flight. On one run Goshin closes the gap before Gohan can turn, catches his wrist and stops them both short of the far mark. Gohan twists free and sweeps back toward the door; Goshin follows without touching down.

They reach the living space breathing hard, but are ready to go again after water.

"I used to need longer to catch my breath," Goshin says.

Gohan lifts off. "This time I'll start behind you!"

Goshin rises ahead, and they race again.

"When we go outside, it's going to feel so light," Gohan says.

Goshin grins. "Then we'll see how fast we can really go."

## The Moving Blocks

More pages fill beneath the clock. Goshin takes six blocks to the marked space, each larger than the four small ones they used during the first week. Gohan puts a target beyond them.

"How far apart do you want them?" he asks.

Goshin arranges the blocks in a wide ring. "About this far."

Gohan returns to the doorway and lifts the target with his ki. Goshin sends the six blocks along separate paths and gathers a wave while they move.

Gohan moves the target across the far side of the ring. Goshin bends the wave between the circling blocks to follow it. The light strikes the target, and a piece breaks free. He catches it with his ki and lowers it while the six blocks continue around him.

When he brings everything down, Gohan walks to the target. "You kept the wave between the blocks while I moved it."

"Move it back the other way next time," Goshin says. "I'll follow that turn too."

Gohan lifts the fallen piece and sets it against a backstop for his own next attempt.

## A Different Landing

Months later, Gohan places three blocks across the white floor. Goshin waits at the far mark with his hands empty.

"I'm going around the middle one," Gohan says. "Try to reach me before the last mark."

Goshin bends his knees. "I'm ready!"

Gohan runs between the first two blocks. Goshin flies toward his right shoulder, but Gohan drops low, plants one hand on the floor and turns beneath him. He rises beside the middle block and fires a narrow wave at the third.

The light hits the mark. Gohan closes his hands, and the wave stops before it reaches the floor behind the target.

Goshin lands at the last line. "You turned the other way."

"You were already going right."

Gohan resets the target. Goshin steps up beside him, and Gohan shows him the movement. They try it together, first on the ground and then in the air.

For the next round, Gohan holds out both hands. "I want to push you past the line."

Goshin takes his stance. "All right."

Gohan comes forward. Goshin catches his wrists, but Gohan turns his shoulder and drives toward the line. Goshin's heel moves across the white floor.

Goshin braces. His ki gathers around his arms, stronger than it was at the beginning of the round. The pressure beneath his feet deepens the marks on the floor, and one of the blocks shifts toward Gohan's ankle. Goshin catches it with his ki before it reaches him, lifts it clear and turns with Gohan's push. He rises over the line and lands behind his brother.

Gohan spins and drives forward again. Goshin sets his feet sooner than at the start of the round; this time his heel stays inside the line. He turns past Gohan's reaching hand and crosses the gap before his brother completes the next step.

Goshin touches his shoulder, and both boys stop.

"You got around me," Gohan says.

"You nearly pushed me over."

Gohan steps back to the first mark. "One more?"

"One more."

This time Gohan changes direction before Goshin can reach his shoulder. They cross the marked floor twice, then stop for water.

Later, under the same heavy gravity, Goshin sets out eight blocks of the same stone and size as the one he carried during the first week. He makes two stacks of four and puts a hand beneath each. The stacks rise with him as he flies the length of the marked space, supporting their weight with his arms. At the door he lands and sets them down gently, one stack at a time.

Gohan looks from the blocks to his brother. "We started with one of those in the first week."

Goshin grins. "I want to see what I can carry next." He takes the pencil from behind his ear.

Gohan looks at the fresh line of boxes. "How many have we done?"

Goshin turns over the completed pages. Together they count the marks, and Gohan writes the number at the top of the new sheet.

## Outside the Door

The sun is low over the Lookout when Bulma returns. Chi-Chi sits beneath the trees with the school forms across her lap.

"I brought the telephone number for the school office," Bulma says. "The director can use it if she wants another person to call."

"I'll ask her first."

Bulma sits beside her. "How long have they been in there now?"

Popo looks at the clock near the passage. "For the boys, several months."

Chi-Chi folds the top page. "Goshin likes the rice a little softer. I showed them how much water to use, but I should have written it down."

"You wrote down enough for a small library," Bulma says.

"I could have written one more line."

Bulma takes the forms before the wind can lift them. "They can open the door if they need you."

"I know."

Chi-Chi looks toward the passage. Popo brings a cup of tea and sets it on the bench beside her.

Far below, Krillin and Yamcha land on a stretch of empty coast. Puar brings water to the rock where they have left their bags. Krillin wipes his face and raises his hands again.

"One more round?" he asks.

Yamcha sets the cup down. "I was about to ask you."

## The Last Page

Inside the room, Gohan pulls a clean shirt over his head. The sleeves of his old one have become short at the wrists. Goshin finds the next size in the capsule Chi-Chi packed and takes it to his own bed.

"Mother knew," Gohan says.

"She packed more than one size."

Gohan holds the old shirt against himself. "I want to show her this one too."

They fold it with the completed pages. Goshin's black hair still rises in the same wild spikes as Goku's, and his face carries his father's likeness with Chi-Chi's eyes. Both boys have grown during the months inside; their tails move behind them as they gather the training blocks.

Gohan sets three targets in a line. He flies between the first two, turns in the air and fires. His wave passes through the gap he chose and cuts across the mark on the third target.

He closes his hands. The light stops, leaving the block standing.

"That one didn't fall," Gohan says.

"You stopped before the bottom."

Gohan goes to inspect the cut. "I'm going to show Mother when we get out."

He looks out across the white floor. "I want to try more power too. Farther away from the door."

They set three spare stone blocks in a line, far from the door. Goshin moves aside; Gohan rises into the air and cups his hands.

"Kamehameha!"

The broad wave tears through all three blocks and gouges a long channel in the white floor. Gohan turns it upward, stops it and catches the nearest falling fragments with his ki.

Goshin flies over to him. "You stayed in the air the whole time!"

Gohan lowers the pieces. "I couldn't do that with so much power in the first week. Let's see yours."

At the far mark, Goshin raises eight larger blocks, each on a different path. He sets their first-week target behind two more blocks of the same size.

"Can I stand by the door?" Gohan asks.

"Yes."

Gohan moves to the living space. Goshin cups his hands, holding the eight blocks in motion as his ki gathers. Their shadows move across the floor.

"Kamehameha!"

The narrow wave cuts through the first two targets and strikes the old block. The moving blocks stay on their paths as the targets split and fall apart.

Goshin catches the pieces with his ki, lowers them and brings the other blocks down beside them.

Gohan runs a finger along the cut in the old target, past the mark from their first week. He carries a piece back to the table and sets it beside the scrap from the mountain house.

Later, far across the empty floor, Goshin sets the eight larger blocks moving again, well clear of his aim. He cups his hands and sends a broad Kamehameha into the white distance. The floor shudders and splits along its path, throwing fragments high into the air. Gohan braces against the rushing force, his clothes snapping around him.

Goshin turns the sustained wave upward, narrows it to a brilliant stream and stops it cleanly. The eight blocks keep moving until he chooses to bring them down; fragments continue to fall along the long cut in the floor.

"You can turn it even when it's that strong," Gohan says.

"I want to see what happens when we both use ours," Goshin answers.

Before their last night, the boys go out onto the white floor, well away from the living space. Gohan brings his hands to his side, and blue light gathers between them.

"Stronger than last time?" he asks.

Goshin grins and cups his hands. "Let's try!"

Their Kamehamehas meet between them with a booming crack. Pressure races out across the empty floor. Gohan holds his ground in the heavy gravity and feeds more ki into his wave. Goshin answers; his own power swells, pushing the meeting point toward his brother. Gohan shifts his stance and holds it clear of the living space.

"Enough!" Gohan calls.

Both stop their waves, and the light between them fades. Goshin drops onto the floor, laughing, and Gohan sits beside him until their breathing settles.

After the last night, Goshin marks the final box on the last page. The first seven marks he and Gohan made together are on the first line; every full day since has a mark of its own.

"A whole year," Gohan says.

"We did it together," Goshin says. His voice catches, and he presses the folded pages against his chest.

Gohan checks the pages once more. "Mother will have been waiting one day."

Goshin folds the last sheet and puts it with the others. "Will she be at the door?"

"She said she would."

They return the training blocks to Bulma's capsules and pack them with their books, the drawings and the two pieces of target. Goshin ties his outgrown shoes to one bag. Gohan takes the other, and they leave the room only after the count is complete.

## Back at the Lookout

The door opens onto the Lookout passage. Chi-Chi stands beside Popo with her hands clasped in front of her. Morning light reaches the tiles beyond them.

Gohan steps out first. Goshin follows, carrying his bag and the folded pages. Chi-Chi catches both of them in her arms before they can put anything down.

The instant the brothers come through the door, Piccolo stops in the middle of a strike far across the wasteland. Two distinct ki signatures fill his senses: Gohan's and Goshin's, both immediately familiar. Each is immensely powerful, far beyond what he felt from either boy at the clearing. Even after his own training, both have grown far stronger than he is.

"Gohan? Goshin?"

He stares toward the Lookout, then launches into the air, leaving a cloud of dust behind him.

On the Lookout tiles, Kami feels them too. His hand tightens around his staff.

On the coast, Krillin's fist stops short of Yamcha. Both turn toward the same distant point.

"That's Gohan," Krillin says. "And Goshin."

Yamcha lowers his hands. "How did both of them get so strong?"

In the mountains, Tien stops beside Chiaotzu. They look toward the Lookout too, feeling the two powerful ki signatures.

Back at the doorway, Gohan's voice is muffled against Chi-Chi's shoulder. "You're here."

"I said I would be." Chi-Chi holds them a moment longer. "I missed you both."

She holds them away from her and looks at both their faces. Goshin sets down his bag and pages, smiling back at her. His sleeves fit now, but the old shoes tied to his bag are too small. Gohan reaches up and touches her cheek, as if checking that she is really there.

"You both grew," Chi-Chi says. Her voice catches. She smooths Gohan's hair and then Goshin's, taking her time over each familiar, changed face.

"We brought the old clothes," Gohan says. "And the pages."

Goshin picks up the stack and gives it to her. She turns past the first seven marks the boys made and looks at the lines below.

"You kept every day."

"Gohan made the second page."

Popo carries their bags out to the bench and sets them beside it. Gohan stops at the edge of the tiles and looks over the clouds.

"The sky's still here."

"And the mountain," Chi-Chi says. "We'll go home together."

Goshin springs up, shoots above the nearest tree and laughs at how easily he rises. He turns in the air and lands beside Gohan, who is already grinning at him.

Goshin takes a capsule from his bag and presses its button. A puff of smoke clears to reveal eight of the training blocks. He lifts one hand, and six rise and circle above the tiles. He brings up the other two, guides them between the trees and back again, then lowers each one beside Popo without touching the floor hard.

Gohan lifts off beside the blocks, sweeps across the open tiles and turns back to land beside Chi-Chi. The familiar movement is much faster and easier outside the room's gravity.

"That turn is new," Chi-Chi says, smiling. "You've both improved."

Popo looks at the blocks, then at each of the boys. "You have both become very strong."

"We practised every day," Gohan says.

Gohan pulls his own page from the bag. "I want to show you where I stopped the wave."

He spreads the sheet on the bench and points to the three marks. Chi-Chi sits beside him while Goshin fetches the old target piece.

## Beyond the Planet

King Kai watches Goku step across the grass with red light held close around his body. Goku stops before the house and lets the light fade without sending it into the tree.

"Again," King Kai says.

Goku rolls his shoulders. "This time I'll turn sooner."

Far away, two round ships travel through the dark. Nappa looks at the display in front of him.

"Are we close?"

Vegeta studies his own instrument. "Close enough to get ready."

The ships continue toward Earth.

**NARRATOR:** A year has passed for both brothers inside the room, and roughly a day has passed outside. Goku's training continues, and the Saiyans are still on their way.
