## Chronology and scale: still open

Chapters 7 and 14 explicitly give Snake Way's **traditional approximate** length as one million kilometres; earlier review language saying no *exact* distance was established should not be read as though the manuscript gave no estimate at all. The author regards Goku as light-speed or faster already in original *Dragon Ball* as a working interpretation for this branch. Neither that interpretation nor the approximate road length establishes a verified exact speed or an unconditional average speed for every journey.

The outward trip is intercut with several Earth days, but Goku chooses to train on the way and is actually delayed by Princess Snake. The return is harder: he says he is hurrying as fast as he can, while the Saiyan landing, battle, ordinary-speed civilian response, multiple evacuations and initial aftermath occur before he arrives. Chapter 26 also shows him flying across a bend. If the million-kilometre estimate is treated as the full distance **and** he averages light speed over all of it, the ideal travel time would be roughly 3.3 seconds; those assumptions have not been established on-page. The ordinary-speed scenes make it unsafe simply to assume that every intervening event occurred in a few seconds. The revised Kaiō-ken moment shows an active effort, not a reconciliation of elapsed time.

A real repair may require changing the departure and communications, or allowing an earlier arrival and rewriting the affected action, rescue and reunion consequences across several chapters. Neither option has been silently imposed here. Do not solve the conflict by inventing an afterlife speed cap, a convenient unexplained delay or unsupported time dilation; do not call an exact impossibility proved by an author interpretation that does not require a constant travel speed.

The older date and early-day compression questions also remain bounded: the selected Chapter 3 ends while there is still light and does not require the historical, superseded overnight ending. The previously recorded competing calendar accounts are not converted into a newly verified on-page date.

## Continuing handoff and craft

Gohan, Goshin and Vegeta retain their tails and know the full-moon danger. Goshin remembers and can make a Power Ball, but has not produced a full one of his own on-page. His evolving Talent can enable space adaptation given time; neither an exact time nor completed unaided space survival is shown. Gohan retains his own development and decisions. A doctor advises avoiding hard blows while sore; Chi-Chi separately forbids fighting until the boys are no longer sore **and she says so**. No match, winner, Goshin Kaiō-ken mastery or new scouter maximum is established.

The city and home threads give Chi-Chi, the boys, helpers and civilians real agency. They also recur through several consecutive chapters, as do rescue precautions and care handoffs after the battle. Their accumulation can slow the action and deserves further literary judgment; it is not evidence that those characters should be removed, that every small action is a power cap, or that the author's scale direction has been fully satisfied. Ship safety, Namek passenger decisions, the children's longer placement, C-6's loan and repair, the covered machine, Baba's attempt to contact Grandpa Gohan, Upa's flask, the city inquiry and guarded Saiyan care remain open.
