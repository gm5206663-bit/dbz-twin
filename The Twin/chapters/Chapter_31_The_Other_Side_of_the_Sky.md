# Chapter 31 — The Other Side of the Sky

*Anime episode reference: Dragon Ball Z, Episodes 36 (King Kai's Namek inquiry, brought forward) and 38 (early preparation and family decisions, adapted in part).*

## The Way Back

The panel beeps.

Bulma freezes beside the seat. Gohan and Goshin stop laughing. Even Piccolo takes a step away from the wall.

The compartment beside Bulma slides shut, taking the toilet with it.

Goshin blinks. "It listened to you."

"Good," Bulma says. "At least somebody did."

Jupiter still fills the window. Goshin looks back at its bands of cloud, staying where he can see only the planet.

"We should turn away before the ship moves," Gohan says. "There might be moons outside."

His tail brushes the seat as he steps back. Goshin moves with him, and Piccolo stays by the window.

"If we looked at a full moon, we'd change," Goshin says. "And if I made one like Vegeta's in here, we'd break the ship."

"Please don't," Bulma says. "Or touch anything. Or say anything that sounds like anything."

"That could make the trip very quiet," Popo says.

Piccolo looks at the planet a moment longer. "We should return. Kami is waiting."

The words sober Gohan. Mother is waiting too, and they promised her they would come straight back.

"Ready," he says.

Popo speaks to the panel in the unfamiliar language, giving it the words for Earth and their return. A tone answers. This time Gohan catches the edge of the seat before the floor shivers.

Goshin remembers the sudden movement on the way out. He sets his feet differently and reaches for Bulma as the ship surges. She loses her grip on the seat, but his hand catches her arm before she can slide away.

"Thanks," she says, pulling herself upright.

"I knew it was going to do that again."

"Then next time warn me!"

Jupiter slips away. The brothers keep their backs to the window as stars move across it. When the blue curve of Earth fills the glass, Piccolo checks the view.

"Earth," he says. "No moon in the window."

The boys turn. "I can see it!" Goshin calls.

Clouds and oceans fill the glass. They have seen Earth from this ship before, but now Gohan finds himself searching for the land beneath the clouds. Somewhere down there are his parents, the city, their home and the quarry where they left their friends.

The ship descends through the bright sky. Bare ridges rise beneath it, and its supports settle into the hollow at Yunzabit with a heavy thump.

Popo speaks the entrance word. The platform lowers into the wind.

Kami is waiting just beyond the ship. He has not moved far from the place where he stood when it left. Gohan steps down first, followed by Goshin, Piccolo, Bulma and Popo.

"You came back," Kami says.

"We saw Jupiter!" Goshin tells him. "It was enormous."

"The house really is a spaceship," Gohan adds. "Popo brought us right back."

Kami rests his hand on the curved side of the craft. He looks at the boys, then at Popo and Bulma. The empty hollow has given him something back, even if it has not yet told him who sent it there.

Bulma is already taking her tool bag from the platform. "I'd like Dad to look at it. May we bring him here? We won't take anything apart without asking."

Kami considers the ship. "Yes. I would like to know more about it too."

Popo reaches back inside and retrieves the folded carpet from beside the entrance, where he had tucked it for the flight. "I can bring Dr. Brief."

"I'll come with you," Bulma says. "He'll need the proper tools, and I want to make sure he doesn't start without me."

Gohan feels for his parents' ki. "We should tell Mother and Father we're back."

"I'll come," Piccolo says.

Kami watches as the three lift above the ridge. Their ki is already racing toward the quarry when Popo unfolds the carpet, and Bulma has only just climbed aboard when they disappear from sight.

## Back at the Quarry

Chi-Chi hears them before she can see them. Two familiar voices come over the ridge at once, followed by a rush of air that stirs her hair.

The boys land in front of her with Piccolo a pace behind. Goshin opens his mouth, but Chi-Chi catches both children and pulls them close first.

"You came straight back?"

"We did," Gohan says against her shoulder. "We went to Jupiter and came straight back."

She holds him away from her long enough to look at his face, then checks Goshin's. Neither seems upset; Goshin looks ready to burst.

"Can we tell Dad now?"

"You can tell me too!"

"It has stripes," Goshin says. "And a storm bigger than Earth!"

Goku joins them with Krillin. "A storm that big?"

Gohan spreads his arms. "The planet filled the whole window. Piccolo checked for moons first, so we could look at the clouds."

"And the spaceship put Bulma to bed," Goshin says.

Krillin stares. "What?"

Goshin demonstrates by wrapping his arms around himself and falling backward. Gohan catches him, laughing, and nearly loses his grip when Goshin continues the story from upside down.

Goku laughs with them. "I really did miss something!"

"You missed the toilet," Piccolo says.

"There was a toilet?" Krillin asks.

"There was," Gohan says. "And Bulma said we weren't allowed to talk about it."

"She didn't say we couldn't tell Dad," Goshin replies.

Chi-Chi has been smiling despite herself. She smooths Gohan's hair away from his face and looks at Piccolo. "Thank you for bringing them back."

Piccolo folds his arms. "They knew the way."

"I was thanking you anyway."

A scrape sounds behind Krillin. Vegeta has shifted against the quarry wall, his tail curled against his damaged armour, and Goku turns at once. Across the broken tracks, Tien and Chiaotzu remain beside Nappa's gully. Launch is near Tien; Yajirobe sits on a rock with Upa's flask beside him.

Vegeta studies the boys. "So you've found a ship."

Goku steps between him and the family. "You stay where you are."

"I haven't moved, Kakarot."

"You moved enough for me," Krillin says.

Vegeta's gaze passes over Goshin. He has felt the boy's power since waking and has seen what happened to his own attack. He makes no attempt to rise, but his expression sharpens.

"There are people beyond this planet who won't be impressed by a trip to Jupiter."

"Who?" Gohan asks.

Goku's eyes brighten. "Are they that strong?"

"Goku!" Chi-Chi says.

"I know. If they're coming to hurt anybody, we'll stop them." He looks back at Vegeta. "But I'd sure like to find out how strong they are."

Vegeta smiles without answering either question.

"You think whoever comes looking will help you?" Nappa calls from the gully. "You tried to finish me yourself."

"You failed me," Vegeta says.

Nappa plants a hand on the ground. Tien steps forward, and Chiaotzu rises beside him. For an instant Nappa looks ready to drag himself out of the gully; then he settles back against the rock, breathing hard.

"We're still trying to find a way to get you care," Tien tells him. "Don't make the doctor regret coming."

Nappa scowls at him but stays where he is. Krillin keeps an eye on Vegeta's hands.

"We need somewhere he can't see the moon, if it comes up full," Krillin says quietly.

"And we'll have to stop him making another one," Goku says.

Piccolo watches both Saiyans for another moment, then motions Goku a little way from the prince. Krillin remains beside Vegeta.

"The ship belonged to Kami," Piccolo says quietly. "Nappa called us Namekians and said Namek was our world. Kami doesn't remember it. We still don't know where it is."

Goku glances at the two Saiyans. "King Kai might."

"The one you trained with?"

"Yeah. He can see a long way, and he's talked to me from his planet before. I'll ask him."

Piccolo looks toward the gully. "Not where those two can hear the answer."

"I was thinking the same thing."

Goku closes his eyes.

## A Voice from Far Away

On his small planet, King Kai has just sat down with a book when Bubbles bounds across the grass and tries to take it from him.

"Not that one!" King Kai says, holding it above the monkey's reach. "That's the one with the good jokes."

Bubbles chatters and grabs for the corner. A voice reaches King Kai before he can rescue the book.

*King Kai? It's me, Goku.*

"Goku!" King Kai drops the book. Bubbles picks it up and immediately runs off. "How are you getting along?"

*Everyone's here. Those two Saiyans are still hurt, and we're watching them. Can you talk to Piccolo and Kami too? There's something we need to ask, but we don't want Vegeta and Nappa hearing it.*

King Kai pauses. He can feel the gathered lives at the quarry and the old guardian far away in the highlands.

*I can speak to the people I choose,* he tells Goku. *Who else?*

*Chi-Chi and the boys are here. They ought to know too.*

At the quarry, a voice sounds in Piccolo's thoughts. Gohan grips his mother's hand. Goshin looks around, delighted, until Goku points to his own head.

*King Kai,* Goku says, *do you know a planet called Namek?*

On the little planet, King Kai straightens. "Of course I know Namek. I am King Kai, you know."

Bubbles comes back with the joke book open over his head. King Kai takes it from him, sets it aside and pulls a folded star chart from his pocket.

*Where is it?* Piccolo asks.

*Let's see. In the way you Earth people would describe it...*

King Kai scans the chart, squints and turns it sideways. Bubbles points to a line with one long finger.

*SW sixty-six... No, wait. SU eighty-three. And nine-zero-four-five XY.*

Goku silently repeats the numbers. Piccolo fixes them in his memory. At Yunzabit, Kami raises his head; the voice has found him as well.

*Will that get us there?* Goku asks.

*It tells you where to begin looking. You will still need someone who knows how to use it, and a way to reach it.* King Kai's tone changes. *Namek was a beautiful world. A long time ago, terrible weather swept over it. I feared the people there had died out.*

The wind moves over the empty hollow at Yunzabit. Kami closes his fingers around the head of his staff.

*Died?* Gohan asks.

*Wait. Let me look.*

King Kai settles on the grass and concentrates. Bubbles sits beside him, the chart spread on the grass between them. Far below, the quarry seems to hold its breath.

Piccolo can hear the scrape of Vegeta's armour as the prince shifts again. He keeps his eyes on him, but the quarry and the injured Saiyan are suddenly very far from the question in his mind.

A child once waited in a lonely place for a family that did not come. If those storms are the reason, he may never find the people who sent the child away.

*Ah! There they are!* King Kai says. *Namekians. Not many, but they're alive.*

Goshin's face lights up. Gohan squeezes Chi-Chi's hand. Piccolo does not speak at once.

*You're certain?*

*I can sense them. There are fewer than a hundred that I can make out, but there are living people on Namek.*

Kami lowers himself onto a stone beside the ship. He has lived on Earth for so long that the ridges of Yunzabit are as familiar as any place he knows. Beyond them, somewhere he cannot see, people of his kind have survived.

Piccolo looks toward the sky. "They're there," he says aloud, too softly for Vegeta to make out the words.

*Can they make Dragon Balls too?* Goku asks. *Kami made the ones on Earth.*

*I don't know,* King Kai says. *You'll have to ask them. Finding a planet is not the same as knowing what its people have made.*

Goku accepts that. Piccolo has another question, but he cannot decide which one to ask first.

*The old weather might explain why Kami was sent away,* King Kai says, *but I cannot tell you what happened to his family. And a distant view won't tell you everything about a world today. Be careful if you decide to go looking.*

The voice fades. Goku opens his eyes.

Gohan is smiling at Piccolo. "There are people there."

Piccolo looks back at him. He has wanted an answer since Nappa first spoke the name. Now that he has one, there are more questions behind it.

"I heard."

Vegeta watches their faces. Whatever they have heard, nobody repeats the direction for him.

Chi-Chi puts a hand on each boy's shoulder. "And now you're both going to the doctor."

Goshin had almost forgotten. "Now?"

"You said you knew when you came back."

Goku grins. "You did say that."

"You were supposed to help me," Goshin says.

"I am. I'm helping your mother."

Goshin gives him an injured look. Then he goes to the bags and brings them to Chi-Chi.

Piccolo turns toward the highlands. "I'll tell Bulma and Popo what King Kai found."

"Kami heard him," Gohan says.

"I know. I want to talk to him too."

He rises and is gone over the ridge. Goshin offers Chi-Chi his hand.

"I'll carry you."

"And the bags?"

"Those too."

He lifts her easily, keeping the bags clear of her feet. Gohan rises beside them. Chi-Chi looks back at Goku, who has returned to Krillin's side.

"I'll bring them home after the doctor," she says.

"I'll be here."

The quarry drops away beneath them. Goshin flies with care until Chi-Chi has a good hold, then follows Gohan toward the city. Goku watches their ki move away before turning back to Vegeta.

## A Ship for More Than One

At Capsule Corporation, Yamcha is waiting by the laboratory window when Popo's carpet appears again.

"How did it go?" he asks as Bulma climbs inside.

"We went to Jupiter."

Yamcha looks at Popo. Popo nods.

"You actually went?"

"And came back," Bulma says. "Dad! Get your tools."

Dr. Brief emerges from behind the workbench with a pair of glasses in one hand. Puar floats up beside him.

"I've told the officers about the signal," Dr. Brief says. "They know to keep people back from the other landing site too."

Bulma glances at the scouters. The tested device and its cell are separate again, alongside the other wrapped pieces.

"I took the cell out after you left," Yamcha says. "I didn't press any controls."

She doesn't know what either scouter may have sent before the cells came out, or whether there is another source of power hidden inside.

"Don't switch them on," she tells her father.

"I thought you wanted me to get my tools."

"The tools for the spaceship. Not the things that make spaceships explode."

"Ah. Different drawer."

Yamcha picks up the case Dr. Brief indicates. "Want me to come?"

Bulma looks at the equipment laid out around the scouters. "Could you stay with Puar and handle the calls here? If the officers learn anything about the other pod, I want to know. And don't let Dad's assistants turn these on either."

"We can do that," Puar says.

Yamcha hands the case to Dr. Brief. "You can tell me about Jupiter when you get back."

"I'll tell you about it from the spaceship," Bulma says. "Come on, Dad."

Popo makes room on the carpet. Dr. Brief settles beside his daughter, holding the case on his knees. An instant later the laboratory and Yamcha's wave are behind them.

When they reach Yunzabit, Piccolo is standing with Kami beside the ship. Bulma hops down before the carpet has quite settled.

"King Kai found Namek," Piccolo tells her. "There are people there."

She stops. "He did? How far away?"

Piccolo gives her the direction and numbers King Kai supplied. Bulma takes a notebook from her pocket and makes him repeat them while she writes. Then she checks the page against what Kami heard.

"This gives me somewhere to start," she says. "It doesn't tell the ship how to get there."

Dr. Brief has reached the craft. He circles one of the supports and looks up at its rounded body.

"You lived in this?" he asks Kami.

"For many years."

"And it still flies." Dr. Brief taps the outer surface with a knuckle, listening to the sound. "Whoever made it knew a great deal more about these things than I do."

Bulma stares at him. "You're impressed?"

"Very."

"Good. Be impressed inside. I want to know how it works."

Popo speaks the entrance word, and the platform descends. Dr. Brief steps onto it with his case. Bulma follows, carrying her notebook, while Kami, Piccolo and Popo remain within reach of the entrance.

Inside, Dr. Brief studies the panel without touching it. He listens as Bulma describes the flight, the sudden acceleration and the ship's responses to their voices. Then he opens his case and takes out a compact instrument.

"We'll need measurements before we change anything," he says. "A trip to Jupiter tells us it can travel quickly. It doesn't tell us how it chooses a course to another star, or whether it will keep everyone fed and breathing for that long."

"I know. I want a proper shower, too. And better beds."

"I thought it already put you to bed."

"Piccolo told you?"

"You told me. On the carpet."

Bulma snatches the notebook back from him. Dr. Brief chuckles and returns to the panel.

They work while the light shifts over the highlands. Bulma compares the direction Piccolo gave her with a star chart and the rough time she remembers from the Jupiter test. Dr. Brief examines the cabin and asks Popo to repeat the commands he knows.

At last Bulma comes down the platform with her notebook covered in figures.

"If the ship can keep travelling anything like that fast, and if my conversion is right, Namek might be about a month away. One way."

Gohan's and Goshin's mother is not here to hear that, and Bulma is glad she has time to make the calculation less uncertain before telling her.

"Might?" Piccolo asks.

"It's an estimate. We haven't tested an interstellar route, and I don't know how long the ship can keep its passengers supplied." She closes the notebook. "Dad and I can find out what needs changing. First we have to understand what we would be changing."

"And how to tell it where to go," Dr. Brief adds.

Bulma looks at Popo. "You'll have to teach me more of those words. I was about to ask you to come with us."

Popo glances at Kami. "Popo cannot leave the Lookout for so long."

"Even if it's only two months?"

"It may be longer. Kami must look after Earth, and Popo must look after the Lookout. I will help you learn the words before you go."

"Piccolo understands the language too," Kami says.

"Some of it," Piccolo says. "I knew what you were saying at the tournament. That doesn't mean I know every command this ship uses."

"Then we find out together." Bulma points her pencil at him. "I can record the words and work on a translator."

Piccolo looks at the open doorway. "I'm going to Namek."

Bulma lowers the pencil. "You haven't even heard whether the ship is ready."

"I didn't say I was leaving now."

Kami watches him. They are bound in a way neither can ignore: if Piccolo dies, Kami dies too. But Piccolo has crossed the quarry, followed a clue to Yunzabit and stood at Jupiter because he chose to find answers.

"I would like to know what became of them," Kami says. "The people who sent me away, if any remember."

"Then I'll ask."

Kami inclines his head. "And ask what you wish for yourself."

Piccolo looks away toward the highlands. "I intend to."

Bulma opens her notebook again. "I'm not sending you off in my father's handiwork without someone who can fix it. If we can make it safe enough to go, I want to see Namek too."

"Your father's handiwork?" Dr. Brief calls from inside the ship. "I thought we weren't changing anything yet."

"We're about to!"

She stops herself before shouting another word at the panel. Popo smiles, and Piccolo's mouth twitches.

## Thirty-Eight

At the city hospital, the doctor asks Gohan to lift his arm and show her where it hurts. He raises it carefully, then turns his shoulder as she directs. Goshin waits on the next examination bed, swinging his borrowed boots until Chi-Chi asks him to stop.

The doctor listens to their breathing, examines Goshin's sore side and asks each of them what happened. Chi-Chi tells her about Dr. Mori's earlier visit. The doctor sends the boys for X-rays and looks at the images when they return.

"I don't see a fracture on these," she tells Chi-Chi. "They're moving comfortably, and I haven't found anything that needs them to stay here tonight. They should avoid hard blows while they're sore. If the pain worsens, or they have trouble breathing, bring them back."

"No fighting," Chi-Chi says to the boys.

"Until we're not sore?" Goshin asks.

"And until I say so."

Gohan and Goshin exchange a look. The doctor hides a smile behind the chart.

"What about Nappa?" Gohan asks. "Dr. Mori said he needed more help, but she couldn't bring him here safely."

"She spoke to our team about equipment," the doctor says. "They're looking into what could be taken out to him. They would need to talk to the people watching him first, and I can't tell you when it will be ready."

"We'll tell them," Chi-Chi says.

While Chi-Chi goes to collect their papers, Gohan finds an empty stretch of corridor beside the waiting room. He puts his book on a chair and places his hands on the floor.

"What are you doing?" Goshin asks.

"Seeing how my shoulder feels. I'm not fighting."

Gohan lowers himself and pushes up again, easily lifting his own weight. Goshin crouches beside him and starts counting.

"Nine. Ten. Eleven."

"You missed the first eight."

"I was counting in my head."

At thirty-eight, Gohan feels a pull in his shoulder. His arms are nowhere near tired; they have managed far more than his own weight in the room. Even so, he stops and rocks back onto his knees. He remembers what the doctor just said.

"Does it hurt?" Goshin asks.

"A little. I'm going to wait."

Goshin holds out a hand. Gohan takes it and stands. By the time Chi-Chi returns, he has picked up his book and is trying to sit as if he has been reading all along.

She looks from the book to his face. "What were you doing?"

"A few exercises. I stopped when my shoulder hurt."

"He did thirty-eight," Goshin says.

"Goshin!"

"You said it didn't count as fighting."

Chi-Chi puts the papers in her bag. "The doctor said no hard blows. She also said to come back if it gets worse. I want to know if it hurts again, not after you've done another thirty-eight."

"All right," Gohan says.

She brushes a strand of hair out of his eyes, then looks at Goshin. "That goes for both of you."

"I only counted."

"I know you."

Goshin leans against her side. "Can we go home now?"

"Yes. And we have something to read."

Outside the hospital, Goshin offers to carry her again. She asks about his side before taking his hand; he turns carefully and tells her how it feels. Then he lifts her and the bags, with Gohan flying beside them.

## The Letter at Home

The mountain house looks just as they left it. The path bends past the trees, and the little target board still stands where the boys can see it from the yard.

Chi-Chi opens the door. Goshin sets the bags down, and Gohan goes straight to the table. The house is quiet without Goku, but his place is still there, and the boys' books are on the shelf.

Gohan brings the folded letter from the bag. Its creases have gone soft during the year in the room. He places it in front of Chi-Chi and climbs onto the bench beside her; Goshin sits on her other side.

"Now?" she asks.

"Now."

She opens the page. Gohan has written about the rice sticking to the pot, the books they brought and the first time he landed beyond the third mark. Near the bottom is Goshin's drawing of their target board. Gohan has added the four sticks and a figure beside the path.

"That's you," Goshin says.

"I can see that." Chi-Chi traces the figure with a finger, careful not to cover it. "What happened to the rice?"

"We ate it," Goshin says. "Most of it."

"He scraped the pot," Gohan adds. "I thought we'd have to throw it away."

"You didn't?"

"We needed it for supper."

Chi-Chi laughs, then reads the lines again more slowly. She remembers the day they went through the door and how suddenly they had returned to her, taller and stronger, with a year behind their eyes. On this page they are still in the middle of that year, trying to cook supper and thinking about home.

She puts an arm around each of them. "I'm glad you wrote to me."

Gohan leans against her. "I wanted you to know what we were doing."

"You can show Father too," Goshin says. "He'll want to see the board."

"We will."

The telephone rings. Gohan jumps down and reaches it before the second ring.

"Hello?"

"Gohan? It's Bulma. Is your mother there?"

"Yes. Did you find out about the ship?"

"Some things. I thought she'd want to hear them too."

Chi-Chi takes the receiver. The boys crowd close enough to hear Bulma's voice on the other end.

Bulma explains that King Kai's direction has given her a place to begin, and that Dr. Brief is examining the craft. She tells Chi-Chi about her estimate: perhaps a month there and another back, if the calculations and the ship's systems hold up.

"That's not a promise," Bulma adds. "We still have a lot to check."

Chi-Chi looks at the letter lying open on the table. "Who is going?"

"Piccolo wants to. I want to as well, if we can get the ship ready. Popo will help us with the language, but he says he can't leave the Lookout for that long. We haven't settled anything else."

Gohan opens his mouth. Then he looks at Chi-Chi and waits for her to finish listening.

"Thank you for telling me," she says. "Don't put my boys on any list. We need to talk to Goku first."

"I haven't put anybody on a list. I'm still trying to work out where we'd put the beds."

Chi-Chi hangs up. For a moment, all three look at the telephone.

"I want to go," Gohan says.

She turns to him. "Gohan—"

"I want to help Piccolo find his people."

"You just came home from Jupiter. You haven't even had supper, and now you want to go away for months?"

"I know it would be a long time." Gohan's fingers tighten on the edge of the table. "Piccolo helped us when Vegeta made that light. He showed me where to aim, and he listened when I said I didn't want to look. I want to help him too."

"Piccolo is alive and standing on his own two feet. He hasn't asked you to go."

"I know. I'm asking."

Chi-Chi opens her mouth, but Gohan has more to say.

"And it isn't only because of him. There are people on another world, Mother. I want to meet them. I want to know what happened to Kami's family."

"You don't know what could happen out there."

"Then we'll find out what we can before we go."

"You are not going until we know that ship is safe, and even then—"

"Please listen to me!"

Gohan's voice surprises them all. The lid jumps on the pot by the stove. Goshin catches it before it can fall, his own ki stirring in answer to his brother's. He holds the lid still in his palm and sets it back gently.

Gohan's face goes red. "I'm sorry I shouted. But I do want to go."

Chi-Chi looks at him. He is still her son, the boy who wanted to sit beside her while she read his letter. He is also the boy who stood beside Piccolo at the quarry and fired at a light in the sky without looking up.

"I'm listening," she says. "I haven't said yes."

"I want to go too," Goshin says.

"Of course you do."

"Not just because Gohan does." He rests both hands on the table. "I want to see what's there. If someone needs help, I can help. And I want to learn about the ship, so I know what to do if something goes wrong."

The excitement of Jupiter is still in him: the vast planet, the darkness around it, the feeling of his brother's hand. The strength he gained in the fight and as a giant is still his, present in his own body and ki. His ki rises with his excitement and wish to help until the edge of the letter stirs. He draws it close instead of letting it shake the room.

Chi-Chi notices the care he takes with the table. "I know you can help people. I saw you both fight. I also saw what you had to fight."

Goshin's tail curls around the leg of the bench. He remembers Vegeta's light gathering toward his mother and the fear that came before he turned it away. He reaches for her hand.

"I don't want you to be scared."

"I'm your mother. That isn't something you can fix by getting stronger."

He holds her hand anyway. She squeezes his fingers.

"I want to know how that ship will keep you safe," she says. "I want to know who would be with you and how you'd come home. Your father has only just returned, and we still have two injured Saiyans at the quarry. We haven't even worked out how to get them proper care."

"Dad's watching Vegeta," Gohan says.

"He can't stay awake forever. Neither can Krillin, Tien or Chiaotzu."

Goshin thinks of the prince watching them from the wall. "We could take turns."

"We can talk about that. We can talk about all of it—with your father here." Chi-Chi folds the letter along its worn creases. "But nobody is deciding this afternoon that my sons are leaving the planet for months."

Gohan's shoulders drop a little. It isn't the answer he wants, but she has not told him the conversation is over.

"All right," he says. "Can we ask Bulma our questions?"

"Yes. And I'll have some of my own."

Goshin looks at the rice pot. "Can we ask after supper?"

Chi-Chi stares at him. "You haven't eaten since the quarry?"

"I was looking at Jupiter."

"That is not a meal."

"It looked pretty big," Gohan says.

Chi-Chi points both boys toward the kitchen. "Wash your hands. Then you can tell me which part of the pot you burned."

## Something to Take Back

The boys help prepare supper. Gohan measures the rice while Goshin brings bowls to the table. Chi-Chi makes extra and packs it into a covered container with enough to share at the quarry. She puts two bottles of water into the bag.

"Take this to your father and the others," she says. "Ask whether the Saiyans need food or water too. They still need watching, but that doesn't mean we leave them hungry."

Gohan folds the letter and puts it in the bag beside the bottles. "May we show Father?"

"You wrote it. Of course."

Goshin is already at the door, holding the container level. "We'll come straight back."

Chi-Chi gives him a look. "I know where I've heard that."

"This time it's only the quarry."

"And tell your father I want all four of us to talk before anyone makes plans for the boys."

"We will," Gohan says.

The brothers lift above the roof. Goshin balances the food in both hands, taking the first turn gently so the lid stays in place. Once they are clear of the house, the hills race beneath them. They find their father's ki and reach the quarry in a flash.

Krillin sees the container and nearly cheers. Tien comes over when Chiaotzu offers to keep his place by the gully. Goku stays close to Vegeta while Gohan gives him his share.

"Mother says to ask if they need anything too," Gohan tells him. "The doctor said people are looking into bringing equipment for Nappa. They want to talk to the guards first."

"Good. We'll speak to them." Goku looks inside the container. "She sent all this?"

"She said you can't watch forever without eating," Goshin says.

"Your mother is right about a lot of things."

"You can tell her that yourself," Gohan says.

Goku laughs. He takes one of the bottles Gohan brought and holds it out toward Vegeta. "Want some water? There's food too."

Vegeta looks at the bottle. "I don't need your charity."

"All right. It's here if you change your mind." Goku sets it on the stone beside him; Krillin stays close to the prince.

Gohan carries a portion of food and the other bottle to the gully. Tien returns with him, and Chiaotzu keeps watch while Nappa takes them.

"This doesn't mean I've forgotten what you did," Nappa tells the boy.

"I haven't forgotten either," Gohan says. He waits until Nappa has a grip on the bottle before returning to Goku.

Gohan takes the letter from the bag and opens it for his father, holding down the curled edge. Goku reads about the rice, the books and the mark in the room. His finger stops on the little drawing.

"You put your mother by the board."

"We were missing her," Gohan says.

Goku looks at both boys. "I missed you too."

Goshin moves close enough to lean against him. He has Goku's unruly black hair and almost the same face, but the eyes he turns up to his father are Chi-Chi's. Gohan stands on Goku's other side while he folds the letter again. Nearby, Krillin holds his food beside Vegeta. Across the tracks, Tien and Chiaotzu remain by Nappa.

"We want to talk about Namek," Gohan says quietly. "Mother does too."

"Then we'll talk," Goku says. "All of us. First we have to work out how to look after things here."

Goshin looks up. "I want to go one day. Maybe I could even fly out there myself someday. I don't know what it's like without air, though."

"Neither do I," Goku says. "Sounds like something we'd better learn about before anybody tries it."

"Bulma said the ship needs air," Gohan adds. "And beds."

"And a toilet," Goshin says.

Krillin chokes on his first mouthful. Goku starts laughing, and Gohan has to hold the letter out of the way as his father reaches for both of them.

For now the ship is on Earth. There are people on Namek, and a way to begin looking for them. Between the two worlds there is still a great deal to learn—and, at home, a mother waiting for her sons to come back for supper.
