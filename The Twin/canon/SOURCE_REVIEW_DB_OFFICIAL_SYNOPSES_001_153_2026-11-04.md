# Original Dragon Ball — review of Toei's 153 episode synopses

**Owns: what the original-Dragon-Ball official synopsis pass actually establishes, selected continuity implications and source defects.** Review date: 4 November 2026. This is a synopsis-level research pass, not a chapter, reader acceptance or a complete franchise review.

## Scope and provenance

I individually read the **153 short Japanese Toei episode synopses** for the original *Dragon Ball* TV series from the existing local cache, episodes 1–153, and rechecked selected corresponding live Toei pages. The cache contains exactly one nonempty record for each of those episode numbers; the inventory below identifies its raw record hashes and official links (`W-113`). Their publisher and series identification are known, but each synopsis is a brief editorial description, not an episode transcript, full scene, verified timing, manga chapter or an independently watched episode. Some synopsis statements are wrong or internally inconsistent, as documented below. I did **not** watch original *Dragon Ball* episodes or read its whole manga during this pass.

The full mixed-series cache is `Research/The Twin/Original Episode and Franchise Research/sources/toei_franchise_official_synopses.jsonl`, SHA-256 `b0cd727cb8ffcb5dfd6d9711f26b92988a9df87ae2ff7085a3b36e1fd89f5672`. It was created in the earlier franchise-indexing pass; this continuation changes its status from merely indexed to **individually read for its 153 original-Dragon-Ball synopsis records only**. Other series' cached records do not thereby become fully read. The original-Z local caption review is separate: at this synopsis checkpoint 115 distinct episodes had recorded full spoken-caption coverage; the subsequent Z 108–117 review raised the current total to 125. Neither count is video/audio viewing.

## Chronological synopsis synthesis and branch implications

- **1–13 — meeting, early journey and the first wish.** Toei introduces Goku and Bulma, then Oolong, Yamcha, Puar, Ox King and Chi-Chi. The first Pilaf journey ends with a full moon and Goku becoming a rampaging Great Ape (12–13). This is the publisher's description of **one Goku instance**, not a universal rule that Goshin's first eventual ape must be afraid or uncontrolled. In the selected branch his first eventual Great Ape is explicitly aware and controlled.
- **14–29 — Turtle School and the first shown tournament.** Goku and Krillin become Roshi's students and train together through demanding tasks (14–20). Toei describes Krillin's first tournament fight in 21 and Yamcha's in 22; Goku loses the final to Jackie Chun after the moon-and-ape incident (27–29). These descriptions support an established relationship and prior tournament participation, not exact off-screen techniques or a numeric power chart.
- **30–45 — Red Ribbon pursuit and early family history.** The episode-31 synopsis says Shu impersonates Goku and Ox King holds a celebration for the supposed Goku and Chi-Chi. This is a **deception**, not evidence that the real Goku and Chi-Chi married then. Episode 39 portrays gentle Android Eight choosing friendship over the command to fight; episode 43 places Bulma's family and Capsule Corporation in West City. Preserve version-specific details without assuming every TV-original event belongs to the manga or this branch.
- **46–68 — underwater search, Penguin Village and Korin.** Bulma and Krillin accompany Goku on the underwater search (46–54). In 61–62 Korin's apparently empowering water is ordinary water and the pursuit itself serves as training; do not conflate this with the separately described dangerous water of 115–117, or assign a numeric growth factor to Goshin. The episode-56 synopsis has a likely name error, noted below.
- **69–78 — Baba and Grandpa Gohan.** Toei says Baba calls Goku's deceased adoptive grandfather back for one day, followed by an emotional reunion (76); the separate official manga-based feature previously recorded a comparable background account (`C178`, `W-62`). The old return does not itself arrange a **new** appointment, guarantee an answer to Roshi's branch request or establish Baba's current knowledge.
- **79–101 — second shown tournament and changing loyalties.** Toei's 96 synopsis says Goku had trained away his tail-grip weakness; that is a specific learned case, not proof that the boys acquire automatic immunity simply by observing it (`C147`). The tournament descriptions show Tien's developing conflict with Crane Hermit and choice of a fair contest (94–100). The episode-85 synopsis misstates prior participation by Yamcha and Krillin; do not silently inherit its claim.
- **102–122 — King Piccolo's threat.** Krillin's death, the threat to martial artists, Roshi's failed sealing attempt, Goku's recovery and the eventual confrontation appear across the short descriptions. The 115–117 water is described as dangerous and power-revealing; that particular TV account does not establish a universal safe awakening method. Tien is an active participant with distinct choices, not merely a static power number.
- **123–132 — Kami, Popo and Goku's training.** The 123–126 synopses describe King Piccolo's egg, the Lookout, Kami's shared origin with King Piccolo, and the Dragon Balls' restoration. Episode 129 calls a device/place the **“room of time”** and describes a visit to the past. That short wording does **not** prove it is identical to, or definitively distinct from, the later Room of Spirit and Time; neither a matching translated name nor this synopsis alone establishes the relationship (`C272`). The selected branch's earlier mental exercise and later full physical chamber year remain separate authored events.
- **133–148 — the 23rd tournament.** Toei describes a three-year interval after King Piccolo's defeat (133), Chi-Chi entering and facing Goku before his public acceptance of the old marriage promise (137), and Krillin displaying flight and trained fighting against Majunior (138). The latter upgrades support for his pre-Z flight from a secondary account to an official TV synopsis, but provides no long-distance speed, endurance or trip duration (`C173`). In 142–146 Kami attempts a sealing technique and is trapped instead, and Goku retrieves him. The 148 synopsis describes Goku using flight and winning; it does not by itself narrate every subsequent action or establish the complete Piccolo/Goku relationship.
- **149–153 — wedding-preparation adventure.** Toei describes Goku and Chi-Chi seeking a way to save her father from the fire; in 151 Chi-Chi finds the fan while helping an older woman. These descriptions add practical initiative and shared history, not a mandate to restage the adventure, a full account of a marriage ceremony, or proof she is invulnerable. Episode 137 and the separate official manga-based tournament feature are distinct witnesses (`C133`, `W-106`).

## Source defects and non-equivalences

1. **DB-85 versus DB-21/22:** Toei's episode-85 synopsis says Yamcha and Krillin had not participated in the previous tournament. Its own episode-21 synopsis describes Krillin's match and episode 22 describes Yamcha's match. The inconsistency is within the **official synopses themselves**, not a determination that the underlying episodes contradict each other. Selected live pages confirmed all three texts. Do not use the episode-85 line as a factual correction to the branch (`C273`).
2. **DB-56 name:** The live and cached episode-56 synopsis says “Bulma” had a broken rocket and wandered seeking a vehicle, while the surrounding 55 and 57 synopses describe Blue's pursuit and appearance. This is a suspicious possible editorial name error, not an authenticated reconstruction of the missing scene. Check video or a reliable transcript before correcting staging or identity.
3. **DB-31 fake wedding versus 137 onward:** The former synopsis explicitly identifies Shu as an impersonator. The real character's later promise and the 149–153 adventure should not be compressed into a single earlier marriage scene.
4. **DB-129 time room versus later Z physical chamber:** The titles and synopsis alone do not prove identity, an unlimited room allowance, or a branch transfer. Separately sourced Z-147/155/168 caption cues and the authored chapter chronology retain their own evidentiary boundaries.
5. **Rage, first ape and growth:** Goku's source Great Ape rampage and Krillin's ability to expose a weakness do not override the author's controlled first-Great-Ape direction, cumulative whole-being Talent or the full post-chamber strength checkpoint for both boys.
