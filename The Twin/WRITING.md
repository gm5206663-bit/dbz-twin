## 1 · The mix

Anime-like immersion with manga-like clarity: write enough for the reader to see the characters, place and movement, while keeping the prose purposeful. A spare or lush passage is not automatically better; choose the detail, rhythm and length the scene needs. Readability and emotional impact matter more than a formula.

- **Anime gives:** time to sit in a room, weather that is doing something, a reaction held two seconds too long.
- **Manga gives:** the cut that arrives before you are ready, a page with one line on it, no transition at all.

---

## 2 · Give the story enough room

The author has asked for immersive story prose, not minimal beats or a numerical target. Dramatize the important action, decisions, surroundings, emotions and consequences on the page. Expand where a scene needs it; cut only what is genuinely redundant.

`tools/check.py` still reports its existing length, sentence and dialogue bands. They are mechanical diagnostics, not proof of prose quality or acceptance. Do not weaken the checker or distort a scene merely to make a number pass. Report a real out-of-band result honestly. Chapters One and Two were rebuilt as episode-based scene adaptations on 4 November; their older scene-length versions remain preserved.

---

## 3 · Sentences and character

Use varied sentence and paragraph lengths. Concrete detail, dialogue, physical expression and appropriate interiority can all serve a scene. Let important characters have recognizable voices and independent choices; emotion may be shown, spoken or felt. Avoid both unexplained shorthand and decorative repetition. Respect any specific viewpoint or camera restriction that actually applies to the chapter.

---

## 4 · Space, action and consequence

Establish who and what the reader needs to see: distinctive appearance, where people stand, terrain and nearby hazards. In a fight, follow the fighters' intentions and readable movement through contact, force, injury or environmental response and the next choice. Let reactions and pauses register. Do not compress a clash to a prescribed number of words, ban adjectives at impact, or turn repeated punches into a substitute for choreography.

---

## 5 · Devices as options

Cuts, holds, reaction shots, sound before sight, silence, home scenes and previews can create an anime-like rhythm. They are tools, not mandatory quotas. A cut away or held moment can be as long as the narrative needs; technical labels should not interrupt the reading experience. The scene must remain intelligible and emotionally grounded without relying on a named shot.

---

## 6 · Before you write a scene

Consider what the characters want, where they are, what changes and whose reactions or choices matter. Use those questions to strengthen the scene, not as a compulsory pattern.

---

## 7 · Make the world legible

Use established facts consistently, but do not assume every reader remembers the anime. Provide context when the scene needs it, without stopping for an unnecessary lecture. For example, the cloud's purity rule remains part of continuity (`C115`, `O023`); it need not become a separate test scene unless the story gives one a reason to happen.

## 8 · Drawing a family resemblance

At the present age, show his child-sized face and his father's black spiked hair, and carry his tail and scene-specific condition. Describe the likeness plainly and naturally; there is no need to put a numerical percentage into narrative prose. The existing design also gives him his twin's height and a slightly heavier build across the shoulders.

**Appearance is not behaviour.** A gesture or glance does not tell the reader what Goshin looks like, and older mechanical mouth-watching or family-position rituals are not a character law. Include visual cues when an introduction, interaction, setting, injury or time change makes them useful, rather than repeating an inventory. Do not invent a fixed uniform or eye colour. The unaccepted chamber year is lived time and visibly changes both boys' bodies.

**Expression is contextual.** Observation, quick reaction, a pause, a question or a physical action can reveal thought. An obligatory half-second lag is an assistant invention, not a verified character rule.

## §10 · The episode grammar — how a chapter is built like an anime

**Episode-based storytelling** can use a cold open, a narrator, a title, cuts, an eye-catch or a preview when they improve the story. No set number of these devices is mandatory, and a fully dramatized adaptation need not display production labels. Do not declare a form to disguise a diagnostic failure. The selected Chapters One and Two use expanded scene adaptations; Chapter Three retains its separate external-camera restriction and its schooling-reference title.

**Visible episode reference is mandatory.** Immediately after each chapter title, include a reader-visible anime episode reference naming the episode or episodes whose material the chapter actually draws on. Qualify partial, noncontiguous or brought-forward elements where helpful. Do not imply that every scene of a cited range appears unchanged, or that a label verifies source accuracy.

**No one-chapter/one-episode rule.** The author explicitly permits a chapter to incorporate multiple episodes, and an episode to extend across chapters, whenever the actual story and pacing call for it. The Vegeta fight and other threads may draw naturally from several episodes. Do not pad, compress, skip important material or stop a coherent scene merely to force a one-to-one mapping; let changed branch causality determine what happens. Record every relevant source reference without manufacturing complete coverage.

**Keep the grammar readable.** Narrator, title cards, eye-catches and previews may be signposted as in Chapter Three; do not clutter the story with technical timecodes or a shot list. Use bold countdowns, hard cuts and held frames when they serve the episode.

**Camera and scope.** Chapter Three was expressly written with an external camera and no character POV. Do not generalize that specific ruling into a verified universal user ban on all interiority. Elsewhere, maintain a coherent chosen viewpoint, respect any actual current instruction, and let emotions be legible without imposing a fixed delayed reaction.

### Sequence and coverage gate

The user directs: **do not miss things next time**. Before adapting material from one or several episodes, study the available complete episodes where possible and make an ordered inventory of their scenes and identifiable beats, including conversations, fight exchanges, reactions, dreams, flashbacks and cutaways—not just the climax. If complete footage is unavailable, read the available complete captions and consult identified synopses or visual witnesses while recording their limits. Check each beat against the manuscript: dramatized on the page; already dramatized in the relevant overlapping scene; altered or no longer possible because of a stated, consequential divergence; or explicitly tracked for a later chapter. Do not replace a supported source flashback with a one-line summary, silently skip an independent thread, or claim a deferred scene has already happened.

Start from the actual end of the preceding chapter. Give each scene the room its pacing needs; a chapter may cover several episodes or one episode may continue across several chapters. Do not omit a scene merely to meet a word band. Goshin's presence can change what happens; show how, rather than forcing the untouched canon outcome. The scene inventory and its coverage check are research records, not a substitute for the story.

Captions establish spoken material, not every silent frame. Use additional visual evidence when available and mark any unresolved visual details as unverified or authored; a complete caption read is not a claim to have watched or verified every image.

### Hybrid emotion and the remembered moon

Goshin is both Saiyan and Earthling. Give his emotions genuine narrative and physical weight: like Gohan, his concern, anger and other feelings may be connected with a substantial release of latent power, but his voice, choices and feelings are his own. He can also be joyful, playful, compassionate, afraid or calm. Do not make rage the sole growth mechanism, a fixed multiplier, a compulsory loss of control or the explanation for every action. Gohan's separate agency and growth remain (`C100`, `C142`, `O056`, `O230`).

His **whole Saiyan–Earthling hybrid bloodline itself** grows purer, deeper and stronger with his developing Adaptation Talent: its inherited qualities and potential can be refined, develop and take distinctive new expressions as his own hybrid nature, not merely give him a stronger current output. The Talent develops with Goshin and concerns his complete existence, while his bloodline, Talent and other qualities can interact with one another and with life, training, fighting, experience and recovery (`O230`, `O243`, `O244`). These are examples, not a closed list or a requirement that every aspect grow at the same rate.

Let genuine, possibly rapid and substantial growth affect scenes naturally, not as a technical lecture or only a repetitive statement about his parents. Integrated gains remain; new abilities or expressions may become possible without falsely asserting a specific unshown feat. Do not impose arbitrary ceilings, delays or compulsory weaknesses. ## §11 · How to write a fight

A fight must be followable in one pass. Establish who is present, where they are, what they are trying to do and what stands in the way. The reader should know who acts, where the action goes, what makes contact and what changes.

- **Geography:** establish relevant ground, boundaries, cover and bystanders before they matter. Give each movement a destination.
- **Sequence:** put the cause, action, impact and response in a clear order. Use names when a pronoun could mean either twin.
- **Capability:** show Goshin flying, using or learning techniques when the scene calls for it. Gohan acts and grows too. Their relative standing is an authored rule, not a fixed scouter number. For the current Kame House abduction, the user rules that Raditz cannot take Goshin conscious: his power rises in anger and he must be knocked out first (`O080`). Once he wakes, write the consequences of his capabilities, not a passive re-capture. This encounter-specific rule is not an invented general battle-power formula.
- **Scale:** choose effects from demonstrated feats, the chosen attack, aim, condition, control and surroundings (`§16`). Follow the damage through to its consequences.
- **Dialogue:** let an authority give a clear order and a character act. Do not have a character announce a fact already visible. Named canon techniques and appropriately declared invented techniques may be used; a scouter can print a sourced or authored number when its presence makes sense.
- **Speed:** give fast movements enough displacement, contact and aftermath to follow. A character may be unable to track a blow, but do not rely on invisibility as the only description of a whole fight.
- **Adaptation:** seeing a technique can help Goshin learn it; training and resistance have to be shown where relevant. In deliberate training he masters or perfects the abilities he already has before pursuing new ones, and gives the foundations serious attention (`O104`, `O105`). The user allows instinctive copying during emergencies. Show ordinary practice and eventual mastery, not a parade of new moves or a claim that nothing he knows is ever finished. A temporary defeat or exhausted landing does not erase retained skill.

A fight may contain many supernatural actions. It does not require a predetermined number of blows, an onlooker cut, an already-stated twist, a mandatory charge or a specifically small aftermath. The plain-words crib in `CRAFT.md` is a test of clarity, not prose that must be repeated in a chapter.

## §12 · How to write the supernatural

Treat flight, ki, transformation and impact as events in the scene, not as riddles. Name the actor when needed, show the movement and make the result clear. The writing may describe light, force, sound, injury and the landscape directly; it need not hide an attack's source or name.

A reader should understand an action from its cause and consequence, even when a character cannot track it. Sensory comparison is welcome when it clarifies rather than trivializes the scale. Sound can arrive later where distance makes that appropriate, but there is no mandatory delay and no limit of one supernatural event per scene.

**Era:** at four Goshin is mortal and has ordinary ki, flight and techniques. No divine ki or early divine transformation. Energy effects can be visible; their existence does not make him a god. The separate future rules stay locked until the story reaches them.

## §13 · Keep characters identifiable

Use names, relationships and descriptive cues naturally. In scenes with both brothers, make it easy to tell who speaks and acts. A visual description is useful at an introduction or meaningful change; do not cycle through ornamental epithets instead of clear identification. Unnamed people still have agency and reactions when the scene calls for them. The existing checker catches a small configured set of ambiguity patterns; it does not judge every pronoun or description.

---

## §14 · Clarity without flattening voices

The reader should be able to follow the action, relationships and stakes. Characters may joke, evade, misunderstand, be uncertain or keep secrets when those choices fit them. Do not force every desire into the opening lines or answer every question immediately. Ambiguity can serve the story; unintentional confusion about who acted or what physically happened should be repaired.

---

## §15 · Supernatural action and flow

Follow causes through movement, contact, reaction and consequences, at whatever length the moment needs. A character may fail to see a strike; the surrounding action should still orient the reader. Comparisons, commentary and spoken reactions are permitted when they clarify a character's experience or the scale. Do not reduce every exchange to the same sentence pattern.

## §16 · Fight scale — feats, context and consequences

**World-scale correction from the author, 6 November:** Goshin’s current ordinary body is capable of destroying Earth (`O225`). Roshi and Piccolo’s original-continuity Moon feats and Vegeta’s planet-threatening Galick Gun are real scale context (`C294`, `C307`), even though the Piccolo/Moon event did not occur in this branch. The author places Goku at light speed or higher during original Dragon Ball and expects comparable context-appropriate extraordinary speed, not ordinary-athlete or aircraft-limited action. This is the project’s working author interpretation, not a newly verified exact speed or obligatory maximum-speed movement. Controlled small actions and injuries remain possible without treating small collateral as a true cap. See [world scale and Episode 37](canon/WORLD_POWER_AND_EPISODE_37_2026-11-06.md).

**No fixed conversion:** a printed battle-power reading does not mechanically determine collateral, and it does not guarantee a fight's winner. A reading can change with suppression, condition or a concentrated attack. Do not assign a moon feat to Piccolo's earlier 408 reading or Nappa's city attack a measured 4,000: those values were obtained separately (`C130`, `C141`).

**But a real gap must matter.** Carry established training, displayed speed and power into the choreography; show a credible reason if a much weaker fighter gains an advantage. Experienced opponents notice what happened, use what they know and adapt their tactics. Avoiding a rigid formula is not permission to make power differences or character intelligence disappear for a convenient fight.

Before the fight, establish:

1. **Participants and location:** where each person is, what they want and who is at risk.
2. **Ability and condition:** relevant demonstrated feats, age, training, fatigue, injury and control. Include a reading only when sourced or openly authored for that moment.
3. **Action:** the attack, target, aim and surroundings. A body strike may be devastating or local depending on context; not all large feats require a lengthy charge.
4. **Aftermath:** injury, terrain, bystanders and resources in the causal chain. A deliberate small spar can leave a trench or cracked rock; a large targeted attack can destroy a city. Neither follows from arithmetic on a scouter number.

The current 400 was an authored Kame House snapshot, not a permanent ceiling or minimum. Both boys grow; Goshin uses flight and techniques and remains no less capable than Gohan by the user's direction. Do not force canon casualties or prescribe a lower-than-Nappa ceiling. See `canon/RESEARCH.md` for the evidence and limits.

**Growth is not a recap of his kit.** His exceptional Talent, whole hybrid inheritance and other qualities can develop together through training, life, combat and recovery; battle growth can be rapid and substantial. Where the story supports meaningful development, make its consequences perceptible—in capability and power as well as control, recovery, perception or application. Repeating a smaller version of a previously shown feat is not itself proof of progress. Separate a fighter releasing already-held power, a temporarily concentrated technique and an actual lasting gain; a scouter alone cannot establish which occurred. Anger and being pushed hard may matter but are not mandatory: growth is not restricted to extreme situations, and no fixed rate, numerical ceiling, required injury, form or automatic gain per exchange follows (`O188`). The full year in the physical chamber counts as lived training and growth in the unaccepted Chapter 22; carry its shown consequences forward and keep the actual approaching arrival close (`O155`). Account separately for its cumulative per-person time allowance and exit risk (`C234`); that room rule is not a limit on Goshin's power.
