## Source and coverage findings

### Episode 1 and the missing hat transition

The local captions establish Gohan's lost-hat dialogue, Goku questioning an animal about the hat, and the rescue dialogue. The detailed Kanzenshuu synopsis (`W-65`) **reports**, as a secondary account, that Goku recovers the hat from the cat and returns it after the rescue; it also reports a city flyover after the rescue. Chapter One recounts the cat and log after the family reunion, but does not show the forest adventure, rescue or hat return. It places the city cut earlier. Chapter Two has the hat, but its historical footer's claimed retelling is absent from its body (`C181`, `O009`).

`canon/SCENE_COVERAGE_001.md` now sets out the captioned events, secondary visual descriptions, omissions and authored changes in source order. This identifies missing on-page material without inventing it or calling the synopsis checked footage. The synopsis supports neither the accepted chapter's claim that the tree was the largest on that side nor complete frame-level chronology.

### Caption boundary

The existing index already excludes cues at or after **29:00**; that is a search heuristic, not a verified universal episode cutoff. The episode-1 DBZ ending in the local file runs to approximately 27:56, followed at 30:44–30:49 by unrelated dialogue mentioning Kageyama and Karasuno. Episodes 3 and 4 likewise contain identifiable unrelated post-ending volleyball dialogue. Those specific examples were outside the index window and were not treated as DBZ evidence here.

A one-time scan found **275 of 291** files with at least one cue beginning at or after 30:00. This is a screening count only: it does **not** establish that all 275 contain unrelated material, nor that earlier cues or every boundary are clean. Check each episode's own ending and preview; do not infer a broadcaster, source master or universal clean boundary.

### Other source qualifications

- The episode-1 meal/departure line is unlabelled in the local captions; the secondary W-65 synopsis attributes it to Chi-Chi, while Chapter One gives it to Goku (`C003`, `C079`). The episode-2 scholar question is also unlabelled in the local captions. The accepted chapter's Roshi attribution is authored; the caption alone does not prove it (`C076`).
- The project selects a guidebook-derived **12 October, Age 761** arrival date, while another secondary account gives **28 November**. Neither is dialogue. Chapter Three's **4 December** date remains a separate continuity conflict (`C047`, `C067`).
- A natural full moon is not the only possible Great Ape trigger: other accounts include artificial or pod-projected moons (`C068`, `C148`).
- A secondary ritual account says the Super Saiyan God participants are Super Saiyans; another describes unborn Pan's contribution. Do not infer the exact participant transformation requirement without checking the relevant film and *Super* versions directly (`C117`, `W-32`, `W-66`). Goshin's later divine mechanics and Blue exclusion remain authored rules, not general canon claims.

## Decisions required before changing accepted prose

### 1. Missing and inconsistent accepted scenes

- **Chapter One:** forest/hat recovery is not on-page; the field line is misplaced; Piccolo says he does not intend to fight despite the captioned challenge. The city is moved relative to the secondary synopsis.
- **Chapter Two:** the headings run out of order, the Nimbus arrival appears replayed without a clear transition, and Raditz's account remains out of source order. The false footer retelling is corrected in a dated note, not retroactively invented.
- **Chapter Three:** the date and compressed clock remain unresolved. Its bedroom insert shows one pair of boots while the narrator counts two; the rooms, mats and the intended diners at the four-place setting are ambiguous. The earlier overnight outcome and Snake Way projection are already superseded in the living-Goku draft, not rewritten in the accepted body.

**Available approaches:** keep the accepted bodies and commission separately placed supplements where a scene is missing, leaving directly contradictory passages explicitly flagged; or authorize targeted revisions to the affected accepted passages, with proposed edits presented for approval. A supplement alone cannot erase a direct contradiction inside an accepted scene.

### 2. Character and style rules

Chapter One does not show whether Goshin watched his rice and egg being prepared; this is a gap, not proof he failed to watch off-page. It also has him leave after being told to stay and boast about speed. Some instructions are followed without his specified word-for-word repetition. In the provisional island fight he acts despite Goku telling him to stay away; that emergency defense is part of the user's directed correction and conflicts with a literal no-exceptions obedience rule. The other absolute invitation, posture and path habits also need scene-by-scene reconciliation.

Accepted prose sometimes attributes thoughts or decisions to canon characters despite the external-camera rule, and Chapter Two uses descriptive labels for named characters. The checker previously passed because it catches only a configured set of phrases. Decide whether to authorize targeted prose repairs or specify narrow exceptions; neither the accepted bodies nor the character rules have been silently changed to force agreement.

A later design note also says Goshin is comfortable as a Great Ape, while the SS4 note says fear is paid at the moon. What fear, if any, is intended and when should be clarified before that future arc; neither rule has been changed here.

## Mechanical verification and limitations

The printed results are the validation record; a green result does not close `OPEN-17`.
