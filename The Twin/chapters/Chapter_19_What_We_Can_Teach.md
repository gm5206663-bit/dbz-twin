# Chapter Nineteen — What We Can Teach

*Immersion-revised review draft; not yet accepted.*

*Review draft, continuing the selected Chapter 18.*

Raditz's boot pins Goku against the stone. Gohan picks up a broken piece of the ridge.

**WHAT WE CAN TEACH**

**NARRATOR:** Goku is still travelling toward King Kai. At home, Gohan and Goshin have more training ahead of them before he returns.

## A Lesson to Begin

Gohan wakes with his hands clenched in the blanket, Raditz's voice still close in his ears. For a breath he cannot see the crater or his father.

Then he hears the familiar creak of the house and sees the pale square of the window. Morning air moves the curtain. His hat rests on the table beneath it, with Hatch's drawing tucked safely underneath.

Goku is far away, travelling toward King Kai.

Gohan sits up and looks toward the closed bedroom door.

Across the room, Goshin opens his eyes.

"Gohan?"

"I had a dream."

Goshin pushes his blanket aside and comes over. His tail trails behind him across the floor.

"About the men in the room?"

Gohan shakes his head. "Raditz."

Goshin sits beside him on the edge of the bed while Gohan explains what he saw: their father on the ground, Raditz raising his foot, the place where the dream stopped.

"Father got up," Goshin says.

"I know. I wanted to help him sooner. When I woke up, I thought he was still under Raditz's foot."

Goshin grips the edge of the blanket. "I can still hear him telling us to get away. I wanted to pull Raditz off him."

"We didn't get away," Gohan says.

"No. And we helped him."

Goshin puts his hand over Gohan's clenched fist.

"I wish Father was home," Gohan says.

"Me too."

Gohan opens his fingers. The blanket has left creases in his palm.

"In the room at the Lookout," Gohan says, "I could move the stone away from Krillin. I want to get better at that."

"We can practise with the stones again."

"And fighting."

Goshin looks toward the window, then back at his brother. "We can start with that."

Gohan hesitates. "Could you teach me the Kamehameha too?"

Goshin's eyes widen. "You want to learn it?"

"Yes. You did it when Father needed help. And yesterday you used it while you were holding all those pieces of the wall."

"I can show you how I do it," Goshin says.

Gohan smiles. "Will you?"

"Yes!"

The answer is loud enough that both boys look toward the door.

It opens a little.

"Is everything all right?" Chi-Chi asks.

"We're talking," Goshin says.

"I heard that part."

Gohan slips out of bed. "Goshin says he'll teach me the Kamehameha."

Chi-Chi looks at one son, then the other.

"After breakfast," she says. "And after we find somewhere suitable for it."

Goshin looks at Gohan. "We'll need plenty of room."

## The Clearing Beyond the Ridge

In the morning, Chi-Chi puts rice on the stove and cuts vegetables on the board beside it. Goshin brings his stool to the table.

Gohan sits there with a sheet of paper.

He draws two small figures facing a large rock. One has his hat. The other has his tail sticking out behind him.

"Which one is teaching?" Goshin asks.

"You are."

"Then I need bigger hands."

Gohan adds lines around the second figure's hands.

The back door opens, and Ox-King carries in an armful of firewood. He sets it beside the stove and comes over to look at the drawing.

"Those are some big hands."

"They're mine," Goshin says. "I'm teaching Gohan today."

"The Kamehameha," Gohan adds.

Ox-King looks at the rock on the page. "Are you going to need a bigger target?"

"We're finding a safe place first," Chi-Chi says. "And they're eating breakfast before either of them tries."

"Then I'll see to the gate while you're out. It sticks when the wind catches it."

Chi-Chi mixes the vegetables into the rice. Goshin brings the bowls from the cupboard, and she fills them.

He eats his breakfast, then leans over Gohan's shoulder.

"You made my hands bigger than my head."

"So you can show me where they go."

Goshin touches one of the extra lines around his hands. "That's a good reason."

When they finish, Chi-Chi gathers the bowls and looks at her sons.

"Use the clearing beyond the ridge. Keep your shots away from the house."

Goshin holds his hands apart as if measuring the clearing. "That'll work."

"And I'm coming with you."

Gohan fetches his hat. Goshin helps Chi-Chi carry the water bottle and a folded cloth for sitting on. On the path up the ridge, he and Gohan discuss where they might set the target.

At the clearing, Chi-Chi walks its edges before she lets them begin. There is a broad bank of bare stone at the far end, with no path behind it and no houses visible through the trees.

"That bank will do for a target," she says. "I'll watch from here."

Gohan studies the stone. "Can we fight first?"

"Of course," Goshin says. "I'll show you what I did when that man came at Chiaotzu."

He puts the water bottle beside Chi-Chi and faces his brother.

The boys raise their hands, and Gohan steps forward with a straight punch. Goshin turns his shoulder, guides the punch past him and reaches toward Gohan's side.

Gohan twists away, but his foot crosses behind his other foot. As Gohan stumbles, Goshin catches his sleeve before he falls.

"You crossed your feet," Goshin says.

"I was trying to get away from your hand."

"Try moving this foot first."

Goshin points. Gohan looks down, shifts his foot and tries the step again.

This time, when Goshin reaches for his side, Gohan moves without crossing his feet. He turns and touches Goshin's shoulder.

"I got you!"

"You did." Goshin grins. "I'll move this time."

Gohan settles into place again. "Start!"

Goshin moves in. Gohan blocks his first hand and tries to touch his shoulder again, but Goshin turns beneath his arm. Gohan pivots after him and sends a quick push of ki toward the ground.

Dust jumps between them, and Goshin hops back while keeping his balance. Gohan comes through the dust and reaches for him. Goshin catches the reaching wrist gently, but Gohan plants both feet and pushes against Goshin's hand with his ki.

Their hands separate, and Gohan backs away while he catches his breath. "You didn't show me that part."

"You found it yourself."

"Will it work if somebody's stronger?"

"We can try it again and see what happens." Goshin raises his hands. "They might hold on harder. Then you could move somewhere else."

Gohan looks at Goshin's raised hands. "Or you could come and help me."

"I could."

Chi-Chi smiles from her place near the trees.

"Try both," she says.

Goshin takes Gohan's wrist again. Instead of pulling straight back, Gohan shifts his weight, slips around Goshin's side and touches his shoulder.

"Now you," Gohan says, and takes hold of Goshin's wrist. Goshin turns and steps clear. When they try again, Gohan anticipates the turn, but Goshin changes direction and leaves them facing one another.

"Almost!" Gohan says. He scuffs his shoe to Goshin's right. When Goshin glances at the moving dirt, Gohan steps left and touches his arm.

"You looked over there," Gohan says.

Goshin looks at the mark Gohan's shoe left, then back at his brother. "Will you show me that after the wave?"

"I will."

## The Long Road

Far above Earth, Goku reaches a stretch of Snake Way where the clouds fall away from both sides of the road.

He slows to look ahead, but the road still curves beyond the bank of clouds.

"You sure are long," he tells it.

He takes out the supply capsule, checks that it is secure and tucks it away again. Then he stretches his legs and starts running.

At the next bend Goku plants one foot and turns around a seam in the road. He takes the same step he tried during his match with Goshin, then turns back toward the long curve ahead.

"I wonder what they're working on today."

He tries the turn once more, keeping his balance as the road narrows beneath him.

"Gohan's going to want that match when I get home."

Goku runs faster, following the curve until the clouds close around the road again.

## A Light Between the Hands

Back in the clearing, Goshin gathers four flat stones.

He sets them on the ground in front of Gohan, then lifts them with his ki. One rises to the height of Gohan's shoulder. The others move around it, each following a different path.

"Watch the one on the left," Goshin says.

Gohan watches it pass in front of him. At the same time, Goshin guides another stone upward and turns the third around his brother's back. The fourth stays almost still.

"How do you know where they all are?" Gohan asks.

"I look when I can. And I can feel where I'm pushing."

Goshin turns his head as the stone behind Gohan comes back into view, then lowers all four.

"Do you want to try one?"

Gohan holds out his hand. He raises a stone from the ground, moves it to the right and stops it before it reaches the trees.

"Can you bring it back without dropping it?"

Gohan draws the stone toward himself. It wobbles, then steadies over his palm.

Goshin smiles. "That's it."

Gohan lowers it carefully. "Now the Kamehameha?"

"Yes! Watch where I send it."

Goshin walks to one side of the clearing, where he has a clear line toward the stone bank. He waits until Chi-Chi and Gohan are behind him.

He places his hands together at his side.

"I saw Father do it when we fought Raditz," he says. "I watched his hands, and I could feel his ki gathering."

Blue light appears between his palms and grows brighter as he keeps them together.

"Bring it together between your hands. Then point where you want it to go. I'll use just enough to mark the stone, so we have room to practise."

He points his hands toward the far bank.

"Kamehameha!"

The wave crosses the clearing. It strikes a marked patch of stone and leaves a round, dark hollow. Loose pieces fall to the ground below.

Gohan comes alongside him. "What would a smaller one look like?"

"I'll show you."

Goshin raises the four stones again. They circle at different heights while he gathers a second light between his hands.

This wave is narrower. Goshin turns his hands, bending the stream across the stone to the edge of the hollow. He stops it there, knocking one loose piece free. He catches the piece with his ki before it lands and guides it down while the other stones continue to circle.

Gohan watches the chip settle beneath them. "You didn't drop the others!"

Goshin grins and sends the four stones circling in the other direction. He has practised the wave and the stones together; now he lets the stones come gently to the ground and turns back to Gohan.

"Did you do that the first time?"

"No. The first time Raditz fired at you and Father. I wanted his blast to go away from you."

Gohan looks toward the hollow. "I want to try."

Goshin comes back beside him and shows him where to place his hands. Gohan copies the shape.

"Do I have to say the words?" he asks.

"I don't know. Father does, and I like shouting them."

Gohan takes a breath and cups his hands, but his shoulders are pulled high.

Goshin watches Gohan hold the position, with his elbows lifted beside his chest.

"Your arms look uncomfortable."

"They are."

"You can lower them a little. You don't have to hold them exactly like mine."

Gohan shifts his hands. "Like this?"

"That looks better."

Gohan gathers his ki. A pale light flickers between his palms, then spreads over his fingers and disappears.

He looks at Goshin. "It went away."

"I saw it."

"I was trying to make it stay."

Goshin cups his own hands without gathering another wave. "When you moved the stone, you didn't push all at once. You kept feeling where it was."

Gohan tries again, and the light forms more quickly between his hands. It grows bright enough to cast a blue edge on his sleeves. He moves his hands forward, and a short burst shoots across the clearing.

It strikes the ground well below the bank.

Gohan watches the dirt settle short of the seam he had chosen on the bank.

"That went the wrong way."

"You made it go," Goshin says.

"But I wanted the rock."

"Your hands went down."

Gohan looks at them. "I was watching the rock. Maybe I can feel where my hands are, like the stone."

Chi-Chi rises and comes closer, stopping behind the boys.

"Would it help to choose a smaller mark?" she asks.

Gohan looks over the bank. "That line."

A dark seam runs across the stone at about his height.

Goshin stands beside him. "I'll watch with you."

Gohan sets his feet. He brings his hands to his side, gathers the light and keeps his eyes on the seam.

"Ka… me… ha… me…"

The light trembles between his palms, and Gohan steadies his breathing before he moves his hands forward.

"Ha!"

A blue wave leaves his palms and crosses the clearing with a rush of air. It strikes the seam. Stone cracks at the point of impact, and a thin piece breaks away from the bank and lands among the grass.

Gohan's hands remain out in front of him after the light has faded. A few sparks wink out against his sleeves. He looks from the broken stone to Goshin, as if he needs his brother to have seen it too.

"Goshin!"

"I saw it!"

"I did the Kamehameha!"

Goshin throws his arms around him. Gohan nearly loses his balance, and they both stumble sideways.

Chi-Chi catches them before either reaches the ground.

"You did," she says. "And you aimed where you meant to."

Gohan looks at the broken piece of stone, then rubs his palms together.

"Are your hands all right?" Chi-Chi asks.

"They're warm. Can I hold the light without sending it?"

"Have some water first," she says.

Chi-Chi opens the bottle and gives it to Gohan. Goshin sits beside him while he drinks. After a short rest, Gohan gives the bottle back, gets to his feet and cups his hands again. Light appears between them and grows brighter. He holds it there, but it begins to spread around his fingers.

"It's getting bigger."

"You can let it go," Goshin says. "You don't have to fire it."

Gohan opens his hands. The light breaks apart above his palms and fades.

"I stopped it."

"Try keeping it there for a little longer," Chi-Chi says.

Gohan gathers the light again. This time he watches the space between his hands and lets the light fade before it reaches his fingers.

"I want to make another wave now."

Goshin smiles. "What do you want to hit?"

Gohan looks at the broken piece of stone. "That one."

Goshin steps back and lifts the piece from the ground with his ki.

"Shall I put it over there for you?"

"Put it a little farther from the bank."

Goshin nods.

He carries it to an open patch of ground. Gohan prepares again, this time without needing Goshin to show him where to put his feet.

## A Question at Kame House

At Kame House, Oolong looks over Master Roshi's shoulder while Roshi speaks into the telephone.

"Is Bulma still working on that scouter?" Oolong asks.

"She said she was looking at several things," Roshi replies.

Bulma's voice comes through the receiver. "I can hear him, you know."

"Good," Oolong says. "Then you can tell me something. If Goku came here in a spaceship, where is it?"

There is a pause on the telephone before Bulma answers from the laboratory.

"I don't know," Bulma says. "We didn't even know he'd come from another planet until Raditz arrived."

Roshi scratches his beard. "Grandpa Gohan found him when he was very small. He might have known."

"And now two more Saiyans are coming," Oolong says. "What if they're all—"

"They aren't all Raditz," Bulma interrupts.

"I didn't say they were!"

"You were about to."

Oolong folds his arms and waits.

"Goku is a Saiyan," Bulma says. "He's also Goku. Gohan and Goshin are his sons. None of that changes because we learned where his family came from."

Roshi nods, though she cannot see him.

"Quite right."

Oolong looks out across the water. "I still wish we knew more about the ones coming here."

"So do I," Roshi says.

At the laboratory, Bulma glances toward the table where C-6 is reading Mina's bridge book.

"We're working on it," she says. "And the boys are working too."

## The Sound Beyond the Bank

Gohan's second Kamehameha strikes the stone Goshin placed in the clearing. It pushes the stone over and sends it rolling toward the bank.

Gohan lowers his hands.

"That was better," he says.

"You kept it on the stone."

"I made it bigger than the first one."

Goshin runs to look at the mark. He crouches beside it, then turns back.

"There's a line all the way across."

Gohan joins him. Chi-Chi follows more slowly, watching where the boys step.

A low electronic sound comes from beyond the bank.

All three stop beside the bank, and Gohan looks at Goshin. "Was that you?"

Goshin shakes his head. Gohan looks at the stone his wave moved, now resting against the bank.

"Did I hit something?"

"I don't know," Chi-Chi says.

The sound comes again, followed by a voice.

"Wake up, Kakarot."

Chi-Chi looks from the trees to Gohan.

"Raditz called Father that at Kame House," he says.

As the voice repeats the name, Goshin moves toward the edge of the bank and stops when Chi-Chi comes beside him. Beyond the stone, the ground falls into a narrow hollow. Trees grow along its sides, and something pale and round is wedged among the rocks below.

A round door stands partly open in the side of the object. Blue light leaks through it.

Gohan catches Goshin's sleeve. "Raditz's ship was round too."

"He shut us inside it," Goshin says.

Chi-Chi puts a hand on each boy's shoulder.

"Wake up, Kakarot."

"Is it calling Father?" Goshin asks.

"I don't know," Chi-Chi says.

The light grows brighter. Above the hollow, a pale circle appears through the branches, with the rocky slope behind it.

Chi-Chi looks up. The circle is round and bright, like a full moon. She turns back to the boys at once.

"Keep your eyes down."

Goshin reaches for Gohan's hand.

He lowers his gaze. Gohan does the same, and Chi-Chi brings both boys close to the stone bank, where its overhang blocks the light.

"What did you see?" Gohan asks.

"It looked like a full moon," Chi-Chi says. "Remember what your father told you at the bridge."

Gohan keeps his eyes on the ground. "He said it could change us."

Goshin watches the moving patch of light on the ground. "Can a moon come out of a machine?"

Before Chi-Chi can answer, a figure descends into the hollow.

Piccolo lands beside the pale object. His cape settles around his feet as he looks from the open door to the light above it. A narrow beam runs from the machine toward the circle.

He sees the boys beneath the overhang, their tails visible against the stone.

"Keep them under there," he tells Chi-Chi.

"That's what I'm doing."

Piccolo moves to one side, where bare stone lies behind the light. He sends a small blast through the edge of the circle.

The blast passes through and strikes the empty slope.

Piccolo lowers his arm. "An image."

He turns back toward Chi-Chi and the boys.

"What are you doing here?"

"We heard the machine," Chi-Chi says. "What are you doing here?"

"I sensed the boys. Then I heard this."

"Wake up, Kakarot," the voice says again.

Piccolo glances at the open door.

"That's what Raditz called your husband."

"Gohan just told me," Chi-Chi says.

Gohan stays beneath the overhang. "Do you think it belongs to Father?"

"I don't know."

Piccolo steps toward the object. A panel shifts on its upper side, and a metal arm rises from inside. The pale circle moves with it.

The arm presses against a slab of rock that has been resting above the machine.

The slab slides toward the open ground, taking loose earth and smaller stones with it.

"Mother!" Gohan calls.

Chi-Chi pulls him back as the slab strikes the ground. Four more pieces break loose from the bank above it.

Goshin raises both hands, and the largest piece stops before it reaches the hollow. The other three hang at different heights around it, dust spilling from their edges.

Piccolo jumps clear. A smaller stone falls toward Chi-Chi, and she knocks it aside with her forearm.

Goshin looks toward the open ground to his right. "There is room over there."

One of the suspended pieces turns. He steadies it, then moves all four away from the family.

The metal arm pushes farther upward. The door of the machine opens wider, and the pale light sweeps across the trees.

Gohan keeps his eyes on the ground. "It's moving again."

Piccolo raises a hand toward the machine. "I can destroy it."

"It might be Father's," Gohan says.

"If that light reaches you, I won't wait to find out."

Chi-Chi points toward the arm's base. "That bracket is holding it up. If it moves, the cover might fall back over the opening."

Gohan follows the direction of her hand without looking toward the light. A narrow metal bracket braces the arm against the side of the machine.

Piccolo lowers his hand, but stays beside the hollow. "Then move it."

Gohan brings his hands together, and Goshin looks across at his brother. "There's a clear space from where you are."

"What about the rocks?"

"I have them."

One of the four pieces drifts toward the bank. Goshin draws it back into line. Above the machine, another narrow slab breaks loose and slides toward Piccolo.

"Piccolo!" Gohan calls.

Piccolo jumps clear. Goshin brings his hands to his side, and light gathers between them while the four rocks remain suspended.

His Kamehameha strikes the edge of the falling slab. It turns away from the hollow and lands on the bare slope to the right, clear of the machine and the people below.

Goshin guides the four held pieces toward the open ground. Gohan watches the metal bracket and sets his feet as he did in the clearing.

"Ka… me… ha… me…"

The light gathers between Gohan's hands while Piccolo moves out of his line. Chi-Chi stays beside Gohan, watching the hollow.

"Ha!"

Gohan's wave strikes the bracket.

The metal bends. For a moment the arm remains where it is. Then its support gives way, and the arm drops back into the machine.

The cover swings after the arm, and the pale circle vanishes from the branches as the voice stops.

Gohan lowers his hands. "Did it work?"

"The light went away," Chi-Chi says. "That doesn't mean the machine can't start again."

Goshin lowers the last of the stones into the open space. He steps beside Gohan and looks at the closed cover.

"You hit the piece you chose."

Gohan smiles, then looks toward Piccolo, who is studying the machine beside the fallen arm.

"You could have destroyed it," Piccolo says.

"It might be Father's," Gohan answers.

"That voice knew his name," Goshin adds.

Piccolo folds his arms. "A name doesn't tell you what it was built to do."

"No," Chi-Chi says. "That's why Bulma should examine it before anybody starts tearing it apart."

Piccolo says nothing for a moment, then looks at the mark Gohan's wave left on the bracket.

"Your aim was better than I expected."

Gohan looks at Goshin. "He taught me."

## A Test of Their Own

Piccolo steps away from the machine and into the clearing.

"Show me what else he taught you."

Chi-Chi moves between him and the boys.

"No one is taking them."

"I didn't say I was taking them."

Gohan looks up at his mother. "I want to try."

"With him?" she asks.

Gohan nods. "Goshin can try too."

Goshin looks at Piccolo, then at Chi-Chi. "I want to."

Chi-Chi meets Piccolo's gaze. "If they choose to spar, that's their choice. They come home with me afterward."

"I came to see what they can do," Piccolo says.

Goshin raises his hands, eager to begin. Chi-Chi returns to the edge of the clearing. The boys stand together opposite Piccolo, and Goshin watches Piccolo's shoulders.

"He moves quickly," he tells Gohan.

"I remember."

Piccolo takes a step forward. Gohan shifts to one side, and Goshin moves with him. Piccolo changes direction at once, reaching toward Gohan.

Gohan turns his shoulder and moves his near foot first, letting Piccolo's hand pass him.

Gohan reaches for Piccolo's arm, but Piccolo twists away. Goshin comes in from the other side, catching Piccolo's wrist before he can turn fully toward Gohan.

Piccolo pulls free and jumps back.

"Better."

Two Piccolos stand where one stood before, and Gohan looks from one to the other.

"Like Tien!" he says.

"Then let's keep both of them busy!" Goshin raises four loose stones. Two travel toward the figure on the left; the others move toward the one on the right.

Both figures dodge, one rising over the stones while the other steps left. The stones aimed at the left-hand figure skim the dry ground, throwing dust across Gohan's view.

Gohan closes his eyes for an instant and hears a shoe scrape the ground to his left, as Krillin's did on the Lookout. He opens his eyes and moves toward the sound.

The left-hand Piccolo reaches for him. Gohan sends a short push of ki toward his feet, making him shift his weight. Goshin guides one stone past Piccolo's shoulder, and the figure turns to avoid it.

"Now!" Gohan calls.

Goshin moves in while Piccolo is turning and touches his sleeve.

The second Piccolo comes toward Gohan. Gohan gathers light between his hands, but Goshin is still close to the first figure.

Gohan stops before firing; the light fades between his hands, and Piccolo lowers his guard.

"You had a shot."

"Goshin was in the way."

"He won't always be."

Gohan changes his position. Goshin sees where he is going and moves the four stones upward, clearing the space between them.

Gohan gathers his ki again.

Piccolo jumps before the wave is released. Gohan turns with him but sends the Kamehameha into the empty bank instead. It strikes stone and breaks into blue light.

The two Piccolos become one, and he lands on the far side of the clearing facing both boys.

Goshin lowers the stones as Piccolo straightens. Gohan lets his hands fall and looks at the clear space between the bank and his brother.

For a moment only the sound of falling grit comes from the bank.

Gohan looks at Goshin. "I saw you move."

"I saw you waiting."

Piccolo glances toward the covered machine.

"Those Saiyans won't wait for either of you to find a safe place to stand."

"We know," Chi-Chi says.

"Then keep practising."

"We'll be ready," Goshin says.

Piccolo rises into the air.

Gohan takes a step forward. "Piccolo!"

Piccolo pauses above the edge of the clearing.

"Can we try again another day?"

Chi-Chi looks at her son but does not answer for him.

Piccolo studies Gohan, then Goshin.

"If I come back," he says, "I won't be moving any slower."

"We'll practise before then," Goshin says.

Piccolo flies away over the trees.

Chi-Chi joins the boys and looks at the mark Gohan's wave left in the bank.

"You made room for each other," she says. "Next time, try not to show him where you're turning."

Gohan studies his footprints. "He watched my feet."

"Then give him something else to watch," Chi-Chi says. She glances toward the hollow. "Let's call Bulma about that machine."

Goshin picks up the water bottle and walks beside Gohan.

## The Machine and the Telephone

Bulma arrives in an aircar with a low carrying sled attached behind it.

Chi-Chi takes her to the clearing while the boys show her the hollow from beneath the overhang. The machine's cover is still closed, but a faint light remains visible through the seam.

"Don't assume it's switched off," Bulma says. "The cover may only have blocked the projector."

Goshin steps back from the seam, keeping his hands to himself.

She walks around the object, examining it without touching the open seam.

"Could it be Father's?" Gohan asks.

"It could be. I'll need to look at it properly before I say."

"The voice called him Kakarot," Goshin tells her.

"That's worth finding out about."

Bulma positions the sled beside the machine. Goshin lifts the rounded shell a short distance with his ki while Chi-Chi slides the sled beneath it. Bulma secures the straps and places an opaque cover over the opening.

"I'll take it to the lab," she says. "I'm not promising I can make it talk safely today."

"Or that it belongs to Goku," Chi-Chi adds.

"Or that," Bulma agrees.

Bulma starts the aircar, and the loaded sled rises behind it. Chi-Chi and the boys watch the covered machine clear the trees before they walk home together.

At the house, Chi-Chi telephones the children's home.

The worker who answers tells her that Pigero has come for his afternoon visit. After a moment, he comes to the telephone.

"Did they stay together again?" Chi-Chi asks.

"They did. Chiko says Ena talks in her sleep."

From somewhere near the receiver comes Chiko's voice. "I didn't say that!"

Pigero laughs. "You said it to me."

Gohan takes the telephone when Chi-Chi offers it.

"C-6 saw Hatch's drawing," he says. "He liked the door."

Hatch calls something from the background.

"He wants to know whether C-6 saw the library," Pigero says.

"Not yet. We left the bigger drawing with you. But I made another picture for it."

Goshin leans close enough to hear Pigero's answer.

"Tell Hatch there are still three doors," he says.

"There are still three doors," Pigero tells the others.

Gohan looks at his brother and grins.

After the call, Chi-Chi tells the boys that the director has not yet made a longer-term decision about the room.

"We'll keep asking," she says.

Gohan folds his new library drawing and puts it beside Hatch's original.

## The Little Planet

Goku comes around another bend and sees the end of Snake Way.

He stops where the road ends in a rounded tip, with nothing beyond it.

"That's it?"

He walks to the edge and looks down, then back along the road. At last he looks up.

A small planet hangs above him, green against the open sky, with a little house and a single tree visible on its curve. Goku's face brightens. "There you are!"

He flies toward it. As he nears the surface, his body is pulled sharply downward. He lands on the grass and finds himself pressed against it, cheek close to the blades as his clothes drag heavily against him.

"Whoa!"

He pushes with both hands. His arms tremble, but he gets one knee beneath him, then the other. When he tries to stand, his foot slides and he drops back onto the grass.

"This place is heavy."

Goku plants his palms again and works one foot under himself. He rises slowly, keeps his knees bent and takes a step toward the house.

"I came all this way. I'm not stopping here."

There are apples in a tree beside the path. Goku reaches for one, but his foot slips. He catches the trunk and stays there, breathing hard.

A small monkey watches him from a branch.

Goku looks up.

"King Kai?"

The monkey makes a sound and tilts its head.

Goku smiles. "You got up there without any trouble. You must be really strong. I'm Goku. I came here to train."

Goku looks at the apples again.

"Could I have one before we start?"

The monkey picks an apple and drops it.

The apple strikes the ground beside Goku and sinks into the soil.

Goku stares at the hole in the soil and looks back up at the branch.

"Could you throw me another one?"

The monkey does. Goku catches it with both hands, though the weight pulls his arms down toward the grass.

"Got it!"

He eats the apple, then looks back up.

"Will you teach me to fight?"

The monkey climbs down and begins moving around the tree. Goku follows, copying its steps as well as he can.

"This is training already?"

The monkey moves one way. Goku moves the same way. The monkey hops; Goku attempts the hop and lands heavily on both feet.

"Ooh! Ooh!" the monkey calls.

"Ooh! Ooh!" Goku answers. If this is a lesson, he doesn't want to miss any of it. The monkey scrambles back up the tree, shaking an apple loose. It drops into the grass and starts to roll.

A voice comes from behind the house. A small, round blue man stands watching, dressed in dark robes with a circular emblem on the front. Two antennae rise above his head, and dark glasses hide his eyes.

"What are you doing?"

Goku looks at the monkey, then turns toward the blue man.

"Are you King Kai?"

## Two Ways to Show Father

That evening, Ox-King is at the gate with a screwdriver in his hand. He opens and closes the gate once, then looks up as the boys come into the yard.

"How did the lesson go?"

"I hit the place I chose," Gohan says. "Goshin showed me how."

He cups his hands at his side, gathers a little light and lets it fade. Beside him, Goshin keeps four pebbles moving in a slow circle.

Ox-King sets the screwdriver on the gatepost. "You've both been busy."

"When Father comes home," Gohan says, "I want to show him."

"I want to show him too."

"Then we can do it together."

Chi-Chi comes to the doorway. "I hope he gets to see it soon. Now wash your hands, both of you."

Goshin grins at Gohan.

"We can practise tomorrow," he tells Gohan.

Gohan looks toward the clearing beyond the ridge, then back at his brother.

"Will you teach me more about moving my feet?"

"Yes. And you can show me how you made me look the wrong way."

Ox-King picks up the screwdriver, and the family goes inside together, leaving the yard quiet behind them.

**NARRATOR:** Goku has reached the little planet above Snake Way. On Earth, his sons are learning from one another, and there is more for both of them to discover.
