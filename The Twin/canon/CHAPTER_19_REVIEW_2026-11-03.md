## What I changed and why

- **Goshin's characterization:** removed two decision-nods and replaced an internal description with observable action. His response to Chi-Chi's sparring conditions now follows his repeat-it-back habit. His teaching, retained skills and ability to hold four rocks while firing remain.
- **Gohan and the twins:** Gohan's dream is tied more clearly to the actual fight. He makes Goshin look the wrong way during their practice, so the later request to learn that move has something concrete behind it. Gohan's own aim, choice and progress remain his; a short water break follows his first completed wave.
- **The strange machine:** Gohan identifies the name from what Raditz said at Kame House, and the boys recall the round ship in which Raditz shut them. This is a resemblance, not proof that the new machine belongs to Goku. Chi-Chi's knowledge comes from Gohan on the page.
- **Piccolo and the danger:** he reacts to the boys' tails and the earlier moon warning, and states his own readiness to destroy the machine if the light reaches them. Chi-Chi identifies the bracket; Gohan makes the shot. Goshin keeps four pieces suspended while diverting a separate falling slab onto a clear slope, rather than firing into an unstable bank above the hollow. Neither child is forced to transform; the natural moon and both tails remain.
- **Family and Goku:** Ox-King, last shown at the house in Chapter 18 with no departure established, appears at breakfast and again at the gate in the evening. Goku's struggle on the heavy planet and his meeting with Bubbles have a little more room, including his request for food. The ending still leaves the blue man's identity unanswered.

These changes preserve the chapter's existing outcomes and unresolved machine identity. They are specific corrections and small additions, not a claim that a mechanical review can settle the writing's quality.
