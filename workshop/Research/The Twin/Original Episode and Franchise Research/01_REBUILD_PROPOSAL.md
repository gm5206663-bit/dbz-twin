# Dragon Ball: the twins — research and rebuild proposal

**For your decision · 1 November 2026**

This is a plan, not replacement prose. The existing project has not been edited. The previous Chapter Ten is **rejected**, not part of accepted continuity. Chapters Four–Nine are provisional; the revised wording of Chapters One–Three was authorized for implementation but has not been accepted merely because it was written. Earlier accepted versions are preserved separately.

## Recommendation in one page

The problem is not that the twins changed canon. They should change it. The problem is that the adaptation often substitutes **reporting, permission, travel arrangements, examinations and explanations** for the adventure those changes should produce. Goshin is present but too often reduced to a well-managed, serious observer. Gohan is frequently given a task rather than the messy experience that made the original scene matter. The result can be orderly and still not feel like Dragon Ball.

I recommend a **staged rebuild starting at the beginning, in a new copy**, with no replacement of the present files unless you approve. The initial reconstruction would cover the Raditz and immediate aftermath material (current Chapters 1–9), followed by a newly planned robot episode. Review each short block before proceeding; do not write a whole new saga at once.

1. **Bring the original scenes back to life:** the forest, the island, the abduction, the truck, the fight and the aftermath need their actual adventure, jokes, fear, reversals, setting and emotional reactions—not just a list of required beats.
2. **Let the boys affect events from inside them.** Goshin can play, get frightened or angry, fly, learn and fight; Gohan can be frightened, curious, clever and brave without becoming a miniature adult. Neither is the other’s prop.
3. **Keep consequences, not rails.** Raditz must take both boys, and he cannot carry Goshin conscious in this particular encounter. After that, do not pre-decide whether Goku lives or dies, whether Piccolo gets the children, whether the moon is destroyed, or which allies survive. Work outward from what people actually do.
4. **Robot episode: put the encounter in the boys’ hands.** Once a credible route compatible with whichever preceding version is approved has been established, the boys need direct time with C-6: getting into trouble, discovering him, dealing with his impatience, helping and needing help, building a bond, and confronting a genuine cave crisis. Do not promise either C-6’s destruction or rescue in advance. Goku or other adults may act if their presence is earned, but an adult rescue performed over the boys’ radio is not a substitute for their adventure.
5. **Keep the user’s rules, remove our production formula from the driver’s seat.** The project’s numerical dialogue quota, shot-length prescriptions, automatic eye-catch and mandatory short scene endings did not prevent the failure. Their provenance as user instructions is unconfirmed. They should be proposed as optional craft tools, not treated as evidence of quality. No standing character habit should be silently changed to make that easier.
6. **Compare original sources before each section, then read the result as a story.** Maintain ordered scene/beat records and explicit source limits, but never mistake a completed checklist for a good chapter. Bring actual rewritten prose to you for judgment before proceeding.

## What would be preserved

### Explicitly recorded user direction

The project’s dated history records: Son Goshin is Gohan’s native twin, not an outsider with foreknowledge; Raditz first speaks his Saiyan name, Broc. His design includes the specified strong resemblance to Goku and Chi-Chi’s eyes; he is emotional, plays fully, and grows frighteningly in a fight. He was born at a stable 100; use printed digits where the setting prints them. He has the locally expressed Adaptation Talent, with no free numerical power boost from simply observing; the two boys are taken by Raditz. The anime is the main starting source, with the manga and films also researched; differences need a ruling, not an invented averaged version. See `dbz-twin/HISTORY.md` around the 30 October rulings.

Later recorded corrections: Goshin cannot be carried off conscious by Raditz in this encounter; his capability and anger-driven growth must be visible. He is not to be written less capable than Gohan. His first copied Kamehameha in this encounter has more raw ki than Goku’s but less practiced control. Deliberate training prioritizes mastery/perfection of what he already has and serious foundations; instinctive emergency copying remains allowed. Senzu completely heal and restore. These directions do **not** mean Gohan cannot grow, every fight has a predetermined winner, the twins are invulnerable, Goshin is confined to a fixed Nappa-era ceiling, or the Talent automatically adds a printed power number.

The existing future-form and tail rulings remain untouched. At four, Goshin is mortal and no early golden form or god ki is introduced. He keeps his tail under the project’s standing ruling. Later ape/god mechanics are not imported into the current action; the project already flags a future **comfortable-as-ape versus fear-at-the-moon** ambiguity for later clarification.

### Chapter-specific instruction

`HISTORY.md` explicitly describes the user’s ruling for Chapter Three as **all yard / external camera / no one’s interior POV**. Treat that as binding for that chapter. It does not by itself prove that the user ordered every chapter in the franchise to use that camera rule. Any proposal that changes the original Chapter Three restriction requires separate approval.

### Existing habits and approved repairs

The current design also has food-witnessing, instruction repetition, father-invitation, downhill-position and mother-inspection habits. Their original provenance is not fully recoverable from the surviving history, and the user approved specific repairs without adding exceptions. Therefore **preserve them as current constraints unless you authorize a change**. The remedy for wooden dialogue is not to omit an applicable repetition: avoid inventing unnecessary orders, and give the necessary ones a natural reason and emotional context.

The earlier `STATUS.md` calls “99% Goku” a retired *design expression*. The history records it as the author’s direction. The files do not establish a separate user withdrawal of the resemblance itself. Do not treat the assistant’s stylistic paraphrase as permission to erase the intended resemblance.

### Scope that remains open

“All films” are in the research scope. Each film still needs an explicit continuity treatment when relevant; the historical project’s `OPEN-6` is unresolved. **In scope is not the same as a mandate to paste mutually contradictory films unchanged into one chronology.** The same distinction applies to GT, DAIMA, manga/TV variants and the enhanced 2026 *Super: Beerus*. Do not unilaterally exclude or merge them.

## A workable robot-episode setup, not a predetermined plot

If we retain the provisional ending of Chapter Nine as the starting point, Goshin still has a head injury, has refused an unwitnessed Senzu, and has been told to rest and avoid strenuous practice; the family has a clear reason to guard both boys after Piccolo’s attempt. Simply sending them unattended into the desert or declaring the injury gone would break that state. A new version would have to **earn any recovery and any journey**, respect the actual instructions given, and make separation or access to the ruins plausible. If an approved rebuild changes the earlier fight or its recovery, the new robot setup must use that new state instead.

The episode’s essential dramatic sequence is not the precise address of a cave: a vulnerable child encounters a grumpy, damaged robot; repeated need and small acts create a relationship; a rescue attempt makes danger worse; the robot’s choice saves the child at great cost; the child faces the result. With twins, both children can participate differently in those beats. The exact animal sequence, terrain, wire choreography and robot outcome must be sourced or transparently authored; the two secondary accounts conflict on how the bird encounter occurs. If help plausibly changes C-6’s fate, let it change; if it does not, do not protect him by authorial fiat. In either case, the feeling and the choice must play on the page.

## What this research does and does not establish

- All ten current chapter bodies and the project’s rules, history and episode-coverage records were read for this audit. Japanese caption files for episodes 9 and 10 were read directly for this pass; prior project records report full caption reads for Z 1–36 and some selected later episodes. The captions’ origin and exact cut are unverified.
- Official Toei synopsis pages for Z 1–35 were read as a short-plot guide. They are **not the episodes**, do not list every scene, and do not verify silent staging. Additional franchise pages were indexed separately; fetching a synopsis is not a viewing or a full review.
- No complete episode video or film has been watched for this audit. Manga/film/version-specific research and full franchise visual verification remain substantial work. See the source ledger and coverage map; do not market this as a completed watch-through.
- The local project, previous ZIPs, Drive snapshots and GitHub history have not been overwritten. **The current public `main` and latest Drive snapshot still label the rejected Chapter Ten provisional rather than rejected.** They are historical artifacts, not endorsements. Updating those labels or publishing any new research requires a deliberate separate action.
