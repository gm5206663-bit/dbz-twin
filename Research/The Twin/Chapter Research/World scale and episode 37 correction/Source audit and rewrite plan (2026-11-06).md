## Why the previous correction failed

The author clarified that the criticism concerned the scale of the entire Dragon Ball world, including speed and planet-threatening power, and that the chapter also failed as an Episode 37 adaptation and as Dragon Ball. The assistant instead treated it as a small demonstration and made up a comparison with the brothers' earlier combined waves. That comparison was a new authored assertion, not a necessary conclusion from the shown fight, and is not retained as an established fact in this revision. Clearing clouds is not an adequate proxy for the author's planet-scale baseline. A controlled small action can be valid, but prose and characterization must not mistake it for the fighter's capacity.

The opening and source sequence are not the only problems: the previous Chapter 30 spends most of its length on repeated handoffs, precautions, medical logistics, neighbour administration, and subdued explanation. Bulma is separated from the discovery; the craft is only a door and a light when the chapter stops. The test flight is omitted even though this chapter claims Episode 37. Those omissions were acknowledged as partial, but a disclosure was not a substitute for adapting the source's core adventure. The prior green mechanical checks cannot judge scale, tone, pacing, source completeness, or reader acceptance.

**Scope:** repair the unaccepted Chapter 30 from its actual Chapter 29 ending and record the wider power and style direction for all subsequent writing. ## Power and speed evidence

- Dragon Ball Official explicitly identifies both Kamesennin's and Piccolo's Moon destructions in the original continuity: [Moon article](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_2131.html). Roshi's specific 21st-Tournament maximum Kamehameha is also described in the [Jackie Chun character profile](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_1502.html). The **selected branch's natural moon remains intact** because the source transformation and Piccolo's destruction did not occur in this branch; do not invent that as an on-page branch event.
- Dragon Ball Official's [Vegeta Saiyan-arc profile](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_3929.html) describes his planet-threatening Galick Gun and Goku's interception. It is a genuine world-threatening baseline; in the selected Chapter 25 Vegeta explicitly aims a Galick Gun at Earth and Goshin and Gohan together turn it away. Source Vegeta and selected-branch Vegeta are not identical in every action or measured condition.
- The author directs that present post-fight, post-first-Great-Ape Goshin is capable of destroying Earth. His serious, cumulative ordinary-body strength and ki growth carry forward; he does not need to destroy anything to prove it, but the story must operate at that scale. The momentary 19,400 scouter reading preceded additional growth and is not a cap.
- The author regards Goku as light-speed or faster already in original Dragon Ball, and wants the world's combat/movement speed written accordingly. Treat this as the author's working interpretation for the branch. It is not presented as a newly verified exact source speed, precise measurement for every fighter, unconditional travel speed, or a physics equation for every scene. No general speed maximum is imposed.
- Precise planet-destruction thresholds and exact speed figures are not newly invented. Ki control and injury or effort can matter without reducing planet-level fighters to mundane humans or turning each harmless movement into a planetary disaster. Both brothers retain agency and growth, and Goshin is never less capable than Gohan by the standing authored rule.
