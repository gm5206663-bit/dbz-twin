# Chapter-by-chapter audit: story, voice and source coverage

**Reading basis:** all ten chapter bodies as they existed at the start of this research pass; their project coverage notes and history; Toei’s short synopses for Z 1–35; a fresh read of the local Z 9 and 10 Japanese captions; selected official character profiles and secondary episode guides. No full video was watched. References such as `§4` point to the current chapter, before its footer.

**Status:** the previously accepted bodies of Chapters 1–3 exist in a pre-revision snapshot. Their newer, authorized revisions still await review. Chapters 4–9 are provisional. Chapter 10 has been rejected. A passage’s presence in a draft is not its acceptance. Findings below are editorial judgments, not claims that a mechanical test can measure canon style.

## Pattern across the project

The drafts often preserve a *description* of what should matter—childhood wonder, a frightened family, an approaching enemy, a chance to train—while spending the scene on instructions and explanations about those things. The consequence is a rhythm of “ask, clarify, repeat, confirm, plan, report.” Those exchanges are sometimes necessary, especially because the story itself introduced injuries and danger. They become a problem when they replace distinctive adventure, comedy, character friction, risk and action for entire scenes.

The hard numerical band and shot budgets in `WRITING.md` are production choices. The surviving records do not show that the user imposed those exact formulae. Their effect should be reviewed, not misattributed to the user. By contrast, the character habits now in force must not be discarded merely because following them is difficult: the user approved particular repairs and did not grant exceptions. Write fewer gratuitous commands; do not silently remove an applicable repeat-back.

A particularly revealing contradiction is `STATUS.md`’s statement that the boy is the only child who does not move, alongside its statement that he fidgets, and the user’s recorded direction that he *plays fully* and is emotional. Quiet concentration or quiet rage can coexist with energetic, funny play. A prose implementation that makes him solemn all the time does not satisfy that fuller picture. The original user wording and any later direct clarification should be checked before altering a character law.

## Chapter 1 — Two Names

**What works.** The two-son premise enters an ordinary, recognisable family morning; Goshin is visible at the woodpile; the farmer and Piccolo scenes allow the threat to approach independently. The authorized revision restored the lost hat, river and rescue sequence, instead of continuing to imply it happened in an unseen retelling.

**What is off.** The restored forest/river adventure is packed into §4, about 200 words by a simple count, while several preceding and following sections spend considerably longer on household rhythm. Gohan’s curiosity, alarm, escapes, reactions and his father’s search are largely compressed into a succession of factual actions. This is the correct *list* of many reported events, but not yet the same felt adventure. The decisive forest visuals are still derived from a secondary synopsis; the SRT does not verify every motion.

**Rebuild treatment.** Give the butterfly, cat, stolen hat, dangerous river and rescue room to breathe in their original order, with Gohan’s childlike responses and Goku’s search dramatised. Keep the family introduction and Goshin’s individuality, but make its length earn the delay in the adventure. Do not invent that the boys both witnessed the forest sequence merely to put the twin into every scene. Verify actual visual blocking if a lawful video source becomes available.

## Chapter 2 — The Second Name

**What works.** The crab game, bubbles and stone-skipping let both boys behave as children. The restored Saiyan-business explanation and source-order repairs address genuine earlier omissions. Raditz’s arrival interrupts a lively reunion.

**What is off.** The narration describes Gohan’s scholar answer as a phrase learned through repetition. His official manga-based profile identifies being a scholar as *his dream*; the one line does not prove he lacks a personal wish, but it risks making the ambition sound merely programmed. Other lines flatten distinctive voices into careful, explanatory exchanges. The chapter hands the fight to Chapter 4, which is viable only if the handoff feels like a real cliffhanger rather than a forced accounting boundary.

**Rebuild treatment.** Keep the specific play, sweets, stones, family introductions, moon discussion, Raditz’s explanations and sequence. Let Gohan’s education be his own desire as well as his mother’s concern; allow Goku, Krillin, Bulma and Roshi to react with their own rhythms. Goshin’s special traits should appear through living behaviour, not a list of tells. Preserve the introduction of the second name and lead straight into the earned abduction.

## Chapter 3 — Eight Hours

**What works.** The separate home perspective makes Chi-Chi and the Ox-King present while everyone else is away. The schoolwork, returned paper, gifts and unanswered call come from real episode-3-to-6 home material, although their intervening domestic staging is authored. The revised one-day framing removes the former rigid countdown conflict.

**What is off.** The prolonged household routine risks feeling static when read alongside the much more compressed Gohan adventure. More importantly, several of these same home events are dramatised again in Chapters 4–7. This is reader-facing repetition, even though the footers correctly say they are the same events, not literal second deliveries or visits.

**Critical constraint.** The historical record calls the all-yard external camera/no interiority a **user ruling for this chapter**. Do not propose cutting it away as if it were only an assistant preference. Its present form can remain a contained Chi-Chi chapter while the other chapters stop replaying its events. If a different interleaving or camera treatment is desired, obtain explicit permission first. The current Chapter 3 is a revision under review, not automatically re-accepted.

## Chapter 4 — The Rescue

**What works.** Goshin actually fights Raditz on the island, flies and grows angry before an earned knockout; he is not simply carried away conscious. Gohan helps his unconscious brother under the farmer’s pickup; the truck incident, Nimbus call and Piccolo travel exchange were restored after the reader caught omissions. Both brothers subsequently escape the pod.

**What is off.** The fight can read as a chain of positions and compliance checks rather than the frightening, fast-moving interruption of a family reunion. Goshin’s capabilities are present, but the pacing sometimes explains them immediately after showing them. The home insert in §6 replays Chapter 3 §1. These are editorial problems, not grounds to undo the user’s required knockout or to re-stage the boys’ abduction without them.

**Rebuild treatment.** Preserve the island confrontation, boys’ capture, truck, pod, Nimbus/alliance and meeting with Raditz. Design each fight exchange around a concrete intention, obstacle, reversal and consequence. Vary Gohan’s actions beyond calling for help and moving his brother; let Goshin’s strength and emotions alter the struggle without making Raditz harmless. Once the brothers are out of the pod, accept the resulting change to the crater scene.

## Chapter 5 — Piccolo’s New Attack

**What works.** Piccolo’s injury, the time needed to charge, Goku’s first wave, the near miss, the tail and Raditz’s deceit are present. Goshin’s copied Kamehameha is on-page, has its own authored scan, and meets the user’s comparison: more raw ki than Goku’s wave at that moment, with less practiced control. Gohan’s warning helps change Goku’s decision.

**What is off.** Many volleys resemble repeated “move / call / move / warn” cycles. The invented 1,450 reading is not a user-specified number or universal power law. The tail severance is an authored change with a stated basis, not an original episode-4 event. Chi-Chi’s paper and washing in §10 replay Chapter 3 §§2–3.

**Rebuild treatment.** Keep the attack’s setup, individual character motives and readable geography; remove exchanges that produce no new tactical or emotional turn. Preserve the user’s first-wave power/control direction, but label any particular new scan as an authored estimate and retain or reconsider it only within an approved rebuild. Let Raditz’s plea, Goku’s choice and Piccolo’s impatience play as character, not as a chain of footnotes. Use the home moment only once.

## Chapter 6 — The Brothers’ Attack

**What works.** Gohan’s headbutt matters; Goshin makes a separate attack; Goku’s survival follows a proposed combination of positioning, Gohan’s diversion, Goshin’s leg shot and Goku’s own release. The story does not pretend the beam passed through Goku. Raditz’s warning reaches the other Saiyans, and the Dragon Ball information remains consequential.

**What is off.** These are still provisional authored choices, not an approved fate. The simultaneous actions and numerical readings risk making the emotional peak feel engineered around a display rather than a desperate family fight. Gohan can feel like an accessory to Goshin’s more technical moves if his strike and choices receive less dramatic weight. §5 repeats the same Ox-King truck and gift visit already shown in Chapter 3 §§5–6.

**Rebuild treatment.** Begin at the actual cliffhanger and make both boys’ actions distinct and causally indispensable where the scene supports it. Show the second beam and its danger clearly. Let Goku live or die only as the rebuilt actions warrant; neither outcome is a user-mandated destination. Preserve Raditz’s death/threat only if the new action earns them, and recompute what the scouter transmitted. Do not replay the grandfather’s visit.

## Chapter 7 — The Year Ahead

**What works.** Piccolo’s attempt to seize the children remains dangerous and self-interested; Goshin’s copied pull and flight matter, and Gohan’s burst and warning matter too. Roshi tells Chi-Chi promptly rather than artificially withholding news. Goku and Gohan receive full Senzu healing; Goshin refuses the unwitnessed bean. The resulting medical concern has a real cause.

**What is off.** Multiple threads—crater, Yemma, phone, Senzu, transport—are all explained at length. §7 repeats the same Chapter 3 §10 unanswered call and visit. Some healing dialogue explicitly confirms the mechanics in a way that sounds like a test result rather than a spontaneous reunion. The new Yemma inquiry is an invented branch complication, not a mandatory replacement for Snake Way.

**Rebuild treatment.** Preserve the real consequences of whichever fight actually occurs: injury, Chi-Chi’s information, Piccolo’s threat, the Senzu rule and the one-year warning. Make the family news and Piccolo encounter dramatic through the people involved, not merely through documentation. If Goku is alive, do not force him onto dead-Goku Snake Way; an earned different route may be explored or left unresolved. Show the home call once.

## Chapter 8 — The Long Way Home

**What works.** Chi-Chi reunites with both sons and Goku; she responds to Piccolo’s attack. The family cares for Goshin’s head and hand, and a doctor’s warning is a credible consequence of the draft’s knockout and refused bean. Gohan’s fear and study are acknowledged. The family meal gives the cast some warmth; Goku’s question about Grandpa Gohan is a meaningful consequence of the moon warning.

**What is off.** The doctor is introduced in §§3 and 6, followed by a long scene (§7) in which characters re-explain much of what the reader already saw, then another meal and discussion of training. The material repeatedly pauses to verify that everyone knows the same things. The episode-7 wilderness adventure and its fear/problem-solving have been displaced because the branch kept the boys home; replacing them solely with a conversation and a small exercise loses much of their dramatic energy.

**Rebuild treatment.** Keep urgent care, the refusal and the loving reunion; compress redundant reports rather than erasing medical advice. Present meaningful consequences through interaction and decisions. If the family remains together, invent an earned adventure appropriate to that state rather than asserting Piccolo secretly has custody. Gohan can be brave and still need comfort; Chi-Chi can be protective, funny and forceful without being reduced to the sensible administrator.

## Chapter 9 — The Message from the Tower

**What works.** Yajirobe, Korin, Kami, Baba, the fighters, Bulma’s scouter and the Dragon Balls remain active as separate threads. Gohan frees a bird with care, and he chooses to learn a basic escape response while still wanting to study. The family takes the earlier moon warning seriously; the scouter test expressly does not prove that transmissions are safe.

**What is off.** The message, road marker, laboratory, home examination, Dragon Ball plan and nightly safety discussion add up to a large amount of logistics. The bird scene has independent value but is much safer and smaller than the stranded-cliff and Great Ape crisis it replaces. Goshin is mostly watching because the draft’s injury is still active; that is a consequence to solve causally, not a reason to pretend he is fully active. The original moon is intact and both tails remain only in this **provisional** branch.

**Rebuild treatment.** Keep separate character threads and the good bird moment if they earn their space. Avoid rechecking every arrangement in each chapter. Have the recovery progress consistently, with earned clearance before strenuous travel or training; if the earlier incident changes, re-evaluate recovery from that revised cause. Do not force a transformation or lunar destruction, but do not systematically replace danger with meetings. Yamcha’s actual baseball and social thread still lies ahead—not merely a newspaper mention.

## Chapter 10 — The Book and the Road — rejected

**What the draft did.** A further doctor’s examination permits a restricted passenger trip. The family visits Korin/Kami, learns of a desert report, and remains at a lookout while Goku and workers go inside. Gohan participates by radio, and Goshin contributes to the diagram. Adults bring C-6 out; the robot later chooses shutdown with an uncertain future.

**Why it failed.** The children do not share sustained direct time in the cave with C-6 as danger, irritation, dependence and affection develop. The robot is treated as a rescue coordination problem. The exciting, funny and frightening first encounter is mediated by adults, and the emotional core lands after the crucial choices have already been made elsewhere. Goshin’s injury turns him into an observer for yet another chapter. The chapter is coherent about its own logistics; that is not the requested Dragon Ball experience.

**Correct treatment now.** The user rejected the whole draft. Do not use its adult team, radio, medical clearance, timing, rescue, C-6’s shutdown or `O124`–`O129` as accepted branch facts. The episode-9 research can still be examined, with the source limitations in its own file. In a candidate rebuild, establish an earned route to direct interaction for both boys. Preserve risk, humor and grief, and allow the result to change only through actions and available help. No premature promise that the robot must die or survive.

## Voice tests for actual rewritten scenes

- **Gohan:** four, polite and curious; frightened and sometimes crying, but capable of compassion, resourcefulness and decisive anger. His wish to be a scholar is his, not merely something his mother made him memorise. Do not make him always wise, always helpless or automatically a copy of Goku.
- **Goshin:** the user explicitly wanted emotional, fully playful, frightening growth in combat and strong resemblance to Goku with Chi-Chi’s eyes. Distinct habits and quiet concentration do not require a permanently solemn bureaucrat. He can use established abilities and grow; he does not know future canon. Respect his current food/instruction/family habits and training priorities.
- **Goku:** warm, direct, hungry, playful and a capable fighter who responds to actual danger. His intelligence is often physical and situational. Concern for his sons need not sound like an extended committee briefing; serious grief and planning are still possible where earned.
- **Chi-Chi:** protective, proud, hot-tempered and affectionate, with her own history as a martial artist; not merely a comic obstacle or a paperwork-and-safety officer. Her anger should respond to what she knows and who has endangered her family.
- **Piccolo, this early:** dangerous, proud and self-interested, able to change slowly through experiences that actually happen. Do not begin with the later mentor already fully formed, or make cruelty his only trait.
- **Krillin:** brave, anxious, loyal and often funny; competent and independent even when frightened. His canonical failure to tell Chi-Chi need not recur if the branch has already told her.
- **Bulma:** inventive, outspoken, socially sharp and brave; the scouter should invite curiosity and personality as well as justified caution.
- **Yamcha and Puar:** the episode-10 baseball, banter, fighting spirit, relationship friction and recruitment are an independent character strand. Do not replace it with the bare fact that Yamcha plays baseball.
- **C-6:** grumpy, exhausted, initially resistant to being awakened; his care develops through repeated direct encounters and choice under pressure. Do not turn him into a generic polite radio adviser. The exact silent actions and voice performance require video review.
