# Chapter Sixteen — The Sea All Around

*Anime episode reference: Dragon Ball Z, Episode 15.*

*Revised story draft; not yet accepted.*

A drop of rain rests on the Four-Star Ball. It trembles when the wind reaches the hat, then rolls down the brim and falls onto an open book.

## The Third Basket

In his dream, Gohan stands in the stone basin with his father. Goku sets his feet and raises his hands.

"Ready?"

"Ready!" Gohan darts forward, and Goku turns to meet him. From the edge of the basin, Chi-Chi calls for them to keep their blasts away from the house. Goshin laughs and tells Gohan to try the other side.

Gohan wakes in a bed at the Lookout. The room is quiet; Father is still away on the long road. Goshin is curled up in the next bed, and Gohan settles back under his blanket until he hears Chi-Chi in the kitchen.

Later, Gohan draws three baskets in the margin of his schoolbook. The first has four apples. The second has four apples. The third has three apples and a hole in the bottom.

Chi-Chi leans over his shoulder.

"Does the question say one fell out?"

"No."

"Then why is there a hole?"

"Goshin asked what would happen if there was."

Goshin measures the hole in the drawing with his finger. "It would depend how big the apples were," he says.

Gohan looks at the three apples he has drawn. "These are bigger than the hole."

"Then they're all right." Goshin covers one with his thumb. "Unless Father gets here first."

Gohan pushes his hand away. "I'm trying to count them!"

Chi-Chi gives Gohan the pencil. "How many apples altogether?"

Gohan adds another apple to the third basket, then counts them. He writes twelve on the line and turns the book around for her.

"That's right."

Gohan grins. "Father would have eaten one before I finished counting."

"Your father would have eaten all twelve."

"Would he eat the baskets?" Goshin asks.

"Not if he knew they were baskets," Chi-Chi says.

Both boys laugh. Chi-Chi takes the pan off the stove and divides the rice between their bowls.

A fourth bowl stands at the end of the table, where Goshin put it for Goku the day before. Chi-Chi clears Gohan's book away from the rice and leaves the bowl in its place.

Gohan looks at it. "Do you think Father found King Kai yet?"

"Kami said the road was long," Chi-Chi says. "We'll ask if he's heard anything when we come back."

"From C-6?"

"From our visit to C-6."

Gohan checks the wrapping around Mina's bridge book. A corner of the cloth has come loose, so he tucks it beneath the knot.

"He'll like the bridge with the little one behind it."

Goshin takes a mouthful of rice. "The little one counts."

"I know. We counted seven."

"We might have missed one."

Gohan puts down his chopsticks. "We'll count them with C-6."

## Two Piccolos

Rain strikes the broken stone in the wasteland. Piccolo stands above a narrow valley with water running from the edge of his cloak.

He raises two fingers to his forehead.

The rain gives way to the crater in his memory. Goshin fires at Raditz's left, and Gohan pulls Goku out of the line. Piccolo extends his fingers.

The spiralling beam races toward Raditz. At the last instant Raditz twists, and the shot tears through the edge of his shoulder armour instead of his chest. Beyond him, a ridge splits and falls.

Later in the same fight, Gohan throws dirt into Raditz's eyes. Goshin pulls Goku aside, then tears his sleeve free of Raditz's grip and flies out of the way. Piccolo fires again as Raditz struggles for footing. This time the beam strikes him.

Rain runs down Piccolo's face as he lowers his hand.

"He saw it coming."

He pulls off his cloak and turban and sets them beneath an overhang. Then he steps back into the rain. His ki rises around him, pressing the water away from his body.

A second Piccolo emerges from him and lands on the opposite side of the valley.

Both stand with their feet apart. Rain falls between them.

The second Piccolo moves first. He crosses the valley and drives a fist toward Piccolo's face. Piccolo turns it aside, but the other hand strikes his ribs and sends him into the rock behind him.

Piccolo pushes off the wall. He catches his double by the wrist, turns and throws him across the valley. The double lands on one foot, leaves a furrow in the wet stone and comes straight back.

Piccolo rises above him. The double touches two fingers to his forehead, and light begins to gather.

Piccolo dives toward him. The double slips behind a boulder, keeping his fingers in place while the light brightens. Piccolo circles the rock. The double jumps clear, turns and extends his hand.

"Makankosappo!"

Piccolo drops. The beam passes over him and cuts into a ridge behind the valley. Stone breaks away and crashes down the far slope.

He looks at the opening, then at his double.

"Less time to gather power than I had against Raditz. Again."

The double comes at him through the rain.

## A Book for C-6

Chi-Chi lands Dr. Brief's borrowed aircar outside Capsule Corporation. Gohan climbs out with the bridge book under his arm, and Goshin follows him across the courtyard.

Gohan reaches the laboratory door first, hugging the wrapped book. Bulma opens it with a screwdriver tucked behind one ear.

"Did you bring C-6 something?"

"Mina said he could borrow it. It has a bridge on the cover, and there's a little one behind another bridge inside. We nearly missed it."

"He counted seven bridges," Goshin says. "I'm going to look for eight."

"They're not all on one page," Gohan tells Bulma.

"I'd have liked to see that," she says, and ushers them in.

C-6 is on the laboratory table with a padded support beneath his bent arm. Dr. Brief's cat sits on the back of a chair, watching a length of cord on the floor.

"Mina said you could borrow it," Gohan tells C-6. "She wants it back when you're finished."

"That's fair," C-6 says. "I wouldn't want to lose my book either."

Gohan unties the cloth. C-6 turns his glass eye toward the cover.

"That bridge has two levels."

"There's another bridge behind it," Goshin says. "A small one."

"You found that from the cover?"

Gohan opens the book to the picture they counted on the Lookout. The smaller bridge is tucked between two hills, with a line of trees partly hiding one end.

C-6 studies it. "I see it now."

"I found it," Goshin says.

"And I counted it," Gohan says.

"Then I suppose I owe both of you a thank-you."

The cat jumps onto the table. Gohan catches the edge of the book as the cat steps onto the wrapping cloth.

"Not on the pages," C-6 says.

Bulma lifts the cat down. "He knows which things people don't want him to sit on."

"He likes being noticed," Dr. Brief says.

The cat catches the cord between its paws. Nobody has to move it again.

On the next bench, the scouter lies where Bulma left it. Its power cell is in the closed box beside it. Goshin goes to look at the coloured lens, stopping short of the bench.

"Leave it where it is," Chi-Chi says.

Goshin leans closer without touching it. The lens gives his nose a green edge. He steps to one side, and the green edge moves with him.

"It makes my nose look green."

"That's not the worst thing it can do," Bulma says. "We're keeping the cell out until we know more."

Dr. Brief brings a small mirror to C-6's side.

"May I use this to look under the shoulder plate? I won't disconnect anything."

"Will you move the support?"

"No. I'll leave your arm where it is."

C-6 turns his eye toward the mirror. "All right. Tell me if you find the clicking."

Bulma brings a lamp closer. Dr. Brief loosens the plate and lifts its edge enough to angle the mirror beneath it. A thin tube runs beside the bent joint. There is a dark mark where it passes the metal.

"That may be where it's rubbing," Dr. Brief says. "I can't tell yet whether it's the only place."

"Can you see it without taking the arm off?" C-6 asks.

"I can see part of it. I'd like to examine it more closely before we decide what to do."

"I'd like to know what you're going to do before you do it."

"Of course."

Dr. Brief withdraws the mirror and fastens the plate again. C-6 moves his working hand a little, then stops when the click sounds inside his casing.

"It's still there."

"Yes," Dr. Brief says. "We'll leave it alone for now."

Gohan turns the book so C-6 can see the next page. A bridge crosses a stretch of water toward an island. There are boats beneath it, each one with a small white sail.

"How do they make the bridge reach that far?" Gohan asks.

C-6 studies the picture. "They don't make it all at once. They build supports first."

"In the water?"

"Some of them."

Goshin looks from the boats to the edge of the page. "Does the water go all the way around the island?"

"That's what makes it an island," Bulma says.

"Like Master Roshi's," Gohan says.

"A lot bigger, in this picture."

Goshin measures it with his fingers. "Not in the book."

Bulma picks up a pencil from the bench. "That's because the book wouldn't fit on the table otherwise."

The telephone rings.

## Grandfather's Pot

Bulma answers, then holds the receiver out to Chi-Chi. "It's your father."

"Father?"

Ox-King's voice comes so loudly through the receiver that Gohan hears it from the table.

"Chi-Chi! I've been to the house. I brought you some porridge, but nobody was there."

"We're at Bulma's. The boys came to see C-6."

"Are you eating enough?"

"Yes, Father."

"I made a whole pot."

"Then don't eat it all before we get there."

Ox-King is quiet for a moment. "Is Goku on that road already?"

Chi-Chi looks toward Gohan and Goshin. "He is. We're all right, and we're training at Kami's Lookout."

"Can I see the boys?"

"We're coming by the house later. Gohan wants to tell you about a bridge book."

Gohan takes the receiver when she offers it.

"Grandfather, it has boats too. One has a sail nearly as big as the bridge."

"That's a fine boat!"

"Not really as big," Gohan says. "The boat is in front."

"Oh. Well, that's a fine picture."

Gohan gives the receiver back to Chi-Chi, who promises to let him know if their plans change. When she hangs up, Bulma has the bridge book open to the island page again.

"I have to check a weather station on an island this afternoon," she says. "The wind gauge has been sending me the same reading for two days. I think it's stuck."

Gohan looks at the picture. "Could we see it?"

"The gauge?"

"The island. And the gauge."

"I could use the company."

Chi-Chi looks through the laboratory window. The clouds over the city are thin, but a darker bank sits beyond the buildings.

"What about the weather?"

"The report says the storm is still offshore. We'll take a look and leave if it turns toward us."

Both boys turn toward Chi-Chi.

"We can go for a little while," she says. "Your grandfather is waiting for us at home."

Gohan closes the bridge book carefully and leaves it with C-6.

"We'll tell you what we see."

"Tell me whether it has a bridge," C-6 says.

Goshin leans closer. "What if it doesn't?"

"Then tell me that."

Goshin grins. "I can remember that."

## The Wind Gauge

The aircar passes over a long stretch of water. Gohan presses close to the window. Goshin is beside him, with the seat strap across his lap and both hands resting on the glass.

A patch of sand appears ahead. Behind it rise dunes, a ridge of dark rock and a stand of trees bent away from the sea.

"Is that it?" Gohan asks.

"That's it," Bulma says. "The station's on the near side."

Chi-Chi brings the borrowed car down beside a small shed above the beach. A pole rises from its roof. At the top, a metal arrow points across the water and stays there, even when the wind tugs at Bulma's hair.

"That's supposed to turn," Bulma says.

Goshin follows the direction of the arrow. "Is the wind over there?"

A gust lifts the end of his sleeve from the other side.

"Not today."

Bulma opens the shed and takes a case of tools inside. Chi-Chi follows her. From the doorway, Gohan can see the sea on one side and a broad sand slope on the other.

A length of faded cloth lies beside a pile of driftwood near the shed. Gohan picks up one end. The wind opens it against his hands.

"It looks like a sail."

Bulma glances out. "It used to cover the equipment when I brought it here. You can use it if you don't mind getting it sandy."

"Could we make something that moves?"

"You could try."

Gohan sets the cloth down and examines the driftwood. One piece is broad and flat. Another has a fork at the end. Goshin raises the flat piece and turns it over; sand runs from a crack along its underside.

"This part can go on the ground," Gohan says. "The sail can go here."

"Where will you sit?"

Gohan crouches on the board. His knees come up nearly to his chin.

"Here."

Goshin climbs on behind him. The other end of the board rises, and Gohan puts both hands down before he tips off.

Goshin lifts the whole board over his head, Gohan and all. "Now it can fly!"

"Put me down!" Gohan laughs. "We haven't made the sail yet."

Goshin sets the board on the sand. "Maybe we need a longer one."

They find two pieces they can fasten side by side. Gohan threads a length of cord through a gap in the wood. Goshin holds the pieces level while Gohan pulls the cord tight, then passes the loose end around a second time.

The forked branch becomes a mast. They brace it between the boards, and Gohan ties the cloth to it. When the wind fills the cloth, the mast leans toward him.

"Can you hold it while I fix the knot?" he asks.

"Yes."

Goshin holds the mast upright. Gohan makes another loop and pulls until the branch stays in place on its own.

Chi-Chi comes out of the shed. "What have you made?"

"A boat for the sand."

"Does it have a way to stop?"

Gohan looks at his feet. "Those."

"Try the small slope first, and stay together."

Goshin nudges Gohan. "You steer. I'll hold on."

Gohan takes the cord attached to the sail. Goshin settles behind him. A gust fills the cloth, and the board slides down the slope.

It travels three feet and stops against a stone.

Gohan pushes them free. The sail catches the wind again. This time they reach the bottom, where Gohan puts out a foot and turns them sideways.

"It stopped!" he calls.

"I can see that," Chi-Chi says. "Now can you get it back up?"

Goshin lifts one end. Gohan takes the other, and they carry it up together.

## A Shot That Moves

In the wasteland, Piccolo's double drives him against the slope again. Piccolo plants both feet on the rock and pushes away before the next blow lands.

He flies low across the valley. His double follows.

Piccolo touches two fingers to his forehead while he moves. Light gathers there, thin at first, then brighter. The double sends a burst at the ground ahead of him.

Stone erupts. Piccolo rises through the edge of the dust, and the double changes direction to meet him.

Piccolo fires.

The double twists aside. The beam passes close to his shoulder and bores into the empty ridge. A slab breaks loose below the new hole.

Piccolo catches the slab with his ki before it falls into the valley. He turns it across his double's path, then closes the distance behind it. The double strikes through the stone; Piccolo is already at his side.

Their arms meet. The remaining pieces of the slab fall between them.

Piccolo draws back and lands on the opposite bank. He looks at the cut in the ridge, then at his double.

"I had the shot sooner."

The double raises his hands. Piccolo raises his own.

## The Long Slope

Gohan and Goshin carry their sand boat to a higher point on the dune. The shed is still visible below them, with Chi-Chi at its door and Bulma on a ladder beside the wind gauge.

Gohan pulls the sail toward him. The cloth fills and snaps against the mast.

"Ready?"

"Ready."

The boards slide. Sand sprays out behind them. Gohan leans to one side, and the boat turns around a patch of scrub. Goshin leans with him, laughing as the slope carries them faster.

"It's working!"

A shadow crosses the sail.

Gohan looks up. A great bird is coming down from the ridge with its talons spread. It reaches for the moving cloth, catches the edge and pulls the mast sideways.

The boat swerves toward a rock.

Gohan ducks, keeping hold of the cord. Goshin catches the mast with both hands before it strikes him. The bird beats its wings, lifting the sail and one end of the board off the sand.

"It has the cloth!" Gohan says.

Goshin rises with the board. Gohan scrambles onto his knees and works at the knot where the cloth is tied to the mast. The bird pulls again; the cord draws tight around the wood.

Gohan presses a hand against the knot. A small burst of ki loosens the packed sand around it. He pulls the cord free.

The cloth slips from the bird's talons. The bird climbs above them, circles once and flies toward the trees.

Goshin lowers the board, but a gust catches the loose sail. It drags across the sand and takes both boys around the end of the ridge.

The board hits a hollow. Gohan tumbles off one side and Goshin off the other. The mast comes down between them, and the sail settles over Gohan's head.

Goshin pulls it away.

"Are you all right?"

Gohan spits out a little sand. "I think I stopped it with my face."

His hat lies a few feet away, the Four-Star Ball still fastened to the front. He picks it up and shakes the sand from inside.

"Mother will want to see whether it broke."

"Did it?"

"No. But she'll want to see."

The shed is hidden behind the ridge. Beyond the boys, another beach curves toward a point of rock. The sea reaches the sand and draws back, leaving a bright strip behind it.

A growl comes from the scrub.

A sabre-toothed tiger steps out, its head low and its long teeth showing. Gohan takes a step back. Goshin moves beside him, between Gohan and the animal, and rises just above the sand.

The tiger advances. Gohan picks up the end of the fallen sail. When the wind opens it, the tiger jumps sideways and backs toward the scrub.

"It doesn't like the cloth," Gohan says.

The tiger growls again. Goshin moves the loose edge of the sail with his ki. It flaps toward the animal, which turns and bounds up the slope.

"It doesn't like that either."

Gohan gathers the cloth. "We should find Mother."

They start up the path beside the ridge. Sand shifts under Gohan's feet, and he takes a quicker step to keep his balance.

At the top, Gohan stops.

Water lies beyond the trees, beyond the dunes and past the far end of the ridge. He turns all the way around. Wherever the land stops, the sea begins.

On their mountain a path goes down through trees and comes out somewhere else. Here every path Goshin can see ends in water. He likes that for a moment. Then he looks for the shed.

"We're on an island," Gohan says.

"Bulma said we were."

"I know. I didn't know it went all the way around."

Goshin looks too. From here he can see the shed roof and the aircar on the other side of the dune. Chi-Chi stands beside the car while Bulma comes down the ladder.

"They're there."

Gohan waves, but Chi-Chi is looking toward Bulma. The boys are too far away for her to see his hand.

Below them, the two boards from their sand boat lie near a shallow inlet. The cloth has caught on a piece of driftwood.

Gohan looks at the inlet, then back at the shed.

"It would go around the point."

"What would?"

"A boat. A real one."

Goshin follows his gaze. "We could fly back."

"I know. I want to see if we can make it float first. If we need to, I can call Nimbus."

Goshin studies the boards. "We'll need the other piece."

They go down to the beach together.

## The Road Above the Clouds

Goku lands on Snake Way and opens Dr. Brief's supply capsule. He takes a bottle of water, drinks and puts the supplies away again.

The road bends out of sight in both directions.

"I lost time at that house," he says. "I'd better make it up."

He runs to the next seam, jumps and turns in the air. His boots touch down facing back the way he came. He pivots and runs forward without stopping.

At the following seam he tries it again, faster. His first step after the turn lands short. He corrects it at the next seam and keeps going.

"King Kai," he says, grinning into the wind. "I wonder what kind of training you do."

If the training is as good as the road is long, it ought to be something. Goku laughs and lifts off, flying around the bend.

## A Boat on the Water

Gohan lays the boards side by side at the edge of the inlet. Goshin brings the piece of driftwood, and together they bind it across the boards to keep them from spreading apart.

The tiger watches from the ridge. It is far enough away that Gohan can work, but each time he looks up, it is still there.

"Do you think it lives here?" he asks.

"It was here before us."

"So was the bird."

"Maybe the bird lives in the tree."

Gohan glances at the trees. "I hope it doesn't want the sail again."

He ties the cloth to a shorter piece of wood. Goshin steadies it with one hand, then lets go and holds it upright with his ki. The mast tilts. He brings it back and keeps it there while Gohan knots the cord around its base.

They push the boat into the shallows. It floats, though water comes between the two boards and wets Gohan's shoes when he steps on.

"It floats," he says.

"Your feet don't."

Gohan lifts one foot. Water runs off his sock and through the gap.

"They do when I'm on Nimbus."

"Then the boat isn't finished."

Gohan laughs and pulls a third piece of wood across the gap. It gives them a dry place to stand, as long as the boat does not tip too far.

Goshin steps on beside him. He keeps one hand near the mast while Gohan takes the cord. The sail fills, and the little boat moves out of the inlet.

Gohan looks back at the beach. "We made it."

The tiger has come down from the ridge. It stands at the water's edge, watching them.

"We're going back to Mother," Gohan tells it. "We're not taking the island."

The tiger puts one paw into the water, then pulls it out again.

The boat rounds the point. Beyond it, the shed should be visible across the next cove, but a curtain of rain is moving over the water between them and the far shore. The sea has turned dark beneath it. A narrow funnel reaches down from the cloud beyond the point; when it touches the surface, a spinning crown of spray rises around its base.

The little boards lift and fall on a swell that has reached the cove ahead of the rain.

Goshin looks up. "It wasn't there before."

The wind strikes the sail from the side. Gohan lets the cord out, but the boat turns sharply and takes a wave across the boards.

"We should go back," he says.

Goshin rises into the air. "I can lift it."

Gohan opens his mouth to call Nimbus. A snarl cuts across the water.

The tiger is on the rocks beside the point. It has followed them, but a wave has washed over the ledge behind it. As it tries to jump higher, something catches around one foreleg and pulls tight.

Gohan sees a length of old net caught beneath a branch. The tiger struggles, drawing the branch toward the water.

"It's stuck."

Goshin looks from the animal to the approaching rain. "I can hold the branch."

Gohan calls Nimbus. The yellow cloud appears above the ridge and drops beside the boat.

"I can stand on it while I loosen the net," he says. "If the tiger jumps, we can back away."

## The Storm

The first heavy drops strike the boards. Gohan climbs onto Nimbus while Goshin flies to the rocks. The tiger pulls at its leg, dragging the branch down another inch.

Goshin raises both hands. The branch stops moving. He lifts it clear of the water, keeping the net from tightening farther around the animal.

Gohan brings Nimbus close to the ledge. The tiger shows its teeth at him. He stops outside its reach and studies the net.

"It's twisted around the rock too."

He catches a loose loop and pulls. It comes away from the rock, but another loop remains beneath the tiger's paw.

The tiger jerks against it. Goshin keeps the branch up while Gohan tries to work the second loop free.

"I can't see under its foot."

A wave strikes the ledge. Gohan grips the net and leans back. The tiger scrambles higher, giving him a glimpse of the loop.

Across the cove, Chi-Chi comes out from behind the shed. She sees the little sail on the water, then the boys beside the rocks.

"Bulma! They're at the point!"

Bulma looks at the gauge. The freed arrow has swung toward the island, and the needle below it is climbing.

"The storm changed direction."

Chi-Chi is already running to the car. Bulma takes the tool case from the ladder and follows her. The aircar rises over the dune and turns toward the point.

At the rocks, Gohan slips one end of the net over a stone. The loop beneath the tiger's paw loosens.

"I nearly have it."

The wind catches Nimbus. Gohan reaches for the rock, but the next wave hits the ledge and knocks him off the cloud.

Cold water closes over his head. For a moment the rain and the voices disappear, replaced by a heavy rush in Gohan's ears.

He kicks. The net is still in his hand, tugging at his wrist. He lets it go, finds the light above him and gathers his ki to rise.

Goshin sees the place where Gohan went under. He keeps the branch suspended above the tiger without strain and dives after his brother. Gohan is already starting upward when Goshin reaches him. With one arm around Gohan, Goshin lifts him clear, still holding the branch aloft with his ki. Both boys break the surface together.

Gohan coughs against Goshin's shoulder. Nimbus darts beneath them, and Goshin sets him on it.

"I can't swim," Gohan says, pulling in a breath. "I thought I was going to stay down there."

Goshin takes his hand. "You're here now. I've got you."

Gohan grips his fingers until he can breathe without coughing. Then he wipes water from his face. The tiger is still on the ledge, its leg caught in the net. The wind pushes against the raised branch, but Goshin holds it clear of the animal and shifts it farther from the water.

"The loop is almost off," Gohan says.

Chi-Chi lands the car on the higher sand and runs toward them. Bulma follows, carrying a length of rope from her tool case.

"Gohan! Goshin!"

"We're here!" Gohan calls.

Chi-Chi reaches the rocks and sees the tiger. A wave is climbing the ledge beneath it.

"Stay together," she says.

Goshin moves closer to Nimbus without lowering the branch. Gohan points to the net beneath the tiger's paw.

"That loop has to come over the stone."

Bulma catches a loose end of the net with her rope, keeping it away from the tiger's paw. Chi-Chi pulls it toward a higher rock while Goshin raises the branch farther with his ki.

The loop slackens.

Gohan guides Nimbus alongside the ledge. The tiger bares its teeth again, but Gohan keeps the cloud beyond its reach. He stretches out his hand and sends a small, careful push of ki against the loose net.

The loop slides over the stone. The tiger lifts its paw, and the net falls away.

For a moment the animal stays where it is. Then it jumps to the higher rock, bounds past Chi-Chi and disappears into the scrub. Goshin lowers the branch onto the ledge.

A second wave strikes the point and rolls the branch toward Gohan. Chi-Chi catches it with both hands and shoves it back onto the ledge before it reaches Nimbus.

"Come back to the sand, Gohan," she says.

Gohan guides Nimbus toward the sand. Goshin brings the fallen net clear of the water and sets it well above the tide. Then he flies beside his brother to the car.

Their little boat is still beyond the point, the sail flattened against the boards. A gust takes it farther out.

Gohan watches it go.

"We can make another one," Goshin says.

"With more boards."

"And a place for your feet."

Chi-Chi reaches them. Water runs from Gohan's hair onto his collar. She wraps an arm around each boy and holds them against her for a moment.

"Are either of you hurt?"

"I swallowed some water," Gohan says. "I'm all right now."

Goshin gives her a wet, sandy grin. "The tiger got away."

She checks them both before letting go. Bulma brings the aircar closer to the sand, where they can climb in without returning to the rocks.

"I saw the sail from the shed," Chi-Chi says. "I didn't know you'd gone onto the water until I saw you by those rocks. And then Gohan went under."

Gohan looks down at his wet shoes. "I wanted to see if the boat would float. We thought we could come around the point."

"I got him out," Goshin says, taking his brother's hand.

"I saw." Chi-Chi pulls him close again, then looks at Gohan. "You frightened me half to death. If you're going out of sight, tell me first."

"I'm sorry, Mother."

She squeezes his shoulder. "Come on. Your grandfather's waiting for us."

The tiger appears briefly on the higher ridge. It shakes water from its coat and watches the car. Then it turns away among the trees.

"It got up," Gohan says.

Bulma closes the tool case. "And my gauge is working. It could have told us about that storm a little sooner."

"You had to fix it first," Goshin says.

"I know. I'm still allowed to be annoyed with it."

They climb into the aircar. Gohan takes the window seat, and Goshin settles beside him. Chi-Chi checks their straps before getting in.

## What Piccolo Felt

Piccolo and his double stop at the same moment. Beyond the rain over the wasteland, two familiar powers have risen near the sea.

The double steps back into Piccolo and disappears. Piccolo retrieves his cloak and turban, then flies toward the coast.

By the time he reaches the island, the aircar is lifting from the sand. Goshin sees him first and puts a hand against Gohan's arm. Gohan turns toward the window.

"Piccolo."

Chi-Chi brings the car down again on the clear ground beside the shed. Bulma opens her door, ready to step out, but Chi-Chi is already standing between Piccolo and the boys.

Gohan climbs out on one side of her. Goshin rises on the other, his feet just above the ground.

Piccolo looks from one brother to the other. His gaze settles on the wet net above the tide.

"I felt both of you from the wasteland. I thought the Saiyans might have come early. What are you doing here?"

"We came with Mother," Gohan says. "A tiger was caught in the net."

"You're supposed to be getting stronger. Come train with me."

"We are getting stronger," Gohan says. "We're training at the Lookout."

Piccolo's eyes narrow. "With Kami? He won't push you hard enough."

"With the others too. And I'm staying with my family."

Chi-Chi keeps her hands raised. "They chose where they want to train. They aren't going with you."

Piccolo looks at Goshin. "And you?"

"I'm training with my brother," Goshin says.

Piccolo steps toward them. Goshin holds his ground. Gohan comes forward beside him, with his wet hat in his hand.

For a moment no one moves. Rain runs down the edges of Piccolo's white cape and taps against the roof of the shed. Gohan's fingers tighten on his hat; Goshin remains beside him, level with his shoulder.

Then Piccolo turns toward the dark water.

"I'll see what you've learned."

He rises above the shed and flies back into the rain.

Chi-Chi waits until he is out of sight. Then she puts a hand on each boy's shoulder and guides them back to the car. Bulma starts the engine.

"We can tell Kami we saw him," Gohan says.

"We will," Chi-Chi says.

---

## A Pot for Everyone

Ox-King has set four bowls on the table by the time the aircar reaches the mountain house. He comes out when he hears it, filling the doorway as the boys climb down.

"There you are! I was beginning to wonder about that storm."

"It caught us at the island," Chi-Chi says. "We're safe."

Gohan runs to him with his hat under one arm. "We made a boat."

"A boat! Did it float?"

"Yes. It floated away."

Ox-King's face falls. Goshin comes up beside Gohan.

"It floated very well," he says.

Ox-King laughs and gathers both boys against him. Gohan's damp sleeve leaves a mark on his shirt. He looks at it, then at the boys.

"I've got dry towels. And porridge."

Inside, Chi-Chi sets Gohan's hat by the window, where the Four-Star Ball catches what little light remains. Bulma takes a towel and rubs her hair. Ox-King brings the pot to the table.

Goshin peers into the pot, then looks up at his grandfather.

"Is there enough for everyone?"

"If there isn't, we'll make more. Can you bring a bowl for Bulma?"

Goshin fetches another bowl from the cupboard and sets it for her. Then he puts the biggest of the four already on the table in front of Ox-King, who looks down at it and laughs.

"You know me too well."

"I was hoping you'd share," Goshin says.

At the table, Gohan draws their boat on a scrap of paper for C-6. He adds the sail, then draws a gap between the boards.

"That's where my feet went."

"You'll put another board there next time," Bulma says.

"And make the mast stronger."

"And leave the bird's tree out of the way," Goshin adds.

Gohan looks up. "The bird wasn't in its tree. It came after us."

"Then we'll have to leave the whole bird out of the way."

Gohan draws it above the sail with its wings spread. The wings reach both edges of the paper.

Ox-King fills the bowls from the pot. Goshin sits beside Gohan and eats while Chi-Chi checks the drawing.

"You can show C-6 when we visit again," she says. "And when he's finished Mina's book, we'll return it to her."

Gohan nods. "He'll want to know there wasn't a bridge."

"There was a boat," Goshin says.

"There was."

Rain taps the window. Chi-Chi looks at it, then toward the sky above the mountain.

Ox-King sets his bowl down. "Any news of Goku?"

"Not yet. Kami promised to tell us when he hears."

Gohan folds the drawing carefully. "Father said he'd have another match with me when he comes back."

"He told me he wanted to see my Kamehameha too," Goshin says.

Chi-Chi rests her hand on the table between them. "Then you'll both have something to show him."

---

## The Other End

On Snake Way, Goku lands beside a seam in the road. He looks at the bend ahead, sets his feet and starts running again.

On the mountain, Gohan places his drawing beneath the hat so the wind from the open door cannot carry it away. The Four-Star Ball holds a pale square of evening sky.

Goshin touches the corner of the paper.

"Do you think Father's road goes all the way around?"

Gohan looks at the sky with him. "We can ask when he gets home."

Chi-Chi closes the door before the rain reaches the table.
