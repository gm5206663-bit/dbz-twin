# Chapter 36 — The World That Moved

*Anime episode reference: Dragon Ball Z, Episodes 42–43 (the search, exposure and monster threat, adapted) and Episode 44 (part of the explanation and its aftermath, brought forward and changed).*

## The Answer

Raiti takes his hand off the door of his little vehicle.

"The animal fell into the marsh," he says. "I told you how dangerous it was."

"My hands went through it," Gohan says.

"You were moving very fast. Perhaps you missed."

Gohan remembers the creature's neck passing between his arms. He had been frightened for it, and then too surprised to understand what he had felt. He is certain of one thing.

"I didn't miss."

Raiti's smile returns, but only for a moment. "I'm sure the elder can explain it."

"Then tell us where to find him," Piccolo says.

"Beyond those trees."

"How far?"

"Not far."

"Which village?" Bulma asks. "What's it called?"

Raiti looks at her. "We simply call it the elder's village."

"And the elder has charts?"

"He does."

"Of the current we came through?"

"Yes."

Bulma closes her hand around the little power cell. The radar in her other hand is dark. She has not heard the beep again, but she can still remember how clearly it sounded after she removed the cell.

Raiti has an answer for everything, yet he has not named a single place she could find on a chart. She moves the radar behind her back, unwilling to let him take it from her while they are still talking.

"How did your vehicle get Dad's name?" Goshin asks.

Raiti glances toward the little craft. "I told you. It's a common name."

"Zākuro told me that. You said you didn't know it was Dad's name."

"Both can be true," Zākuro says.

Goshin looks at him. "They can. But I don't think they are."

The grass turns around the ship in its slow circle. A loose thread on Bulma's sleeve hangs straight down.

Piccolo moves a little closer to Raiti. "If the village is there, point to it. Not the trees. The village."

Raiti opens his mouth.

A roar rises behind them.

Gohan turns toward the sound but keeps his eyes below the trees. A column of pale dust is coming across the valley, its funnel rising out of his view. Branches spin around its lower edge, and an orange flash moves inside it.

"Another ball!" Raiti cries. "Quickly, before the wind carries it away!"

Goshin feels no wind on his face. He keeps his tail close to his waist and stays beside the hatch.

Bulma squints at the approaching column. "That isn't where the radar said it was."

"The storm must have picked it up!"

Gohan takes a step toward the valley, then stops. The thing in the funnel looks like the sphere he saw disappear into the creature's mouth. He also remembers reaching for that creature and finding nothing he could hold.

He picks up a small stone.

"What are you doing?" Raiti asks.

Gohan tosses it in front of him. The stone passes through the edge of the whirling dust and drops onto the ground. It does not roll.

The roar continues. The grass bends in its circle. Gohan's stone lies still.

"Piccolo," he says, "look."

Piccolo has been watching the stone. He lifts his eyes toward Raiti, not toward the top of the column.

"Gohan, stay with Bulma."

"I'm right here," Bulma says. She steps back until her shoulder touches the ship. "And nobody is going into a storm for that thing."

Raiti's expression hardens. "You wanted the balls."

"I wanted a chart. You offered me one."

"I offered to help!"

"Then start answering us."

The column rushes toward them.

Gohan throws out his arm in front of Bulma. Dust seems to cover the ground and sweep over their feet. He closes his eyes for an instant as the roar fills his ears. Nothing strikes him. When he opens them, Bulma is holding her hair away from her face, though not a strand has been blown loose.

Raiti has moved from his vehicle. He is much closer to Bulma now.

Gohan steps between them. "Don't."

Raiti stops. His gaze flicks to Zākuro.

A sharp click comes from beneath the hatch.

Goshin turns. Zākuro curls his fingers toward the narrow plate, and the access catch lifts again before he is close enough to touch it.

Goshin presses it shut with his ki without leaving Zākuro. At the same time a stone jumps from the ground near the man's foot and darts toward Bulma. Gohan catches it in his hand before it reaches her.

This stone has weight. Its rough edge bites into his palm.

Zākuro pulls his hand back to his side.

"You did that," Goshin says.

"You can't prove—"

The stone twists in Gohan's grip. He closes his fingers around it. It stops moving.

Bulma stares at Zākuro. "You tried to hit me?"

"I was trying to get your attention."

"You've got it."

The roar cuts off. For a heartbeat the air is quiet. Then a different cry comes from the trees, long and harsh, and the ground seems to rise beneath Gohan's feet.

"Down," Piccolo says.

Both boys lower their eyes, and Gohan's tail tightens around his waist. Piccolo looks over the part of the sky suddenly visible above the ridge. The clouds have shifted, but he sees no full, moonlike disc in that section. He does not tell the boys to look up.

The trees are moving toward them. Their rounded crowns open into enormous wings. A dark shape climbs above the valley, and another follows it, beating the air with leaves and feathers.

Bulma presses harder against the ship. "Those weren't birds a minute ago."

"They weren't," Piccolo says.

One swoops low, its shadow crossing the hatch. Goshin keeps his gaze on the ground. He can feel the ship beneath his hand, Gohan's familiar ki beside Bulma, and the two strangers. He cannot feel anything where the bird seems to be.

That does not mean there is nothing dangerous here. A real stone has just flown at Bulma.

"Gohan," he says, "can you feel the bird?"

"No. I can feel Raiti. He's still in front of me."

Raiti takes another step. Gohan catches his wrist.

The old man's arm is solid. Gohan holds it away from Bulma, firmly enough that Raiti cannot reach her.

"Let go!"

"Stay away from her."

Zākuro lunges for the hatch. Goshin catches his sleeve and pulls him back in a single movement. The man's feet skid across the ground. Goshin stops him before he hits the hull.

"I said nobody opens it."

Zākuro twists, trying to free his arm. Goshin keeps hold without tightening his fingers.

Behind Raiti, the ridge becomes a high wall. A tower rises from it, then another. A castle stands where the trees were. Something enormous moves through an opening in the wall.

Bulma gasps. A horned giant bends over the roof, its hand closing around a piece of stone. It hurls the stone toward them.

Piccolo steps in front of her. The boulder passes through him and the ship without a sound.

"It's not there," he says.

"I saw it hit you!"

"I know. I'm still here."

The giant reaches again. Bulma flinches, then looks at Piccolo's hand, which is resting on the hull. The metal has not bent.

Goshin sees something else.

Gohan is on the other side of the valley, caught beneath a fallen piece of the castle wall. He is calling for help. Goshin lets go of Zākuro's sleeve and starts forward.

Gohan drops the stone and catches his arm with the free hand.

"Goshin! I'm here."

Gohan stands beside him, still holding Raiti's wrist. His voice comes from close by, but the other Gohan is still pinned beneath the wall, struggling to get out.

For an instant Goshin wants to fly to both of them. He feels his brother's ki at his side, strong and familiar. He feels Gohan's fingers gripping his arm. The boy beneath the wall has Gohan's face and voice, but Goshin cannot find his presence there.

Zākuro runs toward the hatch.

Goshin pulls him back with ki and catches him again. This time he keeps his hold even when the boy beneath the wall cries out. The sound hurts to hear. Goshin turns toward his brother instead.

"I can see you over there," he says.

Gohan looks toward the wall. "I can't."

"You're under it."

"I'm not." Gohan tightens his grip. "I'm right here."

Goshin draws a breath. The image remains, but he can feel the difference more clearly now: Gohan's hand, his ki, the solid ground under both of them. He reaches past the false wall with his senses rather than following the picture. Zākuro is beside him; Raiti is a few steps away, held by Gohan. Bulma and Piccolo are at the ship.

The pressure inside Goshin rises with his fear and his anger. He lets it flow outward, not as a blast, but to hold Zākuro and the access catch in place. Even as the false cry comes again, he finds the real positions sooner.

As he steadies his hold, the strength in his body deepens with the pressure of his ki. It remains when he eases that pressure, along with his clearer sense of where everyone stands. The voice beneath the wall still sounds like Gohan, and Goshin keeps his brother's hand against his arm until the picture begins to fade.

The picture of Gohan beneath the wall flickers. The real Gohan is still at his side.

"You used our heads," Goshin says to Zākuro.

Zākuro's face changes. Not all at once. The green around his eyes shimmers, and a deeper reddish colour shows through.

Raiti sees it. He pulls against Gohan, then jerks his free arm toward Bulma.

Gohan moves with him. Before Raiti can reach her, Gohan has turned the arm behind his back and brought him down onto one knee. He does not drive him into the ground.

"Stop trying to grab her."

"Get off me!"

"Then stop."

Piccolo comes around Bulma and stands over Raiti. "What is this place?"

The giant opens its mouth. No sound comes from it. The wall behind it bends, the tower folding into the shape of a tree, then into a thin grey line.

Raiti looks at Zākuro. Zākuro looks down at Goshin's hand on his sleeve.

"We can't take it," Zākuro says.

"I can see that!"

The giant vanishes. The castle goes with it. The grass fades from the ground under their feet, leaving damp grey soil and patches of coarse, dark growth. Beyond the broken cliff lies a broad stretch of rocks, with water moving between them under a low bank of mist.

The ship is still where Goshin set it down. Bulma's hand is still against its side. The nearest pool lies perhaps a dozen paces away, separated from them by a ridge of low stones.

Raiti's little vehicle and the shed beside it are gone. Until Piccolo can check the changed sky again, both brothers keep their attention on the ground, the strangers and the ship.

Raiti and Zākuro are no longer green. Their skin is reddish pink, their faces broad and ridged, their bodies heavier than the shapes they first presented. Zākuro's sleeve is real enough to remain in Goshin's hand, though it is a different colour now.

Bulma looks from one to the other. "You're not Namekians."

"No," Raiti says.

## The Bottle

On Earth, Vegeta reaches for the water Chi-Chi left beside him.

Goku sees his hand move and rises from his crouch. Krillin is already watching. The prince takes the bottle, turns it once and twists off the cap.

"It isn't poisoned," Chi-Chi says.

"I didn't ask."

He drinks. When he has finished, he sets the bottle beside the wall rather than handing it back.

Krillin lets out a quiet breath. "There's more if you want it."

Vegeta ignores him. He tests the ground with one hand and tries to sit straighter. His damaged armour catches against the stone; he shifts, jaw tight, until his back is clear of the wall.

"Do you want help?" Goku asks.

"No."

"All right."

Goku stays close. Across the tracks, Tien is speaking to Nappa while Chiaotzu stands on the other side of the gully. A car has stopped at the top of the slope. Dr. Mori gets out with her bag, and Chi-Chi waves to her.

"Thank you for coming back," Chi-Chi calls. The doctor waves in return, then picks her way down the track toward Nappa.

The doctor approaches Tien first. He tells her where he and Chiaotzu will stand. Nappa watches the bag.

"I can look at your arm again," Dr. Mori says. "I brought clean dressings. I'd also like to listen to your breathing if you'll allow it."

"What for?"

"Your side was hurt. I want to hear how you're breathing now, and whether anything has changed."

Nappa glances at Vegeta. The prince is watching him.

"If I ask you to stop, you stop?" Nappa asks.

"Yes."

Nappa holds out his injured arm. Tien remains where he can see both the Saiyan's hands while the doctor carefully removes the dressing. Chiaotzu keeps watch from the other side.

Vegeta makes a sound of disgust.

"She's not doing it for you," Nappa says.

"I didn't ask her to."

"No. You were going to finish me off."

Goku looks from one Saiyan to the other. He has heard about that from his sons. Vegeta does not deny it.

Dr. Mori sets the old dressing aside. "This may sting," she tells Nappa. He holds still while she cleans the scrape.

Chi-Chi watches until she is satisfied that the doctor has room to work. Then she turns back to Goku.

"You need to eat too. I left rice in the bag."

"I was saving it."

"For how long?"

"Until I got hungry."

Krillin laughs. "That must have been difficult."

Goku opens the container without taking his eyes off Vegeta. Chi-Chi hands Krillin the other one, and the two of them eat beside the prince's patch of shade.

Vegeta watches Goku take a second mouthful. "You could have gone with your brats."

"I wanted to."

"But you stayed to stare at me."

"Somebody had to." Goku swallows. "You still want to hurt people here."

"You don't know what I want."

"You keep telling us."

Vegeta's fingers close against the ground. Krillin puts down his food. Goku shifts his weight forward, ready to move, and the prince notices.

For a moment Vegeta seems about to rise. Then his breath catches. He stays sitting, furious at the pain and at the two men who have seen it.

Goku does not smile.

"I still want that match someday," he says. "But I'm not letting you attack anyone while you wait for it."

"You think you'll get the chance?"

"I hope so. I want to see what you can do when you aren't hurt."

Chi-Chi gives him a long look. "And I want you to remember why you're here now."

"I do."

Dr. Mori finishes dressing Nappa's arm and asks again about his breathing. This time he allows her to listen. She warms the end of the instrument in her hand, then tells him where she will place it before she touches him.

Nappa watches her face while she works. She tells him what she can observe and what she cannot tell without further examination. Nappa refuses a needle and refuses to leave the quarry with her.

"Then I'll bring what I can here," she says. "That won't be the same as a full examination."

"I know."

Dr. Mori turns toward Vegeta from the other side of the tracks. "I can check your breathing again if you want me to."

"No."

"All right. If you change your mind about another examination, tell the people watching you."

She packs her case while Tien and Chiaotzu stay where Nappa can see them. His eyes follow the bag, then shift across the tracks to Vegeta.

Chi-Chi gathers the empty containers. Goku takes her hand before she goes.

"I'll tell you if we hear anything," he says.

"You'd better. Both boys. Not just whichever one starts talking first."

"Both boys," Goku promises.

She leaves him with Krillin. Vegeta's tail lies free against the stone. Krillin draws his food closer without taking his eyes off the prince.

## Two Stones

Bulma opens her bag.

The first orange sphere is there, smooth and bright. For a moment she can even see its five red stars. She reaches in, closes her fingers around it and lifts out a grey stone.

She drops it. It lands with a hard clack against the ground.

The second object is smaller and darker. No stars shine inside it. Gohan looks at the stone, remembering how carefully he lifted the apparent sphere from between the skull's teeth.

"The grave?" he asks.

"A rock hollow," Raiti says. "There was no grave."

"The animal in the marsh?"

"No animal."

Gohan points to the stone Zākuro sent at Bulma. It is lying where he dropped it beside the ship, separate from the two that came out of the bag.

"That one was real," he says.

"Yes," Zākuro mutters.

Bulma turns the powerless radar over. "And the sound?"

Raiti does not answer.

"I built it," she says. "I know what it should do when there's no power in it."

"We made you hear it," Zākuro says.

Bulma slips the cell into a separate pocket instead of putting it back into the radar. She wants to test the instrument herself later, when nobody is trying to give her an answer they have invented.

"How?"

He looks at Raiti, who is still on one knee beneath Gohan's hand.

"We can show people things," Raiti says. "Pictures. Sounds. We looked into your thoughts when your ship came down. We saw the place you wanted to find and the faces you expected to meet."

Piccolo's hand closes into a fist.

"You saw Namekians in our minds," he says.

"And your Dragon Balls. And the ship." Raiti looks at Bulma. "Your little machine gave you a reason to look for them."

Bulma's face goes red. "You were inside my head?"

"You were looking for a world. We gave you one."

"You lied to us."

"Yes."

Raiti says it without the friendly smile. He looks tired now, and angry at being made to answer. Zākuro has stopped trying to pull away from Goshin.

Piccolo crouches in front of Raiti. "The storms you spoke about. Did you know anything about them?"

"You did."

Piccolo thinks of Kami waiting at Yunzabit for a family that never came. He had asked for an elder because he wanted someone who might remember. Raiti had watched him ask and offered him exactly that.

For an instant Piccolo wants to shake the answer out of him. He looks at Gohan's hand holding the stranger, then rises instead.

"You never met Kami," he says.

"No."

Gohan looks up at Piccolo. "King Kai still found people, didn't he?"

"Yes." Piccolo looks across the bare rocks. "Somewhere else."

The false valley had looked beautiful from the ship. He had let himself hope before he knew what was beneath it. The hope hurts now, but Raiti's lie has not changed what King Kai sensed.

He looks at the stones Bulma has taken from the bag. They are ordinary, uneven things, and he cannot tell which hollow either of them came from. He would have carried them all the way to a stranger and asked for help from someone who did not exist.

Bulma points at the empty ground where the little vehicle stood. "You made a machine called Goku because you saw his name in our thoughts?"

Zākuro makes a face. "We thought you'd like it."

"My dad's not a machine," Goshin says.

"His car was less noisy than the real one," Bulma says. "I'll give you that."

Gohan laughs before he can stop himself. The sound surprises him. Raiti looks offended.

"It was a good vehicle."

"It wasn't a vehicle," Bulma says.

"It was a good picture of one."

Goshin loosens his grip on Zākuro's sleeve a little. "Why did you want to get into our ship?"

Neither stranger answers immediately. From somewhere beyond the rocks comes a low scraping sound.

"We came through the current too," Zākuro says at last. "Our ship broke when we landed. We can't get off this world in it."

"So you were going to steal ours," Bulma says.

Raiti lifts his chin. "A ship finally came. We weren't going to wait for another one."

"You could have asked."

"And you'd have taken us?"

Bulma thinks of the access plate moving under Goshin's hand. "I don't know what I would have said. You didn't give me the chance."

"We weren't going to kill you," Raiti says.

"You were going to leave us here," Gohan replies.

Raiti says nothing.

The scraping comes again, closer this time. A thin ripple passes across the dark water between the rocks.

Piccolo turns his head. "Something's there."

Raiti's eyes widen. He looks past Piccolo toward the shore.

"Move away from the water."

Bulma does not move. "Is this another picture?"

"No!"

A long, wet limb shoots over the nearest rock and coils around Zākuro's ankle.

## What Came Out of the Water

Zākuro cries out as the limb tightens. It pulls him off his feet and drags him toward the pool. Goshin keeps hold of his sleeve, and the two of them stop so abruptly that the fabric tears.

The thing in the water pulls again. Stones rattle down the bank.

Gohan can feel it now: a living presence close to the surface. Another stirs beneath it. He releases Raiti and flies after Zākuro, catching him around the waist before he reaches the rocks.

"Hold on!"

Zākuro clutches Gohan's shoulders. The limb is thick and slick, with a row of pale suckers pressed against his leg. Gohan slips his hands beneath it and pries it away. The grip gives under his strength. He carries Zākuro back toward the ship just above the rocks, keeping his eyes below the unchecked sky.

Another limb lashes out of the pool. Piccolo catches it before it reaches Bulma. Water sprays across his sleeve. He pushes the limb down against the rock rather than firing at the creature hidden beneath it.

"Get behind me!"

Bulma catches Raiti by the arm and pulls him toward the hull. He stumbles, staring at the water.

A broad shell rises between the rocks, followed by a dark head and a ring of reaching limbs. Its eye fixes on Gohan and the struggling Zākuro. The second animal comes up beside it, smaller but just as quick.

Goshin reaches out with his ki. He feels the weight of the first shell, the second creature moving beneath the surface, and the ship at his back. He lifts the larger animal just enough to turn its reaching limbs away from Gohan, then guides it back into deeper water.

The smaller creature darts around the first. Gohan sets Zākuro down beside Bulma and meets the next limb with both hands. He turns it aside and rises out of reach. It strikes a rock and slides back into the pool.

"Over here!" Raiti shouts.

He has picked up a loose stone. He throws it into the water well away from the ship. The smaller creature turns toward the splash. Its companion follows as Goshin releases his hold.

They disappear beneath the ripples.

Gohan lands beside Zākuro. The man is breathing hard and rubbing his ankle. His fingers come away wet.

"Are you hurt?" Gohan asks.

"I don't think it broke anything." Zākuro looks at him. "You came after me."

"It was pulling you into the water."

"After what we did?"

Gohan glances at Bulma and Piccolo. He has not forgotten the stone that Zākuro aimed at her, or the hand Raiti tried to put on her when she could not see the danger.

"I wanted you away from Bulma," he says. "That doesn't mean I wanted it to eat you."

Goshin picks up the torn piece of sleeve and holds it out. Zākuro takes it, then looks at his arm as if unsure what to do with it.

"Your sleeve was real too," Goshin says.

"Most of me is."

"Good. I was trying not to squeeze too hard."

Zākuro looks down at the small hand that had held him. "I noticed."

Piccolo watches the water until the ripples have spread out. His own sleeve is wet where he caught the limb. He turns to Raiti.

"Do those animals live here?"

"In the water. Sometimes they come closer when there's noise."

"And the cliff?" Bulma asks. "Did you make that fall?"

Raiti looks toward the broken shelf. "No. We wanted your ship, not a pile of pieces."

"You still let us land there."

"We didn't know it was going to fall."

Bulma studies his face. She cannot tell whether that answer is true. She walks to the nearest landing foot and checks the ground around it again. Goshin comes with her, keeping Zākuro in view.

"The rock under this side is solid," he says. "I can feel it."

"Good. I want to look underneath before we take off."

Raiti starts toward them. Piccolo steps into his path.

"You stay where you are."

"I was going to help."

"You've offered that before."

Raiti stops.

Gohan looks at the low mist beyond the pool. "What happened to your ship?"

"It came down behind those rocks," Zākuro says. "The bottom broke open. We pulled out what we could, but it won't fly."

"Can you show us?" Gohan asks.

"Yes."

"Gohan," Bulma says, "we don't know if he's telling the truth."

"I know. But if their ship really is broken, they'll still be here when we leave."

Goshin remembers Zākuro crouching beside his drawing, asking about the mountain near home. He had thought the man was being friendly. He had liked talking to him.

"You said you came a long way," Goshin says.

"We did."

"Were you ever going to tell me?"

Zākuro looks at the torn sleeve in his hand. "I wanted your ship."

It is not an apology. Goshin folds his arms. "You should have asked anyway."

Zākuro does not look at him. Goshin wishes the man would say he is sorry, though he is not sure whether he would believe him if he did. The memory of their conversation remains his own, even if the grass they sat on was never there.

Raiti looks between them. "There's a current above this place. Most of the time it goes around the whole world. Once in a while there's a gap. The mist comes in when it moves."

Bulma looks toward the bank of mist, then back at him. "You expect me to believe that because you said it?"

"No. Look at your instruments. We watched the current for a long time."

"Can you show me how you watched it?"

"Some of the equipment is near the wreck."

"If it's really there, I may be able to make sense of it," Bulma says. "But you don't get to pick what I see for me again. Stay out of our thoughts."

Raiti's mouth tightens. "All right."

She does not know how to test that promise. She does know she will not leave him alone with the ship while she finds out.

Piccolo folds his arms. "If it exists."

"It exists," Zākuro says. "So do we, in case you hadn't noticed."

"We've noticed," Bulma says.

Gohan turns to her. "Could we look?"

"I want to check our ship first." She glances at the strangers. "And nobody is getting aboard it while I'm doing that."

"I'll stay with you," Goshin says.

"I'll stay too," Piccolo says.

Gohan looks at Raiti and Zākuro. "Then you can show me where it is from here. We don't have to go near the water."

Raiti opens his mouth to argue, then sees Piccolo looking at him.

"From here," he agrees.

They walk a few steps along the dry ground, with Gohan between the strangers and the ship. Zākuro points past a line of black rocks to something long and broken in the mist. Gohan can make out a metal edge, but not enough to tell what it belongs to.

Bulma watches them while she takes a light from her case. She has not promised anyone a ride, and she is not leaving two strangers beside her hatch again. But if there is a wreck, she wants to see it for herself. There may be a way to repair it, or the equipment may tell her something about the current that brought them here.

Neither possibility is certain. First she needs to know whether the ship carrying her friends can lift off without shaking a damaged landing foot loose.

Goshin crouches at the landing foot. The finer sense he found while holding the falling ship is still there. He reaches under the curved hull and follows the weight of it to the ground, checking each foot while Bulma shines her light beneath the rim.

"This one too," he tells her.

She moves the light. "I see it. Keep going."

Goshin smiles at the work, even with the damp ground under his knees. The ship is here. Gohan is nearby. There are answers still to find, and he can help with this part.

## A Distant Recording

In a room far from Earth, a small light blinks on a console.

A soldier touches the screen and sends a saved transmission to his own scouter. A recording breaks through a wash of static: a man's voice, sharp and impatient, speaking to someone he calls Vegeta.

"He's a Namekian, from Planet Namek."

The soldier lets the fragment run. Another voice asks, "You made the Dragon Balls, didn't you?"

"I didn't," a third voice answers. The recording ends in a burst of noise.

"That's all of it?" Cui asks. His skin is purple, and his battle armour has raised shoulder guards. A green lens covers one eye.

"That's the part we recovered from the Earth transmission, sir."

The soldier removes his own scouter and passes it to Cui, who listens again. Vegeta's name is attached to the signal, though the device has sent no clear report of what happened afterward. Cui has been waiting for news of the prince. This is not the news he expected.

"Find the older record," he says. "The one from Raditz."

The soldier finds it on the console and sends it to the scouter Cui is holding. A different voice speaks of objects on Earth that can grant a wish, even bring back someone who has died. Cui listens without interrupting. When the recording ends, he takes the scouter and walks out.

Freeza is waiting in a bright chamber, seated in his hovering chair. His pale face is framed by dark horns and a smooth violet crown; his small body rests easily against the chair's curved back. Two attendants stand behind him. Cui bows and offers the device.

"A report from the Saiyans' journey to Earth, Lord Freeza."

"At last." Freeza's tail curls around the side of the chair. "Has Vegeta found what he went looking for?"

"We don't know. His scouter sent this before the signal was lost."

Freeza listens to the two fragments. At the name of the planet, his eyes narrow slightly. He listens to the older account a second time.

"A wish," he says. "How interesting."

"Vegeta seems to think the Namekian made the objects on Earth. There may be others on his home world."

"There may be." Freeza rests one hand on the arm of his chair. "And Vegeta has neglected to tell me about them."

Cui smiles. "It would seem so."

"Don't be too pleased. We still don't know whether this story is true." Freeza returns the scouter. "Find Planet Namek. I want a course, and I want to know what has become of Vegeta."

"Yes, Lord Freeza."

Cui leaves with the recording. Freeza turns toward the dark window beyond his attendants.

If there is a thing in the universe that can grant a wish, he intends to see it before Vegeta does.
