# Chapter 33 — What the Mirror Hides

*Anime episode reference: Dragon Ball Z, Episode 39 (the voyage and encounter, adapted for the living-character branch).*

## The Seventh Day

On the seventh day after leaving Earth, Bulma opens a cupboard and finds a sock inside a saucepan.

She holds it up by two fingers. "Whose is this?"

Goshin looks over from his seat. "Does it have a hole in the heel?"

"It does."

"Mine, then."

"Why is it in my saucepan?"

"I was looking for it."

Gohan lowers his book. "You put it there while you were looking for it?"

"I found it first, and then I couldn't find the other one."

Bulma stares at him. Piccolo, sitting with his back to the curved wall, opens one eye.

"Did you find the other one?" he asks.

Goshin lifts a bare foot. "I'm still looking."

"Well, you can look while you clean." Bulma drops the sock into his lap. "All three of you. This place is a mess."

Gohan looks at the open tool case, the papers spread beside it and the empty cups near Bulma's seat. "Most of that's yours."

"I'm working."

"You said you were bored," Goshin reminds her.

"I can be bored and busy. It takes talent."

She gives him a cloth and points him toward the saucepan. Goshin looks inside it, then at his brother.

"I'll wash it," Gohan says, "if you find the other sock before she asks me to cook in it."

The ship has no morning sun through the covered window. Bulma keeps the days on her watch and the supplies in careful rows, though the rows have a way of becoming shorter whenever she turns her back. They have enough food, she says, provided the boys stop treating every interruption as a reason for another meal.

"It was only a little meal," Goshin says.

"You ate three." Bulma checks a mark in her notebook. "And somebody drank the juice I was saving."

Piccolo lifts his cup of water. Gohan looks at Goshin. Goshin looks at the cupboard.

"It might be with my sock."

When the saucepan is clean and Bulma has rescued her papers, the boys settle on the floor again. Piccolo sits opposite them. The little room has space to stretch and to move, but not for a contest like the one over the quarry. They have found other ways to practise.

"You keep watching the hand you expect to strike," Piccolo tells Gohan. "What happens if the other one comes first?"

Gohan closes his eyes.

In the fight he imagines, the quarry ridge lies beneath him. His father moves across the sky, reaching for his shoulder. Gohan turns under the familiar hand, ready to come around behind him, as he did in their match.

This time the imagined Goku stops short. His other hand comes up.

Gohan checks his turn. Instead of continuing around, he drops toward the ridge and sends a burst of ki past his father's side. Goku follows it; Gohan changes direction again and comes up on the other side. His fingers reach the shoulder of the orange shirt.

Gohan opens his eyes and smiles. "I don't have to finish the turn just because I started it."

"That's better," Piccolo says. "Now picture him noticing the burst."

"He might."

"He will if you do it the same way twice."

Goshin grins. "Dad would make us do it again until we couldn't stop laughing."

"Then picture that too," Piccolo says.

Goshin closes his eyes. In his own imagined match, red light gathers around Goku, and the air breaks as his father comes forward. Goshin remembers the pressure of Goku's hands when they met above the ridge. He turns to follow the movement, gathers his strength and—

Something taps him on the forehead.

His eyes fly open. Bulma stands over him with a rolled-up sheet of paper.

"I got you," she says.

"You weren't in the match!"

"That's why it worked. How long are you going to sit there?"

"Until I catch you." Goshin reaches for the paper. Bulma whisks it behind her back, but he has already taken the other end. With his father's face and black spiked hair, he looks so much like a little Goku that she nearly laughs. Then he looks up at her, and his eyes are Chi-Chi's.

"No fighting in the cabin," she says.

"You started it."

"And I'm stopping it. Give me my notes."

He lets go. Bulma smooths the page against her knee and tries to look stern, but Gohan is laughing and even Piccolo's mouth has moved.

Later, while Bulma checks the air monitors, Goshin sets a folded blanket on the floor and stands in the middle of it. Gohan watches from the bed. They have practised their breathing, their balance and the movement of ki each day, taking care with the small space and the people sharing it.

Goshin brings his hands together. He remembers his father's voice: gather the strength first, then let it rise. He draws on only a small part of his ki for the cabin.

"Kaiō-ken, times two."

The power he was using doubles, and the red light comes close around him. It brightens his hair and the edges of the blanket without touching the walls.

He holds it for a moment, feeling the quickened force in his arms and legs, then lets it go. The blanket settles beneath his feet.

"You did it again," Gohan says.

"I wanted to see if I could make it come without rushing."

"Did you?"

"I think so." Goshin looks at his hands. "It felt steadier than it did at the quarry."

Piccolo has risen from the wall. He reaches out, and Goshin presses his palm against Piccolo's. Both are careful with their strength. Piccolo feels the boy's ki after the red has disappeared, and his expression changes.

"You're stronger than when we left," he says.

Goshin pushes a little harder. Piccolo gives ground, and Goshin releases him at once so that he does not strike the equipment behind him.

"I am!" Goshin says. "Gohan, feel it."

Gohan jumps down and meets his hand. The strength is there without the red light, deeper than it was when the ship first climbed away from Yunzabit. Goshin feels it in his body too, in the easy way his arm answers him.

The nature he inherited from his Saiyan father and Earthling mother is growing with him, its qualities deepening as his life and his Talent shape one another. Goshin curls his fingers and grins at his brother.

"We'll have to show Dad," he says.

"Both of us," Gohan replies. He steps onto the blanket when Goshin moves aside. "I want to practise the first part Father showed us too."

He settles his feet and draws his ki close. The edge of the blanket lifts, then falls as he steadies it. Goshin watches his brother's hands.

"You caught it before it spread," he says.

Gohan tries again, smiling at the little movement of cloth. "I did."

Then he turns to Piccolo. "Will you watch my turn again? I want to try it without sending the burst first."

"Show me."

Bulma looks up from her notebook. "If you two start flying around in here, I'm charging rent."

Gohan stays on the floor, but his feet shift as he thinks through the movement. Goshin copies the turn with him, and both brothers stop before they reach the equipment.

"You'd have to catch us first," Goshin says.

Bulma picks up the rolled paper again. Both boys duck.

## The View from Home

Chi-Chi hangs two small shirts on the line and steps back when the wind catches them. They are spares the boys left behind. One sleeve flaps against the other, and for a moment she can almost hear them arguing about whose turn it is to bring in the washing.

The sky is clear above the mountains. She looks up anyway.

At Yunzabit, Gohan had stood beside her in the round ship and said, "I want to go with him, Mother." Goshin had wanted to go too, with his brother and Piccolo and Bulma. She had listened to their questions, asked her own and finally told them yes.

She does not wish she had given them a different answer. She wishes the sky were small enough for her to see where they are.

"They'll have eaten all the food by now."

Chi-Chi turns. Master Roshi stands at the gate, leaning on his staff with a covered basket in his other hand.

"Bulma packed plenty," she says.

"Then they'll have eaten most of it."

"That's more likely. What have you brought?"

"Something for Goku and Krillin. I thought I'd see how the watch was going."

Chi-Chi lifts the lid. "These are sweets."

"They need their strength."

"They need lunch. Come inside. You can help me pack it."

Roshi follows her, protecting the basket from the edge of the door. By the time they leave for the quarry, he is carrying two more containers and has been told not to put the sweets on top of the warm rice.

Goku is doing push-ups on a flat stretch of stone, facing Vegeta. Krillin stands nearby with his attention on the prince. Across the old tracks, Tien and Chiaotzu remain with Nappa, who has moved himself into the shade of the gully wall.

"Ninety-eight," Goku says. "Ninety-nine—"

Vegeta's hand shifts against the ground.

Goku is on his feet before the count is finished. Krillin steps to the other side of the prince, leaving Goku a clear line between Vegeta and the arriving visitors. Vegeta lifts his hand a little farther, then lets a pebble fall from it.

"You're easily distracted, Kakarot."

"You moved," Goku says.

"I noticed."

Vegeta's tail remains curled beside him beneath the loose cover. Krillin stays close as Goku looks toward Chi-Chi.

"You brought Roshi!"

"He brought dessert," Chi-Chi says. "I brought lunch."

"I knew I liked both of you."

Roshi sets down his containers. "I was counting for you from the road. You missed one."

"I did not!"

"You stopped at ninety-nine."

"Vegeta moved."

"A sound reason. But it's still ninety-nine."

Goku laughs. "All right. I'll start again after I eat."

Chi-Chi hands a container to Krillin and calls to Tien across the tracks. Chiaotzu comes partway to meet her, keeping Nappa in sight while Tien stays beside him. There is water in the bag as well. Nappa accepts a bottle and twists off the cap himself.

"The doctor said she could come back," Tien tells him. "You can tell her what you want checked."

Nappa drinks before answering. "I don't want a crowd."

"I'll tell her."

Vegeta watches the exchange with a tight expression. Goku offers him food; the prince refuses it and turns his face away.

Roshi lowers himself onto a stone. "Any word from the ship?"

Chi-Chi shakes her head. She has listened for the telephone even when she knows it is not sensible to expect a call. Bulma promised to tell her what she could, not that a message would find its way home from anywhere in space.

Goku looks up. "I keep thinking about what they'll see. Piccolo's going to have a lot of questions."

"So are the boys," Chi-Chi says.

"And Bulma," Krillin adds. "Mostly about why they ate all her food."

Chi-Chi smiles, then folds the cloth from the empty container more neatly than it needs. "I hope they ask someone how to come back."

Goku's grin softens. "They will. And when they get here, I'm going to see what they've learned."

"After they've eaten and slept," Chi-Chi says.

"I can wait that long."

Roshi raises an eyebrow. "Can you?"

Goku considers it. "If they sleep quickly."

Chi-Chi swats his shoulder with the folded cloth. He catches it, laughing, and then turns back toward Vegeta as the prince shifts again. Krillin has not moved away from his post.

Far beyond the sky Chi-Chi can see, the travellers continue toward a world none of them has yet found.

## Another Ship

"If the Saiyan planet is gone," Bulma says, "where did the two of them get their ships?"

She sits beside the controls with her notebook open. Gohan and Goshin are fastening a supply box beneath a bed, and Piccolo is examining the page on which Popo wrote the return words.

"Raditz said they took planets and sold them," Gohan replies. "Maybe the people who bought them had ships."

"He said they killed the people living there first," Goshin adds. The buckle clicks under his hand. "He wanted Dad to help."

Bulma's pen stops. "So there could be other people out here working with Saiyans."

"Or people like them," Piccolo says. "We don't know."

Gohan thinks of Vegeta at the quarry, injured and still watching them. "Father and Krillin were with Vegeta when we left. They'll stop him if he tries to hurt anyone."

"And Tien and Chiaotzu were with Nappa," Goshin says.

"I know." Bulma closes the notebook. "I just don't like how much we don't know about the rest of space."

The panel gives a sharp tone.

Bulma leans forward. A light flashes across one of the instruments, followed by a line that moves toward the mark representing their ship.

"That's not a star," she says.

Goshin starts toward the window, then stops himself. "Can you see what it is?"

"Not yet. Turn around, both of you."

The brothers face the wall. Piccolo stands beside Bulma while she releases the catch and draws back part of the solid cover. He checks the unfamiliar view with her, keeping himself between the opening and the boys.

A brief glint passes ahead of the ship. Beyond it are scattered points of light. Nothing about the glint tells Bulma whether it is a moon, another craft or something else entirely.

"Anything?" Gohan asks.

"Something moved," Piccolo says. "Keep looking away."

The warning sounds again. A cluster of small round shapes sweeps into Bulma's view, their dark undersides lit by the glare of their own weapons.

"Ships!" she cries.

The first shot strikes their hull. The floor jumps. Gohan grabs the edge of a bed, and Goshin catches the supply box before it slides into the aisle.

"Everybody in a seat!" Bulma reaches for Popo's recorder. "Piccolo, close that cover."

The solid panel snaps shut. Bulma plays a recorded command, and their ship turns. Another shot follows; the air monitor flickers but continues to show a steady reading.

"Are they shooting at us?" Gohan asks.

"Yes!" Bulma scrolls through the labels on the recorder. "Popo, please have given me the right one."

She finds the entry for defense and plays it. The ship answers with a tone. A low pulse runs through the floor, and on the instrument panel two approaching marks break apart.

"It has weapons?" Goshin says.

"Apparently it does. I'd have preferred to find out another way!"

Piccolo watches the panel. "I can feel people ahead. More than one."

Gohan grips the arms of his seat. "Are they inside those little ships?"

"I can't tell."

Another impact rocks the cabin. Bulma searches for a way between the incoming marks rather than firing again. "Hang on."

She plays the course command, then pulls the cover aside just enough for herself and Piccolo to see. Ahead, a round craft rushes toward them.

It has the same curved body, the same legs and the same window as their own ship.

"That's ours," Bulma says.

"We haven't turned around," Piccolo replies.

"Then why is it coming straight at us?"

She sends their ship to the left. The other craft turns in precisely the same direction. Bulma changes course again; it follows. Its window flashes, and the reflected light spreads across something much larger behind it.

"A mirror," Piccolo says.

Bulma sees it then: the image of their ship bending across a vast curved surface. The stars bend too. A seam opens in the shining hull, and the image splits.

"That thing's enormous!"

She orders their ship back. The engines hum, but the other vessel continues to fill the view. A broad ring around the opening shines, and their cabin shudders as an unseen pull takes hold.

Goshin gets out of his seat. "Can I push it away?"

"Not through our wall," Bulma says. "We don't know what's outside, and I don't want a hole in this ship."

"There's a whole ship full of people over there too," Gohan says.

Goshin plants his feet. He could strike with far more force than the little cabin could bear. He keeps his hands at his sides while Bulma tries another command.

For a moment their ship checks its movement. Then the ring brightens and draws them through the opening. The reflected stars vanish. The cabin lurches, the warning sounds once more, and the ship settles with a heavy clang.

Silence follows, except for the hum of the air system.

Bulma checks the two monitors. Both still agree. "We're breathing. The ship hasn't told us anything else useful."

"Can it go back?" Gohan asks.

"I'll try."

She plays the return command. The engines answer, but the craft does not rise. Through the partially opened cover, Piccolo sees metal arms locked around its supports. Beyond them stretches an immense enclosed bay.

"We're being held down," he says.

Bulma shuts the cover. "And I can't see the door we came through from here."

"I could break the arms," Goshin offers.

"I know you could. But if the outside door is shut, we'd still be inside their ship." She listens to the recorder's quiet hiss and switches it off. "I'd rather find the controls than fly through a wall with people on the other side."

Piccolo folds his arms. "We'll find who's here and ask them to open it."

"Ask them after they shot at us?" Bulma says.

"You have a better idea?"

She looks at the locked supports. "I'll bring my tools."

## Inside the Mirror

Bulma tests the air through a narrow opening at their door before she steps out. The portable meter gives a reading close to the one inside their cabin. It does not tell her everything about the air, but it gives them a reason to try. Piccolo checks the bay through the gap first: solid walls, a high ceiling and no open view of the sky from the doorway.

"Stay close," he says.

The platform lowers. Gohan and Goshin step down on either side of Bulma, and Piccolo follows. Their ship looks much smaller in the middle of the vast bay. Metal arms grip its supports, and a heavy door is set into the wall beyond them.

Goshin puts a hand on one of the arms and bends it away from the nearest support. The metal folds under his fingers. The ship stays where it is, held by the others.

"See?" he says.

"I saw," Bulma replies. "Please don't bend the ship along with it."

"I wasn't going to."

"Good. Let me find out what the rest of them are attached to."

She walks along a thick cable to a panel near the wall. Its symbols are unfamiliar. Bulma opens the casing with a tool, studies the rows of connections and closes it again.

"I don't know what that will release. If I pull the wrong one, it might do something to the air or the outer door."

Piccolo turns toward the heavy door. "The people I sensed are farther in."

"Do they know we're here?" Gohan asks.

A light above the door changes from red to green. The door slides open.

Bulma looks at it. "I'd say so."

"Maybe they want to talk," Gohan says.

"Maybe," Piccolo replies. He goes first, without assuming it. Gohan follows beside Bulma, and Goshin brings up the rear, looking back at their ship until the doorway closes behind him.

The corridor beyond is narrow and brightly lit. Their footsteps ring on the floor. Bulma tries to keep track of the turns, marking each one in her notebook.

A click comes from the wall.

Piccolo's hand flashes out. He catches Bulma around the waist and pulls her aside as a barrel emerges from the panel where she had been standing. Other barrels push out along both walls.

"Down!" Gohan calls.

The guns fire. Gohan moves in front of Bulma and knocks one shot upward with his palm. Goshin crosses the corridor and turns two barrels into the ceiling before they can fire a second time. Piccolo tears another from its mounting and sets it against the wall, still hot.

The entire volley has scarcely begun before it ends. Light fades from the damaged panels. Goshin glances at Bulma.

"Are you all right?"

"I'm fine." She picks up the tool case she dropped. "Whoever built this place could have put up a sign."

"They did," Gohan says, pointing at a row of symbols above the nearest gun.

"A sign I can read."

Piccolo listens. Beyond the walls the life he sensed is moving. He cannot see the people yet, but they are still there.

"They know what they're doing," he says. "Watch the walls."

"And the floor," Bulma adds. "After that, I'm watching everything."

They come to another door. It opens as they approach, revealing a square room with a second door on the far side. Bulma takes two steps inside and stops.

"Piccolo. Look at the hinges."

The first door slams behind them. The far one closes a moment later.

A pipe opens above their heads, and liquid pours from it in a shining sheet. A drop lands on a scrap of packing paper beside Bulma's boot. The paper curls and smokes.

Gohan catches Bulma and lifts her clear of the spreading pool. Goshin throws out his hands. His ki catches the falling stream and bends it away from them while Piccolo drives his fingers into the door they entered through.

The metal shrieks. Piccolo pulls the door out of its frame, keeping the blow away from the outer hull. Gohan carries Bulma through the opening; Goshin follows, holding the stream aside until he is clear. The liquid splashes against the empty floor of the room behind them.

Bulma looks back. "They weren't planning to ask us questions."

"We can get back to our ship," Goshin says. "I can open the doors."

"We can," Piccolo agrees. "But unless we know how this place is arranged, breaking its outer wall could put the people inside in danger."

Gohan looks from the smoking room to the corridor. "They might be afraid of us. They attacked before they could see who we were."

"They can see us now," Bulma says. "They've still got awful manners."

"Let's find them," Gohan says. "If they'll listen, we can ask them to let us go."

Piccolo studies him, then nods toward the corridor. "Stay together."

## A Door Beneath Their Feet

The passage seems to curve back on itself. Bulma consults her marks, frowns and turns the page around.

"We've passed this joint before."

Goshin looks up. "There's a door on the ceiling."

Piccolo rises and puts a hand against it. He pulls; nothing moves. Before he tries again, a panel slides aside in the wall.

"That one opened for us," Gohan says.

"Which makes me suspicious of it," Bulma replies.

Piccolo drops back to the floor. "The ceiling panel isn't a door. It's fixed to the frame."

"A fake door?" Goshin asks.

"A very convincing one," Bulma says. "It fooled him for almost a whole second."

Piccolo gives her a look. She smiles and steps carefully toward the opening, keeping her tool case in front of her.

The next chamber is bare. The door closes behind the four of them with a deep, solid sound. Gohan turns toward it.

"That didn't sound like the other one."

A seam opens across the floor.

Bulma's feet slide apart as two metal plates drop away beneath her. Beyond the widening gap, black space and distant points of light appear.

Gohan does not look down. He catches Bulma under her arms and flies straight up, bringing her against the ceiling. Air rushes toward the opening, pulling her loose papers from the notebook.

"The door's sealed!" Piccolo calls. "Don't open it while that hatch is open."

Goshin keeps his eyes on the edge of the floor rather than the unknown sky. He drops to one knee, grips a hatch plate and drives it inward. The motor strains against him. He catches the opposite plate with his ki and pulls it toward the first.

The two halves meet. Their seals press together, and the roar of escaping air stops. Goshin holds them shut while Piccolo wrenches the hatch motor out of its mounting.

"Now," Piccolo says.

Gohan lowers Bulma beside the wall. Her hands are clenched around his sleeves.

"You can let go," he says.

"I know." She releases him one hand at a time. "I was thinking about it."

Goshin tests the hatch. It stays closed. Only then does Piccolo open the door into the adjoining passage.

The four of them move through, and Piccolo pulls the door shut behind them. Bulma takes a breath, then another. Gohan stays beside her without crowding her against the wall.

"Thank you," she says.

Gohan's cheeks warm. "You're welcome."

Goshin lets out the breath he was holding. "I don't like their floor."

"Neither do I," Bulma says. "Put that in your notebook, Gohan. When we get home, I'm telling everyone."

"I left it on our ship."

"Then remember it for later."

"What should I call them?"

"The people who tried to throw me into space."

Goshin looks down the passage. "It's a long name."

"They've earned it."

They continue, alert for another moving panel. Piccolo can sense life ahead and to either side now. Whoever is on this ship has not left. Gohan wonders whether the people behind the walls are watching every door they open.

Then a different smell reaches them.

Goshin stops. "Food."

"I smell it too," Gohan says.

Bulma tightens her grip on the tool case. "After the guns and the floor, do either of you think someone's left us a nice lunch?"

"No," Goshin says. His stomach makes a noise. "But it smells like one."

A door opens at the end of the passage. Beyond it is a table laid with plates, fruit and covered dishes. Steam curls from a bowl in the middle. Chairs stand neatly around the table, as though the people who set it have only just stepped away.

Gohan looks toward Piccolo. "The people you felt—are they in there?"

"Close. Behind the walls, too."

Bulma peers through the doorway. "There might be controls in that room. And if there are people on the other side, perhaps they'll finally speak to us. Nobody touches the food."

"I wasn't going to," Goshin says.

His stomach makes the noise again. Gohan laughs despite himself.

"I heard that," Goshin tells it.

## The People Behind the Walls

Bulma enters first, looking at the walls rather than the dishes. The others spread out just enough to see around the table. Gohan notices a row of small holes near the ceiling, each pointed toward a different part of the room.

"Those could be more guns," he says.

Piccolo moves beside the doorway. "Don't stand under them."

Bulma reaches the far end of the table and spots a panel behind a hanging cloth. She lifts the cloth with the handle of her tool. There are buttons beneath it, but no labels she understands.

"I was hoping for a picture of the outer door," she says. "Or a very large button that says 'let us out.'"

A knife leaps from the table.

Gohan sees it turn toward Bulma. He catches it by the handle before it reaches her and stops beside her chair. The blade quivers in his hand.

"Bulma, move away from the table."

A small voice sounds from somewhere above them. "He caught it!"

A switch clicks.

A transparent dome drops over Bulma. Goshin is there before it reaches the tabletop. He catches the rim in one hand, holding it high enough for her to duck out. At the same instant a mechanical arm shoots down from the ceiling. Piccolo seizes its wrist and bends the claw away from her.

"Out," he tells Bulma.

"I'm out!"

She scrambles clear. Goshin lifts the dome completely off the table and sets it on its side. The dishes rattle but do not fall.

Panels open around the room. Figures rise from beneath the floor, each holding a gun. One stands on a ledge above the table; two more appear beside the far wall. Their clothes are loose and their faces are pale with excitement and fear.

Gohan stares. The one on the ledge cannot be much older than some of the children who visited their house. A smaller girl clutches a weapon almost as long as her arm.

"They're kids," he says.

"Don't move!" the boy on the ledge shouts. His hands shake around his gun. "All of you, stay where you are!"

"We were staying where we were," Bulma says. "Your machines kept trying to kill us!"

The smaller girl jerks at Bulma's raised voice. Her finger tightens. A shot flashes from her gun toward the table, where Bulma is still standing.

Gohan is already between them. The shot strikes his open palm and goes out in a burst of light. He lowers his hand; there is no injury. The girl stares at him, then at the gun.

Before anyone can fire again, Goshin reaches out with his ki. Six weapons lift out of their holders' hands and turn away from the people below. He keeps them suspended near the ceiling, their muzzles pointing at the blank wall. Piccolo still has the mechanical claw folded safely in his grip.

"Hey!" the boy on the ledge cries. "Give those back!"

"Not while you're pointing them at my friends," Goshin says.

Another child darts from a side panel and reaches for a weapon on the floor. Gohan steps across his path. He does not strike; he picks up the gun first and offers the child his empty hand instead.

"Please stop. Nobody has to get hurt."

The child backs away. Gohan leaves him room to move.

Bulma has both hands on her hips now, though she keeps an eye on every opening. "You could have started by asking who we were!"

"You could have stayed out of our ship!" the boy on the ledge shouts back.

"You pulled us into it! Were there people in those little ships you sent after us?"

"They're machines. You broke two of them!"

"They were shooting at us!"

He opens his mouth, then glances at the other children. One of them is rubbing the fingers from which Goshin lifted the gun. The little girl has begun to cry without making a sound.

Goshin sees her face, and the walls around her bring back another small room. He had woken in Raditz's pod with his head hurting and Gohan holding his hand. The hatch was shut, its handle bent from Gohan's attempts to open it.

"Move back," he had told his brother. His burst snapped the lock, and the hatch flew outward into the daylight. But Raditz was still outside. A broken door had not made the stranger disappear.

Now the girl is looking at Piccolo's hand around the metal claw, at Gohan beside the fallen gun, and at Goshin's own raised hands. He lowers the weapons farther from the children and holds them still. His ki answers him readily; keeping six separate guns in place while watching the moving panels feels steadier than it would have at the beginning of the voyage. The greater strength beneath that control is still his ordinary strength, not the red burst he practised earlier.

"We came from Earth," Gohan says to the boy on the ledge. "Our ship was going somewhere else."

"Where?"

"To Namek."

The boy's face changes, though Gohan cannot tell what he is thinking. Another child presses close to his side and whispers in his ear.

"We want our ship back," Bulma says. "And we'd like the big door open."

"This is our ship," the boy says. He takes a breath and tries to sound older than he looks. "You can't tell us what to do."

Piccolo releases the bent claw. It hangs harmlessly from the ceiling. "Then tell us why you brought us aboard."

For a moment the only answer is the hum of machinery. Somewhere behind the wall, more feet hurry past.

The boy looks at Piccolo, then at Gohan and Goshin. His gaze catches on the tails behind them. He grips the edge of the ledge hard enough to whiten his knuckles.

"Are you soldiers?" he asks.

"No," Goshin says. "I'm Goshin. This is my brother, Gohan."

Gohan takes a step forward. The children on the far side of the room flinch, so he stops.

"We're not here to take your ship," he says. "We didn't even know it was here."

The boy looks toward the little girl. She wipes her face with her sleeve but does not look away from the travellers.

"Then why can you do things like them?" he asks.

"We learned to fly and fight," Gohan says. "That doesn't make us soldiers."

The little girl takes hold of the boy's sleeve. He looks down at her, then back at the two brothers.

Goshin remembers the closed hatch of Raditz's pod, and how badly he had wanted the stranger outside it to go away. He keeps the guns well above the children.

"We don't want to hurt you," he says. "Who are they? The people you're afraid of?"

The boy opens his mouth. A child behind him shakes her head, and he stops.

Above them, the six guns hang motionless. The outer door remains closed, and the people who control it are watching.
