# Kamehameha: variations, sources and *The Twin*

**Purpose:** A practical study of the many ways the Kamehameha is used, what each cited source actually supports, and what Goku, Goshin and Gohan have demonstrated in the selected story through Chapter 43. The selected revisions and Chapter 43 remain unaccepted; the separately preserved original Chapter 20 remains accepted. This is a source-bounded working guide, not a claim to list every move in every game, dub or adaptation.

## The short answer

The Kamehameha is not just one rigid pose followed by one straight beam. Its users have applied the same technique to attack, aim around a defence, move themselves, misdirect an opponent and respond to another wave. Some names describe **a different execution**, some describe **the same attack used with another ability or another person**, and others are **a game's names for a particular animation**. A later source example does not automatically become a present skill in this branch. Equally, the absence of a particular later name is not a reason to erase something these characters have already shown.

Master Roshi/Kamesennin created the technique. The [official Roshi profile](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_141.html) describes concentrating latent ki and releasing it from the hands; the [early-Goku compendium](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_140.html) says Goku learned it by watching Roshi once. This is a general explanation, not a rule that every use must have two functioning hands, a long chant, the same output, or an identical effect on the surroundings.

**Source hierarchy and limits.** Dragon Ball Official's manga-based [technique compendium](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_480.html) identifies several distinct applications. Its separate [manga counting survey](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_659.html) is an editorial investigation, **explicitly not an officially recognized statement of fact**: it applies the editors' own criteria to the Japanese original manga, not to all anime, movies and games. The 67 uses are their count under those criteria, not a franchise-wide total. Toei's episode summaries describe the original Z anime, but are short reports rather than complete footage. The local Japanese caption archive is directly readable, but its original release/cut are unverified and captions do not show silent movement. Later-series profiles, movie commentary and game labels are identified separately below. This study did not watch complete episode or movie video or hear original audio.

## Forms and applications grounded in the original manga or original Z

| Form or useful description | What the evidence shows | What it does **not** prove |
|---|---|---|
| **Ordinary Kamehameha** | Roshi concentrates ki for the Kame School attack; young Goku copies him after one demonstration. Krillin, Yamcha, Tenshinhan and Grandpa Gohan also appear in the original-manga [editorial survey](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_659.html). | A blue visual, identical words or the same fixed multiplier in every source and scene. A single act of learning is not equal mastery. |
| **Roshi's maximum-power use** | The survey identifies Jackie Chun's enormous wave that destroys the moon; the [Jackie Chun profile](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_1502.html) separately describes his full-power clash with Goku and the maximum-power shot at the moon. | That every Kamehameha is moon-destroying, that all users need that form, or a reproducible numerical multiplier. |
| **Bending Kamehameha** | The [official manga-based compendium](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_480.html) explicitly calls the technique *Bending Kamehameha*: Goku changes its trajectory to get around Great Demon King Piccolo in Tale 157. This is an established source application **before** the Raditz story. | That complete original-Z Episode 4 footage has been checked. Two secondary episode accounts report bending in that later anime fight (`C165`); the local caption alone cannot confirm the visible path. Nor does Goku's source feat automatically teach every other user. |
| **Propulsion by hand** | The [survey](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_659.html) identifies Jackie Chun using a wave to avoid falling out of bounds (Tale 43) and Goku using waves for midair manoeuvres. | That a character must always use it for travel, or that normal flight and propulsion are the same technique. Recoil, position and surroundings still matter in a scene. |
| **Foot Kamehameha** | The [official compendium](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_480.html) identifies Goku firing from his feet to propel himself while keeping his hands free against Piccolo Jr. in Tale 188. | That the brothers have done it, or that a source feat proves any unshown branch choreography. It is a real source variation, not an obligatory upgrade. |
| **Super Kamehameha** | The same [compendium](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_480.html) identifies a stronger application by Goku in Tale 185. The survey separately notes an intended release that Goku stops and a later fired wave in that encounter. | A universal fixed ratio, an automatic Super Saiyan requirement, or proof that every game move with “Super” in its title is that identical manga moment. |
| **Kaiō-ken Kamehameha** | This combines a temporary ki-amplification technique with the wave. In the retained Z 31 captions, Goku calls a ×3 Kaiō-ken Kamehameha at 12:07–12:18 and raises Kaiō-ken to ×4 at 16:01–16:06 during the contest; [Toei 31](https://lineup.toei-anim.co.jp/ja/tv/dragonz/episode/31/) independently summarizes the clash, and the [manga-based Vegeta profile](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_3929.html) identifies the ×4 response. | That ×4 means four times an already ×3-boosted state, a universal wave multiplier, or a permanently increased base. The caption and manga-based feature are separate witnesses. |
| **×20 Kaiō-ken Kamehameha** | [Toei Z 91](https://lineup.toei-anim.co.jp/ja/tv/dragonz/episode/91/) identifies Goku's ×20 wave against Freeza; [Z 92](https://lineup.toei-anim.co.jp/ja/tv/dragonz/episode/92/) says it fails to defeat him but injures his hand. In the retained Z 92 caption, King Kai's judgment that ×20 exceeded Goku's limit is **09:51–09:57**; the preceding 09:47–09:51 cue says Freeza is frighteningly strong. | A fixed ceiling for Goshin, a mandatory injury or identical future result. Goku's source condition and Freeza's source hand injury are not compulsory branch outcomes. See `C263`, `C308`–`C309`. |
| **Separate one-handed shots underwater** | In Tale 309 the [editorial survey](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_659.html) interprets Goku's two separate, one-from-each-hand ki pulses underwater as Kamehamehas, noting the unorthodox positioning and his “ha.” It counts one scene, not two uses, by the survey's counting rule. | The exact original-Z audiovisual sequence, a universal rule for what any two ki bullets are, or a new skill already demonstrated by either branch brother. |
| **Silent or interrupted use** | The manga survey identifies silent uses by hand position, and explicitly excludes some preparations that are stopped before firing. The [game/manga comparison](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_3039.html) notes adult Gohan's apparently silent manga use at Buu's cocoon. | That every unlabeled light is a Kamehameha, that chanting is always impossible or unnecessary for every learner, or that a preparation that fades necessarily hit anything. |
| **One-handed Gohan / father–son scene** | In the later Cell encounter, the [Gohan profile](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_1166.html) identifies a right-handed Kamehameha; the [Cell profile](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_1257.html) calls it a father–son Kamehameha with Goku's spirit supporting him. The editorial survey explains why its team counted the unusual pose and incomplete call. | An independently fired second beam must physically come from Goku in every version. The official Gohan and Cell features use different wording about Goku's support/power; do not resolve that physical mechanism by assertion or transplant this later scene into present Namek. |
| **Concurrent waves / brothers' combination** | Multiple people may cooperate without becoming one fighter. In this branch Gohan and Goshin separately fire and work together in Chapters 24 and 25. The later movie's family attack below is a different scene and continuity. | A mandatory named special move, equal contributions, automatic victory, fusion or proof that either brother could never accomplish the task alone in another situation. |

**Keep look-alikes distinct.** Vegeta's [Galick Gun](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_3929.html) is described as similar to the Kamehameha, but it is his separately named technique. The [official Saibaiman profile](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_2894.html) calls Krillin's Saibaiman attack a *Scattering Bullet*; do not automatically relabel it a scattering Kamehameha just because it is ki and separates. Masenko, Piccolo's Makankōsappō and a generic ki blast are not automatically Kamehamehas either. A similar pose, colour, shout or game label alone is insufficient to prove that every attack with it is the same technique.

## Later continuities, movies and game naming

These examples help the author understand the wider franchise, not prescribe what happens next in this story.

| Example | Where and how to use the name | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Instant Transmission Kamehameha** | The later Cell Games example combines a charged wave with Goku's movement to a new position. An [official *Sparking! ZERO* comparison](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_3039.html) uses that name for the game recreation and compares it with the manga moment. | The game's camera, dialogue and label are not automatically the original manga's wording. Instant Transmission is a separate prerequisite, not shown for the branch family at Chapter 43. Do not silently grant it. |
| **Cell and Buu's Kamehamehas** | The [manga survey](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_659.html) discusses Cell's inherited cell data and Buu copying Goku. These are later characters and different learning circumstances. | Their presence in a later source does not introduce them or their skills into the present branch. |
| **Goten's “Kamekameha”** | The [official Goten profile](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_1226.html) and [game/manga comparison](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_3039.html) describe a child misnaming the technique. | Do not treat the pronunciation alone as an independently engineered energy technique. |
| **Family / three-person Kamehameha** | An [official-site interview](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_1402.html) describes Gohan, Goten and Goku's Family Kamehameha in *Dragon Ball Z: Broly – Second Coming*. This is a movie example; the interviewee is describing a scene, not supplying a mechanics specification. | It is not the original-manga Cell father–son scene, and it does not retroactively name Gohan and Goshin's Chapter 25 combination. Do not assume the movie's exact metaphysics or a numerical “tripling.” |
| **Black Kamehameha** | The [*Sparking! ZERO* comparison](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_3039.html) labels Goku Black's game move and compares it with a *Dragon Ball Super* manga image. It describes the game's dark-purple presentation. | A game colour or move name does not prove every manga/anime line, an entirely different physical law or an available Namek-era branch technique. |
| **Slide into a close-range wave** | A [Kanzenshuu discussion summary of *Super* episode 116](https://www.kanzenshuu.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=40385) reports Goku charging while dodging, sliding over Kefla's attack, then firing at close range. The [official *Legends* article](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_970.html) separately identifies its recreation of Goku's Kamehameha against Kefla. | The slide's choreography here is a **secondary episode account**, not personally watched footage, and is later *Super* anime rather than original-Z evidence or a demonstrated branch feat. |
| **10× Kamehameha** | The [official GT Wu-Shinron profile](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_3979.html) identifies Goku's 10× Kamehameha and the dragon bouncing it back. The [*Legends* article](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_970.html) separately names its own “Lightspeed 10x Kamehameha.” | GT's named attack is not the same thing as Kaiō-ken ×10. Do not transplant its number, game title or animation into the earlier branch as a measured multiplier. |
| **Big Bang Kamehameha** | The [official GT Gogeta profile](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_3311.html) calls it a combination of Vegeta's Big Bang Attack and Goku's Kamehameha, used by their fusion. | Neither fusion nor the combination is a skill already shown for these separate people. |
| **“100× Big Bang Kamehameha” and “20× Kaioken God Kamehameha”** | The official articles use these as named *Dragon Ball Legends* [Gogeta](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_971.html) and [Goku](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_970.html) abilities; the latter article explicitly describes a game-flavoured sequence. | A game move name is not proof of a literal, transferable measurement or the same scene's dialogue in GT, *Super* anime or manga. These are not present branch skills. |
| **Final Kamehameha** | An [official card-game article](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_2579.html) identifies Vegito's “Final Kamehameha stance.” | This is a named later fusion-related reference, not evidence that either branch brother knows it, that it was used in original Z, or that the card establishes all mechanics. |

Other fan or game labels—such as “continuous,” “scatter,” “angry,” “full power,” or “ultimate”—must be checked **in the particular work and scene** before assigning their names, chronology or meaning. Repeated blasts, a different emotion and a bigger animation do not, by themselves, establish a new named technique or fixed power formula.

## What the selected branch has actually shown

| Person | On-page evidence and retained skill | Boundary at Chapter 43 |
|---|---|---|
| **Goku** | In Chapter 5 he charges the Kamehameha and pivots his hands to curve the sustained wave after Raditz. He already has practiced control. The official manga source separately establishes the earlier King Piccolo bending feat, Super Kamehameha and foot use. | Chapter 5's bend is branch prose; the original-Z Episode 4 curve is supported by secondary descriptions, not verified audio/video. Do not claim the foot application was newly performed on this branch's page merely because the source has it; its possible use depends on the inherited earlier history and the actual scene. No Instant Transmission or automatic future form is established. |
| **Goshin** | In Chapter 5 he sees his father and copies the wave in an emergency, diverting Raditz's attack. His first wave has **more raw ki than Goku's wave in that moment but less practiced control**, by the author's clarification. Chapter 15 shows deliberate cutoff while moving four stones and catching fragments; Chapter 19 shows varied size, direction and instruction; Chapters 22–23 show sustained steering, narrowing and cutoff while separately controlling moving blocks. Chapters 24–25 show his independent wave and cooperation with Gohan. | He retains these learned skills, exceptional Talent, hybrid nature and the genuine subsequent gains. No arbitrary maximum, compulsory mastery level or compulsory failure is imposed. New applications can be developed or copied where the story and author support them, but the later source labels do not prove he has performed them already. His ×30 Kaiō-ken in Chapter 43 is not itself a shown ×30 Kamehameha. |
| **Gohan** | He **asks** Goshin to teach him in Chapter 19, performs and repeats his own wave, aims it at a machine bracket and makes independent choices about when to fire. In Chapters 22–23 he turns and stops his wave, including a stronger moving application; in Chapters 24–25 he chooses to combine it with Goshin's and uses it against Nappa. In Chapter 43 his own focused wave strikes Freeza's firing hand from the side and lets Piccolo move. | Gohan's success is his own, despite being taught. The precise interruption is not a measurement equating him and Freeza, and it need not be treated as a weak or borrowed attack. No Gohan Kaiō-ken, later Cell-game one-hand scene, teleportation or fusion technique is shown. |

**Chapter 43 seam:** Goshin's temporary ×30 materially overpowers the currently demonstrated long-headed Freeza in their direct exchange; his sustained exertion matters in this instance and his real body/ki growth remains when the red aura ends. He later uses ×20 again. Gohan reaches the evacuated grove independently, while Piccolo and Goshin continue the contested fight. None of the ×20/×30 calls fixes future limits or obliges source Goku's particular injuries. The Kamehameha study does not draft Chapter 44.

## Issues corrected during this review

- **Source registry:** the old core technique entry only mentioned a secondary report of the Episode 4 bend. The live register now also records the separate, stronger manga-based source for Goku bending against Great Demon King Piccolo. This does **not** promote the Episode 4 visual to an independently watched fact.
- **Caption pinpoint:** the Z 92 King Kai judgment was previously cited as 09:47–09:51. That is the preceding statement about Freeza; the judgment itself is 09:51–09:57. The previous wording is retained in the JSON history, and the earlier review is marked with a subsequent correction.
- **Chapter 43 staging:** Gohan's already-diverted stone is no longer lowered a second time; Goshin settles the remaining pieces, and Goshin, Gohan and Piccolo's landing positions are explicit. Freeza listens for movement rather than hearing three voices before their nearby conversation is shown. The fight's outcome and character abilities do not change.
- **Chapter 33 knowledge:** the brothers identify the quarry guards as the arrangement when they left, with Gohan's continuing confidence as a hope/expectation rather than an unexplained live report.
- **Repetition, not a power rollback:** a few successive statements about retained gains and Vegeta's unsecured shade in Chapters 33–36 were varied or shortened. Actual ongoing growth, his free tail and hands, the active guard and Gohan's independent action remain. These are editorial revisions, not a claim of literary perfection.
- **Still open:** the return along Snake Way remains a genuine chronology/scale tension. It cannot be honestly closed merely by assuming a speed cap, an unexplained delay or time dilation. See [the follow-up audit](../../../../The%20Twin/canon/KAMEHAMEHA_RESEARCH_AND_CORRECTION_FOLLOWUP.md).
