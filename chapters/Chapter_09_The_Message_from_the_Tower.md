## COLD OPEN

Morning light reaches the beach at Kame House. Inside, a sheet hangs over the boards nailed across the damaged wall. A breeze pushes it inward, then lets it fall flat again.

Gohan wakes on a blanket beside the bed. He sits up, looks at his brother, and waits until he sees Goshin's chest rise beneath the cover.

Chi-Chi is asleep in the chair between them. Goku sits on the floor with his back against the bed. When Gohan moves, he opens his eyes.

"Morning," Goku says quietly.

"Morning. Did you sleep?"

"A little."

Goku reaches up and rests a hand on the edge of the mattress. "Are you awake, Goshin?"

Goshin turns toward him, blinking. "Is it morning already?"

**THE MESSAGE FROM THE TOWER**

**NARRATOR:** Two Saiyans are coming to Earth. Raditz said the journey would take about a year, and Earth's defenders have begun to prepare. Goku and his family have one another again—but the question of how to protect them all is only beginning.

## 1 · BREAKFAST AND A TELEPHONE CALL

Chi-Chi wakes when Goshin sits up. She moves her chair closer to look him over; his hands go behind his back before she touches his forehead.

"How does your head feel?"

"It still hurts a little. Less than last night."

"Are you dizzy?"

"No."

"And your hand?"

Goshin lifts the bandaged one. "It hurts when I squeeze it."

"Then don't squeeze it. Let me look at the cloth."

"Then don't squeeze it. Let me look at the cloth," Goshin repeats.

She checks the wrapping without undoing it. Gohan fetches the doctor's instructions and gives them to her.

"She said to call if anything got worse," he says.

"That's right." Chi-Chi reads the page again. "She also wanted to see him today. I'll call as soon as she's likely to be awake."

A snore sounds from the other side of the room. The Ox-King is sitting against the repaired wall with his hat tipped over his face. Another snore lifts its brim.

"Goku," he murmurs. "There's plenty more. Eat the whole thing."

Goku grins. "Even in his sleep he wants to feed me."

The Ox-King's hat falls off. He wakes to find the boys watching and puts it back on. Gohan finds the hatband and hands it to him.

"I was dreaming I had a roast as big as the table."

"I don't think I could eat it," Goku says.

"You turned down dinner, and now you're turning down breakfast?"

"The Senzu filled me up. Gohan too."

Gohan nods. "I still feel full."

"Their injuries are gone as well," Chi-Chi says. She looks at Goku before adding, "All of them."

"Good," the Ox-King says. "Then I'll eat your share until you need it again."

Goshin sits on a chair where he can see the kitchen counter. Chi-Chi washes rice, puts it on the stove and cracks eggs into a bowl. The Ox-King sets out plates, and Goshin watches his mother beat the eggs and cook them.

Chi-Chi sees Goshin watching the pan. "What comes next?"

"You turn them when the edges look like that."

"Usually." She tilts the pan. "This one needs a little longer."

When the rice is ready, she brings him a bowl and a plate of egg. He picks up his spoon with his good hand. Gohan pulls a chair alongside him and stays while the others eat.

Roshi comes out with the little cloth bag in his hand. He shows Chi-Chi the tied string, then turns to Goshin. "Your bean is still safe. I'll keep it with me."

Goshin looks at the bag. "Thank you."

"Korin knows more about the beans than I do. You can ask him your questions when you see him."

"Can I watch one grow?"

"That's a question for him too."

After breakfast Chi-Chi calls the village doctor. Goku waits beside her while she reports that Goshin's head hurts less and that he has not been dizzy or sick.

"We'd like to take him home," Chi-Chi says. "His grandfather's truck is on the mainland. Can he make a gentle trip, and could you see him at the house?"

She listens, her free hand resting on the instructions. "All right. We'll stop if he feels worse. And if any of those warning signs appear, we'll get urgent care instead of waiting."

She gives the doctor directions to the house and puts down the receiver.

"She can come this afternoon. Goshin can travel if we keep the journey gentle and watch him."

"I can carry you," Goku says. "The boys can ride Nimbus together, the way they did coming here. I'll stay beside them."

"And my truck?" the Ox-King asks.

Goku looks toward the sea. "We have to get you to it first."

## 2 · ABOVE THE TOWER

Mr. Popo walks across the Lookout to where Kami is waiting. A rolled carpet rests under Popo's arm.

He pauses at the edge and watches Kame House below. At breakfast, Goshin asks Roshi about watching a bean grow. Popo listens to Roshi's answer before turning to Kami.

"Korin will need the names," Kami says.

"Krillin, Yamcha, Tien and Chiaotzu."

"And Yajirobe. Ask them to gather at Korin Tower. When they have come, we will train them."

Popo unrolls the carpet at the edge of the platform. It hovers above the stone as he steps onto it.

"What shall I tell Goku?"

"Ask him to come and speak with me. His family may come too, when the injured boy can make the trip. We have much to discuss."

"The Saiyans, and Piccolo?"

"Yes. And the Dragon Balls."

Kami rests both hands on his staff. "Tell Goku I asked King Yemma whether a living man could journey to King Kai. Yemma has not agreed to it. I will explain what I saw."

Popo bows, and the carpet descends through the clouds.

Korin is waiting on the open floor of his tower. Yajirobe sits nearby with a bowl in his lap, scraping the last of its contents together with his chopsticks.

The clouds below the railing part around the descending carpet. Korin looks up from the bowl he has been setting on a shelf.

"You came a long way for an empty bowl," Korin says.

"I came to live here. The bowl is a benefit."

Popo's carpet rises into view beyond the railing. Yajirobe stops scraping long enough to watch it settle.

"Kami has a message," Popo says.

Korin listens to the names. Yajirobe lowers his bowl when he hears his own.

"Wait. I'm on the list?"

"You are," Popo says.

"I already helped. Krillin came for the beans, and I was right here."

"And that was useful," Korin says. "Kami wants you to train as well."

"Against the men who are stronger than Raditz?"

"To prepare for them."

Yajirobe looks down at his sword, propped against a pillar, then back at the bowl. "You make that sound much better than it is."

Popo gives Korin the rest of the message: the invitation to Goku and his family, and Kami's inquiry to Yemma about King Kai. He makes clear that Yemma has given no permission.

"One son refused his bean because he hadn't watched it being made," Popo adds. "He wants to ask Korin if he can watch."

Korin repeats the news to Yajirobe and makes him repeat the names back.

"Krillin, Yamcha, Tien, Chiaotzu, and me," Yajirobe says. "Meet here. Kami's going to train us. Goku can come and talk, and his family can come when the little one's well enough. Kami asked Yemma about King Kai, but Yemma hasn't agreed to anything."

"Good."

"I didn't say I liked it."

"I heard you the first time."

Yajirobe picks up his sword and ties it at his side. He leaves the empty bowl on the floor for Korin, then turns back and puts it on the shelf instead.

At the railing he stops. "If the boy asks about beans, what am I supposed to tell him?"

"Tell him I grow them. He can ask me about watching. I don't know exactly when another will be ready."

"And I shouldn't promise him one."

"Now you've got it."

Yajirobe collects a capsule from his pocket and goes down from the tower. Before long, an aircar rises past the trees and turns toward the sea.

## 3 · YAMCHA, TIEN, CHIAOTZU AND—

The sound of an engine approaches Kame House. Gohan's hand closes around the edge of the chair.

The noise grows louder over the water. Goshin turns his head toward it too, and Chi-Chi moves between the boys and the open doorway.

Chi-Chi kneels so Gohan can see her. "It's outside. We don't know who it is yet."

Goku moves to the door. Krillin comes out from the other room behind him, pulling on his shirt. He pauses beside Gohan.

"We'll look," Krillin says.

Gohan goes with them as far as the doorway. Goshin stays seated, watching through the open door while Goku steps onto the beach.

The aircar slows above the island. Its driver leans over the side.

"Hey! Down there!"

"Yajirobe!" Goku waves. "Come on down."

The car lands on the sand clear of the house. Yajirobe climbs out, straightens his sword and looks at the boards across the wall.

Sand settles around the wheels. Gohan releases the doorframe, but stays beside Krillin while Goku goes to greet the visitor.

"I crossed all that water just to find your house. You ought to put up a bigger sign."

"You're not going to tell me you missed the house," Krillin says. "It's the only one on the island."

"The ocean's bigger than the island."

Goku introduces the boys and Chi-Chi. Yajirobe gives Goshin's bandage a brief look, then points toward the house. "Can we sit? I have a message, and it's long."

Inside, Roshi brings out chairs. Gohan sits beside Goshin, and Chi-Chi stays close enough to see both of them. Yajirobe counts names on his fingers as he speaks.

"Korin says Kami wants Krillin, Yamcha, Tien and Chiaotzu to come to the tower. Kami will train us."

Krillin leans forward. "Us? You're coming too?"

"Apparently."

"That's great!"

"For you, maybe. I told them I didn't want to fight a pair of aliens. It didn't help."

Roshi rubs his chin. "Kami is taking this seriously."

"We all are," Krillin says. "I can find Yamcha. Then we'll look for Tien and Chiaotzu."

"Don't leave until you have a way to reach us," Roshi says. "Bulma may know where to start."

"She took the scouter home," Goku says. "I hope she can figure out how it works."

"Without telling the other Saiyans everything we say," Chi-Chi adds.

Yajirobe raises another finger. "Goku, Kami wants to talk to you too. Your family can come when the boy's well enough to travel."

Goku looks at Goshin. "What did you want to ask?"

"Can I ask Korin about the beans?"

"I asked for you. He grows them, and he said you could ask him about watching. He didn't promise when there'd be another one."

Goshin considers that. "All right. I'll ask him."

"What does Kami want to talk about?" Goku asks.

"The Saiyans. The Dragon Balls. Piccolo. And Kami asked King Yemma about a teacher called King Kai, but Yemma hasn't agreed to anything."

"King Kai?" Goku asks.

"I don't know anything about him. Ask Kami."

Chi-Chi watches her husband. "Do you want to go?"

"I want to hear what Kami says. I don't know what going to King Kai would mean yet."

"We'll all talk about it," she says. "And Goshin sees the doctor before we plan a long trip."

"We will."

Gohan looks up from his hands. "Can I come if we go?"

"You and your brother are invited to visit with us," Chi-Chi says. "Nobody's sending you away."

"I want to see the tower," Gohan says. "And I want to ask Kami about the Saiyans."

Goku smiles at him. "Then we'll ask."

The Ox-King peers out the door at the aircar. "Does that thing carry two?"

"Depends on the two," Yajirobe says.

"My truck's on the mainland. If you could take me as far as the ninth road marker, I'd be much obliged."

Yajirobe looks at the Ox-King, then at the car. "You might be most of the two."

"I can squeeze."

"Will there be lunch at the other end?"

"At my daughter's house there will."

Chi-Chi puts a hand on her hip. "Will there?"

"I'll help make it," the Ox-King says quickly.

"Then perhaps there will."

Yajirobe gets up. "All right. But you sit where I tell you."

## 4 · BABA'S VISIT

A round shadow passes over the doorway. Fortuneteller Baba floats down on her crystal ball and comes in without knocking.

"I leave my brother alone for a little while, and he lets someone knock a wall out of his house."

"Hello to you too, Sister," Roshi says.

Baba looks at Goku. "You're standing. That makes the message easier."

"You have one too?" Krillin asks.

"From a guide in Otherworld. Kami asked King Yemma about sending Goku to learn from King Kai. Yemma said he would consider it if Goku recovered and wanted to go. He did not give permission."

"I have recovered," Goku says. "A Senzu took care of everything."

"Then the guide's news was out of date. Whether you want to go is still your decision, and whether you can go is still Yemma's."

"How far is it?"

"The guide didn't tell me. Ask Kami what he knows."

Goku rests his elbows on his knees. "I'd like to meet a teacher even Kami went to ask about. But I can't decide until I know what the trip is."

Chi-Chi sits beside him. "And until we know how the boys will be protected. Piccolo tried to take them once."

"I won't leave them for him," Goku says. He looks at both children. "We'll decide together."

Baba glances at Gohan's hat on the table. The Four-Star Ball catches the light from the open door.

"You kept that," she says.

"It was Grandpa's ball," Gohan says.

"Your father had it before you."

Goku reaches for the hat and turns it so the stars face him. "You brought Grandpa to see me once. He fought me with a mask on, and I didn't know it was him until afterward."

"He was pleased to see how much you'd grown," Baba says.

"I was happy to see him too." Goku puts the hat back where Gohan can reach it. "Baba, if you see him again, would you carry a question for me?"

"What question?"

Goku looks at Chi-Chi. She takes his hand.

"Roshi told me what happened when I looked at the full moon. Grandpa warned me about a monster before he died. I want to ask whether that monster was me."

Baba's crystal ball settles a little lower. "I can try to pass the question to him. I can't promise when I'll see him, or arrange a meeting just by saying so."

The room is quiet. Goku keeps one hand around Chi-Chi's, and Gohan holds his hat against his chest.

"A message is enough to start."

"Could you tell him about us too?" Gohan asks.

"Tell him he has two great-grandsons?"

"And their names," Gohan says. "I'm Gohan, like him."

Baba looks at the other boy. "And your name?"

Goshin sits up straighter. "I'm Goshin."

Baba reaches into her sleeve and produces a stub of pencil. "I'll need paper if the whole family intends to send messages."

Gohan finds a blank sheet on the table. He writes his name, then pushes the pencil toward his brother's good hand. Goshin writes beneath it, taking care not to move his bandaged palm against the paper.

Goku adds, "Tell him I'm glad I got to see him. And if he wants to answer, we'll listen."

Baba folds the page and tucks it away. "If I find him, I'll give him this and ask your question. That's all I can promise."

"Thank you," Goku says.

She turns to Roshi. "And you. Fix the wall properly."

Roshi looks at the sheet covering the boards. One corner has come loose, and he reaches up to tuck it back in.

"I was planning to."

"I know. That's why I'm reminding you."

Baba rises on her crystal ball and leaves through the door. Gohan watches until she is over the water, then puts his hat on.

## 5 · THE ROAD MARKER

Roshi stands on the beach with Krillin and Turtle while the family prepares to leave. Chi-Chi carries the doctor's instructions and a small bag of clean dressings. Goshin comes out holding her hand; Gohan carries the blanket he folded that morning.

"You can keep that here," Roshi tells him.

"I thought Goshin might want it on the way."

"Then take it."

Goku calls Nimbus. The yellow cloud drops beside the porch, and Gohan climbs onto it first. He makes a place next to him, and Chi-Chi helps Goshin sit down without putting weight on his sore palm.

"Tell me if the movement bothers your head," she says.

"Tell me if the movement bothers your head."

Gohan spreads the blanket over their knees. "I'll stay next to you."

"I'll be right here too," Goku says. He lifts Chi-Chi carefully and rises beside the cloud. "Ready?"

She holds on around his shoulders. "Slowly."

They cross the water together, Goku keeping pace with Nimbus. When the cloud passes through a patch of cool mist, Goshin pulls the blanket up to his chin.

The island falls behind them. Gohan keeps one hand on the blanket and uses the other to point out the first line of trees on the mainland.

Their father turns toward the road beyond them.

Yajirobe's car is already beside the ninth marker. The Ox-King stands next to his truck, opening and closing its door to make sure it is still locked properly.

"There you are!" he calls.

Goku lands with Chi-Chi. Nimbus settles close enough for her to help the boys down.

"How was the ride?" she asks Goshin.

"My head's the same. The cloud didn't bounce."

"Good. We'll ride home in the truck, and you can tell me if anything changes."

The Ox-King opens the passenger door. Chi-Chi climbs in with the boys beside her, leaving Goshin room to rest his hand on the folded blanket.

The seat is close, but all three are inside the cab. The Ox-King waits until Chi-Chi shuts the door before he goes around to the driver's side. Gohan puts his hat carefully on his own knee so its ball does not knock anyone when the truck moves.

Yajirobe leans against his car. "I should tell Korin I delivered the message."

"I'll see you at the tower," Goku says.

"Bring food."

"You were coming for lunch," the Ox-King reminds him.

"I was thinking about it. Korin will ask why I took so long."

Chi-Chi takes a wrapped parcel from her bag. "I made extra egg and rice this morning. You can take it if you like."

"I like."

Yajirobe accepts it and climbs into his car. He waits until the truck has pulled clear of the road, then lifts off and circles once before heading inland.

Goku rises beside the truck. The Ox-King drives steadily, and Goku follows above the verge, close enough to see through the window. Once, when the truck stops at a rough stretch, Gohan rolls the window down.

"Father, are you coming all the way?"

"All the way home."

Gohan closes the window, and the truck continues between the trees.

## 6 · THE SCREEN

At her laboratory, Bulma draws a length of fine metal mesh across the opening of a test enclosure. The scouter lies on a table inside, its cell still in a separate container. A monitor, tools and a small power supply occupy the rest of the table.

Krillin arrives while she is fastening the last edge. He taps on the lab door and holds up the packet Roshi gave him for the trip.

"I brought lunch. I thought you might forget."

"Put it there, where it won't get wires in it."

He sets the packet down and peers through the mesh. "Is that going to keep it from sending?"

"It's a shield for ordinary radio signals. I've disconnected the circuit I think handles transmission, and I can test the display in here. I also disconnected what looks like its microphone."

Bulma points to the sealed container holding the original cell. It remains separate from the device.

"So it's safe?"

"I didn't say that. It's alien equipment. There could be another way it sends that I haven't found, and this mesh may not stop that. I won't use it out in the open until I know more."

"And you won't put the original cell back?"

"Not for this test. I said I'd learn what I could with it off. I have, but the display needs power. The original cell stays out until I know how to stop it sending. This separate supply is still a risk."

She points to the modified display. "I changed the characters into numbers we can read. I can see what it reports; that doesn't tell me how accurately it measures anyone."

Her notebook is open beside the controls. Several pages are filled with sketches of the device, its wiring and the symbols she found on the display.

Krillin looks at the enclosure. "Do you need somebody to stand in there?"

"If you don't mind. I need someone who can tell me what he's doing while the reading changes."

"What about the signal?"

"The suspect circuit is disconnected, and we'll close the shield. That's the best precaution I have for a short test, not a guarantee. We can wait if you'd rather."

Krillin opens the mesh door. "We have to learn about it sometime. Tell me where to stand."

Bulma checks the seams once more before joining him. The mesh covers the sides and top of the enclosure, with a fastening across the doorway.

Bulma joins him inside and closes the opening. She checks the connections, powers the test setup and raises the scouter so its lens faces him. The screen lights.

"Two hundred and six," she says.

"Is that good?"

"It's a number. Now stand still and breathe normally."

"I am breathing normally."

"You were holding your stomach in."

He lets it out. Bulma watches the display settle. "Two hundred and six again. Can you raise your energy a little? No blasts."

Krillin closes his hands and concentrates. The figures climb. When he relaxes, they drop again.

"It changes," he says.

"Yes. Raditz saw that happen at the fight too. A single reading isn't everything you can do."

She switches off the test power and sets the scouter down before opening the enclosure. Krillin follows her out.

The display goes dark. Bulma leaves the power supply disconnected while she copies the observed reading into her notebook.

"Could it find Yamcha or Tien?" he asks.

"Maybe, once I know how its search works and how to keep it from talking to anyone else. I'm not letting it loose on the city today."

Krillin takes a bite of lunch while she notes the result. "Kami wants me to find both of them."

"Yamcha's been playing baseball," Bulma says. "I saw his name in the sports pages. Try the city stadium; someone there should know how to reach him."

"You kept up with him?"

"I read the paper. Don't make a story out of it."

Krillin chews and nods with exaggerated seriousness. "Just the paper."

Bulma reaches for a wrench. Krillin moves out of her reach.

"I don't know where Tien and Chiaotzu are," she says. "Ask Yamcha if he's heard from them. And tell the others what the screen can and can't prove."

"I will. Thanks, Bulma."

"Don't thank me yet. I still have to make sure this thing isn't calling its friends."

She covers the scouter, leaving its cell separate. Krillin takes the rest of his lunch and heads for the door.

## 7 · THE EXAMINATION AT HOME

The mountain house is quiet when the truck stops outside. Goku reaches the door first and checks the rooms, calling back that everything is where they left it. Gohan follows Chi-Chi inside, with Goshin and the Ox-King behind him.

Gohan's school paper is still fastened to the doorframe, its red mark visible. Chi-Chi takes it down, smooths the corner and puts it on the shelf.

"Did I miss my lesson?" Gohan asks.

"You did. We can talk to your teacher."

"I wanted to show him the rest of the book."

"Then we can do that when things have settled a little."

Chi-Chi sees Goshin looking toward the kitchen. "What is it?"

"Can I sit where I can see you make lunch?"

"Yes. And your grandfather has volunteered to help."

The Ox-King rolls up his sleeves. "I have."

Gohan goes to the cupboard and brings out a cushion for his brother's chair. Goshin puts his bandaged hand on it and watches as Chi-Chi rinses vegetables and cuts them for soup. Their grandfather tends the pot while she prepares rice.

He lifts the lid to let Goshin see the vegetables going in. Steam rises between them, and he moves the pot farther back on the stove.

"You don't have to watch the water boil every time," Gohan says.

"I want to see all of it."

"Then I'll sit with you."

Goku brings wood to the stove. He and Gohan are still full from their beans; neither needs a meal, though Goku accepts a cup of water and sits at the table while everyone else eats.

The doctor arrives afterward with her case. She asks Goshin about the journey and compares his answers with the notes she made at Kame House.

"Any vomiting or dizziness?"

"No. My head hurts less."

She checks his eyes and examines his hand after Chi-Chi loosens the wrapping. Goshin watches her clean the burn and put on a fresh dressing.

The doctor lays the used cloth aside and makes a note before closing her case. Gohan waits nearby with the cushion ready.

"It's looking better," she says. "Keep the hand clean, and don't use the palm to strike or lift anything heavy."

"Keep the hand clean, and don't use the palm to strike or lift anything heavy," Goshin repeats.

Goku sees him looking up. "What is it?"

"Can I practice now?"

"No sparring, flying practice or hard exercise yet," the doctor says. "Walk gently if you feel well. If moving makes the headache worse, stop and tell your parents. I want to check you again before harder activity."

"No sparring, flying practice or hard exercise yet. Walk gently if you feel well. If moving makes the headache worse, stop and tell your parents," Goshin repeats. "Can I stand and put my feet in the right place?"

"A few gentle placements, not a long session. Rest if you feel any worse."

"A few gentle placements, not a long session. Rest if you feel any worse," Goshin repeats.

Chi-Chi writes the new instructions beneath yesterday's. "What if we need to travel farther, to Korin Tower?"

"Ask me again after the next check. Don't plan a long journey on the assumption that he's ready today."

Goku walks the doctor to the door. "Thank you for coming all the way out here."

"Call if you need me. The same warning signs still matter."

She leaves by the path through the trees. Goku waits until she is out of sight, then returns to the house.

Goshin is standing beside his chair, his good hand resting on its back. He places one foot slightly forward and checks the space between them.

His hand stays on the chair. Goku does not ask him to take it away.

Goku comes to stand opposite him. "Try letting your weight settle before you move the other one."

"Try letting your weight settle before you move the other one," Goshin repeats. His heel turns too far, and he corrects it. On the next placement, he stops before the heel turns out.

"That felt steadier," he says.

"It looked steadier."

Goshin makes one more careful placement, then sits down. Chi-Chi brings him water.

"Is your head the same?"

"The same."

"Then that's enough for now."

"Then that's enough for now," Goshin repeats. He drinks. Goku moves the chair back to the table, leaving the floor clear.

## 8 · THE TWO BALLS AT HOME

Gohan sets his hat on the table. "The other two are still here, aren't they?"

"They should be," Chi-Chi says. "I haven't moved them."

She opens the cupboard and brings out the Three- and Six-Star Balls. Goku sets them beside the Four-Star Ball on Gohan's hat. Light catches the orange spheres.

Gohan turns his hat so the Four-Star Ball sits securely in its setting. The other two rest on the table between his parents.

"If the Saiyans want a wish, they need all seven," Gohan says.

Goshin counts the balls with his good hand.

"We have three," Goku says. "We don't know where the other four are."

Chi-Chi looks at the cupboard. "I don't want anyone coming here because we've left all three together."

"They found our island by looking for strong people," Gohan says. "Raditz didn't need the Dragon Radar to find Father."

Goku nods. "If they can find people the same way, hiding the balls here may not keep you safe."

Goku sees Goshin looking at him. "What is it?"

"Could we give one to Korin?" Goshin asks.

"We could ask," Goku says. "But the tower might become a target too. We shouldn't leave it there without telling him."

"And we need Bulma's radar to find the others," Chi-Chi says. "If we collect them, we need somewhere to keep them apart until we know what to do."

Gohan lifts his hat. "Can I keep Grandpa's ball for now? I don't want to lose it."

"Yes," Goku says. "If we need to move it later, we'll talk to you first."

Goshin traces the rim of the Six-Star Ball without picking it up. "We could write down who has each one. Not where, in case somebody finds the paper."

Chi-Chi takes the pencil from beside her instructions. "Three, six and four are with us. If we move one, we can make sure everyone who needs to know is told."

"Could the paper just say how many?" Gohan asks. "Then it wouldn't tell anyone which ones."

She writes a large **3** on the corner of a spare sheet. "For now, it says we have three. The plan is still to find a safer place."

The Ox-King carries a wooden box from the shelf. Chi-Chi puts the Three- and Six-Star Balls inside and closes the lid. Gohan keeps the Four-Star Ball on his hat.

The lid closes over the two balls. Goku looks at the box once more before helping the Ox-King return it to the cupboard.

Gohan holds his hat close, keeping the third ball with him.

"We'll ask Kami and Korin before we move anything to the tower," he says.

## 9 · THE SMALL WAY DOWN

Outside, a rustling comes from the low pile of branches beside the path. Gohan steps closer and sees a small bird caught between two twigs. It flaps, but the narrow gap holds one wing in place.

"Father!"

Goku comes to the doorway. Goshin watches from his chair just inside, with Chi-Chi beside him.

"It can't get out," Gohan says.

He reaches toward the branches, then stops when the bird flutters harder.

"I don't want to hurt it."

"Take your time," Goku says.

Gohan crouches. A lizard on the other side of the pile slips down a slanted piece of wood and disappears under a leaf. Gohan watches the piece settle against the ground.

He picks up a loose twig and lays it across the gap where the bird's feet are scrambling. Then he grips the branch above the trapped wing.

The twig rocks when the bird touches it. Gohan steadies its lower end against a flat stone.

"I'm going to lift this one," he says.

Goku stays at the doorway. "Can you see where its wing is?"

"Here. If I lift the other end, it won't drag across it."

Gohan moves around the pile and raises the branch slowly. The bird pulls its wing free but does not fly. It hops onto the twig, makes its way down to the ground and disappears into the grass.

Gohan puts the branch down. "It got out."

The leaves tremble where the bird has gone. Gohan waits until they are still, then carries the loose branch away from the path.

"You gave it a way," Goku says.

Goshin leans toward the open doorway. "You moved the heavy branch without crushing the little one."

"I nearly grabbed it too fast."

"You stopped," Chi-Chi says. "Come and wash your hands before you touch your books."

Gohan washes at the basin and returns to the porch with Goku. He looks at the path where the bird vanished.

"Can we practice the part about getting away?" he asks. "Not a big fight. Just what to do first."

Goku looks toward Chi-Chi. She nods. "Here, where I can see you."

"All right," Goku says. "If somebody comes toward you and you don't know what they want, you can move back and call us. You don't have to wait until they're close."

He takes one slow step forward. Gohan steps back and calls, "Mother! Father!"

"Good. But look where you're stepping too."

They try again. Gohan glances down, catches the edge of the porch step and adjusts his foot before it slips.

Goku lets him find his balance before taking another step. Chi-Chi moves the basin out of Gohan's path.

On the third try he moves to the side instead. "That way I can still see you."

"And the door's behind you," Goku says. "Good choice."

Goshin watches from the chair. "Your feet were closer together that time."

Gohan tries once more, leaving more room between them. His landing is quieter.

"Like that?"

"I think so."

Goku demonstrates a guard with his hands open and low enough not to hide his face. Gohan copies the position, then relaxes when Goku tells him the exercise is finished.

"I was scared when the engine came this morning," Gohan says.

"You came to look anyway."

"I waited until you and Krillin went first."

"That was a good thing to do."

Chi-Chi opens the window a little wider. "And you can call us before you look next time."

Gohan smiles at her. "All right."

## 10 · UNDER THE SAME ROOF

Far from the mountain house, Piccolo stands between two stone pillars. The tops of both have been broken away. He sets a smaller rock on the ground between them, then retreats until the pillars overlap from where he stands.

He fires. The shot passes between the broken tops and strikes the rock, scattering it without bringing down either pillar.

Piccolo changes position and repeats the exercise with another stone. This time a fragment clips the side of the nearer pillar. He goes to examine the mark.

"If there are two of them, one won't wait while I deal with the other."

He places two stones apart on the ground, returns to the ridge and fires at each in quick succession. Both break. The second shot leaves a wider mark in the earth than the first.

Dust travels across the empty space between the pillars. Piccolo waits for it to clear before judging the marks.

Piccolo sets up the stones again.

At the mountain house, evening comes. Chi-Chi checks the curtains before the light fades, and Goku helps her cover a gap beside the back window. Neither boy is asked to look at the sky.

Gohan finds a book about machines on the shelf and brings it to the table. Goshin sits beside him while their grandfather sorts through the parcels he brought on his visit.

Chi-Chi brings out the leather schoolbag her father left at the house. Gohan runs his fingers over the name burned into its flap.

"There's a book for you as well," the Ox-King says, handing Gohan a package. "And this one's for Goshin."

Gohan opens his carefully. Inside is a book with pictures of animals from distant places. Goshin's parcel holds a small wooden puzzle, its pieces fitted tightly together.

"Thank you," both boys say.

Goshin turns the puzzle over with his good hand. "Can you help me hold it, Gohan?"

The pieces have been cut to fit closely. Gohan examines the joins before placing his fingers where Goshin can reach the first loose edge.

Gohan holds one side while his brother slides a piece loose. The Ox-King sits down beside them and offers advice until Chi-Chi tells him to let them work it out.

Goku stands at the window after the curtain is fastened. He checks that it covers the glass and steps back to the table.

"When the doctor says Goshin can travel, we'll ask Kami about the Saiyans, the balls and the moon," he says. "And we'll ask Korin about the beans."

"And King Kai," Gohan adds.

"And King Kai. Asking isn't the same as leaving, though. We still have to hear the answer and decide what to do."

Chi-Chi sits across from him. "Whatever we decide, we make a plan for both boys first."

"Both," Goku agrees.

Goshin frees another piece of the puzzle. Gohan catches it before it falls off the table and sets it beside the first.

Goku turns to Goshin. "What do you want to work on?"

"If I train with Father later," Goshin says, "I want to make the wave I already know go where I mean it to. And I want my feet right every time."

"We can work on both," Goku says. "Balance, breathing, moving and aim. We'll build on what you've already done."

"After the doctor says I can."

"After the doctor says you can."

Gohan slides the animal book closer. "Will you still help me with the steps too?"

"Of course."

He opens the book at a picture of a bird on a branch. Goshin fits one puzzle piece back into place, and Gohan holds the rest steady for him.

Beyond the closed curtains, night settles over the mountain. The truck rests beside the path, and the house door is fastened.

Inside, the lamp stays on until both boys are ready for bed.

**NARRATOR:** The moon still shines above the Earth, and the Saiyans are still on their way. For this family, the first preparations begin together—with questions to ask, friends to find, and a road that must wait until everyone is ready.
