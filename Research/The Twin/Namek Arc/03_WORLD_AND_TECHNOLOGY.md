# World, culture, travel and technology

These are working notes for the original-Z source world, not instructions that *The Twin* must reproduce the original route. For each specific source sequence see the [episode chronicle](01_EPISODE_CHRONICLE.md). Images, sound design, exact colours and silent mechanics were not comprehensively verified against original-Z footage.

## 1. Places and travel routes

```text
Original source, not the branch's required route:

Earth / Lookout / Yunzabit
   └─ Kami's refurbished ship → mirrored children's ship
       → space current → apparent (false) Namek → REAL Namek

Earth ── Goku in Brief's gravity-training ship ──→ real Namek
Earth ── injured Vegeta's pod ──→ Planet Freeza No. 79 ──→ real Namek
King Kai's small afterlife planet ── telepathic messages ──→ the others
Real Namek ── later wish ──→ Earth ── later wish ──→ new Namekian home
```

- **Earth:** the Lookout is the seat of Kami and Popo; Yunzabit Heights holds the old Namekian craft; Capsule Corporation and Dr. Brief supply modifications, communication and the other ship. Kame House, Chi-Chi's home and King Kai's planet form distinct news and family threads. Goku's ship, the first travellers' ship and later Earth rescue craft should not be treated as a single vehicle.
- **The mirrored vessel:** a large, reflective spaceship run by refugee children. It can conceal its position visually through reflection, takes in a smaller craft and contains defensive devices and traps. Source dialogue describes the crew's family losses and a hoped-for refuge. Its exact technical operation beyond what is shown or captioned is not a solved engineering manual.
- **False Namek:** an apparent destination and Dragon Ball hunt created by two non-Namekian impostors who want a ship. Apparent terrain, people, balls and successes must not be confused with the real planet. The attempt belongs to the original-Z journey; the branch may never take this route.
- **Planet Freeza No. 79:** Freeza-force base where injured source Vegeta is treated and learns about Freeza's Namek expedition. It is not Namek and Vegeta's travel clock is not the Earth crew's.
- **Real Namek:** separate villages, local waterways and terrain, the Elder's elevated residence, Freeza's ship and the changing battle sites. The party loses its original ship; later escape choices depend on available vehicles and wishes. The source story does not give a surveyed distance between every point here.
- **King Kai's planet:** afterlife training and communication, not a physical location on Namek. The dead source fighters can train and sometimes communicate through King Kai; source death/afterlife status must not be imported into the living branch.
- **Earth refuge and new home:** the original Namekians spend time on Earth after evacuation, then leave for another planet by wish. "New Namek" is not the original planet restored in its old position. Episode 107 does not establish that Goku knows this happened.
- **Yardrat, later source disclosure:** original-Z episode 123 identifies the destination of Goku's emergency pod and a technique learned there. This is useful aftermath context, but not a place visited by the Namek travellers in episodes 37–107 and not present branch knowledge.

## 2. Environment and time

- **Light:** in episode 76 the source characters remark that ordinary night is absent on Namek when the sky goes dark at Porunga's appearance. An official expert discussion treats Namek as a three-sun world and discusses its apparent environment [1](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_1917.html). The article's speculative explanation of green skies and real-world orbital science is an expert's interpretation, **not established in-story biology or proof of stable real astrophysics**. Do not infer that a darkness effect can never occur.
- **Landscape:** the official ecosystem discussion and original-Z source descriptions provide a general picture of unusual vegetation, settlements and water. The exact shoreline, colour grading and scale of a given location require its identified version and direct visual review. Do not use a false-planet background as the real planet's terrain.
- **Ajissa trees:** Dende says in the retained episode-51 caption that the trees once grew plentifully and that Namekians are replanting them after an earlier catastrophe. Replanting is an act of cultural and environmental continuity, not a token background detail.
- **Water and food:** Dende describes Namekians' water-based sustenance in the source. An official biologist discussion speculates about photosynthesis [2](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_831.html); its proposal is not a revealed universal metabolic mechanism. The Earth travellers bring needs, food and equipment of their own; an environment that supports characters in the story is not proof that every organism or every human could survive indefinitely without supplies.
- **A Namekian year:** Dende explains in episode 107 that the Namek Dragon Balls renew after 130 days, corresponding to Namek's year; the Earth-side narrative then shows two successive 130-day intervals. This is a story clock, not a tested astronomy model or a license to convert all scene timing.
- **The five minutes:** Freeza declares roughly five minutes after damaging the planet. The original Z episodes dramatize that crisis across many episodes. A narrator's or participant's remaining-time statement is part of the story, not an exact synchronization of episode screen time, travel speed and real seconds.

## 3. Namekian society and history

- **Community and elders:** the planet's Namekians live in dispersed communities with elders who protect Dragon Balls. The Great Elder is their senior leader. Villagers face Freeza's demand as people protecting family, home and sacred responsibilities; some fight, some are killed, and Dende survives through outside help. Their losses are not interchangeable.
- **Prior catastrophe:** Dende's source account and a separate official manga-based Elder profile describe a past environmental catastrophe and the Elder's role in restoring the population [1](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_2251.html). Kami/Piccolo derive from a Namekian who reached Earth long before the present story. Modern franchise explanations of Namekian origins are **later-version material**, not silently imported here.
- **Eggs and inheritance:** the manga-based profile describes the Elder laying eggs to repopulate Namek; do not invent a universal account of every Namekian family, reproduction method or lifespan from one profile. The Dragon Clan/warrior distinctions and the episode-70 vs manga-profile warrior count require source and moment labels.
- **Language:** the source Namekian ship responds to spoken commands; the dragon needs its Namekian summons and wishes. Understanding the language is thus a real bottleneck, as well as a relationship with the people who know it. A separate official linguistic discussion explicitly treats its analysis as analysis, not a complete fixed grammar [5](https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_3155.html).
- **Memory and identity:** the Elder's encounter with Krillin includes reading his intentions/memories and releasing dormant power. Piccolo's assimilation with Nail is a specific Namekian act with an important identity question; it is not interchangeable with magical healing, a fusion dance or a generic body swap.
- **Succession:** the Elder appoints Mūri on Earth before dying again. The survival/recovery of the Dragon Balls depends on the source's particular creator-and-successor sequence. Do not assume an unrelated injured Namekian can automatically recreate or upgrade any set.

## 5. What changes at *The Twin*'s frontier

The branch's travellers are **Bulma, Piccolo, Gohan and Goshin** aboard Kami's altered ship, currently held in the mirrored craft's bay. The group is free in a dining room, not locked in the source's exact hostage position; children control the exit. Goku and Krillin remain on Earth with Vegeta; Tien and Chiaotzu are with Nappa. Their custody and future decisions are not settled.

The brothers and Vegeta retain tails; travellers have deliberately kept the ship's window covered and used untailed eyes to check unknown outside skies. No moon has been safely surveyed at a future destination. Goshin has a controlled first Great Ape in his past and can make a similar artificial moon by author direction, but no full Goshin-made moon has been shown. Do not infer that Namek's perpetual ordinary daylight automatically proves there can be no relevant light, moon or artificial-source issue. Nor does this uncertainty prohibit the author's possible adaptations or require a fixed wait.

Goshin's capacity to adapt to unaided space is an author-established possibility whose completion has not been shown; ordinary flight is established. Air, hull integrity and the safety of Bulma and the children therefore remain meaningful without treating Goshin as weak or setting a new cap. See [branch analysis](06_THE_TWIN_BRANCH.md).
