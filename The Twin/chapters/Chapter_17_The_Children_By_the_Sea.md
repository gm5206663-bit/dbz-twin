# Chapter Seventeen — The Children by the Sea

Rainwater drips from the edge of Kami's Lookout. A folded drawing rests beneath Gohan's hat on a dry bench, held in place by the Four-Star Ball.

**THE CHILDREN BY THE SEA**

**NARRATOR:** While Goku travels along Snake Way, his family continues to prepare for the Saiyans. But today, a boat lost in yesterday's storm may lead Gohan and Goshin somewhere they did not expect.

## A Message for Kami

"He came right down beside the weather station," Chi-Chi says. "He'd felt the boys and thought the Saiyans had come early. Then he told them to train with him."

Kami stands with his hands on his staff, facing Chi-Chi and the boys while Bulma leans against the bench.

"Did he try to take you?" Kami asks the boys.

"We told him we were training here," Gohan says.

"He said he'd see what we'd learned," Goshin adds.

"He didn't touch them," Chi-Chi says. "But I wanted you to know he was there."

Kami nods. "You were right to tell me. If he approaches you again, let me know."

"We will," Chi-Chi says. "Have you heard anything about Goku?"

"Nothing yet. The road is long, and he has only recently begun it."

Gohan looks toward the sky. "Can King Kai see him coming?"

"I don't know," Kami says. "But I will tell you when I hear that he has arrived."

A shout rises from the other side of the Lookout, where Krillin runs with a small red ball tucked under one arm and Yamcha closes in behind him.

"You said you wouldn't use both hands!" Krillin calls.

"I haven't caught you yet!"

Krillin cuts left, but Yamcha follows, so Krillin throws the ball over his head into Puar's paws.

"I caught it!" Puar says.

Yamcha stops. "You're on my side."

"Oh! Sorry."

Puar tosses the ball back toward Krillin, but Chi-Chi steps between Krillin and Yamcha and catches it first.

"I thought you weren't using both hands," she says.

Krillin points at Yamcha. "That's what I said!"

Yamcha looks at the ball in Chi-Chi's hands. "Want to take my place?"

"All right."

Gohan pulls on Chi-Chi's sleeve. "Can we play too?"

"Of course."

## The Red Ball

Popo sets four stones on the tiles to mark a square. Inside it, Krillin rubs his hands together as Gohan and Goshin take their places opposite him.

"One of you carries the ball across," Popo says. "Krillin will try to catch you."

"Can we pass it to each other?" Goshin asks.

"Yes," Popo says. "Either of you can carry it across."

"Both of them at once?" Krillin asks.

"Would you rather have Yamcha back?" Chi-Chi says.

"No, no. These two look easier."

"Did you hear that?" Goshin asks Gohan. He takes the ball, grinning. "Let's get him!"

Gohan laughs and moves beside his brother.

"You can go first," Gohan tells him.

Krillin crouches. "Ready?"

"Here I come!" Goshin darts left and rises off the tiles when Krillin moves to meet him. He changes direction in the air, but Krillin jumps after him, reaching for his shirt and the ball.

Goshin passes it to Gohan.

Gohan catches the ball against his chest and flies low toward the far line, but Krillin turns, follows him into the air and catches him around the middle. They land just short of the line.

Gohan's tail curls around Krillin's wrist.

"Got you!"

Gohan twists against Krillin's hold, then stops struggling and looks down at the stone beside his foot.

"My toe was nearly there."

"Nearly isn't across."

Krillin lets him go. Gohan hands the ball to Goshin. "What if you sent it to me sooner?"

"I can try."

They go back to the start. This time Goshin flies to the right. Krillin jumps after him, and Goshin sends the ball low toward Gohan before Krillin reaches him.

Gohan catches it, but instead of running straight across, he throws it back over Krillin's shoulder. As Krillin turns and reaches, Goshin pushes the ball just past his fingers with his ki, then catches it in the air and lands beyond the far line.

"That counts!" Gohan says.

Krillin looks from one brother to the other. "You didn't say you were going to do that."

"You would have caught it," Gohan says.

"I might have."

Yamcha calls from the shade. "Let me try against them."

"Find your own ball," Krillin says.

Chi-Chi gives both boys an approving grin as they return.

"That pass caught him looking the wrong way," she says.

"Krillin nearly caught my shirt, though," Goshin answers.

"I'll get it next time," Krillin says.

Goshin glances at Gohan. "Then we'll have to move the shirt."

## Looking for a Boat

Later, while Gohan fetches his hat and drawing, Bulma checks the dial on Dr. Brief's borrowed aircar. Chi-Chi has brought the car up from the mountain house with the boys and Bulma aboard.

"I should take this back to Father," Bulma says. "He'll start looking for it if I keep it much longer."

Gohan unfolds his raft drawing, with a huge bird above the sail.

"Do you think the boat ever came to land?"

"It might have," Bulma says. "The wind was pushing toward the mainland when we left."

"Could we look?"

Chi-Chi glances at the clouds above the distant hills.

"We can look on the way to Bulma's house," she says. "But if we don't see it, we won't spend all day searching the sea."

"All right."

Gohan folds the drawing again.

Goshin looks over his brother's shoulder. "You made the bird bigger."

"It was big."

"Its feet are bigger than the boat."

Gohan considers the picture. "I'll make the boat bigger next time."

As they say goodbye to Popo and Kami, Krillin waves from the square where Yamcha has finally got his turn with the ball. Chi-Chi starts the aircar, and the Lookout falls away beneath them.

## Piccolo's Next Try

In the wasteland, Piccolo lays his cloak and turban on a boulder among the cuts and craters from his last training session. A second Piccolo separates from him and lands across a stretch of broken rock.

"Don't give me time to stand still," Piccolo tells his double.

The double attacks, and Piccolo blocks one punch and steps aside from another. Two fingers rise toward Piccolo's forehead, but the double kicks the ground beneath Piccolo.

A slab of stone tilts up between them.

Piccolo jumps over it, but the double follows and strikes his arm before he can gather his ki. Piccolo drives an elbow back, sending the double into the broken slab. Both land among the fragments.

"Again."

The double comes at him from the side. Piccolo flies low, keeping the ridge at his back. A burst of ki strikes the ground ahead, throwing dust into his path.

He turns through the cloud and raises two fingers to his forehead. Light gathers while he backs around the end of the ridge. The double follows; Piccolo keeps his hand in place, then turns and fires. The beam tears across the empty slope when the double drops beneath it.

Stone breaks away behind him.

The double catches Piccolo's wrist, but he pulls free and lands across the crater. He looks from the cut in the ridge to his double.

"You moved before I fired. I need an opening, even for the quicker shot."

He raises his hands again.

## What Washed Ashore

Gohan sees a strip of pale cloth flapping against a roof near the shore.

"There! Near the trees!"

Chi-Chi brings the aircar lower, revealing a piece of driftwood beneath the roof, lying across two boards bound with cord. Sand has worked its way into the knots.

"It is ours," Gohan says. "There's the gap where my feet went through."

Beside the boards, a brown-haired boy tugs at the cloth while a girl with dark hair gathered on either side holds the mast steady; both look up as the car settles onto the beach.

"Maybe they saw it come in," Bulma says.

"Wait until I stop the car," Chi-Chi tells the boys.

Goshin grips the door handle and waits for the car to settle.

Gohan gets out and walks toward the children, with Goshin following and Chi-Chi and Bulma behind them. The boys are the same height; Goshin is a little broader across the shoulders. The sea wind lifts his dark hair without flattening the spikes.

The boy pulls the sail loose and puts it behind his back.

"We found it," he says.

"We made it," Gohan says. "It floated away yesterday."

The girl peers at him. "Were you in it?"

"Not when it floated away."

"Good. Rom said somebody might have fallen off."

"I said somebody might," Rom answers. "I didn't say they did."

Goshin points to the cord around the boards. "Gohan tied that knot. I held the wood."

Rom looks at the knot. "It's coming undone."

"It wasn't when we made it," Gohan says.

"The sea must have untied it," Goshin says.

Bulma crouches beside the raft. "The mast's still in one piece. That's more than I expected."

The girl steps closer to Gohan. "I'm Chiko. That's my brother Rom. How old are you?"

"Four."

Chiko beams. "I'm six. You can call me big sister."

Gohan looks at Goshin. "I have a brother."

"You can have a sister too," Chiko says.

"You can have mine," Rom says. "She talks all the time."

Chiko stamps on his foot, and Rom yelps and pulls it away.

Chiko peeks behind Goshin. "You both have tails!"

Goshin lifts his tail, and Gohan curls his around his leg.

"I wish Rom had one," Chiko says.

"You'd pull it," Rom answers.

"Only if you took my food."

Goshin looks at the raft again. "Did you want the wood?"

Rom nods. "There's a hole in our roof. These would cover some of it."

Gohan turns to his brother. "We could make another boat."

"With a floor," Goshin says.

Gohan tells Rom, "You can keep it. We were going to make a better one anyway."

Rom lowers the sail. "Well, thanks."

Rom's head snaps around at a whistle from beyond the trees.

"They're back."

He catches Chiko's hand and starts toward the old street behind the beach, where a girl's voice comes from between the buildings.

"Let go! You're hurting me!"

Chiko pulls against Rom's hand. "That's Ena!"

Gohan looks up at Chi-Chi. "Mother, can we help?"

Chi-Chi is already moving. "Come with me."

Goshin runs beside Gohan, reaching for his hand when they turn into the street.

## The People from the Home

The street behind the beach is lined with damaged houses. Salt has bleached the timber, a wall has fallen across one doorway, and grass grows through cracks in the road. Sea wind moves a torn curtain in an upstairs window where no glass remains.

At the far end, a man holds a girl with purple hair and a blue bow by the arm while another waits beside a dark van.

The man tightens his grip as Ena tries to pull away.

"I said let go!"

Gohan reaches them first and pulls the man's wrist away from Ena's arm. Ena runs behind him.

"What's the matter with you?" the man says. He reaches past Gohan, but Chi-Chi steps in front of him.

"She told you that you were hurting her."

"We're taking these children to the home. They can't stay here by themselves."

A tall boy with a red headband drops from the low wall, landing between Ena and the van.

"They can stay with me."

"Pigero!" Chiko calls.

The second man grabs Pigero by the shoulder, but Pigero twists free and shoves him back. When the first lunges, Pigero ducks, sending him stumbling into the side of the van.

Goshin catches a stone thrown from a roof before it reaches the man's head.

Another stone lands beside his foot.

"Don't throw those!" Chi-Chi calls.

On the roof, one boy lowers his arm while two others lean over the edge beside him.

A woman comes around the van. "Please, listen. We have rooms for you. There are beds, clean clothes and hot food."

"A boy who got out told us they hit you with slippers," Rom says.

"And it smells like medicine!" calls a boy from the roof.

"I don't like that either," says another.

Chiko holds up her hands. "They cut your nails every night."

Goshin looks at his fingers. "Every night?"

Chi-Chi turns to the woman. "What about what that boy said?"

"I won't hit any of you. I don't know what happened to him, but I'll ask."

"Ena told that man he was hurting her," Rom says.

The woman looks at Ena's arm. "I should have stopped him. I'll speak to him."

Chiko clings to Rom's arm. "Will we all be in the same room?"

The woman looks at the children, then at the van. "The rooms are arranged by age. I would have to speak to the director about where everyone could sleep."

"So you don't know," Rom says.

"Not yet."

Pigero puts an arm out in front of Ena. "We're staying here."

The woman looks toward the broken roofs. "We'll come back. You need more than these buildings."

After the men climb into the van, the woman gets in and backs it down the street toward the main road.

The children stay where they are until it disappears.

Then Chiko lets out a breath. "They didn't get Ena."

Ena rubs her arm. "I told him to stop."

Gohan looks at the red mark. "Does it still hurt?"

"A little."

Chi-Chi kneels beside her. "May I have a look?"

Ena hesitates, then holds out her arm.

Chi-Chi checks it without moving her from where she stands.

"I don't think it's broken. We can put something cool on it."

Pigero watches her. "Who are you?"

"I'm Chi-Chi. These are my sons, Gohan and Goshin."

"And I'm Bulma," Bulma says. "We came to look for their boat."

Pigero glances toward the beach. "You picked a busy day."

## Seven Children

Farther up the street, the children's house has one roofed room and a sheet in place of its door. Rom and Goshin carry the raft boards there from the beach and lean them against the wall.

Rom points to the boys from the roof. "That's Jink, Hatch and Yordon."

Jink's pointed fox ears twitch as Hatch adjusts his blue cap and Yordon pushes his goggles up on his red flying cap, above his dog's muzzle.

"There used to be more people here," Rom says. "Two years ago a huge wave came through the town. It took the houses and the roads."

Chiko looks toward the sea. "And our parents."

Gohan takes off his hat and holds it in both hands. He looks toward Chi-Chi, who has stopped beside the broken wall. She does not speak for him.

Goshin looks back toward the sea. Yesterday he watched their raft disappear on the water; the broken houses are much farther up the shore.

"Did the wave come all the way up here?"

"Higher," Rom says.

Goshin looks at the hill above the house, then at the broken roof. Yesterday the sea had risen around their little boat. He had found Gohan quickly when he fell, but the water had been everywhere.

Gohan moves closer to Chiko.

"I'm sorry," Gohan says.

Chiko presses close to Rom. He puts an arm around her shoulders without looking away from the house.

Rom shrugs. "We've got each other. Pigero found this place, and we fixed the roof as much as we could."

"It still leaks over my bed," Hatch says.

"That's because you picked the bed under the hole," Yordon answers.

"The other one has a spring in it."

Pigero returns from the house with a dented pot and tips it to show Chi-Chi the handful of rice at the bottom.

"We'll get more."

"We can help with that," Chi-Chi says.

"We know where to get food."

Rom pulls a face at the pot. "We're going to the market."

Gohan looks toward the road beyond the houses. "Can we come?"

Pigero opens his mouth, but Chiko takes Gohan's hand first.

"He can come with me."

"I'm going too," Chi-Chi says. "I need a few things myself."

Bulma starts toward the car. "I'll bring a bag. We left it on the seat."

Chi-Chi turns to the boys. "Stay where I can see you."

Goshin hurries after Gohan, then waits for the others at the foot of the road.

On the road rising toward the newer part of town, the boys walk with Rom and Chiko, who argue about whether the fruit seller will be at his usual stall.

## The Fruit Stall

In the market square above the old town, a woman sells flowers while a fruit seller stacks oranges and apples outside his shop.

Gohan stops at the apples. "They're bigger than the ones in my schoolbook."

"If we eat them, you won't have to count them," Goshin says.

"Mother will want us to pay first."

Hatch crouches beside a water barrel, clutching his stomach. "Ow!"

The seller bends toward him. The flower seller comes over too, and Rom fills a paper bag with apples while Yordon takes two oranges.

Gohan sees them. "Those aren't ours!"

The bag tears. An apple bounces off Goshin's shoe, and the seller looks up.

"Hey!"

Rom snatches the apple and runs. Yordon follows, with Hatch right behind him, his stomach apparently better. An officer cuts across the square toward them.

Gohan chases Rom. Goshin catches up first and cups the bottom of the torn bag before the apples fall out.

"We need them," Rom says, holding the bag tight.

The officer steps in front of them. "Give those back."

The seller arrives, breathing hard. "They've taken things before. I can't keep giving away my stock."

Chi-Chi reaches into her purse. Rom lets the seller take the apples from the torn bag, and Yordon returns the oranges. Goshin folds the empty bag while the seller counts the fruit. The seller takes Chi-Chi's money and puts the apples and oranges in a fresh bag.

"I'm paying for these," she tells Rom. "But don't take his things again."

"We didn't ask you to pay."

"I know. You still took them."

After a moment, Rom accepts the bag. The officer looks toward the old town.

"The people from the home have been looking for you."

Rom pulls the bag close. "We know."

Pigero comes through the square with Chiko and Ena. "What happened?"

"Nothing," Rom says.

"He took fruit," Gohan says. "Mother paid for it."

Rom turns on him. "You didn't have to tell him!"

"He asked."

Pigero takes the bag and looks inside, finding enough for each child to have a piece. He hands it back to Rom. "Come on."

Chi-Chi buys rice and vegetables before they leave. On the road down, Rom walks ahead, with Chiko hurrying after him. Gohan watches them disappear around the bend.

"Is he angry with me?"

"Yes," Chi-Chi says. "Give him a little time."

"Do you think he'll still want to be my friend?"

"I hope so, sweetheart."

## Supper at the Broken House

At the house, Chi-Chi sets the rice and vegetables by the stove while Rom puts the fruit on a shelf, out of Chiko's reach.

Pigero stands in the doorway.

"We can cook for ourselves."

"Good," Chi-Chi says. "Where do you keep the pot?"

Pigero brings it outside, and Bulma tightens its loose handle with a tool from her bag.

Ena brings water, and Chi-Chi rinses the rice. Goshin washes the vegetables for her to cut, while Chiko finds a spoon for the pot.

"I know how to make rice," Chiko says. "You put the water in, and then you wait."

"How much water?" Goshin asks.

Chiko looks into the pot. "That much."

Chi-Chi gives her the spoon. "Would you like to stir it?"

"Yes!"

Chiko stirs, then Ena takes a turn. When the pot begins to boil, Pigero moves a stone closer to the little stove to keep the wind off the flame.

Gohan sits on an overturned crate with his drawing open across his knees.

Hatch leans over his shoulder.

"The bird's too big."

"I know," Gohan says.

"Give me that." Hatch finds a short pencil in his pocket and draws another boat beside Gohan's. His has a roof, a flag and a row of windows.

"How does it move?" Goshin asks.

Hatch adds an enormous wheel at the back.

"Like that."

Goshin smooths the torn fruit bag beside the drawing. "There's room for another boat on this."

Hatch looks at the paper. "I could use it for a bigger one."

When the rice is ready, Chi-Chi fills the bowls the children bring her. Goshin carries one to Hatch before taking his own, and Gohan sits between Chiko and Rom on the doorstep, balancing his bowl carefully.

"Do you go to school?" Jink asks him.

"Mother teaches me at home. And I have a tutor."

Chiko's spoon pauses. "Do you like it?"

"Some things. I like reading. The sums can take a long time."

"He draws apples when he does them," Goshin says.

"Sometimes."

Hatch lowers his spoon. "I was good at drawing. I didn't like arithmetic."

Ena looks down into her bowl. "I've never been to school. I was too little before the wave."

"You could come and look at my books," Gohan says. "I have one about animals, and another about stars."

"Do you have one about boats?" Chiko asks.

"Mina lent C-6 a book about bridges. We have to return it to her when he's finished, but I could ask if you'd be allowed to look at it."

"Who's C-6?" Rom asks.

"Our friend. He's at Bulma's house."

Bulma sits on the step with her bowl. "He's waiting for Gohan to tell him whether the island had a bridge."

"It didn't," Goshin says.

"Then I hope he won't be too disappointed."

Jink looks at Gohan. "Is your mother nice?"

Gohan glances at Chi-Chi. "She makes me do my lessons even when I want to play."

Rom pushes rice around his bowl. "At least she's there."

Gohan's spoon stops. Rom keeps his eyes on his rice, but hasn't taken another bite. Across the step, Chi-Chi helps Ena reach a piece of carrot in the pot.

Gohan edges closer to Rom. "She is nice," he says.

Chi-Chi looks up. "I heard the first answer too."

Gohan nearly drops his bowl, and everyone on the step laughs, including Rom.

## Before Dark

As the sun sinks behind the new town, Chi-Chi gathers the empty bowls and ties the remaining rice in its bag while Bulma folds the cloth they brought from the car.

"We have to go home," Chi-Chi says. "You could all come with us for the night. We can make room."

"Even Pigero?" Rom asks.

"Of course."

Pigero looks at the others. "Thank you. But we don't know your house, and we haven't packed. I'd rather stay here tonight."

Chiko looks at Gohan. "Will you come back?"

"I want to," he says, and looks at Chi-Chi. "Can we?"

"We can come tomorrow. If that's all right with everyone here."

Pigero leans against the doorway. "The people from the home will probably be back."

"Then I'd like to be here when they come," Chi-Chi says.

Rom looks toward the road. "You don't have to."

"I know."

Goshin finds the sail slipping off the raft boards and lays it across them again.

"Will you use the cloth too?"

"Maybe over the window," Chiko says. "The rain gets in there."

Gohan adds a small flag to the boat with the wheel using Hatch's pencil, then returns it before folding the drawing into his pocket. Hatch puts the torn fruit bag on a shelf inside the house.

As Chi-Chi puts the empty shopping bag on the car seat, Goshin looks back to see Pigero lifting the boards with Rom.

Chiko is holding the end of the sail above her head so it does not drag in the dirt.

"Can we show C-6 the drawing when we take Bulma home?" Gohan asks.

"We can ask if he wants visitors," Bulma says.

The children wave as Chi-Chi starts the engine and the car rises over the beach.

## A Boat for C-6

When they reach the laboratory, C-6 is awake with Mina's bridge book open beside him and his bent arm resting on its support.

Dr. Brief is at the next table with his cat on his shoulder.

"We've brought visitors," Bulma tells C-6. "Would you like to see them?"

"I would. Did the island have a bridge?"

"There wasn't one," Goshin says.

"But it had a tiger," Gohan adds, coming to C-6's side.

"That's not quite the same thing."

C-6 studies their raft, the bird and Hatch's boat with the wheel when Gohan takes out the drawing.

"Did the boat float?" he asks.

"The little one did. Hatch drew the big one. We haven't built it yet."

C-6 looks at the row of windows. "How do you get inside?"

Gohan examines the picture. "Hatch didn't draw a door."

"He'll have to put one in before anyone takes a ride."

Dr. Brief comes over, and Bulma moves the loose page of Mina's book away from his cat's reaching paw.

"May we keep the car until tomorrow?" Chi-Chi asks him. "We'd like to visit the children again."

"Of course."

"Come past here for me," Bulma says. "I want to see how they get on."

Gohan folds the drawing into his pocket and waves to C-6, who lifts his working hand from the edge of the book.

## Don't Give Me Time

A burst of light crosses the wasteland as Piccolo moves sideways, letting it strike the ground behind him. His double rushes through the spray of stone.

Piccolo catches the double's wrist and pulls him close, but the double plants a foot against his chest and pushes away. Both land on opposite sides of the crater.

Piccolo touches two fingers to his forehead as his double comes forward and sends a burst of ki at him. Piccolo turns aside without lowering his hand, then rises above the crater.

The double follows. Piccolo drops past him and fires. The beam grazes the double's shoulder and cuts a narrow trench through the empty ground beyond him.

The double lands beside the trench and touches the torn fabric.

Piccolo is still in the air, breathing hard.

"Closer," Piccolo says. He lowers himself opposite the double. "Again."

## The Van Returns

The next morning, Chi-Chi collects Bulma from Capsule Corporation and returns to the old town with both boys. The raft boards cover part of the hole in the roof, and the sail covers the window, though one corner keeps slipping loose.

Goshin flies up to hold the cloth while Rom ties its corner to a nail from the ladder.

"You really can fly," Rom says.

"Yes."

"Can you carry the whole house?"

"Sure!" Goshin looks at the broken wall and the boards on the roof. "But I don't think it would all come with me."

Rom climbs down. "I only asked."

Inside, Pigero puts Hatch's little pencil into a sack with a blanket and a tin cup, then closes it as Chi-Chi enters.

"Where are you going?" she asks.

"I haven't decided."

Gohan looks around the room. "Where are the others?"

"Watching the road."

A whistle comes from outside.

Yordon runs past the window.

"The van's here! And there's a police car!"

Chiko and Ena hurry in behind him as Pigero sets the sack beside the doorway.

The same van has stopped at the end of the street, with a police car behind it. The woman from the home steps out with the two men, while two officers get out of the car.

"Rom, Chiko, get behind the wall. The rest of you too," Pigero tells the children from the house.

As Rom catches Chiko's hand and Hatch helps Ena through the gap beside the house, Gohan and Goshin follow Chi-Chi into the street.

"We came back to talk," the woman says.

"Then tell them to stay away from the children," Chi-Chi answers.

One of the officers approaches Pigero. "We need to know who's responsible for them."

"I am."

"How old are you?"

Pigero does not answer, and behind him Rom starts dragging the sack toward the gap in the wall.

The man who held Ena yesterday grabs Rom's arm.

Chiko cries out and pulls at Rom's sleeve. Ena rushes up beside her. "Don't hurt him too!"

"Let him go!" the woman calls.

"Leave him alone!" Pigero pulls the man by the shoulder until he releases Rom, then shoves him back.

The officer pulls Pigero's arms behind him and cuffs his wrists as he struggles.

"Stop fighting," the officer says.

"Then let me go!"

Gohan gets between Rom and the man. "You hurt Ena yesterday. Please don't grab him."

While Rom backs toward Chiko, rubbing his arm, Goshin comes to Pigero and looks at the cuffs.

"They're too tight. Get them off, please," Pigero says.

Goshin looks at the officer. "They're hurting him. Will you take them off?"

"He was fighting me."

"I'm not fighting now," Pigero says.

The officer reaches for his radio. Goshin looks at Pigero's reddened wrists, then slips two fingers under a cuff. "I'll bend the lock away from you. Hold still."

He eases both locks outward, clear of Pigero's skin. The cuffs come free, still joined by their chain. Pigero pulls his hands back and stares.

"Did I hurt you?" Goshin asks.

"No. I didn't know you could do that."

The officer steps back, his hand on the radio. "You can't just take those off. He was detained."

"He asked me to help him."

The officer looks at the cuffs in Goshin's palm. "Those locks aren't supposed to bend."

His hand tightens around the radio. Rom moves in front of Chiko, and she grips the back of his shirt. Goshin opens his hand so the officer can see the bent locks.

Pigero flexes his hands and looks from the van to the children behind the wall.

"We can get past them," he says quietly to Gohan.

Gohan looks at Chiko and Rom. "Where would you go?"

Pigero closes his hands. "Somewhere they won't find us."

"For how long?" Chi-Chi asks.

"As long as we have to."

"Pigero," Rom says. "We're not leaving you."

"I'm not leaving you either."

The woman steps forward, then stops when Ena retreats.

"I spoke to the director last night," she says. "You can come and see the place before you decide about today."

"And then you'll lock us in," Hatch says.

"I'll drive you myself, and I'll bring you back here afterwards."

"All of us?" Chiko asks.

"Anyone who wants to see."

Rom holds Chiko's hand more tightly. "Together?"

"Together."

The officer turns to the children from the house. "I can't leave you here indefinitely. But we're not grabbing anyone now. Look at the rooms, and we'll talk when you get back."

As the officer looks at the cuffs in Goshin's hand, Chi-Chi moves between him and the boys.

"My sons won't hurt you," she says. "But stop grabbing the children."

The officer looks at Goshin. "I'll take those."

Goshin holds out the cuffs. The officer takes them and puts them in his pocket before stepping back.

## A Look Inside

"I'll go," Chiko says.

Rom turns to her. "You don't have to."

"I want to see the beds. You can come too."

"I'm coming," Pigero says. Rom pushes the sack toward Yordon. "Keep that here," Pigero tells him.

"Can I come with you?" Bulma asks. "I want to know where you'll be."

Chiko nods, and Rom looks at Pigero, who gives a short nod of his own.

The woman opens the van. Rom checks the door from inside before he sits down. Chiko climbs in beside him, with Pigero and Bulma behind them.

The two men stay by the police car as the woman drives toward the new town.

Gohan watches until the van passes the flower stall.

"Will they really bring them back?"

"She said she would," Chi-Chi says. "We'll wait here."

Ena sits on the doorstep. Gohan joins her and takes out the drawing. Hatch comes over at once.

"You folded my wheel."

"It's still there."

Hatch smooths the page on his knee. "I could draw a bigger one."

"It might not fit."

"Then we need more paper."

Goshin finds the torn fruit bag on the shelf where Hatch left it yesterday and gives it to him. Hatch takes his pencil from the sack Yordon is holding, then turns the paper over and begins another boat.

Ena watches him draw the roof.

"Can you put a school on it?"

"On the boat?"

"Then we could go anywhere."

Hatch adds another window. "The teacher sits here."

"Not there," Yordon says. "That's over the wheel."

"Then the teacher has to hold on."

"C-6 said the other boat needed a door," Gohan tells him.

Hatch draws one beneath the windows. "This boat has one."

The van returns before Hatch has finished the chimney.

Chiko jumps out and runs to Rom's friends.

"There's a room with two windows! And the beds have blankets. They had a box of pencils, and the director said we could ask about school."

"Was it locked?" Hatch asks.

"No. We went outside again."

Bulma steps down behind Pigero. "We saw the common room and the sleeping rooms. The director said the six younger ones can stay together in the common room tonight if they want to try it. She couldn't promise how the rooms would be arranged after that."

"It still smells like medicine," Rom says.

"Only by the washroom," Chiko says. "And the beds don't have springs sticking out."

Pigero takes his sack from Yordon. "They'll let me stay for supper. After that we have to talk about where I can be."

"Are you going?" Ena asks him.

He looks at the roof, then at the children.

"I want to see you get a proper meal."

"We had one yesterday," Rom says.

"Because someone else brought it. I don't know if I can find enough tomorrow." Pigero looks at the other children. "I don't want you growing up stealing meals and running from the police. You ought to have a chance at school."

Ena tugs at his sleeve. "Will you come tomorrow, even if they put you somewhere else?"

"I will. I'll make sure they know I'm coming back."

Rom studies the sack. "I don't want Chiko in a different room."

"Neither do I," Pigero says. "Tonight they're saying you can all stay together. We can ask about tomorrow before anybody agrees to it."

"What if we don't like it?" Rom asks the woman. "Will we be allowed to leave?"

"I can't promise what the director or the police will decide tomorrow," she says. "But you can tell me, and I'll listen."

"You can tell us too," Gohan says.

Chi-Chi nods. "We'll come back. And we can still talk about you staying with us."

Chiko takes Ena's hand. "I want to try the bed."

Ena nods. Hatch holds up his pencil. "Can I bring this?"

"Of course," the woman says.

Yordon looks at the woman. "Can we see the food first?" Jink takes the folded blanket from Pigero's sack and puts it under his arm.

Rom is the last to move, looking at Chi-Chi.

"Will you come back?"

"Yes," she says. "We'll come and see how you are."

"Even if we're at the home?"

"Especially then."

Hatch folds the school-boat drawing and gives it to Gohan. "Show C-6 the door."

"I will."

Rom goes inside and brings out the bag of fruit. He gives it to Chiko. "Don't eat mine on the way."

"I won't."

"You said that yesterday."

"Yesterday I was hungry."

Pigero laughs despite himself.

Chiko climbs into the van with the fruit, followed by Ena and the others. Rom gets in beside his sister. Pigero takes the sack and sits where he can see them all.

The woman closes the door.

She does not lock it. After she has spoken with the officers, she gets behind the wheel.

Gohan waves through the window. Rom sits beside Chiko with the bag of fruit in his lap; Pigero has turned to keep the younger children in view.

Chiko presses her hand against the glass until the van turns the corner. When it is gone, Gohan keeps his hand raised for a moment longer, then takes Chi-Chi's.

---

## The Road Home

After taking Bulma home, Chi-Chi brings the aircar down beside the mountain house. Ox-King comes out carrying an armful of split wood.

"There you are! I thought you might stay at the Lookout tonight."

"We made another stop," Chi-Chi says.

Gohan runs to him. "We found our boat. Rom and Chiko are using it to fix their roof."

"A boat on a roof?"

"It wasn't a very good boat," Goshin says.

"It floated," Gohan answers.

"It floated away."

Ox-King laughs.

He sets down the wood and carries the folded shopping bag inside for Chi-Chi.

Gohan spreads the drawing on the table. Hatch's boat with the big wheel sits beside the little raft.

He unfolds the piece of fruit bag too, showing Ox-King the boat with a school on top.

"The teacher has to hold on," Gohan explains.

"That's a good idea," Ox-King says. "I wouldn't want to fall into the wheel."

Goshin looks toward Chi-Chi. "Are we really going back tomorrow?"

"After training, we'll go and see them."

"What if they don't want to stay there?" Gohan asks.

"Then we'll listen and find out what can be done."

Gohan nods and folds the drawing beside his hat, whose Four-Star Ball holds the last light from the window.

---

## Far Away

Goku runs up a rise on Snake Way and springs over a wide seam. In midair he throws two quick punches and a kick, then lands running. His shadow is the only one keeping up with him.

"Gohan, Goshin, you'd better be ready when I get back!" He grins. "And your mother might surprise us all!"

The next bend is still a long way ahead. Goku picks up speed.

On the mountain, Goshin holds Hatch's boat drawing still with one finger while Gohan sets his hat beside it.

"Ena wanted a boat with a school," Gohan says.

"We could make her a bigger drawing."

"With room for the teacher."

Chi-Chi closes the window as the evening breeze comes in.

The paper stays where the boys left it.

**NARRATOR:** Goku is still on his way to King Kai. On Earth, Gohan and Goshin have found new friends—and tomorrow they intend to see them again.
