# CRAFT — three working specimens
**Owns: non-canon demonstrations of clear action, growth and adaptation.** These are exercises, not chapters or fixed future events. Each is legible without a prior scene. The episode-era lock applies: ordinary ki and flight are available; god ki is not.

# SPECIMEN A — spar, counter, tail

**WHAT THIS IS:** Piccolo supervises Gohan and Goshin in a controlled spar. Gohan catches Goshin's tail once; Goshin learns a defense and practises it. Both brothers are capable.

**SCALE:** Two growing hybrid children, a trained adult, a rocky clearing. No current reading is asserted. **The spar rule is no ki blasts**; a short defensive ki guard is allowed. The specific damage here follows these choices, not a numerical damage formula.

## WHO IS WHO

- **Piccolo:** their teacher, controlling the drill.
- **Gohan:** Goshin's twin, resourceful and determined.
- **Goshin:** the other twin, quick to learn what he can see, with a tail.

## THE FIGHT IN PLAIN WORDS

1. Piccolo sets two boundary stones. A touch against your own stone ends the round. He bans ki blasts.
2. Piccolo shows a palm deflection. Goshin watches and repeats it. Both children begin.
3. Gohan drives Goshin toward his stone. Goshin lifts, deflects the next punch and lands beside him. A defensive ki guard turns the next strike.
4. Gohan moves behind Goshin and grips his tail. His strength fails; he drops. Piccolo stops the round and Gohan lets go.
5. Piccolo orders them to repeat the tail approach. Goshin watches Gohan's shoulders, turns and traps his wrist before he can grip the tail.
6. Gohan changes direction. Goshin misses the next catch, recovers and lands inside the boundary. They continue practising. The success is learned defense, not permanent immunity.

## THE PROSE

Piccolo places a stone behind each boy.

"Your back touches your stone, you lose. No ki blasts. Begin when I say."

Gohan raises his hands. Goshin watches Piccolo's hands.

Piccolo steps toward Gohan and catches his punch on an open palm. He turns the wrist away and lets go.

Goshin takes Piccolo's place. Gohan punches. Goshin meets the fist, turns it and steps aside.

"Again," Piccolo says.

Gohan punches twice. Goshin turns the first strike. The second hits his shoulder and pushes him back a step. He resets his feet.

"Begin."

Gohan rushes him. Goshin lifts above the first punch and comes down beside him. Gohan turns and strikes again. A brief sheet of ki forms over Goshin's forearm. Gohan's fist strikes it and slides away.

Gohan ducks, moves behind him and takes his tail in both hands.

Goshin drops to his knees. His hands hit the dirt.

"Enough," Piccolo says.

Gohan lets go. Goshin gets up and faces him.

"Do that again," Piccolo tells Gohan.

Gohan moves in. This time Goshin watches his shoulders. When Gohan turns, Goshin catches his wrist and pivots away. His tail clears Gohan's hand.

Gohan pulls free and comes from the other side. Goshin reaches for the wrist and misses. Gohan's hand closes on his sleeve instead. Goshin twists out of it and lands just inside his stone.

"Again," Piccolo says.

The brothers take their places. Gohan brushes dirt from his knees. Goshin watches his hands.

## WHAT IT IS DOING

The order and the action carry the lesson. Flight, an observed deflection and a ki guard show the talent; Gohan's successful tail grip still has a consequence. A counter learnt in practice is not an anatomical change or a promise that the same attack can never work again. Both can grow.

# SPECIMEN B — the hillside

**WHAT THIS IS:** A fighter aims a broad ki blast into an unoccupied hillside. The exercise follows the attack's path and aftermath.

**SCALE:** A deliberate large attack at an empty hill, not an assigned battle-power value. Size, aim, distance and terrain establish this effect; it is not a universal ceiling for any fighter.

## WHO IS WHO

- **The attacker:** an unnamed fighter below the slope.
- **The target:** an empty stretch of hillside.

## THE FIGHT IN PLAIN WORDS

1. The attacker points up the slope and fires a broad blast.
2. The beam hits the hillside. Rock and earth break away from the impact.
3. Dust travels down the slope. When it settles, a large cut remains and loose rock continues to fall.

## THE PROSE

The fighter raises one hand toward the empty hillside.

Light leaves his palm and strikes halfway up the slope. The rock breaks at the point of impact. The beam cuts higher, tearing a wide channel through the earth.

The fighter lowers his hand. The light goes out.

Dust rolls down into the valley. Stones tumble through it and strike the ground. A slab breaks free near the top of the cut and follows them.

When the dust thins, the channel runs from the middle of the slope to the ridge. Loose earth keeps slipping into it.

## WHAT IT IS DOING

The target is named before the strike. The beam, impact, direction and aftermath are readable in one pass. The scale comes from the chosen feat and context, not a claim that every large attack must be charged for a set time.

# SPECIMEN C — flight and practice

**WHAT THIS IS:** Goshin watches Piccolo fly, copies the motion, then practises flying under his own control.

**SCALE:** Goshin is a growing child; the exercise does not assign him a new scouter reading. The highest flight here is a few feet above level ground.

## WHO IS WHO

- **Piccolo:** the teacher.
- **Goshin:** the pupil, who learns quickly and still needs energy and control.

## THE FLIGHT IN PLAIN WORDS

1. Piccolo rises and asks Goshin to hold his ki steady.
2. Goshin watches, lifts briefly, and lands hard.
3. He tries again, stays up, and moves to a marked rock when ordered.
4. He practises turns. Fatigue makes a landing unsteady, but he has retained the skill.
5. The next day he flies again and controls the landing better. A short ki pulse at a distant rock shows another technique outside the spar.

## THE PROSE

Piccolo rises a foot above the ground.

"Hold your ki steady. Lift."

Goshin watches his feet. He stands still and lifts a few inches. His knees bend as he comes down, and his hands hit the dirt.

"Again."

Goshin stands. This time he lifts to Piccolo's knee and stays there.

Piccolo points to a rock several paces away. "Go there."

Goshin moves toward it. He leans too far, corrects and stops above the rock.

"Back."

He returns. Piccolo makes him turn, stop and turn again. On the last landing Goshin's foot slips. He catches himself on one hand and takes a breath.

"Rest," Piccolo says.

The next morning Goshin lifts at once. He flies to the rock, turns above it and returns to the ground without using his hands.

Piccolo points past the clearing. "That rock. One small blast."

Goshin raises his palm. A short pulse crosses the empty ground and chips the rock. He lowers his hand.

## WHAT IT IS DOING

A demonstrated technique becomes usable and improves with practice. The earlier fall and later tired landing are real without requiring him to forget flight. The ki pulse is aimed away from the spar and its damage is shown, not converted from 400.

# Drills

1. Name the participants, location, goal and restriction before the action.
2. Write cause, movement, contact and consequence in order.
3. Declare known readings as snapshots and mark invented values as authored.
4. Show Goshin learning, flying and using techniques; keep Gohan active and growing.
5. Match collateral to feats, attack, condition, aim and surroundings—not a fixed damage-per-point rule.
6. Keep prose plain: no aphorisms, impossible-event commentary or description of what could not be seen.
7. Do not announce an obvious visual fact: have the authority order and the learner act.
8. Give training, injury, exhaustion and recovery consequences without erasing learned skills.
