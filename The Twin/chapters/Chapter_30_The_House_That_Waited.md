# Chapter 30 — The House That Waited

*Anime episode reference: Dragon Ball Z, Episode 37 (the ship and Jupiter test, adapted).*

## A Promise to Dad

"It was bigger than that," Gohan says.

Goku squints at the three stones on the rail. "How much bigger?"

"The whole stairway came down," Goshin says. He spreads his arms. "I held it up while Ren's crew put the supports in."

"And I went in through the window," Gohan adds. "The woman inside couldn't get to the stairs."

"You both did good." Goku tries to picture it. "I wish I'd seen you."

Goshin's eyes light up. His black hair sticks up like his father's, but the eager eyes he turns on Goku are Chi-Chi's. "You can see my wave! I promised."

Chi-Chi reaches for him as he hops off the rail. "Your side—"

"It's a little sore, but I can do this." He twists, winces a little, and immediately straightens. "I can!"

"I saw that face."

"It was only a little face."

Gohan laughs. Chi-Chi points a finger at both of them. "No fighting each other until the doctor has a proper look."

"I just want to show Dad." Goshin holds up his hands. "One wave."

Goku looks toward Vegeta. Krillin is already standing beside the prince, who is propped against the quarry wall with his damaged armour still on. Across the tracks, Tien and Chiaotzu watch Nappa.

"I'll keep an eye on him," Krillin says. "Go on."

"I'm not going anywhere," Goku says, but he grins at his son. "Let's see it."

Gohan rises above the ridge to check the sky. The city is far behind them; above the empty slope, there is room to aim away from the ground. He waves Goshin over and moves clear.

Goshin follows, lifting his bare feet from the broken stone. The quarry that had seemed so wide during the fight falls away beneath him, and beyond it he can see the road curling toward the city. His father is waiting below with his face turned up, exactly as Goshin had imagined during the long year in the room. This time Goku is really there to watch.

Goku leans forward, while Krillin keeps one eye on the prince and the other on the boys.

The air trembles when Goshin brings his hands together. Goku's smile widens. He had felt the change in his sons when he returned, but now Goshin lets more of his ki rise, and the broken rails hum underfoot.

Piccolo turns from the gully, his hand tightening on his cape. He has felt a power meant to destroy Earth once already today, when Vegeta fired his Galick Gun. Goshin's giant body is gone, but the ki that grew during the fight and transformation is still there. The force gathering in the boy could destroy the planet too. Goshin holds his hands steady and aims beyond the ridge, into the open sky.

"Watch, Dad! Kamehameha!"

Blue light bursts from his hands and climbs above the quarry. It washes the rocks and the faces below in colour. The column stretches through the high cloud and on, its leading edge lost against the sky. Goshin turns his palms, guiding it farther upward, then cuts it off. Its path glows for a moment after the light has vanished.

Far beyond the ridge, people in the city look up. At the quarry, the sound reaches the fighters, and loose grit dances along the rails. Chi-Chi catches the edge of her skirt as the wind rushes past.

Goshin stays in the air, leaning forward to see his father's face through the swirling dust. His hands are still tingling, and he already wants to try the wave again.

Goku lowers the arm he threw up against the wind. He stares at the sky, then breaks into a grin.

Goshin drops beside him. "Did you see it?"

"I sure did! You've gotten so strong! And you can steer it like that?"

"We practised in the room," Gohan says, landing next to them. "I can show you mine too."

Goku plants his feet and lifts his hands. "I'd love to see it up close! Come on, Gohan—let's have that match!"

"Goku," Chi-Chi says. "What did I just tell you?"

He looks at Goshin, who is rubbing his sore side, and lowers his hands. "Right. The doctor first. But I haven't forgotten our match, Gohan. I want a turn with both of you!"

"I want the winner," Goshin says.

"You haven't seen what King Kai taught me yet." Goku grins at both boys. "Want a look?"

He stays close to Vegeta and gathers his ki. Red light flickers around him; the power rising in it makes Piccolo turn. Goku darts three steps along the rail and back, settling beside Krillin before the grit stirred by his feet has fallen. He lets the red light fade.

"That was the Kaiō-ken! King Kai says it can hurt me if I push it too far, but it gives me a big jump in power."

Gohan's eyes widen. "You'll have to show me how you use it in a match."

"You bet!"

Goshin leans forward. "Can I learn it too?"

"I'll show you what King Kai taught me. I want to see what you can do with it."

Chi-Chi folds her arms. "After the doctor."

"Even me?" Goku asks.

"Especially you, if you start fighting them now."

Yajirobe peers at the sky. "If there's a winner, make sure he can still make dinner."

Goku laughs, but Vegeta has not joined in. He watches Goshin return to his father's side, and his hand closes over the broken edge of his armour. He remembers the number on the scouter before he clipped it to his armour, and the much smaller fist that hit him after both apes had changed back.

"Enjoy yourselves," Vegeta says. "You won't have me sitting here forever."

Krillin steps closer. "Then don't try anything."

Vegeta gives him a look that would have made Krillin happier from much farther away. Goku moves beside his old friend.

"He's right," Goku says. "You hurt anybody, we stop you."

For the moment, the prince stays where he is.

Piccolo crosses the tracks. "I'm going to see Kami."

Gohan turns at once. "About Namek?"

"That Saiyan gave me a name. I want to know what Kami has to say about it."

"I'm coming," Gohan says.

"Me too!" Goshin looks at Popo, then down at his bare feet. "Can I borrow some shoes first?"

"Popo will find shoes," Mr. Popo says.

"And something warm," Chi-Chi adds. "Both of you. If your shoulders or your side get worse, you come straight back."

"We will," Gohan says.

Goku glances from Nappa to Tien. "Can you watch him without Piccolo?"

Tien plants himself at the mouth of the gully. "We'll manage."

"I can hear you," Nappa growls.

"Good," Tien says.

Chi-Chi draws the boys close long enough to straighten Gohan's collar and push Goshin's hair out of his eyes. The hair springs straight back up.

"Don't bring home any more Saiyans."

"What if they're friendly?" Goshin asks.

"Especially then."

Piccolo lifts off before Goku can laugh at that. The boys shoot after him, vanishing over the ridge so quickly that Chi-Chi's skirt flutters in the wind they leave behind. Popo's carpet follows.

Goku watches the empty sky, then looks at Vegeta. "They've gotten fast."

"They're Saiyans," Vegeta says bitterly.

"They're my sons."

## The House in the Highlands

The Lookout is white against the blue sky. Kami stands beneath the trees as Piccolo lands with the boys and Popo.

He sees Piccolo's expression. "Something has happened."

"The big Saiyan called me a Namekian," Piccolo says. "He says people who look like us come from a planet called Namek."

Kami grips his staff. "Namek?"

"You know it?"

"No."

Piccolo stares at him. "That's all?"

The wind shifts the leaves overhead while Kami searches his memory.

"The name means nothing to me. But there is a place I remember."

"Yunzabit," Gohan says. "Popo told us you lived there when you were little."

Kami looks toward the edge of the Lookout. "I was alone there. I did not remember where I had come from. I thought I might have struck my head, but I could not be certain."

Piccolo has come expecting the guardian to conceal something from him. Instead, Kami sounds as though he is trying to recall a dream that has faded over many years. That makes the question no less urgent. The Saiyan had looked at Piccolo and known something about him that he himself did not.

"What about your mother and father?" Goshin asks.

Kami rests both hands on the head of his staff, looking past the children at the clouds. Popo has heard him speak of those years once before.

Long ago, the two of them stood together at the edge of the Lookout. Clouds drifted below the tiles, and Kami gazed toward the distant highlands.

"What do you remember from when you were small?" Popo asked.

"A house in a lonely place," Kami said. "I don't remember how I came to be there. There was a letter, telling me to wait for my family."

"Did they come?"

Kami's hand tightened around his staff. "No. But for a long time I believed they would."

In Kami's memory, a small green child sits inside a round room. He unfolds the letter again, tracing the words that tell him to wait. Beyond the doorway, wind passes over bare stone. He goes outside and looks along the empty ridges.

A light crosses the mountains. The child runs after it, stumbling over loose rocks, his heart pounding. He reaches the top of the slope in time to see the light disappear beyond the next ridge. Nobody comes toward him. After a while he walks back to the little house, with the cold wind at his back.

The seasons pass. The boy grows taller. He still searches the sky when he hears a sound, still goes out to the ridge and looks for someone coming. The place offers little to eat, and the letter grows soft along its folds from being opened so often.

At last, no longer a small child, he stands beside the house and looks out across the mountains. He has waited for years. He turns toward the path away from the hollow and begins to walk.

On the Lookout of years ago, Popo listened without interrupting.

"I went back sometimes," Kami told him. "I never found them."

Now Kami faces the two boys and Piccolo. "There was a letter. It told me to wait for my family; they would come for me. So I waited."

"Did they?" Goshin asks.

Kami shakes his head. For a moment Goshin has no answer. He reaches for Gohan's sleeve, and his brother's hand closes around his.

"I stayed for many years," Kami says. "Food was scarce, and no one came. At last I left. I returned sometimes, but I never found them."

Piccolo's jaw tightens. "And you never thought I should hear this?"

"There was a time when you would not have listened."

"You could have tried."

Kami meets his gaze. "Yes. I could have."

Piccolo's hand closes on the edge of his cape. He had come prepared to be angry at Kami, not to hear about a child who had waited alone.

Piccolo and Kami share a life, but that has never meant they share every memory.

Piccolo looks away first. "What did you live in?"

"A house. At least, that was what I called it. It was round, with four legs, and the entrance opened when I spoke a word."

"A word?" Goshin asks. "Like a magic door?"

"Perhaps you will recognize it." Kami looks at Piccolo. "The word was Piccolo."

Gohan blinks. Piccolo scowls.

"You're making that up."

"I assure you, I'm not."

Popo tilts his head. "Kami's home was very unusual. If the Saiyan is right about people from another world, it may not have been a house."

Goshin remembers the round objects in the city. "A spaceship?"

"It might be," Kami says. "I have not seen it in a long time."

"We should ask Bulma," Gohan says. "She knows about machines."

"And if it can fly, she can tell us," Goshin says. "I'll get her!"

"Popo will go too."

"Wait." Piccolo looks at Kami. "Are you taking us there?"

Kami's eyes return to his. "Yes. I think I should."

Popo disappears into the storeroom and comes back with a pair of boots for Goshin and warm wraps for both boys. Goshin is halfway through fastening the first boot when he notices Gohan watching him.

"You can have them when I grow out of them."

"Our feet are the same size."

Goshin looks down at the boot. "Then you'll have to wait until yours get smaller."

He stamps both feet, snatches up his wrap and shoots into the sky. He knows where Bulma's laboratory is, and Yamcha's familiar ki is there. Popo rises after him on the carpet.

## Bulma's Big Experiment

At Capsule Corporation, Bulma's tool case lies open on the workbench. She has left Dr. Mori at the city gym and brought Yamcha and Puar to the lab. The two captured scouters and their cells are still separated on the bench; Raditz's old device remains in its drawer.

Bulma has worked quickly, opening the casing far enough to trace the connections leading from its controls. The markings are unfamiliar, but the small transmitter reminds her of parts she has built herself. Finding a circuit is not the same as understanding the message it sends. She means to find out, and she has already asked the officers to move people away from both craters.

On the television, a reporter stands behind the cordon at one of the two landing craters. The round Saiyan craft sits beyond her, dust-covered and silent.

"Everyone's back from the crater," Yamcha says, lowering the telephone. "The officer says the other site has been cleared too."

"Good." Bulma fits a cell back into the scouter taken from Nappa. The lens lights up with unfamiliar symbols. She keeps it on the bench, turned away from everyone. "I'm not going to have a spaceship land on top of the recovery team."

Dr. Brief leans over her shoulder. "You know which control calls it?"

"I've traced the transmitter. I think this sequence sends an instruction to the ship." She looks at the device. "Which instruction is the interesting part."

"And the dangerous part," Puar says.

"I can't promise nobody else will hear the signal," Bulma says. "But if there's a way to call the ship, I need to find it." She holds up the scouter. "Nappa said he could call his ship with it. If I can make this one answer, we may not need to ask him how."

Yamcha watches the screen. "And if he was lying?"

"Then we'll find out." Bulma gives him a confident smile. "Honestly, you two. I do know what I'm doing."

Puar ducks behind Yamcha. Bulma sees him and rolls her eyes.

She presses the first control. The lens chirps. A second press changes the symbols; a third sends a sharp electronic tone across the room.

The lab falls quiet except for the reporter's voice and the thin electronic signal.

On the television, the craft flashes.

"There!" Bulma says. "It's responding!"

The reporter turns. The pod explodes in a great burst of light and debris. The camera jerks so violently that the picture becomes sky, ground, and then the astonished reporter's face.

Bulma's mouth falls open. She had asked everyone to get back, but for one sick moment she can only see the blast. Then the reporter comes upright again, waving an arm toward the crew beyond the cordon.

Yamcha is beside Bulma before either of them speaks. The camera catches people moving beyond the barrier, and the reporter calls for the emergency crew to remain back while officers look for debris. Bulma lets out her breath. The ship is gone because of her signal, and there may still be danger at the site.

"That didn't look like a landing," Yamcha says.

"I can see that!"

The reporter is speaking rapidly. The crew had moved beyond the cordon before the test, she says; officers are checking the area now. The second object is at its separate crater, out of the camera's view.

Bulma sets the scouter down as though it might bite her. "That must have been a self-destruct command. Or something that made the ship think it should destroy itself."

Dr. Brief adjusts his glasses. "There's still the other ship."

"And another scouter with controls I don't understand." Bulma looks at the darkened device. "I'm not pressing that sequence again."

A face appears outside the laboratory window.

Bulma jumps. "What—?!"

Popo raises a hand from his carpet. Goshin floats beside him, grinning.

"Bulma! We found something!"

She throws the window open. "Don't do that! I nearly broke the other one!"

Goshin looks past her at the television. Smoke hangs over the crater. "What happened to the first one?"

"It was an accident. A scientific accident." She points at him. "What did you find?"

"Kami lived in a round house with legs. Popo thinks it might be a spaceship!"

Bulma's irritation vanishes. "A spaceship?"

"Maybe," Popo says. "Come and see."

"Where is it?"

"Yunzabit."

Yamcha whistles. "That's a long way."

"Not on Popo's carpet," Goshin says.

Bulma looks from the carpet to the picture of the ruined pod. Then she grabs her jacket and a small tool bag.

"Dad, tell the officers exactly what happened. And tell them to keep everyone clear of the other ship."

"I will," Dr. Brief says.

Yamcha moves toward the window. "Want me to come?"

"Stay here and make sure he doesn't try the other scouter."

"Me?" Dr. Brief says.

"You were looking at it."

"I was looking at my daughter. She just blew up a spaceship."

"One more word and I'll take the good tools with me."

Puar giggles. Yamcha raises both hands. "We'll stay."

Bulma climbs onto the carpet behind Popo. Goshin sits on its edge, boots dangling. The carpet lifts from the window.

She takes a firm grip on its edge and looks down at the laboratory grounds.

"Not too fast," Bulma says. "I haven't had lunch, and I'd like to keep what I had for breakfast."

Popo nods. The carpet disappears.

The ground and buildings vanish with it. Bulma's protest carries through the sudden change of scenery.

At the Lookout, Gohan is still trying to convince Piccolo that a house with four legs really could be a spaceship when Bulma's shout reaches them from behind.

"I said *not too fast!*"

She steps off the carpet with her hair across her face. Goshin follows, laughing, and hands her the tool bag she dropped on his foot.

"It was fast," he says.

"I noticed."

Kami tells Bulma what he remembers. She listens to the description of the door, the legs, and the place where he waited, then looks at Piccolo.

"If Nappa knew what you are, that house might be how Kami got here."

"Might," Piccolo says.

"Then let's go and find out."

Popo makes room on the carpet for Kami and Bulma. The boys and Piccolo wait until the carpet vanishes.

They find Kami's ki far away and shoot after it. The sea flashes beneath them, then the dark sweep of land. Mountain ranges rush up and fall away before Gohan has finished turning his head.

The chill of the highlands hits them as they stop. Bulma is still complaining about the carpet when the three fighters land beside her.

## The Word

Yunzabit stretches away in ridges of bare stone. The wind cuts through Bulma's jacket, and she tugs it shut.

There is no road beneath the cliffs, only a dry channel winding between slopes where little grows. The boys hop down from a ledge to follow Kami, their wraps snapping behind them. Popo carries the folded carpet, and Piccolo takes the rear, watching the old guardian pick a path through the gravel.

Kami pauses at a bend and studies the ridgeline. The place has changed slowly, but the wind has worn away some of the landmarks he remembers.

"You lived here?" she asks Kami. "There isn't even a shop."

"There were no shops when I was a boy, either."

"That isn't helping."

Gohan points out the broken standing stone, and Kami nods as he recognizes the hollow beyond it.

Kami leads them around a rocky bend. In the hollow beyond it stands a pale, rounded structure, partly covered in windblown dirt. Four thick supports hold it above the ground. From a distance it looks like an enormous insect that has settled among the rocks.

Kami pauses in the hollow. Once he had waited here for people who never came; now Piccolo and the two boys are following him home.

Bulma runs to it. "Well, it doesn't look like any house I've seen."

Goshin presses a hand to the curved wall. "It's not stone."

"Or ordinary metal." Bulma taps it with a tool, listening to the note. "I need to see inside. Where's the door?"

Kami points beneath the rounded body. "Say the word."

Gohan looks at Piccolo. "You do it."

"Why me?"

"It's your name."

Piccolo folds his arms. "You say it."

"I want to hear you say it," Goshin says.

"I don't need to say my own—"

"Just open it!" Bulma says.

Piccolo gives the two boys a long look. "Piccolo."

A tone sounds from the structure. A circular platform lowers from its underside, stirring dust around their feet.

Goshin's mouth falls open. "It listened to you!"

"It listened to the word," Piccolo says.

"That's what I said."

They step onto the platform. Kami remains still for a moment, his staff against the smooth floor.

Gohan moves beside him.

"Does it look the way you remember?"

"Smaller," Kami says. "I was a child then."

He speaks the word again. The platform rises, carrying them into the rounded room.

Dust slips from the rim as the opening closes beneath their feet. Gohan catches Goshin's hand. In Raditz's pod, he had pulled at the locked hatch while Goshin lay hurt beside him.

"Can we open it from in here?" Gohan asks.

"With the same word," Kami says.

Goshin glances at Piccolo. "I could say it right now."

"Not while I'm standing on the door," Bulma says.

Light comes on above a curved seat and a panel covered in unfamiliar marks. Bulma moves straight to the panel, running her fingers above the symbols without pressing them.

The room curves around the low seat, leaving little space to walk behind it. The markings are set into the material rather than painted on, and a dim line of light runs from the panel toward the roof. Gohan studies a row of shapes while Goshin leans over the seat and peers at the space beneath it. Neither finds anything that looks like an ordinary engine.

"Look at this. The entrance still has power after all these years." She turns to Kami. "You really thought this was a house?"

"It was my home."

His answer takes some of the teasing out of her face. She looks around the little room. A child could have waited here for a very long time.

"Well," she says, more gently, "let's see what kind of home it is."

Piccolo stands by the seat. "The family you were waiting for. You don't know what happened to them?"

"No. I knew only what the letter said."

"And if they were on Namek?"

"Then perhaps there is a reason they could not come." Kami's fingers tighten on his staff. "I cannot tell you what that reason was."

Piccolo looks at the strange writing. The Saiyan's claim has led him here, to a room where another child once waited for people who never arrived. He wants an answer badly enough that it annoys him.

"Let's find out whether this thing flies."

"That's what I'm trying to do," Bulma says.

## A Language for Flying

Bulma tries one end of the panel, then the other. She crouches to look underneath it and emerges with dust on her cheek.

She has built enough machines to know that an unfamiliar control can be hidden in plain sight. She examines the spaces between the marks, tests a raised edge with her fingertip and listens for a response. Nothing moves. Even the entrance has no handle, and she keeps looking back at the place where the platform rose.

"No switches. No throttle. No steering wheel. Did the person who built this ever intend to use it?"

"The door listened," Gohan says. "Could the rest of it work the same way?"

Bulma pauses. "Of course!"

She stands in front of the panel. "All right. Start the engines."

Nothing happens.

"Turn on. Fly. Do something!"

The ship remains silent.

Goshin leans toward the panel. "Please?"

Still nothing.

"It's ignoring you too," he tells Bulma.

"Thank you. That helps a lot."

Popo considers the marks. "The entrance word is not a word we use every day. Perhaps the ship needs another language."

Bulma snaps her fingers. "At the tournament! Kami and Piccolo spoke to each other in a language nobody else understood."

Gohan looks at Piccolo. "You did?"

"We did." Piccolo glances at Kami. "I knew the words. I didn't know where they'd come from."

Bulma remembers the tournament. She is watching from the stands while Piccolo faces the strange man who entered as Hero. The crowd's noise ebbs as the two opponents speak.

Hero says something in words Bulma has never heard. Piccolo answers in the same language, his eyes fixed on the man across the ring. Hero's expression changes, but neither of them explains the exchange to the people watching.

Bulma leans forward, trying to make sense of it. The voices carry clearly enough across the arena; the words mean nothing to her.

Back inside the ship, she looks between Kami and Piccolo. "If Nappa was right, maybe that was the language of Namek."

"Popo learned words from Kami," Popo says. "The word for the entrance means another world."

Goshin looks at Piccolo. "Your name means another world?"

"Apparently."

"That's a good name for a spaceship," Gohan says.

"It wasn't named after the spaceship," Piccolo says.

"We don't know that," Goshin says.

"We're not naming the spaceship after me."

"Nobody asked you to," Bulma says. "Popo, can you tell it where to go?"

"Popo can try."

Bulma taps the panel. "Somewhere close enough for a test. Let's see whether it can get to Jupiter."

"Jupiter?" Gohan says. "The planet?"

"Do you know another one?"

Goshin presses up beside his brother. "I want to go!"

"Me too," Gohan says, then stops. "Mother thinks we're visiting Kami's old house. She doesn't know about a trip into space."

Goshin's tail droops. "She's going to say no."

"She'll definitely say no if we don't tell her."

Goshin looks toward the entrance. "Then let's ask before Bulma leaves without us!"

Kami joins them on the entry platform. He wants to see the old house lift from the ground, and there is someone waiting on Earth for the others to return. Popo speaks the door word; the platform lowers, and the boys wave to Kami before shooting into the air. They follow their father's ki back to the quarry, crossing the distance in a flash.

Chi-Chi looks up as they land. "What is it? Did somebody get hurt?"

"No," Gohan says. "Kami's house might be a spaceship."

"Bulma wants to fly it to Jupiter," Goshin says. "Can we go?"

Chi-Chi stares at them. "She wants to do WHAT?"

Goku's eyes light up. "Jupiter? Can I come too?"

Krillin points at Vegeta. "Goku."

"Oh. Right." Goku scratches his head. "I'll stay."

"You're all staying!" Chi-Chi says. "A moment ago they were going to ask a question, and now they want to leave the planet!"

Gohan takes her hand. "I want to see it, Mother. Bulma and Popo are there, and Piccolo wants to know about the ship too. We came to ask you first."

"It's a test," Goshin says. "We're not going to Namek."

"That is not as reassuring as you think it is."

Goku sees how tightly she is holding Gohan's hand. He remembers the year he missed and the danger she faced while he was away. "They won't be alone, Chi-Chi. And I'll be right here when they get back."

Chi-Chi looks toward the injured Saiyans across the quarry, then back at her sons.

"If that ship breaks, being here won't help them!"

"I know."

Goshin looks between his parents. "I want to go with Gohan. We can come right back after Bulma tests it."

Chi-Chi looks at both boys, then toward the sky. She had promised herself a quiet meal at home, and somehow the day has produced two angry Saiyans and a trip to Jupiter instead.

"You came all the way back to ask me?" she says.

"We didn't want you wondering where we were," Gohan says.

"I'm going to wonder the whole time!" Chi-Chi pulls him close, then reaches for Goshin too. She knows what they did against Vegeta. She also knows that knowing how strong they are did not stop her being afraid.

The boys wait without interrupting her, even Goshin holding still for once.

"Piccolo goes with you," she says at last. "And you come straight back. No wandering off to another planet because you saw something interesting."

Goku opens his mouth.

"That includes you if you ever get on it."

"I didn't say anything!"

"You were about to."

Gohan hugs her. Goshin joins him, nearly knocking Goku into Krillin.

"Thank you, Mother!"

"And when you get back, you're still seeing the doctor!"

"We know!" they call, already rising into the air.

Vegeta watches them vanish. "They have no idea what they might attract out there."

"They're testing the ship," Goku says.

"You think Earth has seen the worst the universe has to offer?"

Krillin glances at Goku, then back at the prince. "If somebody comes looking for trouble, we'll be here."

Vegeta gives a thin smile, but he says no more. Krillin keeps his hand near the pouch holding the last Senzu, and the watch continues.

## Jupiter

The boys return to Yunzabit with Chi-Chi's answer. Kami stands by the lowered platform while Bulma, Popo and Piccolo wait inside.

"I watched this place for my family," Kami tells them. "Now I will watch for you."

The boys step onto the platform, and Popo speaks the word again. As they rise into the cabin, Bulma leans over the panel to look at them.

"Well?" she asks.

"We can go," Gohan says. "If Piccolo comes too."

Piccolo raises an eyebrow. "Who said I was going?"

Gohan looks up at him. "Don't you want to see what it can do?"

Piccolo glances at the ship, then at Kami.

"This ship may tell me where I came from. I'm not leaving it to you two."

"That sounds like a yes," Goshin says.

Below, Kami rests one hand against the curved wall before stepping back with his staff. The platform closes above him, leaving the others in the little room.

Inside, the five travelers gather around the seat and the controls. Piccolo looks down through the viewing panel at Kami, who raises one hand in farewell.

Gohan's tail brushes the back of the seat. "Jupiter has moons. And ours is out there too. We should stay away from the window until we know what we're looking at."

Goshin's tail curls around his waist. "If we looked at a full one, we'd turn into giant apes. We wouldn't fit in here."

"I'll watch the window," Piccolo says.

Bulma settles into the curved seat. "All right. Jupiter. And make sure you know how to tell it to come back."

"Popo knows the words for Earth," Popo says. He speaks to the panel in the unfamiliar language.

Everyone in the cabin listens. Bulma keeps one hand against the seat, ready for whatever follows.

A tone answers. The markings glow.

The reply has come from a machine Kami once called home. Popo looks up at the glowing marks.

"It understood," Popo says.

The floor shudders. Outside, Kami steps away as the four supports lift from the ground. The ship rises over the hollow, hesitates, and then shoots into the sky.

Goshin grabs the edge of the seat, laughing. Gohan catches himself against the wall. Both boys keep their backs to the window while the blue overhead turns dark.

Piccolo checks the view. "It's Earth. No moon in the window."

The brothers turn. White clouds curl around the planet's shrinking curve. Goshin tries to spot the quarry, but it has already vanished among the shapes of land and sea.

"Can you see them?" he asks.

Gohan leans over his shoulder. "Not from here."

"Father will be looking up."

"Mother too," Gohan says. "She'll want to see us coming back."

Their parents are somewhere down there, and this time Goshin has something else to tell them when he gets home. As the ship turns away, Gohan takes his brother's sleeve and they face into the cabin again.

For an instant Bulma forgets to be frightened.

"It works!" Bulma cries. "It actually works!"

The ship surges again, and she slides halfway out of the seat.

Gohan catches her arm before she strikes the panel; Piccolo braces a hand against the wall and steadies them both.

Stars rush across the viewing panel as the ship finds its course. Bulma pushes her hair out of her eyes and stares, her annoyance momentarily overcome by the sight. She has built aircraft all her life; none of them has carried her away from the world she knows at anything like this speed. Beside the panel, Popo stands with his feet apart as calmly as he did on the carpet.

"Would it have killed them to put in a safety belt?" Bulma demands.

The panel beeps. A compartment opens beside her, producing a toilet.

There is a short silence.

"Why is that here?" Bulma asks.

"Your words sounded like a request to use it," Popo says.

Goshin clamps both hands over his mouth. Gohan tries to look serious and fails.

"Don't laugh!" Bulma says. "How am I supposed to fly this ridiculous—"

A second tone interrupts her. Light surrounds her, lifts her out of the seat, and deposits her on a bunk that has unfolded from the wall. A cover snaps neatly over her.

Piccolo watches her struggle to sit up. "Comfortable?"

"That last word sounded like bedtime," Popo says.

"Get me out of here!"

Popo speaks to the ship, and the cover releases. Bulma scrambles back to the panel, pointing at both boys before they can say anything.

"Not one word."

"We didn't say anything," Goshin says.

"Your face did."

Beyond the viewing panel, a vast striped sphere swells out of the darkness. The ship slows. The boys keep their backs to the window while Piccolo checks the view again.

"The planet fills the window," he tells them. "No moons in sight."

Gohan turns, and Goshin joins him. They stay where the clouds fill their view.

"That's Jupiter," Bulma says. For once, even she sounds a little breathless. "We made it."

Popo looks at the planet, then at the boys beside the window. He remembers Kami telling him about the little house and the family who never came. Now there is something new to tell him about the place he once called home.

Cloud bands stretch across the enormous planet. A storm turns among its clouds, big enough to make the Earth they have left behind seem small.

Gohan has read about the planets, but no book had prepared him for the size of the one filling the window. Goshin follows one of the cloud bands with his finger until it curves out of sight.

Goshin has crossed enormous distances on Earth, but he has never been so far from his parents. He reaches for Gohan's hand, and his brother squeezes it without taking his eyes off the planet.

"Do you think that storm ever stops?" Goshin asks.

"I don't know. We can look it up when we get home."

Goshin presses his palm to the glass. "Dad is going to be so jealous."

"Mother said straight back," Gohan reminds him.

"I know. I want to tell him about it."

Piccolo stands behind them, looking past their reflections into space. Somewhere beyond that darkness there might be a world full of people who look like him. He has no idea where it is. For the first time, he is looking at a way they might begin to find it.

Bulma grins at Popo. "We have a spaceship."

"Yes," Popo says. "We do."

Goshin turns from the window. "Can we see the toilet again?"

"No!" Bulma shouts.

The panel beeps.

"Oh, no," she says.
