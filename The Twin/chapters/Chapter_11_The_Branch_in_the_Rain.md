# Chapter Eleven — The Branch in the Rain

*Anime episode reference: Dragon Ball Z, Episode 10.*

*Revised story draft; not yet accepted.*

A length of red string hangs from the fork of a fallen tree. Beneath it, the river has left a dark line on the stones.

## A Telephone for C-6

Gohan turns another page of Mina's book. C-6's light follows the picture, although the writing is too small for him to see.

"That part is a wheel," Goshin says, putting a finger beside the drawing.

"I know what a wheel is."

Goshin spreads his arms. "It goes all the way around the machine."

C-6 looks again. "Then that's a very large wheel."

"I wonder where the driver sits," Gohan says. He turns the book sideways, but the other side of the machine still isn't in the picture.

Goku crouches beside C-6. "Hey, want me to call Bulma right now?"

"You can tell her about my arm and the energy leak. Tell her I want to speak to her before she opens anything."

"You got it."

The doctor brings a telephone directory to the open doorway. Goku finds Capsule Corporation's number, then lifts the receiver from the table inside.

"Bulma? It's me."

"Goku! Is everything all right?"

"We're all right. The boys found a robot under the riverbank, and we brought him out. His name's C-6."

Goku turns toward the yard. C-6's light is visible through the doorway.

"His arm got bent, and he says he has an energy leak. He doesn't know how much power he has left."

"Is he awake?"

"Yes. He wants to talk to you before anyone opens his panel."

"Good. I wouldn't start taking him apart without asking." A drawer slides open at Bulma's end of the call. "Can he get here? I need to see him before I can tell you anything."

"I'll ask him."

"Krillin went looking for Yamcha. One of my calls turned up a baseball game in the city, but I haven't reached Krillin yet. I'll be here for a little while."

"Thanks, Bulma."

Goku puts down the receiver and returns to the yard. "Bulma needs to see you before she can figure out what's wrong. She won't open anything without asking. Want me to take you over?"

"Will I be upside down?"

"Not if I carry you."

C-6 moves his working arm an inch. "Yes. Hold my body. Don't lift me by the other arm."

"All right."

Gohan closes the book around his finger. "Can we come too?"

"Sure! You and Goshin can come on Nimbus."

Mina's father comes back through the gate with a length of damp string. "I found this by the fallen tree. The other parcel was above it, caught in the branches."

"Is it still there?" Gohan asks.

"I think so. I couldn't reach it from the road."

Chi-Chi looks toward the dark clouds over the hills. "That bank gave way this morning. We aren't going back onto it."

"There's a higher path. We can see the tree from there."

"Then we'll look from the path. If it isn't safe, we'll leave the parcel."

"Maybe I can reach it from there," Goshin says.

Gohan looks at Mina's father. "We promised to help find the other books."

He has kept Mina's first book safe through the fall, but he remembers the one that slipped from his hand into the river. If they can reach the parcel, at least that one won't be lost too.

"Oh, yeah!" Goku says. "I'll take C-6 to Bulma and meet you after you've looked for them."

Goshin looks up at C-6's glass eye. "Can Bulma tell him how his eye works before she touches it?"

"Sure. I'll ask her," Goku says.

"I'll ask too," C-6 says. "I was using it before either of you found it."

Gohan smiles. "We'll bring the book when we visit you."

"If I'm awake."

"Then we'll read quietly."

Goku slips his hands beneath C-6's body and lifts him, keeping the damaged arm free. The glass eye turns toward the boys as he rises above the yard.

"Stay with your mom," Goku tells them.

Goshin waves as C-6's eye turns back toward him.

Chi-Chi waves until Goku and C-6 pass above the roof. Gohan keeps one hand on his hat, and the Four-Star Ball catches the light.

Nimbus waits above the gate after Goku has turned toward the city.

## The High Path

At the doctor's basin, Chi-Chi washes rice and puts it on the stove. Goshin helps her shape the cooked rice into balls. His first one comes out a little flat.

"It's rice," Chi-Chi says, "not a rock. You don't have to squeeze it so hard."

He tries again, carefully rounding the next ball between his hands. Chi-Chi wraps them in cloth, then cuts two apples for a second bundle.

"The apples too?" Goshin asks.

"Those are for all of us. The rice is for you and your father."

"Father's gone."

"Then he'll have to be quick."

Goshin glances toward the window and smiles. He takes the rice bundle she offers, holding it a little more gently than the first ball.

Outside, Ox-King has parked his truck beside the doctor's gate. He carries two boards toward the damaged bridge, where a pair of villagers are putting up a warning.

"I'll stay here and help," he tells Chi-Chi. "You shouldn't have to carry those books over a broken bridge twice."

Mina's father leads them along the road above the river. Goshin peers over the stone wall at the damaged bridge, then hurries after Gohan.

Below them, the wagon has been pulled clear of the bridge. A roof tile lies half buried in the mud, with water running across it in thin lines.

"Mina has the two books you brought back," her father says. "She's trying to dry every page at once."

"Did she find the one about flying machines?" Gohan asks.

"That's the one you have. She's counting on you to read it first."

Gohan pats the bag at his side. "I'll bring it back when C-6 has seen the pictures."

A shallow pool sits beside the path where the hillside has caught some of the rainwater. A fish turns beneath the surface, then noses against a stone.

"How did it get up here?" Goshin asks. "Did it climb?"

"The river was much higher last night," Mina's father says. "It must have been left behind when the water fell."

Gohan kneels at the edge. The fish slips away from his first attempt, splashing his sleeve. He waits until it turns, then catches it gently in both hands.

"Can we take it home? Father could cook it."

"If we get home before supper," Chi-Chi says. "Goshin, find me a leaf to wrap it in."

Goshin chooses a broad one from the branches beside the wall. Chi-Chi wraps the fish and puts it in a separate bag; Gohan wipes his wet hands on the grass.

Farther along, the fallen tree reaches from the road toward the water. Mina's parcel hangs in its upper branches, well above the broken bank.

Gohan starts toward the string, then stops. A snake lies along the branch beneath it.

"It's sleeping," he whispers.

"Then let it sleep," Chi-Chi says. "Can we reach the string from here?"

Goshin crouches for a better look. "I don't think he'd like us waking him up."

Gohan thinks of C-6's switch and smiles despite the snake. Mina's father offers him a long stick; he hooks it around the string and draws the parcel toward the road. The branch bends with it.

Goshin reaches out. His ki catches the branch and holds it still while Gohan frees the string. One end tears away and stays caught in the fork. The parcel drops into Gohan's arms without disturbing the snake.

"We found it!" He turns the wet package over, looking for a tear, but the paper has held.

As Gohan backs away, a thorn catches the inside of his wrist. He pulls free and holds up his hand. A narrow scratch runs across the skin.

The sight brings back another afternoon. He had fallen on the path with Father and scraped his knee.

Goku crouches beside him, rubbing a leaf between his fingers until its juice comes out. "This'll help. It might sting a little, though."

Gohan grips his trouser leg. "A little?"

"Let's find out."

Goku presses the crushed leaf gently against the scrape. Gohan jerks and lets out a yelp. His father holds it there a moment longer, then lifts his hand so Gohan can look.

"See? It's on. Can you walk?"

Gohan tests his leg, sniffs and takes Goku's offered hand. Chi-Chi is waiting farther down the path. He tells both of them about the sting all the way home.

Now Gohan looks up from his scratched wrist. "Father put a leaf on my knee when I fell once. It stung."

"I remember hearing about it all the way home." Chi-Chi opens her water flask and rinses the scratch. She takes a clean cloth from her bag and winds it loosely around his wrist.

Thunder sounds above the trees. Mina's father takes the parcel and looks up at the darkening sky.

"There's a stone shelter beyond the bend," he says. "The roof is part of the hillside."

The first drops strike the road as they hurry toward it. Gohan keeps the book beneath his shirt, and Goshin runs beside him.

## The Ballpark

A ball lands beyond the outfield fence, and the crowd rises in a wave of noise. Yamcha sits at the end of the bench with a bat across his knees. His long dark hair falls around his face beneath his baseball cap; the scars on his cheek are visible when he turns toward the field.

Puar, a small blue, catlike figure floating beside him, looks from the ball to Yamcha. "That was your third home run."

"I know."

"Most players would be happy about three."

"Most players are baseball players. I'm a martial artist."

The scoreboard shows six runs for the visiting team and three for Yamcha's. Two runners stand on the bases while the next batter approaches the plate.

Puar settles on the bench beside him. "You said you needed the money."

"I do. I didn't say I needed to enjoy the uniform."

He tugs at the brim of his cap. The crowd is still cheering, but there hasn't been a decent opponent in his path all afternoon.

A shout comes from the far end of the bench. The coach is pushing between the players, waving a scorecard over their heads.

"Yamcha! If this batter reaches base, you're up again. We need one more from you."

"One more what?"

"Home run, of course! The bases will be full."

Yamcha lifts the bat from his knees. "That sounds important."

"It is!"

"How important?"

The coach grips the scorecard. Yamcha looks at the field and lets the bat rest against his shoulder.

"Two million zeni," he says.

"Two million?"

"For a home run with the bases full."

The coach's face changes twice before he puts out his hand. "Get those runs and we'll discuss it."

"We just did."

Puar covers his mouth with one paw. The coach hurries to the rail, where he signals to the batter.

The runners take their leads from first and second, watching the pitcher.

The pitcher sends the ball across the plate. The batter leans toward it, but the umpire calls a strike. The pitcher looks from him to the coach and rolls the next ball in his fingers.

"He's trying to get hit," Puar says.

"Looks that way."

The next pitch strikes the batter's helmet. He throws the bat down and charges the mound. The pitcher meets him halfway, and both benches empty onto the field.

Yamcha gets up and leans his bat against the rail.

"Please don't," Puar says. "Somebody will get hurt."

"I'll hold back."

Yamcha grins for the first time that afternoon and plunges into the brawl. He ducks under one player's arms, turns another by the shoulders and sends him stumbling into a teammate. Both sit down hard in the grass, too surprised to keep swinging.

"See?" Yamcha calls to Puar. "Holding back!"

A punch whistles past his shirt. He bends aside and catches the man's wrist before it reaches the player behind him.

Then a hand closes on Yamcha's shoulder. He turns and raises his free arm, but the hand moves with him. For an instant he feels the difference between this grip and the ones he has been dodging.

He stops. "Krillin?"

"Hi, Yamcha. Can we talk somewhere without a baseball bat coming at us?"

Puar flies over the players and lands on Yamcha's shoulder. Yamcha retrieves his bat from the rail, and they leave with Krillin through the gate by the bench while the umpire blows his whistle behind them.

The coach runs after them. "Yamcha! Where are you going?"

"Something's come up. I can't take the bonus for a hit I didn't make."

"The game isn't finished!"

"Then you'll have to finish it without me. I'll speak to you about the rest when I can."

The coach stares at Krillin's orange clothes. "Is this about another tournament?"

"I wish it were," Krillin says.

He waits until the coach has gone back through the gate. Puar turns toward him, holding the edge of Yamcha's collar.

"What happened?"

"A Saiyan came to Earth," Krillin says. "He was Goku's brother. He attacked Goku and both of his sons."

Yamcha lowers the bat. "Where's Goku?"

"He's alive. He was hurt badly, but I brought a Senzu from Korin. He and the boys are with Chi-Chi now."

"Good," Yamcha says quietly. He keeps hold of the bat, but his grip loosens. For a moment the noise from the stadium seems a long way off.

Puar lets go of the collar. "And the Saiyan?"

"Piccolo killed him. Before he died, he told us two more are coming. He said they're stronger than he was."

The crowd roars again. Yamcha looks through the gate at the field, then sets the bat against the wall.

"How long do we have?"

"About a year from when he told us. Kami sent a message. He wants you, me, Tien and Chiaotzu to come to Korin Tower so we can prepare."

"Kami? He'll train us?"

"That's what Popo and Yajirobe said. I still have to find Tien and Chiaotzu."

Yamcha takes off his cap and runs a hand through his hair. "All right. Let's go."

"What about your things?" Puar asks.

"Can you get my bag?"

Puar flies back through the gate. Yamcha waits until Puar disappears around the bench, then turns to Krillin.

"You said Piccolo fought with Goku."

"Against the Saiyan, yes. Afterward he tried to take the boys. Goku and Chi-Chi aren't letting him."

"So we can't count on him."

"Not the way things are now."

Puar returns with a canvas bag between both paws. Yamcha takes it, and Krillin looks toward the city beyond the stadium.

"Bulma was making calls from her lab. She'll want to know I found you."

Puar's ears lift. "Bulma?"

Yamcha shuts the bag. "Of course she will."

## Under Stone

The shelter stands above the river, with a floor of solid rock and a roof that slopes down from the hillside. Rain reaches the family before they do: fat drops strike the path, then join into a grey curtain across the entrance. Inside, the stone smells cool and damp, and the river can be heard rushing below.

Mina's father puts the parcel on a dry ledge. The paper has come loose at one corner, exposing the cover of a book.

"The string held," he says. "Mina will be pleased."

Gohan lifts Mina's other book from under his shirt. Only the edge is damp. He gives it to her father, who checks the pages and returns it to the bag.

"You promised to read it to C-6. You can keep it until then."

Chi-Chi unwraps the rice and apples on the ledge. Goshin takes a rice ball, and Gohan chooses a piece of apple. Outside, rainwater runs down the face of the fallen tree.

Something tears leaves beyond the opening. Gohan turns in time to see a long neck reach around the shelter and take an apple piece from the ledge.

He freezes with his own piece in his hand. The dinosaur takes a second and chews it while rain runs down its back.

"Those were for all of us," Goshin says.

Chi-Chi pulls the remaining food closer. "You can have one, but leave something for us."

The dinosaur lowers its head again. Gohan puts his apple on the ledge and steps back. Its wide mouth closes over the fruit, and a drop of rain falls from its nose onto his shoe.

Chi-Chi moves the bags away from its feet.

"The book I read at home said some dinosaurs eat plants," Gohan says. "I didn't know they'd eat Mother's apples."

"You should have asked the author," Chi-Chi says.

Goshin offers a leaf at arm's length. The dinosaur closes its mouth around it, then pushes farther beneath the shelter.

Its body knocks against the stone. The dinosaur gives a low cry and sinks onto its front legs.

Gohan moves around the head and sees a broken branch caught in a fold of skin along its side. Blood has darkened the hide around it.

"Mother, it's hurt."

Chi-Chi comes beside him. The dinosaur pulls away when Gohan reaches for the branch, and its tail scrapes against the wet rock.

"Wait until we can see how deep it is," she says.

Goshin holds still. The dinosaur turns its head to watch them.

Mina's father keeps the dinosaur's attention with another apple piece. While it eats, Gohan looks at the wound from both sides.

"The end is here," he says. "It went through this loose part."

Chi-Chi leans close enough to see the broken tip. "Hold that branch still while I get a cloth."

Goshin crouches beside the dinosaur.

He steadies the branch without pressing it farther into the hide. Gohan touches the dinosaur's neck and waits until it stops pulling away.

"I'm going to take it out," he says. "I know it hurts."

The dinosaur shifts its weight. Gohan waits again, then draws the loose branch free. Chi-Chi folds a clean cloth against the wound, and the animal flinches but does not rise.

Gohan looks at the cloth. "Would Father's leaf help?"

"That was for a little scrape. I don't know what it would do to a dinosaur." Chi-Chi keeps the cloth in place. "This needs someone who knows animals."

"There's a veterinarian near the village," Mina's father says. "I'll go and ask her to come."

"Take the high path," Chi-Chi tells him. "The bank is still loose."

He pulls his coat over his head and sets off through the rain. Gohan holds an apple piece where the dinosaur can reach it, while Goshin folds another cloth beside Chi-Chi.

The dinosaur eats slowly. When its head comes down again, it rests beside Gohan's knees. He can feel its breath through the damp cloth of his trousers.

"It's all right," he tells it, keeping the apple where it can reach.

Goshin sits close enough to help Chi-Chi but gives the animal's tail room to move. When it closes its eyes, Gohan stays still so it won't have to lift its head again.

## A Place to Look

Goku lands outside Capsule Corporation with C-6 held against his chest. Bulma opens the lab door before he reaches it.

"Bring him through here. I've cleared a table."

"Is the table strong?" C-6 asks.

"It held an engine I dropped on it last week."

"That's what someone said about the floor of my temple."

Bulma raises an eyebrow. Goku lowers C-6 carefully onto the table, and she holds up a folded pad. "May I put this beneath your arm?"

"You may. Don't pull it straight."

She places the pad beneath the bent arm, leaving it where it rests against his side.

"Can I look at it? I won't pull it."

"You can look."

She moves a lamp closer, and C-6's light turns away from it.

"Is that too bright?"

"Yes."

Bulma moves the lamp back. She studies the arm and the seams around the glass without reaching for a tool.

"I'm not familiar with this model," she says. "What series did you say you were?"

"An SSS-series capsule robot. I was working at an excavation when the roof came down."

"A long time ago," Goku adds.

"About eighty years," C-6 says. "I was counting before the sand got in."

Bulma bends closer to the glass. "Do you know where the energy leak started?"

"I heard it inside my head when I used the reserve. I can't hear it now, but that doesn't mean it's repaired."

"Then I won't tell you it is. I'd like to listen around the casing and check the arm from outside first. After that, I can explain what I think needs opening."

C-6's light moves between her and Goku. "From outside first."

"All right."

Goku looks at the glass. "Goshin asked if you could tell C-6 how his eye works before you touch it."

"I can tell you what I find," Bulma says. "I don't know how it works yet."

"That's better than saying you do," C-6 replies.

A door opens behind them. Dr. Brief comes into the lab with a small cat perched on his shoulder. He looks at the table, then at Bulma.

"Have you brought home another machine?"

"He's C-6. He's been buried for about eighty years, and he doesn't want his panel opened without being asked."

Dr. Brief takes his glasses from his pocket. "That seems fair. May I come closer, C-6?"

"As long as the cat stays where it is."

The cat curls its tail around Dr. Brief's collar. He stops beside the table and peers at the bent arm.

"I can make a support for that without moving it. Would you like one?"

"If you tell me when you're finished."

"I will."

Bulma wraps the scouter with its loose cover and puts them in a drawer. The cell goes into the next drawer.

"Is that Raditz's scouter?" Goku asks.

"In the first drawer. The cell is in the second. I still don't know whether it can transmit, so I'm keeping them apart."

A voice carries in from the hall.

"Bulma?"

Krillin enters with Yamcha and Puar. Goku turns from the bench as Puar flies ahead and circles him once.

"You really are all right!"

"Thanks to Krillin," Goku says.

Yamcha puts his bag down. "He told me about the Saiyan. I'm glad you made it."

"Me too."

Bulma looks at Yamcha's uniform, then at the bag. "So you were at the stadium."

"I needed a job."

"You could have told me that when you missed our date."

Puar drifts behind Yamcha. Krillin finds a place for his hands inside his sleeves.

"I was trying to earn enough to take you somewhere nice."

"A telephone call would have been cheaper. I waited."

Yamcha opens his mouth, then shuts it. "I should have told you. I'm sorry."

Bulma looks away toward the open lab door. "All right. We've got more important things to do today."

Puar's shoulders come down. Krillin takes his hands back out of his sleeves.

Krillin takes a breath. "Korin has a message from Kami. We're supposed to gather at the tower. Yamcha's coming."

"What about Tien and Chiaotzu?" Goku asks.

"I haven't found them yet. We'll keep looking."

Yamcha folds his arms. "Krillin says Piccolo fought Raditz with you. Does that mean he'll fight the others?"

"I don't know," Goku says. "He fought Raditz with me, but he was after my boys again as soon as the fight was over. If he tries to take them, I'll stop him."

"Fair enough."

Goku grins. "Hey, Kami told me about someone even stronger than him! His name's King Kai. I'd love to train with him and see what he can do!"

Goku throws a quick punch into the air, stopping short of a stack of jars on the workbench.

Bulma slides the jars farther away.

"Can you?" Krillin asks.

"Yemma's asking him. I hope he says yes!"

C-6 turns his eye toward the voices. "Are the two of you going somewhere dangerous?"

"We're going to prepare," Krillin says. He touches the small bag inside his shirt. "I've still got one Senzu if someone needs it."

Dr. Brief sets a padded support beside C-6's arm. "May I slide this beneath your wrist? I won't straighten the arm."

"You may."

He fits the support in place. C-6 watches until Dr. Brief steps back.

"Finished," Dr. Brief says.

"Good."

Bulma takes a capsule from a rack by the door. "I'll get the aircar ready. Krillin, you can tell me what you know on the way."

Yamcha picks up his bag. "I'm going with you."

"I heard."

Puar follows them outside. Goku waits beside C-6 while Dr. Brief draws up a chair.

"Do you want to stay here while they have a look?" Goku asks.

"If the doctor keeps telling me what he's doing."

"I can do that," Dr. Brief says.

Goku rests a hand on the edge of the table. "I'll bring Gohan and Goshin back to see you."

"Bring the book too. Gohan had just reached the part about the tunnel."

"I will. They'll be happy you want to hear the rest."

Outside, Goku rises above the buildings and turns toward the village. The aircar opens below him, its roof unfolding as Bulma presses the capsule switch.

## Toward the Tower

Yamcha settles into a seat behind Bulma. Puar floats beside him, and Krillin climbs in with the bag still tucked inside his shirt.

The aircar lifts over the city. Bulma turns it toward the mountains, where Korin Tower stands beyond the haze.

"Two Saiyans stronger than Raditz," she says. "That doesn't leave us much room for mistakes."

"We have time to train," Yamcha says. "Goku's alive, and Kami is going to help us. That's better than waiting for them to arrive."

"If Kami can make us strong enough," Krillin says.

"We'll find out."

Puar leans forward between the seats. "You sound happier than you did at the game."

"I haven't had a good fight in a while."

"Yamcha," Bulma says, "you haven't met these Saiyans yet."

"I know. I want to be ready when I do."

Bulma glances at him in the mirror. "Then take the training seriously."

"I will."

Krillin looks from one of them to the other. "You two are getting along again."

"We're discussing training," Bulma says.

"That's what I said."

Puar hides a smile behind both paws. The aircar passes over a line of clouds and continues toward the tower.

## The Second Footstep

The rain begins to thin. Chi-Chi checks the cloth against the dinosaur's side while Gohan looks toward the road.

"How long will it take Mina's father?"

"Long enough to reach the village and find the veterinarian. Keep the food where this one can see it."

Goshin puts another apple piece on the stone. The dinosaur's tongue curls around the fruit and draws it away.

Gohan imagines the rain stopping and the dinosaur rising easily to its feet. In his daydream, the wound is gone. The animal lowers its neck, and he climbs onto its broad back.

"Come on!" he calls to Goshin.

The dinosaur sets off over the hill, with Goshin running beside it. Its stride is so long that Gohan has to hold on with both hands. He laughs as Goshin catches up and grabs the end of its tail for a few bouncing steps.

They reach an apple tree. The dinosaur stretches up, takes a branch in its mouth and gives it a shake. Apples tumble around them. Gohan catches one and offers it to the dinosaur, but it reaches past him and takes the next one straight off the branch.

"What are you smiling at?" Goshin asks.

Gohan looks down at the animal resting beside him. "I was thinking about what we could do if it gets better."

He glances toward the path again; the dinosaur hasn't tried to stand yet.

Something strikes a tree beyond the shelter. Goshin turns toward the sound.

A second impact shakes water from the branches. The dinosaur lifts its head, and a low sound comes from its throat.

"Is that the one from yesterday?" Gohan asks.

The carnivore pushes through the trees below them. Its head is nearly as wide as the shelter entrance, and mud clings to its teeth. Wet ferns flatten beneath its feet. Behind the boys, the injured dinosaur draws its neck close to the wall.

The fallen tree lies between the path and the river, leaving a clear patch of ground in front of the shelter—but nowhere for the wounded animal to run without passing the carnivore.

"It looks like the one that followed us by the river," Goshin says.

The wounded dinosaur tries to rise. Its front legs slide on the wet stone, and Chi-Chi catches the cloth before it falls away.

"Gohan, get back from the entrance."

Gohan steps beside her. The carnivore lowers its head and pushes into the shelter, reaching past him toward the wounded animal.

"Leave it alone!" Gohan shouts.

Nimbus drops from above the roof. Gohan jumps onto the cloud, and it carries him between the dinosaurs as the carnivore's jaws close around the edge of the other's hide.

Gohan plants both hands against the lower jaw and pushes. The teeth come apart, and the wounded dinosaur pulls free. Chi-Chi guides its head toward the back of the shelter, bracing her feet when its front legs skid.

The carnivore swings its tail across the entrance. Goshin raises one hand. His ki catches the tail before it reaches Chi-Chi and drives it down against the wet ground.

Mud splashes over his shoes. The carnivore twists its tail free and turns toward him. Goshin shifts to the side, drawing its attention away from his mother and the injured animal.

"This way," Gohan calls.

He steers Nimbus across the clearing. The carnivore follows, but its next step carries it toward the shelter again. Goshin moves to the opposite side, keeping the injured dinosaur behind him.

"Away from the shelter," Gohan says.

Goshin points toward an empty patch of ground beyond the animal. A narrow burst strikes the earth there, sending up dirt. The carnivore turns from the shelter toward the open path.

The carnivore's tail lashes the tree as it turns. A heavy branch breaks above Chi-Chi, and Goku drops through the rain to catch it. He sets it against the hillside.

"Is everyone all right?"

"We're all right," Chi-Chi says. "The dinosaur needs help. Mina's father went for an animal doctor."

The carnivore wheels around again. Goku steps between it and Chi-Chi, leaving the path clear for Gohan.

Gohan reaches the ledge and takes the wrapped fish from Chi-Chi's bag. "Father, I have an idea!"

"Hey, that's our supper!"

"I know."

Goku plants one foot in the mud, keeping the carnivore's attention while Gohan opens the leaf. Gohan tosses the fish down the path, beyond the fallen tree. The carnivore turns after it, picks it up and disappears between the trunks with the fish in its mouth.

Gohan watches the branches move until they settle. Nimbus comes down beside him, and he gets off. He keeps a hand on the cloud until his legs feel steady.

"You gave it your fish," Chi-Chi says.

"I didn't want it to come back for this one."

Goku looks down the path. "It'll still be hungry later."

"I know." Gohan turns toward the wounded dinosaur. "But it didn't have to eat our friend."

Goshin goes to the entrance and looks after the carnivore. The empty path is visible as far as the fallen tree.

Goku kneels beside the injured animal. "Mina's father told me where you were. He was almost back to the village."

"Did C-6 see Bulma?" Gohan asks.

"He did. He said they could look from the outside first, and he's staying with Dr. Brief while Bulma takes Krillin and Yamcha toward Korin Tower."

"Is his arm fixed?"

"Not yet. Bulma said she'd explain before opening anything."

Gohan lets out his breath. "We still have Mina's book for him."

A vehicle sounds on the road above the shelter. Mina's father appears through the trees with a woman carrying a large case. She comes down the path, stopping when the dinosaur lifts its head.

"I'm Sato," she tells Chi-Chi. "He said an animal was hurt."

Sato sets down the case and rinses her hands from a flask.

"A branch was caught through the loose skin here," Chi-Chi says. "Gohan took it out. We've held a clean cloth against the wound."

Sato kneels where the dinosaur can see her. Gohan offers the last apple piece, and the animal stretches toward it while she checks the cloth.

"I'll clean this and look for anything else that might be caught in the hide. It needs time and a safe place to rest."

"Will it get better?" Gohan asks.

"I hope so. I can't promise that from a first look."

Mina's father sets down the parcel. "There's a covered field above my house. We could leave the gate open and see whether it will follow us there."

"Let's finish here first," Sato says. "We shouldn't make it walk until we've seen what it can manage."

Goku helps her bring the case nearer without crowding the dinosaur. Chi-Chi stays at its head, and Gohan holds the apple until Sato has finished examining the wound.

---

## Three Stones

By the time the rain stops, Sato has cleaned the wound and placed a fresh dressing over it. The dinosaur is standing, although it favors one side. Mina's father opens the gate to the sheltered field, and the animal follows him slowly toward the grass.

"I'll come back and check it," Sato tells Gohan. "For now, let it rest."

Gohan watches from outside the gate. The dinosaur lowers its head and tears at a patch of leaves beside the fence.

"It found something else to eat," he says.

"And we lost our fish," Goku says.

Chi-Chi raises the wrapped rice. "Not everything."

Goku leans toward the bundle. "Oh, rice balls!"

"You're still full from the Senzu," Chi-Chi says.

"Yeah, but your rice tastes a lot better than beans!"

"You can help me make more when we get home."

"Then we'd better make a lot!"

Goshin takes the rice bundle from her and hands it to Goku. "Can we practise aiming again? I held the branch for Gohan."

Goku unwraps a rice ball and takes a bite, looking toward an empty patch of ground beside the road where the rain has pressed the grass down. Three stones lie there, with a few green blades standing between them.

"You bet! Hey, Gohan, want to try too?"

"Yes. I want to get better too."

Goku flicks a loose leaf into the air with his foot and catches it before setting it between two stones. He presses a third stone onto one end so the breeze will not carry it away.

"Touch the leaf without moving the stones," he tells Goshin.

Goshin smiles and stretches out one finger.

He plants his feet. Light gathers at the end of one finger and crosses the gap. The leaf lifts at its free edge, then settles between the stones.

Goku crouches to look. Neither stone has moved.

"Good. Try the other end now."

Goshin leans toward the pinned end and changes the angle of his hand. The second flash touches the pinned end of the leaf without moving either stone. Goku grins and takes the stone away, letting the leaf slide into the grass.

"Yeah! You hit the end you were aiming for without moving the stones!"

Gohan searches the ground and finds a flat pebble. He sets another leaf upright in a crack in the road, then throws. The pebble strikes the leaf and carries it into the empty space beyond the stones.

"I was aiming for the middle."

"You hit it," Goku says. "Try it again while the wind's moving it."

Gohan replaces the leaf. This time he waits until the breeze lifts one edge, then throws the pebble through its centre.

Chi-Chi watches from beside the gate. "When we get home, there's a book waiting on the table as well."

"Mina's?" Gohan asks.

"Your schoolbook. You can read Mina's to C-6 when we visit."

Gohan retrieves his pebble. "Can I keep practising too?"

"Yes. We'll make time for both."

He looks toward Mina's book, tucked safely in its bag. "Do scholars get to dig up temples?"

"Some do," Chi-Chi says. "After a great deal of studying."

Gohan imagines the carving under the riverbank and the side of the machine the picture never showed. "Then I could make a book that has both sides."

"I'll draw the machines," Goshin says.

"And I'll read it!" Goku reaches for another rice ball. "As long as you tell me what the hard words mean."

Beyond the gate, the dinosaur pulls at a branch. Goshin picks up the practice leaf and puts it in his pocket.

Goku pops the last rice ball into his mouth and wipes his fingers on his trousers.

"Those are good! Hey, we can catch another fish on the way home."

"Two," Gohan says. "The dinosaur ate the first one."

Goku grins. "Good idea! Let's get a big one."

"You can clean them this time, Goku!"

"Okay!"

The red string hangs from the fork of the fallen tree. Below it, the river runs beneath a clear sky.
