## Editorial correction

- Reviewed the selected sequence in context for the recurring issues, with particular attention to domestic scenes, family decisions, established abilities and the changed Saiyan battle. Revised Chapters 1, 6–20 and 22–24. This is a substantive targeted audit, not a guarantee that no literary or continuity problem remains.
- Removed more instances of the assistant-created patterns: Goshin need not repeat instructions, wait for Goku to invite him to speak, take a prescribed position, or watch every ingredient before eating. He initiates conversations, helps, jokes, feels and makes decisions. A situational pause, lesson, observation or expression of concern is not itself prohibited.
- Varied Chi-Chi's actions and relationships rather than treating supervision or scolding as her permanent role. In the selected Chapter 20, the brothers go to the clearing and choose their Piccolo exchanges while she works at home; they tell her afterward, and she cares for Gohan's shoulder. She remains active in later training and in the city evacuation. In Chapter 16 she and Bulma help free the net while Goshin holds the branch without treating ordinary wind as a threat to his established strength.
- Preserve Gohan's independent flight before the chamber, Goshin's first Kamehameha after observing Goku and his earned subsequent mastery and growth, the brothers' full lived chamber year, Piccolo's immediate recognition of their very powerful ki on exit, and their comfortable post-year spars with him. Do not introduce an unsupported Nappa ceiling, guaranteed canon casualties, fixed growth multiplier or automatic victory against Vegeta.

## Chapter 24: current continuity

Vegeta scans approaching signals at **9,200** and **12,400**, then identifies them as Gohan and Goshin respectively. Knowing both exceed his own strength, Nappa attacks civilians to draw them in. Goshin diverts the shot, catches Nappa's punch and decisively knocks him into the crater. Gohan clears debris and helps civilians escape; when Nappa tries for Goshin's tail and threatens those fleeing, Goshin and Gohan each restrain a wrist. Vegeta orders Nappa to stand down. Neither Saiyan behaves as though the gap does not matter.

At the quarry Nappa reads Gohan at **9,200** and Goshin rising to **13,600** as Goshin releases some ki he already held in reserve. Vegeta orders six Saibaimen against the other defenders to test how the brothers respond. The brothers quickly defeat one creature each with short bursts. Vegeta then attacks Piccolo and Krillin; Gohan and Goshin combine Kamehamehas to redirect the attack into empty sky. The scouter reads **11,600** for Gohan and **15,200** for Goshin while they charge these defensive waves, **not** while defeating the Saibaimen. These are concentrated-technique snapshots rather than ordinary baselines. The collision loosens the quarry rim. Goshin catches and carries the slab to safety while Gohan clears smaller falling debris; watching Vegeta, Goshin keeps more previously held-back ki flowing, and the scouter holds at **16,300** at that moment.

Four Saibaimen remain alive, one pinned by Goshin, and Vegeta is gathering light for another attack. Nappa and Vegeta have observed the boys' changing output and are choosing tactics accordingly; the device cannot tell them whether a change is newly developed power or previously held reserve. Goku is still on Snake Way; Yajirobe has begun descending Korin Tower. Some civilians have been rescued and damage and injury are shown, but complete evacuation and total casualties are unknown. Piccolo hears the Namekian claim; Kami is not shown hearing it.

The figures are authored, condition-specific branch snapshots, not source-canon values or demonstrated maxima. Original-Z caption readings include Goku at 7,000, 8,000 and over 8,000 in the relevant sequence (`C130`); an official manga-based Nappa profile describes Goku comfortably evading Nappa before using Kaiō-ken to intervene (`C240`). Those sources inform the common-sense portrayal of a substantial gap without requiring the original fight's outcome in this changed story.

## Author correction: output is not automatic growth

The preceding unaccepted Chapter 24 incorrectly presented the rise after one wave and an easy rescue as a fresh, retained increase. The author rejected that implication: Goshin had not been releasing all the power he already possessed. The revised scene shows him releasing more of it as the threat continues; **16,300 is sustained output at that moment**, not a new permanent base or a maximum. The **15,200** reading belongs to the defensive Kamehameha charge. Changing output, focusing a technique and developing new capacity are different things; a scouter reading alone does not identify which happened (`O187`, `O188`).

The easy Chapter 23 Piccolo spar had the same unsupported implication. It now shows Goshin using speed and ki developed in the chamber and previously held back, not gaining a new permanent increase merely by sparring. Both boys still comfortably outclass Piccolo, and the substantial gains from their full year and the earlier Chapter 21 training remain.

This is a correction to these scenes, **not a restriction on Goshin's Talent or anyone else's growth**. His innate whole-being adaptation and potential for very rapid, substantial combat growth remain. Anger and being pushed hard can be relevant for him or others, but neither is mandatory; development can also occur in other situations. There is no imposed numeric ceiling, fixed rate, mandatory exhaustion or rule that only extreme circumstances can produce growth (`O188`).
