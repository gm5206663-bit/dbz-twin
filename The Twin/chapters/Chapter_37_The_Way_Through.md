# Chapter 37 — The Way Through

*Anime episode reference: Dragon Ball Z, Episode 44 (the impostors' aftermath, departure and arrival, adapted) and Episode 45 (the approaching threat, in part and changed).*

## Beneath the Ship

Bulma moves her light along the underside of the ship. Goshin follows it on his hands and knees, feeling the weight above him and the places where the landing feet meet the rock.

"That one's holding," he says.

"So is the next one." She angles the light toward a narrow bracket. "Wait. Can you see that pin?"

Goshin leans closer. One end of the pin sits farther out than the other. The metal around it is scratched.

"It moved when the cliff fell?"

"Maybe. Or when we put down here." Bulma reaches for it, but the weight on the foot holds the bracket tight. "If it works loose in flight, I'd rather not find out where it lands."

A few steps away, Gohan stands between the ship and the two strangers. Zākuro has pointed out a strip of metal beyond the black rocks, but the mist still hides most of it. Raiti is watching Bulma.

"It's only a pin," he says. "Your ship can fly without a foot."

"Then you won't mind waiting while I put it back."

Piccolo stands near the hatch, where he can see both strangers and the pool. The animals that came out of the water have not returned. He looks toward the ridge, checks the part of the sky above it and then returns his attention to the ground around the ship.

Goshin places his hands against the lower rim. "I'll lift this side. Tell me when it's enough."

The hull rises under his hands, and the feet lift from the ground. Holding the ship level, he gives Bulma room to slide the pin back into place. She fits the little locking piece over the end and tightens it with a tool.

"A bit more," she says.

Goshin raises the whole ship another inch while Bulma checks the joint from underneath.

"That's it. You can put it down."

He lowers the ship gently onto its feet. Bulma tugs at the bracket, then stands and brushes dust from her knees.

"I still want to check the others before we leave, but this one's secure."

Gohan looks toward the metal edge. "Can we go and see it now?"

Bulma closes her case. "Yes. All of us except Piccolo."

Piccolo gives her a look.

"I'd rather have you beside the ship than leave it alone," she says. "If there's trouble, you can see the path from here."

"I'll stay." He looks at the brothers. "Keep your eyes down until we know what the rest of the sky is doing."

Gohan nods. Goshin's tail curls around his waist as he picks up Bulma's case for her. His black hair rises in spikes above a child-sized face so like Goku's, though his eyes are Chi-Chi's. Neither boy looks above the ridge.

Raiti starts toward the black rocks. Zākuro follows, limping a little on the ankle the shore animal caught. Gohan walks beside him; Bulma and Goshin come behind them. The dry path curves away from the water, and the white hull remains visible between two rocks until they reach the mist.

The metal edge belongs to a much larger piece of wreckage. A curved wall lies half buried in grit, with a ragged opening torn along its lower side. Beyond it is a second section, its surface scored and darkened. A bent framework protrudes from the ground like the ribs of an enormous animal.

Gohan puts a hand against the wall. It is cold and rough. His fingers stop at the metal rather than passing through it.

Zākuro watches him. "Satisfied?"

"It's here," Gohan says.

"I said it was."

"You said a lot of things."

Zākuro opens his mouth, then shuts it again.

Raiti ducks through the torn opening. Bulma stops at the entrance and shines her light inside. A cloth has been stretched across one end to keep the mist out. Beneath it are a low container, a pan and a stack of narrow packages. A wire runs from a box on the floor up to a rod fixed outside the broken wall.

There are no chairs at the controls. The remains of the control panel hang crookedly above a floor split down the middle.

"This is where you've been living?" Gohan asks.

"When the weather turns bad," Raiti says. "There's a hollow higher up too. The water doesn't get into that one."

Goshin peers at the pan. "What do you eat?"

"What we find. Roots. Things from the water." Raiti lifts the pan. "Zākuro can make a decent meal if he stops complaining long enough."

"You complained first."

"Because you burned it."

Goshin smiles despite himself. The smell rising from the pan is unfamiliar, but it is food, and there is a little left at the bottom.

Bulma crouches near the split floor. She runs her light along the exposed edge and into the darkness beneath it. A pipe has torn away from its fitting, and another section of the hull is folded inward around it.

"Can you mend it?" Gohan asks.

"Not with what I have in my case." She touches the damaged pipe. "I'd have to find out what's broken farther inside, and some of this would need replacing. I'm not going to tell you it can fly just because I haven't found every problem."

Raiti's shoulders stiffen. "I told you we couldn't use it."

"You told us your home was on the other side of a valley. You'll have to put up with me looking."

Outside, something clicks in the wind. The rod on the wreck has a loose vane at its top. A dial in the box moves a fraction, settles, and then begins to drift again.

Zākuro follows Bulma's gaze. "That's what we used to watch the current. We brought the sensor out of the ship."

"Show me. Don't touch my case."

He kneels beside the box and points to a row of marks. He keeps his hands where she can see them.

"The needle moves when the current shifts. Sometimes the mist comes down at the same time. There can be a space between the currents then."

"Can be?" Bulma asks.

"It doesn't open every time we get fog."

"That would have been useful to mention."

Zākuro grimaces. "We wanted you to hurry."

Bulma watches the dial for a moment. Then she opens her case and takes out a small tester. When she places its lead against the sensor's connection, a light flickers on the tester. She changes the setting; the flicker changes with it.

"There's a signal," she says. "That doesn't tell me what's making it."

She looks back through the mist. The lower curve of their ship is visible beyond the rocks. "I want to compare it with our readings."

"I'll go," Goshin says.

"I'll come too." Gohan turns to Zākuro. "Will you show us what to look at if it moves?"

Zākuro hesitates. Raiti looks at the ship, then at Piccolo's figure beside it. Whatever he is thinking, he stays by the wreck.

Together they carry the box as far as its wire allows. Gohan holds it while Zākuro points out the marks. Bulma walks back with Goshin to the ship, stopping beside Piccolo at the hatch.

She speaks a command into the recorder. The panel just inside the open doorway lights. Lines appear across the display, including the two separated course markers she has been watching since the warning began. One of them has shifted from the position she last recorded.

Goshin looks from the panel to Gohan. "It moved there too!"

"A little," Gohan calls. He holds the box steady as Zākuro adjusts the dial.

Bulma makes a note. She waits through another small movement, watches the ship's display change and asks Zākuro to turn the dial back. The two instruments do not show the same thing in the same way, but both respond over the next few minutes.

"There's a change out there," she says. "I still can't tell you the whole shape of the current."

"You can get through," Raiti calls.

"I can try a route when I have one I like."

"And if you wait too long, it closes."

"Then I'll make up my own mind before it does."

Gohan brings the box back to the wreck with Zākuro. He remembers the false wind roaring around them while a stone lay motionless at his feet. Bulma has touched the wires and checked her own panel, but the two strangers can still show them things that are not there. He cannot tell how much either test would reveal if they tried again.

He looks at Zākuro. "Are you keeping your promise?"

"About your thoughts?"

Gohan nods.

"Yes."

Gohan wants to believe him. He does not know how to check.

## What They Can Leave

The mist grows thicker around the wreck, curling over the rocks until the water is hidden. Bulma and Piccolo stand by the ship while the others return along the dry path. Raiti carries the sensor box; Zākuro has wound up its trailing wire.

"When the line moves past that mark," Zākuro says, "there should be a space above the ridge."

"You keep saying should," Bulma replies.

"You keep saying you don't believe me."

"I'm learning from experience."

Gohan stops beside the ship. "If we get through, what happens to them?"

Raiti's mouth tightens. "We wait for another ship. That's what you were going to do."

"We could take them with us," Gohan says. "At least until we find somewhere else."

Piccolo looks at the strangers. "They tried to leave us here and take the ship. If they do the same thing while Bulma is flying, there may be nowhere for us to land."

"I know." Gohan looks down at the torn sleeve Zākuro is still carrying. "But there isn't another ship here."

"We don't know when we'll find another place to stop," Bulma says. "I packed for the four of us, with some extra, and we've already shared part of it. Two more people would use the air and water faster. I don't know how long the trip ahead will be."

"I can eat less," Gohan says.

Goshin turns to him. "I can too."

"This isn't only about food." Bulma puts her hand on the hull. "They tried to take the controls. They can make me see and hear things that aren't there, and I can't check a promise they make about it. I won't put you both in that cabin with them while I'm trying to fly."

Raiti takes a step forward. Piccolo moves between him and Bulma.

"You don't know how long we've been here," Raiti says.

"No," Bulma replies. "I don't."

"We may never see another ship."

"I'm not saying you'll be all right. I'm saying I can't promise to take you."

"You could, if you wanted to."

The words sting because the ship is standing right there. Bulma could open the hatch. She could let the two men in. She imagines the cabin filling with another false storm while the boys are strapped in beside her, and she keeps her hand away from the door command.

"Not after what you did," she says.

Zākuro looks at Gohan. "You pulled me out of the water."

"I wanted to."

"And now you're leaving us here."

Gohan's face grows hot. He wants Zākuro to understand that those are not the same choice, but he also understands why the man is angry. He looks at the wreck and the mist around it.

"Can we help you another way?" he asks.

Raiti laughs once, without humour. "Bring us a ship."

"If we find people who can come here, I'll ask them."

"You won't find us again."

"I can keep the readings," Bulma says. "And the route we took to get here. I don't know whether they'll be enough for another ship to follow, but I'll save them."

Piccolo turns toward the wreck. "Does that box send anything, or only receive?"

Zākuro lowers the coil of wire. "It sent a signal before the aerial broke. We tried to fix it."

"Show me," Bulma says.

She goes with them to the wreck again while Piccolo stays by the hatch. Gohan and Goshin accompany her. This time Zākuro leads her to a cracked fitting near the broken wall. A length of metal has been bound to it with wire, but the connection beneath the binding is blackened.

Bulma takes it apart, cleans the contact and replaces a short piece of wire from her case. Zākuro steadies the aerial while she works. When she asks for the old binding, he offers it to her rather than reaching for her tools.

"The power cell is weak," she says. "You can use it for a test, but I don't know how long it will last."

Raiti brings a second cell from beneath the cloth. "This one's better."

"Why didn't you say so?"

"You didn't ask."

Bulma gives him a flat look and fits the cell. She sends Goshin back toward the ship with her tester while Gohan remains near the box. When Zākuro presses a control, the tester's light flickers in Goshin's hand. It stops when Zākuro releases the control and returns when he presses it again.

"I got it!" Goshin calls.

"That proves something reached you there," Bulma says. "It doesn't prove anyone beyond the current can hear it."

"But somebody might," Gohan says.

"Somebody might."

She watches Zākuro set the box down. "The people who hear it might not be friendly. We met children whose world was attacked by people who took planets. I don't know who's out here or what they'll do if they find you."

Raiti's expression changes. "Then why fix it?"

"So you can decide whether to use it."

Zākuro looks at the control beneath his finger. He takes his hand away.

"We'll wait," he says. "If we see a ship, we can try it then."

"You can send a signal whenever you choose," Bulma says. "That's all I can tell you about it."

Back at their ship, she brings out two sealed food packs and a full water container. She checks the remaining supplies before closing the storage compartment. There is less in it now; she does not pretend otherwise.

Raiti accepts the packs. He turns one over, trying to work out how it opens.

"Pull there," Goshin says, pointing to the tab. "Not too hard."

Raiti pulls. The top tears open, and a square piece of food slips into his palm. He sniffs it suspiciously.

"You don't have to eat it now," Bulma says.

"I know that."

He takes a bite anyway. Goshin waits for his reaction.

"It's sweet," Raiti says.

"That's the good one."

"You gave me the good one?"

"There were two good ones."

Zākuro sits on a rock to rest his ankle. Goshin goes over to him, leaving space between them.

"You could have asked us," he says.

"You said that already."

"I meant it. I liked talking to you."

Zākuro looks at the strip of torn sleeve in his hand. "Would you have given me the ship?"

"No. It belongs to Kami, and we're using it." Goshin thinks about the little drawing in the dirt, the six feet he added to make the ship safer. "But we could have tried to help without you making things up."

"Your friend has just told us she won't take us."

"I heard her."

Goshin wants to argue for another answer. He also saw the stone Zākuro sent at Bulma and the catch moving beneath his hand. He sits beside the rock for a moment, not looking at the sky.

"I'm still going to ask," he says. "If we find someone with a ship."

Zākuro folds the torn cloth and puts it away. "All right."

It is not forgiveness, and it is not a way off the planet. Goshin gets up when Bulma calls him to help check the last landing foot.

## The Watch on Earth

At the quarry, Chi-Chi sets a fresh container of food beside Goku. The prince is still awake against the stone wall, and Krillin has positioned himself where he can see Vegeta's hands.

Across the broken tracks, Tien and Chiaotzu remain with Nappa. Chi-Chi can see the larger Saiyan from where she stands, though she cannot hear what he says to them.

Goku opens the container and smiles. "You made enough for everyone."

"I made enough for people who have been standing out here all day. Whether the Saiyans want any is up to them."

Krillin takes a portion, then carries another toward the gully for Tien and Chiaotzu. Goku waits until he is back before sitting down, keeping Vegeta in sight.

Chi-Chi notices the loose cover above the prince. It has shifted on its supports.

"That won't stay there if the wind gets under it," she says.

"Or if he decides to pull it down," Krillin adds.

Vegeta's tail lies along the stone. Its tip flicks.

"I don't need your scraps of cloth."

"We'd rather you didn't look up at a full moon by accident," Goku says. "You could make another light yourself, so we'll still be watching you."

"Do you think I don't know what I can do?"

"I know you do."

Goku rises and lifts a long slab of fallen rock from the ground. Krillin stays beside Vegeta while Goku sets the slab against a higher part of the wall, making an overhang above the place where the prince is sitting. A gap remains at the front; it is shade, not a locked room.

Vegeta watches the slab settle. He could move from beneath it, and the people guarding him know it. For the moment, he stays where he is.

"You'd be better off watching your sons," he says.

Chi-Chi's hand closes on the edge of her food container. Goku steps back into his place beside Krillin.

"Piccolo and Bulma are with them."

"That green man has no idea what's waiting beyond this planet."

"Have you fought any of them?" Goku asks. "Are they stronger than you?"

Chi-Chi looks at him, and he gives her hand a quick squeeze. He wants to know who might be out there with their sons. He wants to meet those fighters himself, too.

Vegeta studies him. "Bring me my scouter."

"No."

"Then find out for yourself."

Goku is silent for a moment. He remembers Vegeta saying there were powerful fighters out in space, and he still wants to meet them. He also remembers the prince aiming at Chi-Chi and the boys.

"If there are people who might hurt them, tell me," he says. "I'll listen."

"I don't give away things for nothing, Kakarot."

Krillin glances toward the gully. "We're still not handing you the scouter."

Vegeta closes his eyes, though his hand remains braced on the rock beside him. Goku does not mistake the closed eyes for sleep.

Chi-Chi takes a small wrapped parcel from her bag and puts it down beside Goku. "That was supposed to go in with the boys' things."

He unwraps one corner. "A nail clipper?"

"I found it after they left. I was halfway to the door before I remembered there isn't a delivery service all the way out there."

Goku looks at the little parcel and then at her. "Bulma probably has one."

"Probably isn't the same as knowing."

"No," he agrees. He folds the wrapping closed again. "Gohan will look after Goshin. And Goshin will look after him."

"They're both going to look after themselves too." Chi-Chi sits beside him for a moment. "I just wish I could hear them tell me so."

Goku puts his hand over hers. The quarry is quiet enough for him to hear the cloth rustling under the stone overhang. He keeps watching Vegeta while he eats.

## The Opening

By the time Bulma finishes checking the landing feet, the line on the ship's panel has moved again. The second instrument shows a change as well. Outside, mist presses low against the rocks.

She draws a route in her notebook, compares it with the old course and crosses out her first line. Then she tries another.

"This takes us between the markers," she says. "At least, between where I think they are now."

"You can see it from above," Zākuro says. "The gas moves apart."

"I hope so."

Gohan goes back to the wreck with Zākuro. Raiti follows with the food and water. At the broken wall, Zākuro moves the repaired box beneath the cloth, keeping its wire connected to the aerial. He does not press the transmitter control.

"We'll look for someone who can help," Gohan tells them.

Raiti does not answer. He is staring toward the ship.

Zākuro touches Gohan's arm. "If you do find someone, tell them about the water. Don't let them land beside it without looking."

"I will."

They walk back together as far as the black rocks. Piccolo calls Gohan's name from the hatch, and Zākuro stops there.

"Your ankle?" Gohan asks.

"Still attached."

Gohan smiles. "Good."

Raiti comes up behind Zākuro. "Your ship goes faster than ours did. If you're looking for another planet, you might find one before the next opening here."

"Do you know where our planet is?" Gohan asks.

"No. We only saw what you wanted in your thoughts."

It is the first time Raiti has said it so plainly. Gohan nods and returns to the others, keeping his eyes on the path.

Goshin is already in the doorway. He raises his hand toward Zākuro. After a moment, Zākuro raises his in return. Raiti stands beside him with the opened food pack under one arm.

Bulma speaks the door command. The hatch closes between them.

Inside, the window cover remains latched. The boys take their seats while Piccolo stands beside Bulma at the panel. She enters the course she has chosen and checks it once more against her notes.

"Everybody ready?"

"Ready," Gohan says.

Goshin fastens his belt and puts his hand against the floor. He can feel the weight settling onto the four feet. The pin he helped Bulma secure is holding.

The ship lifts. For a moment the pressure passes cleanly through the hull. Then the floor trembles beneath his palm.

"The right side's moving first," he says.

Bulma changes the angle. The vibration lessens, though it does not disappear. A tone sounds, and the course markers slide across the panel.

"That's the edge," she says. "Come on."

The cabin tilts. Gohan grips his seat. A cup left on the table starts to slide, and he catches it with his ki before it reaches the edge. He sets it down against the wall, where it cannot roll into Bulma's feet.

Goshin reaches farther. Beyond the hull, he feels a piece of rock spinning through the disturbed space ahead. It is broad enough to strike much of the ship's front. Smaller fragments turn with it.

"Something's in the way!"

Bulma looks at the panel. A shape flashes across the display. "Can you move it? I'm keeping us on this line."

Goshin extends his ki through the hull, as he did among the meteors near the children's ship. He catches the broad rock before it reaches them and pushes it aside. The rock turns, scattering the pieces around it.

One fragment continues toward the lower side of the ship. Gohan feels it a moment later and reaches for it himself. His ki meets the stone beyond the wall. He changes its direction sharply, sending it away from the hull.

"Got that one," he says.

Goshin grins at him, then turns his attention back to the larger mass. More pieces are still tumbling in its wake. He guides them clear, holding several apart while Bulma pilots through the space they leave.

The force answering him grows as he works. He can feel it in his arms against the floor and in the ki surrounding the rocks. The larger piece moves farther than he first intended, disappearing from the display; he turns the remaining fragments away without losing track of the ship or his brother beside him.

"We're through the first marker," Bulma says. "One more."

The cabin shudders again. Piccolo braces a hand on the panel but leaves the controls to her. Gohan keeps his ki outside, watching for another fragment, while Goshin feels the broad pressure along the hull.

The tone changes.

Bulma holds the course until the second marker passes behind their position. The shaking eases, then settles into the familiar hum of the ship.

No one speaks for a moment.

"Did we get out?" Goshin asks.

"We're past the part that was blocking the route," Bulma says. She studies the panel a little longer. "The warning has stopped. I'm not calling the whole trip safe, but we got through that."

Gohan lets out his breath. "You moved all of those at once."

"You got the one underneath. I didn't see it."

"I felt it after you called out."

Goshin lifts his hand from the floor and flexes his fingers. The added strength he felt as the rocks turned is still there, settled through his body and ki even after he stops pushing. He leans forward and presses his palm against the floor again, pleased by how readily his energy answers him.

Piccolo looks at the brothers, then at Bulma. "Can you find the course we entered before the current?"

"I saved it." She brings the earlier line onto the panel. "I'll check it against the readings we have now before I give the ship the command."

Gohan looks toward the covered window. Raiti and Zākuro are somewhere below, beside a damaged ship and a transmitter that may or may not reach anyone. He cannot see them now.

"Don't forget the readings for their planet," he says.

"I won't." Bulma closes the notebook around the pages. "They're here."

Over the following days, Bulma checks the course and the supplies while the ship carries them onward. Piccolo goes over the words Popo taught them, repeating a command until Bulma is satisfied the recorder has caught it clearly. They know what the ship does when it hears the word; they do not yet know whether a person on Namek would use it in the same way.

Gohan takes out the folded letter he and Goshin brought from home. On an empty corner, Goshin draws their ship above a rough line of rocks. Gohan adds the broken outline of the wreck, and Goshin puts two figures beside it.

"That one's Raiti," he says, pointing to the figure on the left.

"You drew them the same height."

"He's standing farther away."

Gohan looks at the picture again. "Oh. Then he's much taller."

They leave the letter where they can find it and take turns helping Bulma watch the panel. None of them knows yet whether someone at their destination will have a ship to spare.

## A Planet in the Records

Far from the travellers, Cui returns to Freeza with a chart.

"An old survey, Lord Freeza. The name matches the one in the recording."

Freeza studies the markings. "Namek. How helpful of the Saiyans to discuss it where someone could hear them."

"The record is incomplete," says Zarbon, the green-haired attendant standing at his side. "We would need to confirm the world for ourselves."

Dodoria folds his arms. "If there are people on it, they can tell us what they know."

"Yes," Freeza says. "I think we should ask them."

Cui waits until Freeza looks up. "And Vegeta?"

"Continue looking for news of him. I would like to know what became of him on Earth."

"If he's still alive, I'll be happy to deal with him."

"I'm sure you would." Freeza's smile is small. "First, find him."

His chair turns toward the open doorway. Zarbon takes the chart, and Dodoria follows as Freeza leaves the chamber.

In the days that follow, their ship travels to Namek, the world marked on the old survey. It descends through a green sky above wide stretches of water and pale rock. The Namekians on the ground are not expecting it.

Freeza watches a soldier's scouter as scattered readings appear: twenty-four, fifty-eight, one hundred and thirteen. A distant figure moves between a cluster of rounded buildings, too far away for Freeza to question from here.

"There are inhabitants," Zarbon says.

"Then we have somewhere to begin." Freeza rests one hand on the side of his chair. "Send the scouts. If they find people, I want to know where. And if they find another ship, I want to hear about that too."

Dodoria grins. "What if someone gets in their way?"

"They may defend themselves. I would prefer to have someone left to answer our questions."

The scouts leave. Freeza turns his attention back to the readings, looking for a place to start.

## The World Ahead

Later, Bulma brings their ship down toward the same world. She has no way to know what Freeza has already sent there.

The panel shows broad water, pale land and dark round shapes along a shore. Piccolo stands beside her while the brothers face the closed cover over the window. Bulma compares the ship's position with the course she saved before the false landing.

"This is where that route led us," she says. "The ship's responding to the destination we entered, but after the last planet I'm not naming it just because it looks right."

Piccolo watches the shoreline grow. "We can ask the people here."

"If there are people."

"King Kai sensed them in this direction."

"Then let's find out."

She chooses a level stretch of pale rock beside the water and brings the ship down. The landing is firm enough to make the cups rattle, but the feet settle without the scraping sound she feared.

Goshin puts a hand on the floor again. "All four are down."

"Good." Bulma checks the panel, then starts the atmospheric sampler she fitted to the ship. She watches the result and repeats the test before opening the hatch.

"There's oxygen. The sample doesn't show a problem with the air, but that isn't a guarantee about everything out there."

Piccolo steps out first. Bulma follows him onto the hatchway, looking up and around. The sky above the shore is green, and round blue trees stand beyond the rocks. Piccolo checks the visible sky from the landing place. There are bright discs above the distant water, but he cannot tell the boys that every unfamiliar object in this sky is safe to look at.

"Keep your eyes on the ground for now," he tells them. "We'll find some cover and check again."

Gohan and Goshin come down the steps with their tails close around their waists. Gohan studies the blue-green ground and the water lapping against pale stone. The shapes of the rocks remind him of the wild places where Piccolo trained him, although the colours are different.

"It feels a little like where we practised," he says.

Piccolo looks at the shore. "Some of it does."

Goshin crouches to touch the ground. A small plant bends beneath his finger and springs up again. He tries it a second time, then looks at Gohan.

"This one's really here."

Gohan touches it too. "Unless we're both being fooled."

"Don't start," Bulma says, but she is smiling.

She takes the radar from her bag and fits the power cell back into it. The screen lights. She switches it off, waits and turns it on again. This time she hears a short beep, and several points appear on the display.

"There are signals," she says.

Gohan comes closer. "Could they be Dragon Balls?"

"They could be. I made the radar for the ones on Earth, and we haven't seen what's making these signals. After last time, I'm not calling them balls until we find one."

Piccolo turns toward a low line of hills. He can feel distant presences in several directions, though he cannot tell who they belong to from here.

"There are people somewhere ahead."

"I can feel them too," Gohan says. He pauses. "Some are moving."

Bulma slips the radar back into her bag. "Before we go anywhere, I want to try something."

Inside the ship, she opens her case and takes out a compact communicator. She connects it to the lead she and her father fitted for the ship's aerial before they left Earth, checks the frequency and presses the call control.

Static fills the cabin. Goshin leans close enough to see the little indicator pulse.

"Is that home?"

"I'm trying."

She adjusts the connection. The noise rises, breaks, and becomes a thin ringing sound.

Gohan's eyes widen. "That's our telephone!"

The ringing stops.

"Hello?" Chi-Chi says.

For an instant neither boy moves. Then Goshin crowds beside Bulma, and Gohan reaches the communicator from the other side.

"Mother!" they say together.

There is a sharp intake of breath on the line. "Gohan? Goshin? Are you both all right?"

"We're all right," Gohan says. "We landed on a planet, but we don't know yet if it's Namek."

"Did you eat?"

"Yes," Goshin says. "Bulma made us stop before we could have all the sweet things."

"Because you wanted them for breakfast, lunch and supper," Bulma says.

Chi-Chi laughs, and Goshin hears the strain in it. "Is Piccolo there?"

"I'm here," Piccolo says from the hatch. "Both boys are with me."

"Thank you." Chi-Chi takes another breath. "Have you been watching the sky?"

"Piccolo checked," Gohan says. "We're keeping our eyes down outside."

Bulma tells her about the difficult landing on the previous world, the two stranded people and the supplies and transmitter they left. She does not call the strangers trustworthy or say a rescue is already arranged.

"Gohan and Goshin want to ask for help if we find anyone who can go back," she says. "I saved what I could of the route."

"Then ask," Chi-Chi says. "And tell me if you need help from here."

"We will."

Goshin presses closer to the communicator. "How's Dad?"

"At the quarry with Krillin. Vegeta's still there, and Tien and Chiaotzu are watching Nappa. Your father's fine. I'll tell him you called."

"Tell him we got through the current," Goshin says. "Gohan pushed a rock away from the ship!"

"Goshin moved a bigger one," Gohan says.

"Tell him that too," Goshin adds.

Chi-Chi laughs again, more easily this time. "I will. And when you come home, you're both telling me everything before you go running off to show him."

"We can tell you together," Gohan says.

"After supper," Goshin says.

"Before supper," Chi-Chi replies.

"Then can we eat while we tell you?"

"We'll see."

Gohan is still smiling when he feels two of the distant presences change direction. They are coming closer. He steps toward the hatch.

"Piccolo?"

"I feel them."

Goshin turns from the communicator. The two are moving quickly toward their landing place. He can feel Gohan beside him and Piccolo at the doorway, and farther away, the unfamiliar pair.

"Mother," Gohan says, "there are two people coming. We don't know who they are."

The line is quiet for a moment.

"Stay together," Chi-Chi says. "And if you need to leave, get back in that ship."

"We will," Bulma says. "I'll call again when I can."

She ends the connection and puts the communicator down where she can reach it. The boys step outside with her, keeping their eyes below the sky. Piccolo stays close to the hatch.

Beyond the ridge, two armoured scouts slow in the air. One points toward the white ship on the shore. His green lens settles on Gohan: four hundred and thirty. It shifts to Goshin: five hundred and twenty. The scout glances from the numbers to the two small figures.

"Another vessel," he says quietly. "Tell Lord Freeza."

His companion touches the side of his scouter. They descend toward the landing place.

Bulma looks up and sees their armour. It is not identical to Vegeta's, but the shape of the shoulder guards is familiar.

"Piccolo," she says.

"I see them. And they're wearing scouters."

Gohan remembers Vegeta watching his lens in the city as the readings changed. He lets his ki settle, and Goshin lowers his own. Neither knows what the devices have already detected.

Gohan moves to one side of the hatch. Goshin takes the other, leaving Bulma a clear path back inside. They have not yet seen what the newcomers want, and their ship is still behind them.
