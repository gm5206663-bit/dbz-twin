## The weakness named: fights, and the impossible

**Correctly identified as the biggest hole in the writing.** The docset had laws for voice, evidence and form, and **none for violence.** You cannot run a Dragon Ball serial on domestic scenes for long.

`WRITING §11` — **a fight is a geography, a clock and a bill.** Draw the room first; set terms that are not terms of strength; **render speed as consequence, never as an adverb**; keep a damage ledger in objects; cut to ordinary onlookers; **one impossible thing, then strict physics**; end on the room, not the winner. The turn must come from something said **before** the fight, and the loser gets a habit rather than a speech.

`WRITING §12` — **the impossible is measured, not described.** Effect never mechanism · one miracle per scene · **sound arrives late** · the cost is paid in objects, never in feelings · **kill one thing and do not discuss it** · one wrong-way-round detail · report, don't rhapsodise · go to the world's eyes.

## Name them — the epithets are gone

**Called out:** the prose was calling named people by what they looked like. Chapter Two had **"the woman with blue hair" twenty-three times** when her name is Bulma, and "the old man" for Roshi, and "the tall man" for Raditz; Chapter Three had "the big man" for the Ox-King; the fight specimen — the worst of them — called Piccolo "the demon" and both boys by their height for its whole length.

`WRITING §13` makes it a law with three exceptions and no others: **before the name is known (once) · for a character who is never named at all · inside dialogue.** Quotation marks are a licence; the narration is not.

**Why it matters more here than elsewhere:** an epithet tells the reader *how to see* someone instead of who they are, and puts the camera between reader and character — **which a no-interiority book cannot afford.** It also leaks a judgement the narrator is not allowed to have: "the smaller one" is an opinion, "Goshin" is a fact. And with two four-year-old sons, "the taller boy" is not style, it is **a puzzle**.

**Fixed in all three chapters and in both specimens**, and **gated**: `check_names` fails any chapter or specimen that names a character in one line and describes them by appearance in another. The furniture stays furniture on purpose — the teacher on the bicycle, the man with the hoe, the driver, the exchange — **because they are never named, and that is now a declared choice.**

## It could not be followed — the clarity law

**The honest report:** the fight specimen could not be understood on a first read. Not an English problem on the reader's side. **A writing problem on mine.**

The specimen was built on the effects-only technique — dust in the shape of a boy, a hole in a hill, a silence — and **that technique is beautiful and insufficient.** It asks the reader to infer who moved and in what order, and **inference is exactly where a reader gets lost.** A mood can be inferred. **A fight cannot.**

So `WRITING §11` now opens with **law 0, the clarity law**, ahead of the other seven: **every paragraph answers where are they, who is acting, what changed.** Two hard rules under it — **one action per sentence**, and **no pronoun with two possible owners**, because with two boys in a scene *he* is a guess.

**And every specimen now carries a crib.** Before the prose, each fight appears as **eighteen numbered lines of plain English, no adjectives** (`O052`). *Read that first.* If the crib is unclear, the prose is broken — **and the crib also forces the writer to prove the fight has a sequence at all**, which is the actual lesson: I had written effects without a sequence and had not noticed.

**The specimen is rewritten on that basis.** Same events, same stone, same tail, same reversal — **but now with subjects and an order**, and the reasoning under it names what the old version got wrong. **Gated** by `check_crib`: a specimen without a crib ahead of its prose fails the build.

## It reads as a riddle — the law for invented material

**The second report was worse and more useful than the first:** the chapters can be followed, because they come from canon. **The invented material cannot be followed at all.** Not the sentences this time — **the material.** A teacher talking in proverbs. A boy announcing *"he's got a tail"* as though the reader were meant to gasp. Everywhere, lines that sound wise and have to be decoded.

**That is a riddle, and it was my fault.** `WRITING §14` now bans it:

- **Everybody says what they mean.** Characters may be rude, blunt, silent. **They may not speak in aphorisms.** *"If you can't beat a thing, beat the thing it needs"* is **struck out permanently**; canon's teachers say **"Grab his tail."**
- **The want and the stake in the first three lines.**
- **One character says the meaning of the scene out loud, once, in plain words** — and it must be somebody who would know.
- **A question asked is answered in the scene** — which is exactly what canon does with Goku, twice, at length.
- **The stranger test:** if a reader cannot say what happened in one plain sentence, **the paragraph is wrong even if it sounds good.** Good-sounding and unclear is the failure mode of original prose.

**And every specimen now opens with `WHAT THIS IS`, one sentence, no style**, because a scene that has to be explained afterwards should have been explained at the front.

**So the spar is rewritten, flat.** Piccolo now says the rule, says why he is holding the fight (*"you are both faster than last month, so being fast will not decide it"*), answers Gohan's question about the tail with **"Correct." "So I can just grab it?" "Nobody is stopping you."** — and gives the verdict in plain words: **"You did not beat him. He is stronger than you and he will always be stronger than you. You grabbed his tail, and that worked."** Then: **"Eat, then sleep, then we do it again tomorrow."**

**The honesty clause is in the law itself:** no script can tell an aphorism from dialogue. **This one is kept by the reader**, and a reader saying "I don't understand" is the check failing.

## The flow — three corrections, and one law I had backwards

**Three things named, all of them right.**

1. **"Nobody sees" is a cheat.** It describes an absence instead of an event and asks the reader to be impressed on instruction. **Now:** *"Goshin takes one step. He is at his stone. The ground breaks under his heels in two rings of white dust."*
2. **Do not compare the impossible to something ordinary.** I had been proud of *"like pages turning"* and *"like water out of a tipped bowl"* — and that is the flaw: **every one of those similes cuts the event down to furniture**, and once a boy's strength leaving him is "like a bowl tipped", the reader is looking at a bowl. **The impossible is described in its own physics now:** weight, heat, light, silence, the state of the ground. **And I had it exactly backwards in the notes** — the "wrong-way-round detail" I had praised was the narrator stopping to admire the effect. **Corrected in the law and in the specimens.**
3. **A character may not announce what the scene already shows.** *"He has a tail"* is a revelation to nobody — the reader has watched that tail for two chapters. **So the line is gone, and Piccolo gives the order instead:** *"His tail. Take it. It is the only part of him that is not strong."* **The one with authority speaks; the boy acts.**

**And the shape underneath all three is `WRITING §15`, the flow law:** **cause you can point at → the event → the consequence left in the world**, with no gaps and no commentary. **Goshin puts his hand flat on Gohan's chest and pushes. Gohan is on his back. The dust is still in the air.** Three sentences. **If a step is missing, the reader fills in a wrong one — and that is what "I don't understand" means.**

**Gated:** `check_flow` fails any chapter or specimen whose prose says it could not be seen, or flags the impossible as impossible.

## The scale was false — the worst error so far

**Not the prose this time. The physics.** Two boys of four and five — one at about **400**, one at about **700** — and I had a punch put a hole in a hill and dust come out of it. **That is damage from six hundred episodes later, and no amount of good sentences repairs a false scale.**

## The figures do not convert — the table was false precision
